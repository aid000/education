Description

An introduction to quantum mechanics for physics and engineering students. Techniques covered include solving the Schrödinger equation in one and three dimensions, and operator and matrix methods. Applications include infinite and finite quantum wells, barrier penetration and scattering in one dimension, the harmonic oscillator, angular momentum, central-force problems including the hydrogen atom, and spin.

A Note on Delivery Order

The official syllabus table lists the midterm exam right after the harmonic-oscillator unit. In practice, the professor's actual midterm-review handout also covers the free-particle / Fourier-transform material from the following unit — so this study guide follows the order things were actually taught and tested: Topics 1→2→3→4, then the Midterm, then Topics 5→6→7→8, then the Final, then an enrichment page. If you're cross-checking against the syllabus PDF, that's why the order looks slightly different here.

One more syllabus-vs-actual-delivery mismatch worth flagging: Topic 7 (time development, Ehrenfest's theorem, and parity) is not its own row in the syllabus's weekly table — it's split out of the "formal theory and matrix methods" block (lectures 11–12, Griffiths Ch. 3 + App. A), which the syllabus schedules under both HW #5 and HW #6. This guide separates that block into Topic 6 (HW #5: Hilbert space, operators, matrix methods) and Topic 7 (HW #6: time development and parity) to match how the course actually organized the material — so don't be surprised if a syllabus cross-check makes Topic 7 look like a "missing" week.
