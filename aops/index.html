The Art of Problem Solving, Volume 1: the Basics

A study companion to Sandor Lehoczky & Richard Rusczyk's classic (7th Edition, 2006) — the book that has trained generations of competition mathematicians. Unlike a school textbook, AoPS teaches methods, not memorization: the formulas are derived so they become obvious, and the real learning lives in the examples and exercises.

🏆 What this prepares you for

  • MATHCOUNTS — the premier middle-school contest (school → chapter → state → national)
  • AMC 8 / 10 / 12 — the American Mathematics Competitions
  • AIME — the American Invitational Mathematics Exam (next step after a strong AMC)
  • Mandelbrot, AHSME (legacy), and early USAMTS proof-writing

🧭 How to use this guide

  • Chapters are organized into the four AMC categories — Algebra, Geometry, Combinatorics, Number Theory — each its own page.
  • Read the derivations — every "rule" box is justified, not just stated.
  • Do the exercises before peeking — they're collapsed on purpose.
  • 💣 Watch-out boxes flag classic mistakes; 💡 Big Picture & ⚛️ Physics link boxes connect the math to the wider world.
💡 From the authors "This book is about methods, not memory. If you find yourself memorizing formulas, you are missing the point." Treat every exercise as the actual lesson.

Problem-Solving Strategy

AoPS isn't an AP exam, so instead of an "FRQ strategy" this captures how to attack a hard competition problem — the through-line of the whole book.

  • Reach for a common base / common structure. Many problems collapse the moment you rewrite everything in the same form.
  • Convert between equivalent notations (log ↔ exponential, radical ↔ fractional power) to find the easy view.
  • Look for structure before arithmetic. Cancel, factor, and simplify symbolically — brute force invites errors.
  • Mind the 💣 traps: $0^0$, only-positive radicals, distributing over sums vs. products, stacked exponents.
  • Know your sources. AHSME/AMC reward clean insight; AIME answers are integers 0–999, so estimate the form of your answer.
