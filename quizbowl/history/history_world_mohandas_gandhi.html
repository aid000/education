Quick Overview

Full nameMohandas Karamchand Gandhi
Dates1869–1948 (born Porbandar, Gujarat; assassinated Jan 30, 1948)
TitleMahatma ("Great Soul") — conferred by Rabindranath Tagore
RoleLeader of Indian independence movement against British rule
Core methodSatyagraha ("truth-force" / nonviolent resistance)
AssassinNathuram Godse (Hindu nationalist; shot Gandhi in New Delhi)

Defining traits & classic quizbowl clues

  • Satyagraha — the philosophical/tactical framework of nonviolent resistance; coined in South Africa.
  • Spinning wheel (charkha) — symbol of economic self-sufficiency and resistance to British cloth; appears on the Indian National Congress flag.
  • Dressed in homespun white dhoti and shawl; wore round wire-rimmed glasses.
  • Influenced Martin Luther King Jr., Nelson Mandela, and civil-rights movements worldwide.
  • Called Bapu ("Father") by followers; "Father of the Nation" in India.
  • Frequent fasting as political pressure — "fast unto death" was a signature tactic.

Phases at a glance

  • South Africa (1893–1914) — developed satyagraha fighting anti-Indian discrimination; led Natal Indian Congress.
  • Early India campaigns (1917–1922) — Champaran, Khilafat, Non-Cooperation Movement.
  • Civil disobedience era (1930–1934) — Salt March, Round Table Conferences, Communal Award fasts.
  • Quit India & partition (1942–1947) — Quit India Movement, partition violence, final fasts.

High-Yield Events (Very Common)

The 6 events / acts most likely to be the answer or anchor a tossup. power = hard early clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

The Salt March (Dandi March)
March 12 – April 6, 1930 · Gujarat coast
recog241-mile (388 km) march from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi on the Arabian Sea coast.
recogGandhi and 78 followers picked up salt from the sea to defy the British salt monopoly and salt tax.
recogSparked mass civil disobedience across India; ~60,000 arrested including Gandhi.
powerGandhi wrote a letter to Viceroy Lord Irwin announcing the march eleven days in advance. American journalist Webb Miller reported the Dharasana salt works raid (May 1930) — protesters beaten without resistance — to worldwide horror. The Gandhi–Irwin Pact (March 1931) followed.

Non-Cooperation Movement
1920–1922 · All-India
recogGandhi called on Indians to boycott British goods, courts, schools, and honors; return knighthoods.
recogLaunched in response to the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (Amritsar, April 1919) and the Rowlatt Act.
recogGandhi suspended the movement after the Chauri Chaura incident (Feb 1922): a mob killed 22 police.
powerThe suspension caused a dramatic split with Subhas Chandra Bose and other leaders who wanted continued militancy. Gandhi was arrested in March 1922, tried for sedition, and sentenced to six years (released after two years for appendicitis surgery).
wiki ↗
Quit India Movement
August 8–9, 1942 · All-India
recogGandhi's speech at Gowalia Tank, Bombay: "Do or Die" (Karo ya maro).
recogDemanded immediate British withdrawal from India; the All India Congress Committee passed the resolution.
recogBritish arrested Gandhi and nearly the entire Congress leadership within 24 hours; Gandhi imprisoned at Aga Khan Palace, Pune.
powerGandhi's wife Kasturba Gandhi died in detention at Aga Khan Palace (Feb 1944); Gandhi himself fell ill and was released in May 1944 before the movement formally ended. The Cripps Mission (March 1942) failure preceded the call.
wiki ↗
Champaran Satyagraha
1917 · Champaran, Bihar
recogGandhi's first major civil-disobedience campaign in India — on behalf of indigo farmers.
recogBritish planters forced peasants to grow indigo (neel) on 3/20ths of their land under the tinkathia system.
recogGandhi was initially ordered to leave Champaran; his refusal and arrest galvanized the campaign.
powerThe Champaran Agrarian Act (1918) officially abolished the tinkathia system. Rajendra Prasad (later India's first President) and J.B. Kripalani assisted Gandhi there — a frequent hard clue.
wiki ↗
Satyagraha in South Africa
1906–1914 · Transvaal / Natal, South Africa
recogGandhi organized Indian resistance to the Transvaal Asiatic Registration Act ("Black Act") requiring all Indians to register and carry passes.
recogCoined the term "satyagraha" (lit. "truth-force") to replace "passive resistance."
recogFounded the Natal Indian Congress (1894) and Phoenix Settlement (1904) and Tolstoy Farm (1910) communal ashrams.
powerGandhi's burning of registration certificates at the Hamidia Mosque, Johannesburg (1908) — a frequent hard clue. The campaign ended with the Indian Relief Act (1914), negotiated with General Jan Smuts. Gandhi later said Smuts remarked he was "glad to see the back of" him.
wiki ↗
Assassination & Legacy
January 30, 1948 · Birla House, New Delhi
recogShot three times by Nathuram Godse, a Hindu nationalist, at a multi-faith prayer meeting.
recogGandhi's last words reported as "Hey Ram" ("Oh God").
recogGodse was a member of the Hindu Mahasabha; he and co-conspirator Narayan Apte were hanged in November 1949.
powerThis was the third assassination attempt on Gandhi that month — a bomb had been detonated at a prayer meeting on Jan 20. Jawaharlal Nehru announced his death on radio: "The light has gone out of our lives." Gandhi's birthday, October 2, is observed as the International Day of Non-Violence (UN, 2007).

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-known events, campaigns, and works — one-line each. These appear as early/hard tossup clues.

1888–1891
Law Studies in London
  • Studied at Inner Temple; called to the bar 1891.
  • Vowed vegetarianism to his mother; joined the London Vegetarian Society.
1893
Pietermaritzburg Railway Incident
  • Gandhi — holding a first-class ticket — was thrown off the train at Pietermaritzburg for refusing to move to the "van" coach.
  • Often cited as the turning-point that committed him to fighting racial discrimination.
1904
Phoenix Settlement (Natal)
  • Utopian communal farm near Durban; published the journal Indian Opinion there.
  • Inspired by John Ruskin's Unto This Last.
1917–1918
Kheda Satyagraha
  • Peasants of Kheda district, Gujarat sought revenue relief after crop failure.
  • British ultimately suspended revenue collection — alongside Champaran, establishes Gandhi's agrarian campaigns.
1918
Ahmedabad Mill Strike
  • Gandhi's first hunger strike in India, supporting textile workers in Ahmedabad.
  • Workers won a 35% wage increase via arbitration.
1919
Rowlatt Act Protests / Jallianwala Bagh
  • Gandhi called for a hartal (general strike) against the Rowlatt Act (indefinite detention without trial).
  • Jallianwala Bagh massacre (April 13, 1919): Brig. Gen. Reginald Dyer ordered troops to fire on a crowd in Amritsar — ~379 killed officially (estimates higher).
  • Gandhi returned his Kaiser-i-Hind medal to the Viceroy in protest.
1920–1924
Khilafat Movement Alliance
  • Gandhi allied with Muslim leaders Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali (the Ali Brothers) to defend the Ottoman Caliphate.
  • United Hindu–Muslim opposition to British rule; movement collapsed after Atatürk abolished the Caliphate (1924).
1932
Poona Pact
  • Gandhi fasted against the Communal Award (separate electorates for untouchables / "Depressed Classes").
  • Negotiated with B.R. Ambedkar; outcome: reserved seats within a single Hindu electorate instead of a separate one.
  • Ambedkar criticized the pact as coercing concessions under duress.
1930–1931
Gandhi–Irwin Pact
  • Agreement between Gandhi and Viceroy Lord Irwin (later Lord Halifax).
  • British: release political prisoners; Gandhi: suspend civil disobedience, attend Round Table Conference in London.
1945–1947
Partition and Communal Violence
  • Gandhi opposed partition of India and Pakistan; walked villages in Noakhali (Bengal) calming riots.
  • Fasted in Calcutta (September 1947) and Delhi (January 1948) to stop communal violence.
  • Called "One-Man Boundary Force" by Mountbatten for his peace work.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key terms & concepts

  • Satyagraha — "truth-force / soul-force"; nonviolent resistance
  • Ahimsa — nonviolence; foundational ethical principle
  • Swaraj — self-rule / home rule
  • Swadeshi — economic self-sufficiency; boycott of foreign goods
  • Hartal — general strike / work stoppage
  • Charkha — spinning wheel; symbol on Congress flag
  • Brahmacharya — celibacy; Gandhi's personal vow from 1906
  • Varnashrama — Gandhi's complex views on caste, opposed untouchability

Famous quotes

  • "Do or Die" — Quit India speech, 1942
  • "Be the change you wish to see" — widely attributed (paraphrase)
  • "An eye for an eye makes the whole world blind" — attributed
  • "First they ignore you, then they laugh at you…" — attributed (likely misattributed)
  • "Hey Ram" — last words, Jan 30, 1948
  • "My life is my message" — frequent attribution

Key people & relationships

  • Kasturba Gandhi — wife; died in detention, 1944
  • Rabindranath Tagore — gave Gandhi the title "Mahatma"
  • Jawaharlal Nehru — protégé; first Prime Minister of India
  • B.R. Ambedkar — rival on untouchability; Poona Pact antagonist
  • Muhammad Ali Jinnah — INC rival; founder of Pakistan
  • Subhas Chandra Bose — rival who wanted armed struggle
  • Gopal Krishna Gokhale — Gandhi's political mentor in India
  • Lord Mountbatten — last Viceroy; called Gandhi "One-Man Boundary Force"

Numbers & dates to know

  • 1869 — born Porbandar, Gujarat
  • 241 miles — length of Salt March
  • March 12–April 6, 1930 — Salt March dates
  • ~60,000 — arrested during Salt March civil disobedience
  • ~379 — official death toll, Jallianwala Bagh
  • October 2, 1869 — Gandhi's birthday; UN Non-Violence Day
  • January 30, 1948 — assassination date
  • August 15, 1947 — Indian Independence Day

Ashrams & places

  • Sabarmati Ashram — Ahmedabad; base for Salt March
  • Sevagram Ashram — Wardha; headquarters from 1936
  • Phoenix Settlement — Natal, South Africa
  • Tolstoy Farm — Transvaal, South Africa
  • Aga Khan Palace — Pune; imprisoned here, 1942–44
  • Birla House — New Delhi; assassination site
  • Dandi — coastal village; end of Salt March

Philosophical influences

  • Leo Tolstoy — corresponded with Gandhi; nonviolence / pacifism
  • John Ruskin (Unto This Last) — manual labor & simple living
  • Henry David Thoreau (Civil Disobedience) — influenced satyagraha
  • Bhagavad Gita — Gandhi's "spiritual dictionary"; read daily
  • Jainism — ahimsa, asceticism (Gujarati background)
  • Sermon on the Mount — Christian influence on nonviolence

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Martin Luther King Jr.MLK explicitly cited Gandhi as his model. MLK = American civil-rights movement, 1950s–60s, Montgomery Bus Boycott, "I Have a Dream," Birmingham, Nobel Peace Prize 1964. Gandhi = British India, Salt March, satyagraha. If the clue is India or South Africa, answer Gandhi.
Jawaharlal NehruNehru = first Prime Minister of India (1947–1964), "Discovery of India," Non-Aligned Movement, architect of Indian socialism. Gandhi did NOT become PM; he was assassinated before the first elections.
B.R. AmbedkarAmbedkar = Dalit leader and "Father of the Indian Constitution", architect of constitutional law, vocal critic of Gandhi on caste. Poona Pact is where they appear together — but the answer differs by whether the clue praises or criticizes Gandhi's handling of untouchability.
Muhammad Ali JinnahJinnah = founder/first Governor-General of Pakistan, led the Muslim League, two-nation theory. If the clue says "founder of Pakistan" or "two-nation theory," answer Jinnah, not Gandhi.
Subhas Chandra BoseBose = Indian National Army (INA), allied with Axis powers against Britain, "Netaji," mysterious death 1945. Opposed Gandhi's nonviolence. If the clue involves armed resistance or the INA, answer Bose.
Nelson MandelaMandela = South Africa, ANC, apartheid, Robben Island, 27 years imprisonment, President 1994. Mandela acknowledged Gandhi's influence in South Africa but was willing to use violence (Umkhonto we Sizwe). Different country, different era.
Gopal Krishna GokhaleGokhale = Gandhi's mentor, moderate wing of INC, Servants of India Society. Gokhale died 1915 before major independence campaigns. If the clue is "Gandhi's political guru," this is Gokhale.
Salt March vs. other marchesSalt March (1930) = Gandhi, Dandi, 241 miles, salt tax. Dandi ≠ Dharasana (the Dharasana salt works raid was a follow-up action led by Sarojini Naidu, not Gandhi). Long March = Mao Zedong, China, 1934–35. March on Washington = MLK, 1963.
