Quick Overview

Born / Died15 Aug 1769, Ajaccio, Corsica — 5 May 1821, Saint Helena
TitlesFirst Consul (1799–1804); Emperor of the French (1804–1814, 1815)
DynastyHouse of Bonaparte (Bonapartist line)
Key warsFrench Revolutionary Wars; Napoleonic Wars (1803–1815)
WivesJoséphine de Beauharnais (m. 1796, div. 1809); Marie Louise of Austria (m. 1810)
ExileFirst: Elba (1814–1815); Second: Saint Helena (1815–1821)

Defining traits & why he matters in quizbowl

  • Military genius: the "central position" strategy, rapid marches, corps system. Won ~60 of ~70 pitched battles.
  • Legal/administrative reformer: the Napoleonic Code (Code Civil, 1804) — still the basis of French civil law.
  • Spread of Revolutionary ideals across Europe — nationalism, legal equality, abolition of feudalism in conquered lands.
  • Concordat of 1801 — reconciled France with the Catholic Church after Revolutionary rupture.
  • Louisiana Purchase (1803) — sold Louisiana Territory to the United States after the Saint-Domingue rebellion.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • The hand-in-waistcoat pose and bicorne hat (often the final visual ID).
  • "Grognards" (Old Guard), the Grande Armée, and the Continental System.
  • Exiled to Saint Helena in the South Atlantic — by far the most frequently cited end-of-life clue.
  • Defeated at Waterloo (18 June 1815) by Wellington and Blücher — single most-asked battle.

High-Yield Events (Very Common)

The events, battles, and reforms most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

Battle of Austerlitz
2 December 1805 · Moravia (modern Czech Republic) · vs. Russia & Austria
recogNapoleon feigned weakness on his right, luring the allies to attack; then punched through their weakened center at the Pratzen Heights.
recogResulted in the Treaty of Pressburg; Austria surrendered Venetia and Tyrol; Holy Roman Empire dissolved in 1806.
powerNapoleon called it his greatest victory; he declared "the sun of Austerlitz" before later battles. Fought on the first anniversary of his coronation. Allied commanders: Tsar Alexander I and Emperor Francis II.
wiki ↗
Battle of Waterloo
18 June 1815 · near Brussels, Belgium · vs. Britain & Prussia
recogWellington held the ridge of Mont-Saint-Jean; the Prussian army under Gebhard Leberecht von Blücher arrived late in the day and sealed the defeat.
recogMarshal Ney's cavalry charges failed to break the British squares; the Imperial Guard's final assault was repulsed, triggering the rout.
powerNapoleon delayed the morning attack (perhaps awaiting drier ground); many historians cite this as fatal. Preceded by French victory at Ligny (16 Jun) and a draw at Quatre Bras. Led to his second abdication and exile to Saint Helena.
wiki ↗
Coup of 18 Brumaire
9 November 1799 · Saint-Cloud, Paris region
recogOverthrew the Directory; replaced it with the Consulate, with Napoleon as First Consul.
recogNamed after the Revolutionary calendar date (18 Brumaire Year VIII). Abbot Sieyès was Napoleon's co-conspirator, expecting to dominate the new regime.
powerNapoleon faced hostile deputies at the Council of Five Hundred at Saint-Cloud; his brother Lucien Bonaparte (president of the Council) called in grenadiers to clear the hall. Often cited as the end of the French Revolution.
wiki ↗
Imperial Coronation
2 December 1804 · Notre-Dame Cathedral, Paris
recogPope Pius VII traveled to Paris; Napoleon took the crown from the Pope's hands and crowned himself, then crowned Joséphine.
recogDepicted in Jacques-Louis David's monumental painting The Coronation of Napoleon (~10 × 6 m), now in the Louvre.
powerNapoleon held a plebiscite in 1804 with over 3.5 million votes approving the empire. Beethoven famously scratched Napoleon's name from the Eroica Symphony's dedication upon hearing Napoleon declare himself Emperor.
wiki ↗
French Invasion of Russia
June – December 1812
recogGrande Armée (~600,000 troops) crosses the Niemen River; Russia retreats, scorching the earth. Napoleon enters Moscow in September — only to find it burning.
recogBattle of Borodino (7 Sep 1812) — bloodiest single day of the Napoleonic Wars; Russian commander Kutuzov. Both sides claim victory; Russia retreats.
powerNapoleon waited five weeks in Moscow for Tsar Alexander I to negotiate — no offer came. The winter retreat cost ~380,000 men; crossing the Berezina River (Nov) near-destroyed the remaining army. The disaster triggered the War of the Sixth Coalition.
wiki ↗
Napoleonic Code (Code Civil)
Enacted 21 March 1804 · France (and conquered territories)
recogCodified civil law: equality before the law, property rights, secular authority over the Church, religious toleration.
recogReplaced the patchwork of regional laws across France; spread to Belgium, Netherlands, Italy, Spain, parts of Germany, and Latin America.
powerNapoleon said: "My glory is not to have won forty battles...Waterloo will efface the memory of so many victories...but what nothing will efface, what will live eternally, is my Civil Code." Still the basis of French private law today and influenced the legal systems of Quebec, Louisiana, and over 50 countries.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Titles & epithets

  • "The Little Corporal" — term of affection from his troops
  • "The Emperor" / "Napoleon I"
  • "Boney" — British popular nickname
  • Consul for Life (1802) → Emperor (1804)
  • "l'Ogre de Corse" (the Corsican Ogre) — propaganda name

Key quotes

  • "Impossible is a word found only in the dictionary of fools."
  • "An army marches on its stomach."
  • "From the heights of these Pyramids, forty centuries look down upon us."
  • "Able was I ere I saw Elba." — apocryphal palindrome
  • Beethoven dedicating (then scratching) the Eroica Symphony to Napoleon.

Treaties & numbers

  • Treaty of Campo Formio (1797) — ends Italian Campaign
  • Treaty of Pressburg (1805) — after Austerlitz
  • Treaties of Tilsit (1807) — raft meeting with Alexander I
  • Treaty of Schönbrunn (1809) — after Wagram
  • ~600,000 troops in the Russian invasion
  • 18 June 1815 — Waterloo date (very frequently asked)
  • 21 March 1804 — Napoleonic Code enacted

People around Napoleon

  • Joséphine — first wife; divorced for failing to produce heir
  • Marie Louise of Austria — second wife; mother of Napoleon II (King of Rome)
  • Talleyrand — foreign minister; later betrayed Napoleon
  • Fouché — minister of police
  • Marshal Ney — "the bravest of the brave"; commanded at Waterloo
  • Marshal Davout — undefeated corps commander
  • Kutuzov — Russian commander at Borodino
  • Wellington — defeated him at Waterloo
  • Blücher — Prussian marshal; "Marshal Forwards"
  • Sieyès — co-conspirator in 18 Brumaire coup

Symbols & visual markers

  • Bicorne hat (worn sideways, front-to-back)
  • Hand-in-waistcoat pose (portraits by David, Ingres)
  • Imperial eagle — standard of the Grande Armée
  • Bee — Bonapartist symbol (embroidered on coronation robes)
  • Red porphyry sarcophagus — tomb at Les Invalides, Paris
  • Portraits: Jacques-Louis David and Ingres

Institutions & reforms

  • Napoleonic Code — civil law; equality before the law
  • Lycée system — standardized secondary education
  • Bank of France — founded 1800
  • Legion of Honor — merit-based decoration, still given today
  • Prefect system — centralized local administration
  • Institut de France — member after Egyptian expedition

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Julius CaesarBoth crossed a famous river (Caesar: Rubicon; Napoleon: Niemen/Alps), both staged coups, both became dictators. Key difference: Caesar was Roman, assassinated on the Ides of March (44 BC); Napoleon died on Saint Helena in 1821. If the question mentions Gaul or senators, it's Caesar.
Alexander the GreatBoth conquered vast territories eastward. Alexander: Macedonia, 4th century BC, died in Babylon. Napoleon: 19th-century France; if the Battle of the Hydaspes or Persia appears, it's Alexander.
Frederick the Great (Frederick II of Prussia)Both brilliant field commanders; Frederick's era is Seven Years' War (1756–1763), not Napoleonic Wars. Frederick = Prussia, flute-playing, Enlightened despot. If "Sans-Souci" or Voltaire appears, it's Frederick.
Duke of WellingtonWellington defeated Napoleon at Waterloo; same era, opposite side. Wellington is British (Iron Duke), Victor at Salamanca and Waterloo. Both appear in the same tossups — read which side the clue favors.
Maximilien RobespierreBoth French Revolution figures. Robespierre = Reign of Terror (1793–1794), guillotined; Napoleon came to power after Robespierre's fall. If "Committee of Public Safety" or "Thermidorian Reaction" appears, it's Robespierre.
Czar Alexander I vs. Czar Alexander IIAlexander I fought Napoleon (Austerlitz, Tilsit, 1812 campaign). Alexander II emancipated the serfs (1861) and was assassinated by a bomb. Same name, different century.
Elba vs. Saint HelenaElba = first exile (1814–1815), off Tuscany; Napoleon escaped after ~10 months. Saint Helena = second, permanent exile (1815–1821), remote South Atlantic; he died there.

Cross-discipline big picture Napoleon links across multiple quizbowl categories: Beethoven's Eroica Symphony (music) was dedicated to — then stripped of — his name; Jacques-Louis David's Coronation of Napoleon and Napoleon Crossing the Alps (art); Tolstoy's War and Peace (literature) dramatizes the 1812 invasion; the Rosetta Stone (Egyptology/linguistics) was found by his Egyptian expedition; the Napoleonic Code spread to Louisiana and Quebec, shaping their legal systems today.
