Quick Overview

Full nameVladimir Ilyich Ulyanov; pseudonym "Lenin"
Born / Died22 Apr 1870, Simbirsk — 21 Jan 1924, Gorki
RoleLeader of the Bolshevik Party; first head of the Soviet state
IdeologyMarxism-Leninism; vanguard-party theory
Key textWhat Is to Be Done? (1902) — blueprint for vanguard party
In powerChairman of the Council of People's Commissars, 1917–1924

Defining traits (quizbowl giveaways)

  • Born Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov in Simbirsk (later renamed Ulyanovsk in his honor).
  • Older brother Alexander Ulyanov executed 1887 for plotting to kill Tsar Alexander III — radicalizing event.
  • Led the October Revolution (7 Nov 1917 NS) from the Smolny Institute in Petrograd.
  • Arrived in Russia from Swiss exile via the "Sealed Train" through Germany, April 1917.
  • Issued the April Theses calling for immediate socialist revolution; "All power to the Soviets."
  • Mausoleum on Red Square, Moscow — body embalmed and on display since 1924.

Classic quizbowl answer lines

  • "This man wrote What Is to Be Done?" or "this author of Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism."
  • "This leader arrived in the Sealed Train" or "this man issued the April Theses."
  • "This man's New Economic Policy (NEP)" or "this leader signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk."

High-Yield Events (Timeline)

Key episodes most likely to anchor a tossup. power = obscure early clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

1887
Execution of Alexander Ulyanov
St. Petersburg
recogLenin's older brother hanged for his role in a People's Will plot to assassinate Tsar Alexander III.
powerThe family's fall from respectability pushed Vladimir toward revolutionary Marxism; Lenin later said he took a different path — party organization, not terrorism.
1900–1903
What Is to Be Done? & the Bolshevik-Menshevik Split
Exile (Munich / London)
recog1902 pamphlet argues for a tight, disciplined vanguard party of professional revolutionaries, not a mass socialist party.
recogAt the 1903 RSDLP Congress (Brussels/London), his faction wins a vote → Bolsheviki ("majority"); opponents become Mensheviki ("minority").
powerEpigraph quotes Nikolai Chernyshevsky's novel of the same title; the split on party membership (clause 1) pits Lenin vs. Julius Martov.
April 1917
Sealed Train Return & April Theses
Petrograd (Finland Station)
recogGermany grants Lenin safe passage in a sealed railway car from Zurich to Petrograd, hoping he will destabilize Russia.
recogArrives at Finland Station; immediately issues the April Theses — no support for the Provisional Government, immediate socialist revolution, "All power to the Soviets."
powerThe German foreign ministry wired funds through the intermediary Alexander Parvus (Helphand); the sealed train is a recurring "German spy" charge against Lenin by his opponents.
25 Oct 1917 (OS) / 7 Nov (NS)
October Revolution (Bolshevik Coup)
Petrograd — Smolny Institute / Winter Palace
recogArmed Bolsheviks and the Red Guard seize key Petrograd infrastructure; the Winter Palace (seat of the Provisional Government) falls overnight.
recogLenin directs operations from the Smolny Institute; the Second Congress of Soviets ratifies the transfer of power.
powerLeon Trotsky chairs the Petrograd Soviet Military Revolutionary Committee that organizes the military operation; Menshevik leader Yuli Martov walks out of the Congress protesting.
March 1918
Treaty of Brest-Litovsk
Brest-Litovsk (modern Belarus)
recogSoviet Russia exits World War I, ceding Ukraine, Finland, the Baltic states, Belarus, and the Caucasus to Germany/Austria.
recogLenin overrides Trotsky's "no war, no peace" position and Left SR / Left Bolshevik opposition — he calls it a "robber peace" worth accepting to preserve the revolution.
powerChief Soviet negotiator is initially Trotsky, who stalls; Trotsky's delay triggers a German offensive; Lenin forces through ratification at the Seventh Party Congress. Treaty annulled after Germany's November 1918 defeat.
1918–1920
Russian Civil War & Red Terror
Across former Russian Empire
recogReds (Bolsheviks) vs. Whites (anti-Bolshevik coalition) backed by foreign intervention (Britain, France, USA, Japan).
recogRed Terror (officially from Sep 1918): mass executions of class enemies by the Cheka (secret police founded by Felix Dzerzhinsky).
powerTsar Nicholas II and family executed at Yekaterinburg, July 1918. Lenin's near-assassination by Fanny Kaplan (Aug 1918) accelerates the Red Terror. War Communism (prodrazvyorstka grain requisitions) causes famine.
1921
New Economic Policy (NEP)
Moscow
recogFollowing the Kronstadt Uprising (sailors' revolt, March 1921) and widespread famine, Lenin announces the NEP — a tactical retreat allowing limited private trade.
recogReplaces grain requisitioning with a fixed tax-in-kind (prodnalog); peasants may sell surpluses on the market.
powerLenin calls it "state capitalism" — a temporary compromise with market forces. Left Communists (e.g., Alexandra Kollontai's Workers' Opposition) view it as betrayal. Stalin later reverses it with collectivization.
1922–1924
Illness, "Lenin's Testament," and Death
Gorki estate
recogSeries of strokes (May 1922, Dec 1922, Mar 1923) incapacitate Lenin; he dies 21 January 1924.
recogLenin's Testament (dictated Dec 1922–Jan 1923) warns the party about Stalin's rudeness and concentration of power; recommends removing him as General Secretary.
powerThe Testament also criticizes Trotsky's excessive self-confidence, Zinoviev and Kamenev's October wavering, and Bukharin's unMarxist economics. Stalin suppresses the Testament at the 13th Party Congress (1924) with help from Zinoviev and Kamenev.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-discussed episodes, texts, and facts that appear as early/hard tossup clues.

1895 League of Struggle for the Emancipation of the Working Class Lenin's first revolutionary organization in St. Petersburg; arrested same year, exiled to Siberia (Shushenskoye) 1897–1900.
1900 Iskra ("The Spark") Revolutionary newspaper co-founded by Lenin in Munich; masthead motto: "From a spark a flame will be kindled."
1905 1905 Revolution — "Dress Rehearsal" Lenin calls 1905 the dress rehearsal for 1917; introduces theory of democratic centralism and debates with Trotsky over "permanent revolution."
1914 Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism Written 1916 in Zurich; argues WWI is a capitalist war for colonial markets; defines the theory of imperialism as monopoly capitalism.
Jul 1917 July Days & Lenin's Flight to Finland Spontaneous armed demonstrations in Petrograd; Provisional Government accuses Lenin of being a German agent; he flees to Razliv (Finland).
1917 State and Revolution Written Aug–Sep 1917 while hiding in Finland; argues state is a bourgeois instrument that must be smashed, replaced by the "dictatorship of the proletariat."
Jan 1918 Dissolution of the Constituent Assembly After Socialist Revolutionaries win majority, Lenin dissolves the Assembly after one day — "a step backward" toward bourgeois democracy he called it.
1919 Comintern (Third International) Founded Lenin establishes the Communist International in Moscow to spread world revolution; imposes 21 Conditions for membership.
1921 Famine of 1921–22 Caused by War Communism + drought; ~5 million dead; Lenin accepts American Relief Administration (Herbert Hoover) aid.
1922 Formation of the USSR December 1922; Lenin opposes Stalin's plan to absorb republics into Russia ("Georgianization"); advocates a looser federal union.
1924 Mausoleum & "Leninism" Body embalmed against family's wishes; Petrograd renamed Leningrad; Stalin canonizes "Leninism" as official ideology.

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Leon TrotskyTrotsky organized the October coup's military side (as MRC chair) and later the Red Army. Lenin is the ideological/political leader. Trotsky's theory is "permanent revolution"; Lenin's is the vanguard party. Trotsky is assassinated by Stalin's agent (1940, Mexico City); Lenin dies of natural causes (strokes, 1924).
Joseph StalinStalin comes to power after Lenin's death; Lenin's Testament specifically warned about Stalin. Stalin = collectivization, Five-Year Plans, purges. Lenin = NEP, Brest-Litovsk, October Revolution.
Karl MarxMarx writes the theoretical foundations (Capital, Communist Manifesto, 1848). Lenin applies and adapts Marx to Russian conditions — the vanguard-party innovation is Lenin's. Marx is 19th-c. German; Lenin is 20th-c. Russian.
Alexander KerenskyKerensky led the Provisional Government (Feb–Oct 1917) that Lenin overthrew. Both figures in 1917 Russian politics — Kerensky = moderate socialist; Lenin = radical Bolshevik. Kerensky flees to the West; Lenin wins.
Rosa LuxemburgLuxemburg is a German/Polish Marxist revolutionary, murdered 1919 during the Spartacist uprising in Berlin. She actually critiqued Lenin's vanguard-party model as insufficiently democratic. Different country, different fate.
Mao ZedongMao adapted Leninism to a peasant base in China (1949). Key difference: Lenin relies on the urban proletariat; Mao centers peasants. Mao explicitly claims to build on Lenin but is a distinct 20th-c. figure.

Big Picture — Why Lenin Matters Across Categories Lenin sits at the intersection of European history, political philosophy, and economics. In history: the October Revolution ends Russian involvement in WWI and reshapes the 20th century. In political theory: What Is to Be Done? defines the Leninist vanguard party, debated against Luxemburg's democratic socialism and Kautsky's parliamentary Marxism. In economics: the NEP is a canonical example of market socialism. In geopolitics: the Comintern seeds communist parties worldwide, directly linking Lenin tossups to Chinese, Cuban, and Vietnamese revolutionary history. The sealed train and October Revolution are the most common answer-line clues; the Testament and NEP are the most common hard early clues.
