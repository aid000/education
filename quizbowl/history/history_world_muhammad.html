Quick Overview

Born / Diedc. 570 CE, Mecca — 632 CE, Medina
Tribe / ClanQuraysh tribe, Hashim clan, Arabia
RoleFounder of Islam; Prophet and statesman
Key textQuran (God's word revealed through him); Hadith (his reported sayings)
SuccessionNo designated heir → Abu Bakr becomes first caliph (632)
Epithets"al-Amin" (the Trustworthy); "Seal of the Prophets" (Khatam al-Nabiyyin)

Defining context

  • Born into Meccan merchant family; orphaned early — father Abdullah died before birth, mother Amina died age ~6; raised by grandfather Abd al-Muttalib, then uncle Abu Talib.
  • Worked as a trader; married wealthy widow Khadijah (age ~25 vs her ~40) — his first and for years only wife; she bore his surviving children including daughter Fatimah.
  • First revelation in Cave Hira (610 CE), near Mecca — angel Jibril (Gabriel) commands "Recite" (Iqra).
  • Unified fragmented Arabian tribes under Islam; conquered Mecca 630 CE; died 632 CE.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Cave Hira / first revelation — the single most-given early clue.
  • Hijra (migration Mecca → Medina, 622 CE) — start of the Islamic calendar.
  • Night Journey (Isra and Mi'raj) — ascent through heavens on the creature Buraq.
  • Wife Khadijah and companion-scribe Ali ibn Abi Talib (also cousin and son-in-law).

High-Yield Events (Very Common)

The events / episodes most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early buzz-here clue. recog = middle/recognition clue.

First Revelation — Cave Hira
610 CE · Jabal al-Nour, near Mecca
recogMuhammad meditating alone in Cave Hira on Jabal al-Nour; angel Jibril (Gabriel) appears and commands "Iqra" (Recite/Read).
recogFirst revealed verses = opening of Surah al-Alaq ("Read in the name of your Lord who created"). Muhammad returns shaking; Khadijah comforts him and becomes first Muslim.
powerHe initially feared he was possessed (majnun); Khadijah's cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal — a Christian scholar — confirmed the visitor was the angel of revelation. Revelations continue ~23 years until death.
Hijra — Migration to Medina
622 CE · Mecca → Yathrib (Medina)
recogAfter persecution in Mecca, Muhammad and followers migrate to Yathrib (renamed Medina al-Nabi = "City of the Prophet"). Year 1 of the Islamic (Hijri) calendar.
recogClose companion Abu Bakr accompanies him; they hide in Cave Thawr for three days to evade Quraysh assassins.
powerIn Medina, Muhammad drafts the Constitution of Medina — a compact between Muslim and Jewish tribes defining rights and mutual defense. The ummah (Muslim community) as a political entity is born here.
Battle of Badr
624 CE (2 AH) · Near Badr, Hejaz
recog~313 Muslims defeat a Quraysh force of ~950 near the wells of Badr. First major Muslim military victory; reinforces the divine mandate of the new faith.
recogKey Quraysh leaders killed: Abu Jahl ("Father of Ignorance") slain; spoils divided per new Quranic rules.
powerQuran's Surah al-Anfal (ch. 8) references Badr directly; angels (jibril) said to have fought alongside Muslims. The victory is seen as providential proof — a cornerstone of early Islamic identity.
Battle of Uhud
625 CE · Mount Uhud, near Medina
recogMuslims suffer a setback: Quraysh (~3,000 under Abu Sufyan) defeat the Muslims after archers leave their position against orders. Muhammad himself wounded.
recogMuhammad's uncle Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib ("Lion of God") killed; his liver allegedly mutilated by Hind bint Utbah. A key test of Muslim faith and resilience.
powerQuran addresses the loss in Surah Ali Imran — "Do you think you will enter paradise while God has not yet seen who among you strive hard?" Used to teach believers that defeat tests but does not discredit the faith.
Battle of the Trench (Khandaq)
627 CE · Medina
recogPersian-advised Salman al-Farisi suggests digging a trench (khandaq) around Medina; a coalition of ~10,000 Quraysh and allies fail to cross it and retreat.
recogAlso called the Battle of Ahzab (Confederates). After the siege, Muhammad expels/executes the Banu Qurayza tribe for alleged treachery.
powerThe trench strategy was unprecedented in Arab warfare; after the failed siege the Quraysh never again mounted a comparable offensive, shifting momentum decisively to Muhammad.
Night Journey and Ascension (Isra and Mi'raj)
c. 621 CE · Mecca → Jerusalem → Heaven
recogMuhammad is transported from Mecca to Jerusalem (Isra) on the winged creature Buraq, then ascends through the seven heavens (Mi'raj), meeting earlier prophets (Abraham, Moses, Jesus).
recogIn Jerusalem he leads all prophets in prayer at the al-Aqsa Mosque site; in heaven he receives the command to pray 50 times daily, negotiated down to 5 on Moses's advice.
powerThe rock from which Muhammad ascended is enshrined under the Dome of the Rock (Qubbat al-Sakhra) in Jerusalem, built by Umayyad caliph Abd al-Malik in 691 CE. The Isra Mi'raj is commemorated on 27 Rajab.
Conquest of Mecca (Fath Makkah)
630 CE (8 AH) · Mecca
recogMuslim army of ~10,000 marches on Mecca; Abu Sufyan converts and the city surrenders with minimal bloodshed. Muhammad declares a general amnesty.
recogMuhammad enters the Ka'ba, destroys the 360 idols inside, and re-dedicates it as a purely monotheistic shrine. The Quraysh opposition effectively ends.
powerThe amnesty at Mecca — sparing former enemies including Hind bint Utbah (who mutilated Hamza) — is frequently cited in quizbowl as evidence of Muhammad's statesmanship and a turning point in Arabian unification.
Farewell Pilgrimage and Death
632 CE · Mecca → Medina
recogMuhammad performs his only hajj and delivers the Farewell Sermon (Khutbat al-Wada) at Mount Arafat: equality of all Muslims, sanctity of life/property, treatment of women.
recogDies 8 June 632 CE in Medina in the house of wife Aisha; buried under what becomes the Prophet's Mosque (al-Masjid al-Nabawi). Dispute over succession triggers the Sunni–Shia split.
powerAt his death, Umar ibn al-Khattab refused to believe he had died; Abu Bakr calmed the community quoting: "Whoever worshipped Muhammad, know that Muhammad has died; whoever worshipped God, know that God lives and never dies."

Deeper Cuts (Hard / Early Clues)

Lesser-asked episodes and details that appear as hard/opening clues in tossups. One-line ID each.

Treaty of Hudaybiyyah
628 CE
Ten-year truce with Mecca; Muslims allowed pilgrimage the following year. Quran calls it "a manifest victory." Broken by Quraysh 630 CE, leading to Mecca's conquest.
Battle of Khaybar
628 CE
Defeat of Jewish tribes at the Khaybar oasis north of Medina; Ali ibn Abi Talib earns fame fighting here. Muslims given the right to half the harvest.
Constitution of Medina
622 CE
Compact between Muslim immigrants (Muhajirun), Medinan helpers (Ansar), and Jewish tribes; established Medina as a single political community under Muhammad's leadership.
Battle of Hunayn
630 CE
Fought against Hawazin tribe just after Mecca's conquest; Muslims initially routed but recover. Spoils prompt Quranic verse on faith over material gain (Surah at-Tawbah).
Expulsion of Banu Qaynuqa
624 CE
First Jewish tribe expelled from Medina after altercation in the market. Sets precedent for later expulsions (Banu Nadir 625; Banu Qurayza 627).
Year of Grief (Am al-Huzn)
619 CE
Within months: death of wife Khadijah AND uncle/protector Abu Talib. Muhammad loses his two main supporters in Mecca; intensifies plan to migrate.
First Hijra to Abyssinia
615 CE
Muhammad sends persecuted followers to Christian king Ashama ibn Abjar (Negus) of Axum/Abyssinia for refuge — the first Islamic diaspora. Quraysh delegation fails to extradite them.
Stoning of the Devil at Mina (origin)
Hajj ritual
Muhammad codified the hajj rites at the Farewell Pilgrimage, including the stoning of pillars at Mina representing the devil tempting Abraham. Established Pillar of Islam #5.
Change of Qibla (Prayer Direction)
624 CE
Prayer direction changes from Jerusalem to Mecca (the Ka'ba) during a prayer in Medina, marking Muslim distinction from Jewish practice. The site is called Masjid al-Qiblatain ("Mosque of Two Qiblas").
Wives after Khadijah
c. 619–632 CE
After Khadijah's death, married ~11 women total; most notable: Aisha bint Abu Bakr (daughter of Abu Bakr; controversially young — major Hadith transmitter); Hafsa bint Umar; Zaynab bint Jahsh.
Battle of Mu'tah
629 CE · Jordan
First Muslim military clash with Byzantine forces; Zayd ibn Harithah (Muhammad's adopted son) killed. Khalid ibn al-Walid leads successful withdrawal; first contact with Byzantine world.
Tabuk Campaign
630 CE
Large expedition north toward Byzantine Syria; Byzantines withdraw without battle. Quran's Surah al-Tawbah addresses those who stayed behind ("hypocrites"). Demonstrates reach of Muslim state.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key people

  • Khadijah — first wife; first Muslim; wealthy merchant widow
  • Ali ibn Abi Talib — cousin, son-in-law (married Fatimah); 4th caliph; Shia imams descend from him
  • Abu Bakr al-Siddiq — closest male companion; first caliph
  • Umar ibn al-Khattab — second caliph; known for toughness; converted ~616 CE
  • Aisha bint Abu Bakr — favorite wife post-Khadijah; major Hadith source; "Mother of the Believers"
  • Fatimah al-Zahra — daughter by Khadijah; wife of Ali; only child with surviving descendants
  • Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib — uncle; "Lion of God"; killed at Uhud
  • Bilal ibn Rabah — former Abyssinian slave; freed by Abu Bakr; first muezzin (caller to prayer)
  • Abu Sufyan — Quraysh leader and long-time opponent; converts 630 CE
  • Salman al-Farisi — Persian companion; suggests the trench at Khandaq
  • Zayd ibn Harithah — freed slave, adopted son; killed at Mu'tah

Key places

  • Mecca — birthplace; Ka'ba at center; controlled by Quraysh
  • Medina (Yathrib) — city of migration; base of Islamic state
  • Cave Hira — site of first revelation; Jabal al-Nour
  • Cave Thawr — hiding place during Hijra
  • Mount Arafat — Farewell Sermon delivered here
  • Badr / Uhud / Khandaq — three great early battles
  • al-Masjid al-Nabawi — Prophet's Mosque, Medina; his tomb inside
  • al-Aqsa Mosque / Temple Mount, Jerusalem — Night Journey destination

Key terms & texts

  • Quran — literal word of God; 114 surahs; compiled under Uthman (~650 CE)
  • Hadith — Muhammad's reported sayings/deeds; second source of sharia
  • Sunnah — Muhammad's customary practice; basis of Sunni Islam's name
  • Hijri calendar — lunar calendar starting 622 CE (the Hijra)
  • Ummah — the community of Muslim believers
  • Five Pillars: Shahada, Salat (5 prayers), Zakat, Sawm (Ramadan fast), Hajj
  • Shahada: "There is no god but God, and Muhammad is the messenger of God"
  • Sirah — biographical literature about Muhammad; Ibn Ishaq's is oldest major source
  • Iqra — first word of revelation ("Recite/Read"); first word of Surah al-Alaq

Numbers & quotes

  • c. 570 CE — birth year (Year of the Elephant — Abraha's failed attack on Mecca)
  • 610 CE — first revelation (age ~40)
  • 622 CE — Hijra; Year 1 AH
  • 632 CE — death (age ~62)
  • 23 years — duration of Quranic revelation
  • ~313 — Muslims at Battle of Badr
  • ~10,000 — Muslim army at conquest of Mecca
  • "There is no compulsion in religion" — Quran 2:256
  • "The ink of the scholar is more sacred than the blood of the martyr" — attributed Hadith
  • Abu Bakr's words at Muhammad's death: "Whoever worshipped Muhammad…"

Comparison Traps

Adjacent figures, events, and concepts most often buzzed by mistake.

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Ali ibn Abi TalibMuhammad's cousin and son-in-law, not Muhammad himself. Ali is the 4th caliph and pivot of Shia Islam. Clues about Shia imamate, the Battle of Karbala, or the Alid line point to Ali, not Muhammad.
Abu Bakr al-SiddiqFirst caliph — rules after Muhammad's death. Clues about the Ridda Wars (apostasy wars), "al-Siddiq" (the Truthful), or first caliph = Abu Bakr, not Muhammad.
Umar ibn al-KhattabSecond caliph; conquered Persia, Palestine, Egypt. Clues about the conquest of Jerusalem (638), or "Farooq" (the Distinguisher) = Umar, not Muhammad.
Battle of Karbala (680 CE)Karbala is about Husayn ibn Ali (Muhammad's grandson), not Muhammad. ~50 years after Muhammad's death; pivotal for Shia–Sunni split.
Moses (Musa) / Jesus (Isa)Muhammad reveres both as earlier prophets; he is their successor, not replacement. Clues mixing Exodus or crucifixion imagery with "the Prophet" may trap — Muhammad is explicitly the final (Seal) prophet.
Saladin (Salah ad-Din)12th-century Kurdish sultan who recaptured Jerusalem (1187). Clues about the Crusades or Saladin ≠ Muhammad. Different era by 500+ years.
Ibn Rushd / Averroes12th-century Islamic philosopher, not the Prophet. Confusion arises when questions mention "Islamic" thinkers — check the century.
Sunni vs. Shia splitThe split is about succession after Muhammad, not about Muhammad himself. Sunnis follow Abu Bakr's line; Shia follow Ali's line. Muhammad predates and transcends the split.

On any question about "the Prophet," "the Messenger," or "the Seal of the Prophets," the answer is Muhammad unless another specific prophet is named.
