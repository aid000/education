Career arc — memorize the sequence

High-Yield Topics (Most Common)

The sub-topics most likely to be the answer or anchor a tossup. power = early/hard buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

The Square Deal & Trust-Busting
1901–1909 · Domestic policy label
recogTR's domestic program: "three Cs" — conservation of natural resources, control of corporations, consumer protection.
recogNorthern Securities Company (1902) — railroad holding company formed by J.P. Morgan and James J. Hill; first major trust dissolved under Sherman Antitrust Act.
recog40+ antitrust suits filed during TR's presidency, earning "Trust-Buster" nickname; followed by Standard Oil and American Tobacco breakups under Taft.
powerTR distinguished between "good trusts" (efficient, not predatory) and "bad trusts" — he did not oppose bigness per se, unlike later New Deal liberals. The doctrine of "regulated capitalism" vs. Wilsonian "New Freedom" (break up all big business) is a core comparison trap.

Conservation Program
1901–1909 · Federal lands policy
recogEstablished 5 national parks, 18 national monuments, 51 bird reservations, 4 game preserves, 150 national forests — roughly 230 million acres protected total.
recogGifford Pinchot — first chief of the U.S. Forest Service (1905); TR's close ally and key conservation administrator.
recogNewlands Reclamation Act (1902) — funded irrigation projects in the West from federal land sales; foundational to western water development.
powerTR camped with John Muir at Yosemite (1903); yet TR and Pinchot backed utilitarian conservation (wise use), clashing with Muir's preservationist philosophy — the Hetch Hetchy dam controversy (approved under Wilson) crystallized the split.
wiki ↗
Rough Riders & San Juan Hill
1898 · Cuba, Spanish-American War
recog1st U.S. Volunteer Cavalry Regiment — informal name "Rough Riders"; mix of Ivy Leaguers and western cowboys/Native Americans. Commanded by Leonard Wood; TR was lieutenant colonel (de facto leader).
recogBattle of San Juan Hill (July 1, 1898) — charge up Kettle Hill and then San Juan Heights; decisive action of the Santiago Campaign; made TR a national hero.
powerTR nominated himself for the Medal of Honor after the battle but was denied (War Department objected to his public criticism of conditions). It was posthumously awarded in 2001 — he remains the only president to receive it. The regiment recruited heavily from Harvard and Yale athletic clubs as well as Arizona and New Mexico Territory frontiersmen.
wiki ↗
Big Stick Diplomacy & the Panama Canal
1901–1909 · Foreign policy
recog"Speak softly and carry a big stick; you will go far" — West African proverb TR popularized; doctrine of using military threat as a diplomatic tool.
recogPanama Canal Zone: TR supported the Panamanian revolution against Colombia (1903); U.S. recognized Panama within days; Hay-Bunau-Varilla Treaty gave U.S. the Canal Zone. Canal opened 1914.
recogRoosevelt Corollary (1904) — extended Monroe Doctrine: U.S. has right to intervene in Latin American nations unable to maintain order or pay debts to Europeans.
powerThe corollary was invoked to justify interventions in Dominican Republic (1904), Nicaragua, Haiti. Wilson partially reversed it; FDR replaced it with the Good Neighbor Policy (1933). The Canal construction was overseen by George Washington Goethals; malaria/yellow fever eradicated by William Gorgas.
wiki ↗
Treaty of Portsmouth & Nobel Peace Prize
1905 · Portsmouth, New Hampshire
recogTR mediated peace between Russia and Japan ending the Russo-Japanese War — negotiations held at Portsmouth Naval Shipyard, New Hampshire.
recogAwarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1906 — first American to win any Nobel Prize; donated prize money to a foundation for industrial peace.
powerJapan won the war decisively but felt cheated at Portsmouth (no indemnity); this fueled anti-American resentment. TR also brokered the secret Taft-Katsura Agreement (1905) — U.S. accepted Japanese dominance over Korea in exchange for Japan accepting U.S. control of Philippines. Kept secret until 1924.
wiki ↗
1912 Election & the "Bull Moose" Party
1912 · National election
recogTR challenged Taft for the Republican nomination, lost in a contested convention, then founded the Progressive Party — nicknamed "Bull Moose Party" after TR said he felt "strong as a bull moose."
recogIn Milwaukee (Oct. 14, 1912), John Schrank shot TR before a speech; the bullet was slowed by a folded speech and glasses case; TR still delivered a 90-minute speech with the bullet in his chest.
recogTR came second (88 electoral votes) — the best third-party presidential finish in the 20th century; Taft came third; Woodrow Wilson won with a split opposition.
powerThe Progressive platform called for: direct election of senators (passed as 17th Amendment), women's suffrage, minimum wage, workers' compensation, recall of judicial decisions, and an income tax. Several planks were adopted by Wilson or FDR. TR's candidacy split the GOP, handing Wilson the White House.

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Woodrow WilsonBoth Progressive Era; but Wilson's "New Freedom" opposed bigness itself (break all trusts), while TR's "New Nationalism" regulated them. Wilson created the Federal Reserve and Federal Trade Commission. Wilson won 1912 because TR split the GOP vote.
William Howard TaftTR's hand-picked successor who actually filed more antitrust suits but alienated TR by firing Pinchot and supporting Payne-Aldrich Tariff. Taft later served as Chief Justice (the only person to hold both offices). Their 1912 break gave Wilson the win.
William McKinleyTR's predecessor (25th president); assassinated by Leon Czolgosz at Buffalo Pan-American Exposition 1901. McKinley is the Spanish-American War president, not TR — though TR's Rough Riders fought in it. McKinley was the high-tariff, gold-standard era Republican.
Franklin D. RooseveltTR's distant cousin (FDR married TR's niece Eleanor). FDR = New Deal, WWII, 12 years; TR = Progressive Era, Big Stick, 7.5 years. "TR" vs. "FDR" — exam questions often leave out initials, check era signals.
Gifford Pinchot vs. John MuirBoth associated with TR's conservation. Pinchot = utilitarian (wise use, forests for timber/watershed managed by experts). Muir = preservationist (wilderness sacred, no exploitation). TR aligned more with Pinchot; the Hetch Hetchy dam split them permanently.
1898 vs. 1912"San Juan Hill" = 1898 (Spanish-American War, Cuba). "Shot on campaign trail" = 1912 (Milwaukee, Bull Moose run). "Treaty of Portsmouth" = 1905. These three years cluster in TR tossups — know which decade each belongs to.
