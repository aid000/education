Quick Overview

Freud, c. 1921
Born / Died1856 Freiberg, Moravia — 1939 London
NationalityAustrian (Jewish); emigrated to London, 1938
FieldNeurology → Psychoanalysis
Core claimUnconscious drives determine behavior; repressed wishes cause neurosis
MethodFree association, dream interpretation, transference analysis
Key associatesBreuer, Jung, Adler, Ferenczi, Anna Freud

Defining contributions

  • Founded psychoanalysis — theory of mind and clinical method simultaneously.
  • Topographic model (unconscious / preconscious / conscious) → later replaced by structural model (id / ego / superego).
  • Psychosexual development: oral, anal, phallic (Oedipus), latency, genital stages; fixation at any stage causes adult neurosis.
  • Dream analysis: dreams are "the royal road to the unconscious"; every dream is a disguised wish-fulfillment.
  • Defense mechanisms (repression, projection, displacement, sublimation, reaction-formation) mediated by the ego.

Quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "The royal road to the unconscious" — clue for either Freud or dream interpretation.
  • Oedipus complex, cigar quote, the talking cure (Anna O. → Breuer).
  • Berggasse 19, Vienna — his address; now a museum.
  • "Sometimes a cigar is just a cigar" (attributed, likely apocryphal but perennial question bait).

High-Yield Ideas & Works (Very Common)

The theories and texts most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

The Interpretation of Dreams (Die Traumdeutung)
1899 (dated 1900) · Freud's masterwork
recog"The royal road to the unconscious" — dreams as disguised wish-fulfillments.
recogDistinguishes manifest content (surface narrative) from latent content (hidden wish).
powerFreud used his own Irma's Injection dream (July 24, 1895) as the first fully analyzed specimen; the book was actually printed in November 1899 but Freud dated it 1900 for symbolic weight. Introduces concepts of condensation and displacement as dreamwork mechanisms.
wiki ↗
The Structural Model: Id, Ego, Superego
Introduced in The Ego and the Id (1923)
recogId = entirely unconscious, pleasure-principle drives (Eros & Thanatos); ego = reality principle, mediator; superego = internalized moral authority (parents/society).
recogReplaced the earlier topographic model (unconscious / preconscious / conscious) introduced in The Interpretation of Dreams.
powerIn The Ego and the Id Freud posits the ego as partly unconscious itself — not simply the conscious self. The superego is an outcome of the Oedipus complex resolution; it inherits parental authority. The German das Es / das Ich / das Über-Ich were translated by James Strachey as id/ego/superego.
wiki ↗
The Oedipus Complex
Central to Three Essays on Sexuality (1905) and later works
recogBoy desires the mother, rivalrous with the father; resolved by castration anxiety — boy identifies with father and internalizes his prohibitions, forming the superego.
recogFemale analog: Electra complex (term coined by Jung; Freud preferred "feminine Oedipus complex"); penis envy as the parallel mechanism.
powerNamed for Sophocles' Oedipus Rex; the phallic stage (ages 3–6) is where the complex is lived out. Freud considered the Oedipus complex "the nucleus of neurosis." The Wolf Man case history and the Dora case are key clinical applications. Links to the Totem and Taboo (1913) origin-of-society narrative.
wiki ↗
Studies on Hysteria (with Breuer) / The Talking Cure
1895 · co-authored with Josef Breuer
recog"Talking cure" coined by patient Anna O. (Bertha Pappenheim), Breuer's patient — the founding case of psychoanalysis.
recogPresented catharsis (abreaction) as the mechanism of cure: speaking the traumatic memory discharges its pathogenic energy.
powerBreuer and Freud split over sexuality's role — Freud insisted repressed sexual wishes cause hysteria; Breuer disagreed. The book contains cases of Emmy von N., Lucy R., Katharina, and Elisabeth von R. (Freud's first full analytic patient). Freud later abandoned the seduction theory (actual childhood abuse) in favor of fantasy/wish.
wiki ↗
Civilization and Its Discontents (Das Unbehagen in der Kultur)
1930
recogArgues civilization demands suppression of instinctual drives (Eros and Thanatos), causing collective guilt and unhappiness.
recogIntroduces the "oceanic feeling" (sense of limitless union with the universe) as the source of religious experience — Freud identifies it with the ego's earliest undifferentiated state.
powerWritten as a response to Romain Rolland's description of the oceanic feeling; Freud grants the feeling exists but reduces it to infantile ego regression. The tension between Eros (life/love instinct) and Thanatos (death/destruction instinct) is the book's central dynamic. Discusses the "narcissism of small differences" between neighboring groups.
wiki ↗
Beyond the Pleasure Principle (Jenseits des Lustprinzips)
1920
recogIntroduces the death drive (Thanatos) / repetition compulsion — patients re-enact trauma; organisms inherently drive toward inorganic rest.
recogThe fort-da game: grandson throws and retrieves a spool to master separation anxiety — key illustration of repetition compulsion.
powerThe fort-da child is Freud's grandson Ernst (son of Sophie); "fort" = gone, "da" = there. Freud here revises the pleasure principle (drive toward pleasure / tension reduction) as insufficient to explain trauma repetition. Thanatos as death drive was controversial even among Freud's followers; Melanie Klein accepted it, Adler and many others did not.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-cited works and concepts that appear as early/hard tossup clues — one-line ID each.

Three Essays on Sexuality 1905 Introduces psychosexual stages and infant polymorphous perversity.
The Psychopathology of Everyday Life 1901 Slips of the tongue / pen (Freudian slips, Fehlleistungen) as unconscious eruptions.
Jokes and Their Relation to the Unconscious 1905 Humor as controlled release of repressed aggression or sexual energy.
Totem and Taboo 1913 Applies Oedipus complex to anthropology; primal-horde murder of the father-chief.
On Narcissism 1914 Distinguishes primary narcissism (infantile self-love) from object-love.
Introductory Lectures on Psycho-Analysis 1916–17 Accessible survey; most-read Freud; covers parapraxes, dreams, neurosis.
The Future of an Illusion 1927 Religion as collective neurosis; God = projection of idealized father-figure.
Moses and Monotheism 1939 Moses was Egyptian; Hebrews murdered him; guilt founds monotheism.
The Ego and the Id 1923 Formal introduction of id / ego / superego tripartite structural model.
Inhibitions, Symptoms and Anxiety 1926 Revises anxiety theory: anxiety signals danger to ego, not repression's byproduct.
Fragment of an Analysis of a Case of Hysteria ("Dora") 1905 Ida Bauer case; Freud misses her transference; key feminist critique target.
Notes upon a Case of Obsessional Neurosis ("Rat Man") 1909 Ernst Lanzer terrified of rats forced into father/girlfriend's anus; obsessional logic.
From the History of an Infantile Neurosis ("Wolf Man") 1918 Sergei Pankejeff dreams of wolves; "primal scene" trauma reconstructed.
Schreber Case (Paranoia) 1911 Analyzes judge Schreber's memoir; paranoia as defense against homosexual wish.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key terms & concepts

  • Unconscious — reservoir of repressed wishes
  • Repression — primary defense mechanism
  • Transference — patient projects feelings onto analyst
  • Counter-transference — analyst's reciprocal reaction
  • Free association — say whatever comes to mind
  • Cathexis — investment of libidinal energy in object
  • Parapraxis / Freudian slip (Fehlleistung)
  • Pleasure principle vs. reality principle
  • Libido — sexual/life energy (Eros)
  • Thanatos / death drive

Defense mechanisms

  • Repression — push threatening content out of consciousness
  • Projection — attribute own feelings to others
  • Displacement — redirect affect to substitute target
  • Sublimation — channel drives into socially acceptable acts
  • Reaction-formation — express opposite of real impulse
  • Regression — return to earlier developmental stage
  • Rationalization — logical justification for irrational acts
  • Denial

Key people & cases

  • Josef Breuer — co-founder, Anna O. case, split over sex
  • Anna O. (Bertha Pappenheim) — coined "talking cure"
  • Carl Jung — disciple, split 1912; archetypes, collective unconscious
  • Alfred Adler — split 1911; inferiority complex
  • Sándor Ferenczi — close disciple, active technique
  • Ernest Jones — biographer, brought psychoanalysis to UK
  • Anna Freud — daughter, expanded ego psychology
  • Dora, Wolf Man, Rat Man, Little Hans, Schreber — five key cases

Biographical hard clues

  • Born Freiberg, Moravia (now Pribor, Czech Republic), 1856
  • Raised in Vienna; studied under Charcot in Paris, 1885–86
  • Office / apartment: Berggasse 19, Vienna (now a museum)
  • Fled Nazi Austria in 1938 with help of Marie Bonaparte and Ernest Jones
  • Died in London, September 23, 1939 — euthanized by his physician Max Schur from jaw cancer (oral cancer from cigar smoking)
  • Personal physician: Max Schur
  • Wednesday Psychological Society → Vienna Psychoanalytic Society
  • Founded International Psychoanalytic Association (1910; first president: Jung)

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Carl JungFreud disciple who split in 1912. Jung: archetypes, collective unconscious, introvert/extrovert, anima/animus, synchronicity. No id/ego/superego; no Oedipus complex as central. Jung = Analytical Psychology; Freud = Psychoanalysis.
Alfred AdlerSplit 1911. Adler: inferiority complex, will to power, individual psychology, birth-order effects. Rejects Freud's sexual etiology; emphasizes social striving over unconscious drives.
B. F. SkinnerBehaviorism — no unconscious, no mental states. Operant conditioning, reinforcement schedules. Fundamentally opposed to Freudian introspection and mentalism.
Ivan PavlovClassical conditioning — dogs, bells, salivation. Reflexive, stimulus-response. Contemporary of Freud but entirely different paradigm.
Jean PiagetCognitive development stages (sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete, formal). Children's stages are about logical/cognitive growth, not psychosexual fixation. Swiss, not Austrian.
Karen HorneyNeo-Freudian who critiqued penis envy and "womb envy" counter-argument. Focused on cultural neurosis and basic anxiety from childhood insecurity, not biological drives.
Jacques LacanFrench post-Freudian; reinterprets Freud through linguistics (Saussure). Mirror stage, the Other, "the unconscious is structured like a language." Far more abstract than Freud.
William JamesAmerican functionalist / pragmatist psychologist. Principles of Psychology (1890), stream of consciousness, pragmatism. Slightly earlier than Freud; no clinical method.

Cross-discipline big picture Freud sits at the intersection of psychology, literature, philosophy, and history. His case histories read as literary narratives — Dora echoes Ibsen's heroines; the Wolf Man recalls Gothic fiction. Civilization and Its Discontents is read in political-theory courses. Dalí explicitly cited Freud's dream-work as the theoretical basis for Surrealism, making Freud the rare answer who bridges psychology, art, philosophy, and literature tossups in the same tournament. Lacan and Derrida each made Freud foundational; feminist theory (Horney, Kristeva, Mitchell) either critiques or rehabilitates him. He is also a history-of-science answer: Freud arrived in London 1938, connecting to Nazi persecution and WWII-era exile narratives.
