Quick Overview

Greek namePoseidon (Ποσειδῶν)
Roman nameNeptune
DomainSea, earthquakes, horses
ParentsCronus & Rhea (Titan parents)
SiblingsZeus, Hades, Hera, Demeter, Hestia
Consort (main)Amphitrite (sea goddess; Nereid)
ResidenceUnderwater palace; also Olympus
Symbol / weaponTrident (three-pronged spear)

Defining roles in quizbowl

  • God of the sea — rules all oceans, storms, calm waters, sea creatures.
  • Earthshaker (Ennosigaios) — causes earthquakes by striking the ground with his trident.
  • God of horses — credited with creating the first horse; patron of chariot-racing.
  • One of the three brothers who divided the cosmos after defeating the Titans: Zeus got the sky, Hades the underworld, Poseidon the sea.
  • Most famous mythic role: Athena/Poseidon contest for Athens — he struck a rock to produce a saltwater spring; she gave the olive tree and won.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • The trident — single most common identifying clue.
  • "Earthshaker" epithet (Greek: Ennosigaios or Enosichthon).
  • The contest with Athena over Athens, producing a saltwater spring on the Acropolis.
  • Father of the Cyclops Polyphemus — Odysseus blinds him; Poseidon's wrath drives the Odyssey.

High-Yield Myths & Episodes

The principal myths most likely to be the tossup answer or anchor clue. power = hard, early clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

Contest with Athena for Athens
Founding myth of Athens · Acropolis / Erechtheion
recogPoseidon struck the Acropolis rock with his trident, producing a saltwater spring (or, in some versions, a horse).
recogAthena counter-offered an olive tree; the gods judged it more useful — she won, and Athens was named for her.
powerThe contest was judged by the twelve Olympians (some sources: Cecrops, the first Athenian king). The marks of Poseidon's trident and the sacred olive tree were both kept inside the Erechtheion on the Acropolis. Cecrops testified on Athena's behalf.

Poseidon & Odysseus (The Odyssey)
Homer · Odyssey · ~8th c. BCE
recogPoseidon relentlessly opposes Odysseus's homeward voyage because Odysseus blinded his son Polyphemus the Cyclops.
recogHe sends storms, wrecks Odysseus's raft, and keeps him from reaching Ithaca for years.
powerThe divine stalemate: Zeus forbids Poseidon from killing Odysseus outright; Poseidon can only delay. His absence at an Ethiopian feast is the window Athena uses in Book 1 to begin Odysseus's rescue. Polyphemus's cry invoking his father is the pivotal moment Poseidon hears and intensifies his wrath.

Poseidon & Medusa
Source: Ovid, Metamorphoses IV; Hesiod, Theogony
recogPoseidon lay with Medusa in Athena's temple; Athena punished Medusa by transforming her into a Gorgon.
recogWhen Perseus beheaded Medusa, Pegasus (the winged horse) and the giant Chrysaor sprang from her neck — both children of Poseidon.
powerOvid's version (Metamorphoses) emphasizes Medusa as originally beautiful; Hesiod makes all three Gorgons monstrous from birth. The Poseidon link is specifically Ovidian. Chrysaor (golden sword) is obscure but shows up as a hard clue — father of Geryon.

Amphitrite — Marriage & Courtship
Primary consort; Nereid daughter of Nereus
recogAmphitrite fled Poseidon's advances; Delphinus (the dolphin) found her and persuaded her to return — Poseidon placed the dolphin in the stars as the constellation Delphinus.
recogTheir son is Triton — merman son, messenger of the sea, blows a conch horn.
powerAmphitrite is rarely a tossup answer herself, but she is a frequent clue in Poseidon tossups; she is also named on the Parthenon's east frieze and in Pindar's Pythian Odes. Distinguish her from the Nereids generally — she is specifically Poseidon's queen.
Creation of the Horse
Multiple sources; Pindar, Pythian 4; Hesiod
recogPoseidon is credited with creating the first horse — striking earth with his trident to produce it (some versions say from sea-foam or from a rock).
recogHe is worshipped as Poseidon Hippios ("of horses") and patron of the Isthmian Games, where horse races were central.
powerDuring the Athena contest (see above), one tradition says his gift was a horse, not a spring. This variant appears in Pseudo-Apollodorus (Library 3.14.1). The horse-creation link also connects to his affair with Demeter: he pursued her disguised as a stallion; she took the form of a mare; their offspring was Arion, the divine horse.
Poseidon & Theseus — Aegean Sea / Minotaur Cycle
Sources: Plutarch, Theseus; Ovid, Heroides
recogPoseidon is the divine father of Theseus (one tradition); the same tradition has Theseus retrieve Poseidon's ring from the sea floor to prove his parentage.
recogWhen Theseus's father Aegeus saw the ship's black sails and believed Theseus dead, he threw himself into the sea — the Aegean Sea is named for him.
powerPoseidon/Theseus paternity is contested: Aegeus (human king) vs. Poseidon — the ambiguity is itself a quizbowl clue. Poseidon also sent the bull from the sea that killed Hippolytus (at Theseus's request via Poseidon's three granted wishes) — connects to Euripides' Hippolytus and Racine's Phèdre.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-known myths and figures that appear as early/hard tossup clues. One identifying line each.

Polyphemus
Cyclops son of Poseidon; blinded by Odysseus with a sharpened olive stake.
Triton
Merman son of Poseidon & Amphitrite; blows a conch to calm or raise seas.
Pegasus & Chrysaor
Born from Medusa's neck when Perseus beheads her; both sons of Poseidon.
Arion (divine horse)
Immortal horse born from Poseidon and Demeter (both disguised as horses).
Scylla
Sea monster; one tradition makes her a lover transformed by Amphitrite's jealousy.
Orion
Giant hunter and son of Poseidon; able to walk on water; placed among the stars.
Laomedon & the Walls of Troy
Poseidon and Apollo built Troy's walls for Laomedon; cheated of pay, Poseidon sent a sea monster.
Hippocampus
Horse-fish hybrid creatures; pull Poseidon's underwater chariot.
Caeneus / Caenis
Poseidon granted Caenis's wish to become invulnerable male warrior Caeneus.
Polydectes & Dictys
Sons of Poseidon and Magnes; Dictys rescued Perseus's mother Danae.
Poseidon vs. Hera (Contest for Argos / Corinth)
Lost contests: Hera won Argos; Helios won Corinth — both over Poseidon.
Hippolytus — Bull from the Sea
Poseidon sent a bull that panicked Hippolytus's horses at Theseus's curse; key to Euripides' Hippolytus.
Isthmian Games
Founded in Poseidon's honor at Corinth; one of the four Panhellenic Games.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Epithets (Greek)

  • Ennosigaios / Enosichthon — "Earthshaker"
  • Hippios — "of horses"
  • Pelagaios — "of the deep sea"
  • Asphalios — "securer," invoked for safe sailing
  • Phytalmios — "who nurses plants" (obscure; Troezen)
  • Gaieochos — "Earth-holder"

Symbols & attributes

  • Trident (three-pronged spear — his primary weapon)
  • Dolphin (sacred animal; Delphinus myth)
  • Horse (created by him; also chariot)
  • Bull (sacrifices; Cretan bull / Minos myth)
  • Hippocampi (horse-fish chariot team)
  • Conch shell / sea pine / celery (Isthmian Games crowns)

Key family / relationships

  • Amphitrite — wife, queen of the sea
  • Triton — son with Amphitrite
  • Polyphemus — Cyclops son (mother: Thoosa)
  • Pegasus — son (mother: Medusa)
  • Theseus — son (mother: Aethra) — contested
  • Orion — son (mother: Euryale or earth itself)
  • Arion — divine horse son (mother: Demeter)

Numbers, places & sources

  • Lot assignment: second (Zeus first, Hades third; or drawn by lot)
  • Primary sanctuary: Cape Sounion (Temple of Poseidon, Attica)
  • Isthmia near Corinth — main cult site
  • Homer: Iliad books 13–14 (fights for Greeks at Troy); Odyssey passim
  • Hesiod: Theogony 278, 456, 930
  • Ovid: Metamorphoses (Medusa, Caeneus)
  • Pseudo-Apollodorus: Library I.2.1 (lot division), III.14.1 (Athens contest)

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Zeus / JupiterZeus rules sky and lightning; Poseidon rules sea and earthquakes. Both carry power weapons (thunderbolt vs. trident). If the clue mentions the sea, horses, or earthquakes → Poseidon; if sky, eagles, or thunder → Zeus.
Hades / PlutoThird brother; rules the underworld, not the sea. Hades has the helm of invisibility. They are all sons of Cronus — the domain (sea vs. underworld) is the distinguishing fact.
Neptune (planet)The planet Neptune was named for the Roman sea god; a tossup about the planet will mention Voyager 2, Triton (moon), methane blue color, 1846 discovery (Le Verrier / Adams). A tossup about the god will mention myths, trident, and Athens.
OceanusOceanus is a Titan, not an Olympian; he personifies the world-encircling river, not the managed sea. Oceanus never appears in active myths the way Poseidon does.
NereusThe "Old Man of the Sea" — a Titan/sea-elder, father of the Nereids (including Amphitrite and Thetis). Prophetic, non-violent. Not to be confused with Poseidon.
Triton (son vs. plural tritons)Triton (singular, capitalized) = Poseidon's specific son, merman with conch. "Tritons" (plural) = a class of mermen in his retinue. Also the moon of Neptune — connects to planet trivia.
Ares / Mars (horses)Ares is associated with horses as war animals, but he did not create horses and is not their patron god. Poseidon Hippios is the canonical horse-creator.

Guess-Before-You-Reveal

Read the clue, buzz in your head, then expand to check.

This god struck the Acropolis rock with his trident, producing a saltwater spring, but lost the city to a rival who produced an olive tree.Poseidon (lost Athens to Athena).
Name the epithet meaning "Earthshaker," the most famous Greek for this god.Ennosigaios (also: Enosichthon).
Poseidon sent storms against Odysseus because Odysseus had blinded which Cyclops?Polyphemus (Poseidon's son by the sea-nymph Thoosa).
What two creatures were born from Medusa's neck when Perseus beheaded her, and who was their divine father?Pegasus (winged horse) and Chrysaor; their father was Poseidon.
Which dolphin-related myth explains the origin of the constellation Delphinus?A dolphin (Delphinus) found Amphitrite hiding from Poseidon and persuaded her to marry him; Poseidon set the dolphin among the stars.
Name the immortal horse born from Poseidon's union with Demeter, both disguised as horses.Arion.
Which Panhellenic festival was held at Corinth in Poseidon's honor?The Isthmian Games.
Poseidon and Apollo built the walls of which city, and what happened when the king refused to pay?Troy; king Laomedon refused payment, so Poseidon sent a sea monster to devastate the land.
In Euripides' Hippolytus, what did Poseidon send that killed Hippolytus, and why?A bull from the sea, at the request of Theseus, who used one of Poseidon's three granted wishes to curse his own son.
A woman named Caenis asked Poseidon to transform her into a man. What was his/her name afterward?Caeneus — also granted invulnerability.
Name Poseidon's primary temple site in Attica, at the tip of a cape southeast of Athens.Cape Sounion (Temple of Poseidon).
The Roman god Neptune is most directly identified with which Greek god, and what is his main symbol?Poseidon; symbol is the trident.
🌐 Cross-discipline connections Poseidon threads across multiple tossup categories: Literature — he is the divine antagonist of Homer's Odyssey and father of Polyphemus; he appears in Euripides' Hippolytus and Racine's Phèdre. Science — the planet Neptune (discovered 1846 by Le Verrier/Adams) and its largest moon Triton are named for him. Art — Giambologna's Neptune Fountain in Bologna (1566) and Rubens's Neptune Calming the Seas are canonical fine-art answers. Geography — the Aegean Sea (named for Aegeus, Theseus's father, who drowned himself in it) links the myth to modern geography tossups.
