Quick Overview

Chomsky, 2017
Born1928 Philadelphia, Pennsylvania (Dec. 7)
NationalityAmerican (Jewish); trained at U. of Pennsylvania under Zellig Harris
AffiliationMIT (from 1955); later U. of Arizona
FieldLinguistics (+ cognitive science, philosophy, politics)
Core claimLanguage is an innate, biological faculty governed by a universal grammar
Key associatesZellig Harris, Morris Halle, Edward S. Herman, Steven Pinker (popularizer)

Defining contributions

  • Founded generative grammar / transformational grammar — language as a finite rule system generating infinite sentences.
  • Universal Grammar (UG): humans are born with an innate, species-specific blueprint for language structure.
  • The language acquisition device (LAD) and the poverty of the stimulus argument — children learn language too fast on too little input to do so by imitation alone.
  • The Chomsky hierarchy of formal grammars (regular ⊂ context-free ⊂ context-sensitive ⊂ recursively enumerable) — foundational in computer science.
  • Launched the cognitive revolution, overturning behaviorism via his demolition of Skinner.

Quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "Colorless green ideas sleep furiously" — his sentence showing grammaticality is independent of meaning.
  • Universal grammar, the language acquisition device, "poverty of the stimulus."
  • Deep structure vs. surface structure; Syntactic Structures (1957).
  • His scathing 1959 review of Skinner's Verbal Behavior; professor at MIT.

High-Yield Ideas & Works (Very Common)

The theories and texts most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

Syntactic Structures
1957 · the founding text of generative grammar
recogContains "Colorless green ideas sleep furiously" — grammatical but meaningless, vs. its ungrammatical reversal, to show syntax is autonomous from semantics.
recogIntroduces transformational grammar and argues a finite rule set generates the infinite set of grammatical sentences.
powerA condensed version of lecture notes from his unpublished The Logical Structure of Linguistic Theory; the book grew out of MIT course notes and was published by Mouton in The Hague. It argues that finite-state (Markov) models are inadequate for natural language and that phrase-structure grammar alone is also insufficient — motivating transformations. Reviewed enthusiastically by Robert Lees, which launched its influence.

Universal Grammar, the LAD & Poverty of the Stimulus
Core nativist program (1960s onward)
recogUniversal Grammar: an innate, biologically given set of structural principles shared by all human languages.
recogThe language acquisition device (LAD) is the hypothesized innate mental module; the poverty of the stimulus argument holds that children's linguistic input is too sparse and degraded to explain their rapid mastery.
powerLater formalized as the principles and parameters framework (the "Government and Binding" era, 1981): universal principles plus language-specific parameters set by exposure (e.g., the pro-drop / null-subject parameter). Chomsky frames language as I-language (internal, individual) vs. E-language (external). His nativism revives a rationalist tradition he traces to Descartes and the Port-Royal grammarians.
wiki ↗
Aspects of the Theory of Syntax — Deep vs. Surface Structure
1965 · the "Standard Theory"
recogEstablishes the "Standard Theory"; distinguishes deep structure (underlying meaning-bearing form) from surface structure (the spoken/written form) via transformations.
recogFamous distinction between competence (the idealized speaker-hearer's internalized knowledge) and performance (actual use).
powerPosits an "ideal speaker-listener in a completely homogeneous speech community." Adds a semantic component to the grammar (drawing on Katz–Fodor), with deep structure feeding interpretation — the move that provoked the "linguistics wars" with the generative semantics school (Lakoff, McCawley, Ross). Surface structure feeds phonological interpretation.
wiki ↗
The Chomsky Hierarchy
1956 · formal language theory (cross-listed with CS)
recogA nested containment of grammar/language classes: Type 3 regular ⊂ Type 2 context-free ⊂ Type 1 context-sensitive ⊂ Type 0 recursively enumerable.
recogEach class corresponds to an automaton: regular ↔ finite automaton, context-free ↔ pushdown automaton, context-sensitive ↔ linear-bounded automaton, Type 0 ↔ Turing machine.
powerIntroduced in his 1956 paper "Three Models for the Description of Language" (in IRE Transactions on Information Theory). The hierarchy is a cornerstone of theoretical computer science, compiler design, and parsing — context-free grammars (often given in Backus–Naur form) describe most programming-language syntax. The classes are also called the Chomsky–Schützenberger hierarchy.
wiki ↗
Review of Skinner's Verbal Behavior
1959 · published in the journal Language
recogA devastating critique arguing that behaviorist stimulus–response conditioning cannot account for the creativity and productivity of language.
recogWidely credited with helping launch the cognitive revolution and ending behaviorism's dominance in psychology.
powerTargets B. F. Skinner's 1957 attempt to extend operant conditioning to language; Chomsky argues terms like "stimulus," "response," and "reinforcement" become vacuous when applied to verbal behavior. This is the canonical nativism vs. behaviorism / empiricism clash. Kenneth MacCorquodale published a noted 1970 defense of Skinner in response.
wiki ↗
The Minimalist Program
1995 (book) · his current framework
recogSeeks the simplest possible theory of language; drops deep/surface structure in favor of just two interface levels: Logical Form (LF) and Phonetic Form (PF).
recogReduces syntactic combination to the single recursive operation Merge; language design is governed by "economy" and interface conditions.
powerProposes that the language faculty is an "optimal" or "perfect" solution to legibility conditions imposed by the conceptual–intentional and sensorimotor systems. In the 2002 Science paper with Marc Hauser and Tecumseh Fitch, the "faculty of language in the narrow sense" is reduced largely to recursion — sparking the debate with Daniel Everett over the Amazonian Pirahã language.

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Ferdinand de SaussureFather of structuralism and modern linguistics; Course in General Linguistics (1916). Signifier/signified, langue vs. parole, synchronic vs. diachronic. Earlier, European, sign-based — not innatist or generative.
B. F. SkinnerBehaviorist psychologist whose Verbal Behavior Chomsky demolished. Language = conditioned response; the empiricist opposite of Chomsky's nativism.
Steven PinkerChomsky's most famous popularizer (The Language Instinct), but diverges: Pinker stresses Darwinian natural selection for language, whereas Chomsky downplays gradual adaptationist accounts.
Sapir–Whorf hypothesisLinguistic relativity (Edward Sapir & Benjamin Lee Whorf): language shapes thought. The near-opposite of Chomsky's universalism, where deep structure is shared across all languages.
Daniel EverettArgues the Amazonian Pirahã language lacks recursion, directly challenging Chomsky's claim that recursion is the universal core of language.
Jacques Lacan / Roman JakobsonOther figures who used "structure" + linguistics. Jakobson (Chomsky's Harvard mentor figure) = structuralist phonology / functions of language; Lacan = psychoanalysis through Saussure. Neither is generative grammar.
George LakoffFormer student turned rival; led generative semantics in the "linguistics wars," later founded cognitive linguistics and metaphor theory (Metaphors We Live By). Rejects autonomous syntax.
Leonard BloomfieldLeader of American structuralist/descriptivist linguistics that dominated before Chomsky; behaviorist-leaning, anti-mentalist — the paradigm Chomsky overthrew.

Cross-discipline big picture Chomsky is one of the rare answers who bridges linguistics, computer science, psychology, philosophy, and politics within the same tournament. The Chomsky hierarchy appears in science / CS tossups (automata, parsing, Backus–Naur form) as readily as in linguistics. His demolition of Skinner is a staple of psychology (the cognitive revolution), and his nativism revives the rationalist vs. empiricist debate central to philosophy. As co-author of Manufacturing Consent and a lifelong anarcho-syndicalist activist, he is also a current-events / social-science answer-line. When a question opens with formal-grammar or automata clues, suspect Chomsky early; the giveaways are "colorless green ideas," universal grammar, and MIT.
