High-Yield Figures, Texts & Events

The concepts/events most likely to anchor a tossup. power = early/hard buzz. recog = recognition/giveaway clue.

The Great Schism (East-West Schism)
1054 · splits Roman Catholic from Eastern Orthodox
recogPope Leo IX and Patriarch Michael Cerularius mutually excommunicate each other; excommunications lifted 1964 by Paul VI and Athenagoras I.
recogProximate cause: the Filioque clause — RC adds "and the Son" to Nicene Creed's phrase on Holy Spirit's procession; EO rejects this addition.
powerOther drivers: RC's claim of papal supremacy over all bishops vs. EO's pentarchy (five equal patriarchates: Rome, Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, Jerusalem); dispute over Latin vs. Greek liturgy; celibacy rules for clergy.
wiki ↗
The Protestant Reformation
1517 onward · Martin Luther's 95 Theses spark permanent split from Rome
recogMartin Luther posts (or sends) 95 Theses to Archbishop of Mainz on October 31, 1517; attacks sale of indulgences.
recogLuther's three solas: sola scriptura (Scripture alone), sola fide (faith alone), sola gratia (grace alone) — the core Protestant triple.
powerDiet of Worms (1521): Luther refuses to recant; excommunicated by Pope Leo X. Key collaborator: Philip Melanchthon (wrote the Augsburg Confession, 1530). Ulrich Zwingli leads Swiss Reformation simultaneously; disagreement with Luther at Marburg Colloquy (1529) over Eucharist (real presence vs. symbolic).
wiki ↗
Papal Infallibility / The Papacy
Dogma defined 1870 (First Vatican Council) · RC exclusive
recogThe Pope speaks infallibly only ex cathedra on matters of faith and morals; invoked rarely — the 1950 Assumption of Mary is the most recent formal use.
recogPope is Bishop of Rome, successor of St. Peter (Mt. 16:18 — "upon this rock I will build my church"), and holds universal jurisdiction over all Catholics.
powerVatican I (1869–70) under Pius IX defined infallibility. Vatican II (1962–65) under John XXIII and Paul VI modernized liturgy (vernacular Mass replacing Latin Tridentine rite), opened ecumenical dialogue, produced Lumen Gentium (church constitution) and Gaudium et Spes.
wiki ↗
Sacraments: Seven vs. Two
Core doctrinal dividing line across all three branches
recogRC EO recognize seven sacraments: Baptism, Eucharist, Confirmation, Penance/Reconciliation, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, Matrimony.
recogMainline PR (Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican) recognize only two sacraments ordained by Christ: Baptism and the Lord's Supper; others are rites but not sacraments.
powerEucharist theology splits further: RC = transubstantiation (bread/wine become body/blood in substance, accidents remain); EO = "real presence" (mystery, no Aristotelian explanation required); Lutheran = consubstantiation/sacramental union (body/blood present "in, with, under" bread/wine); Reformed/Calvinist = spiritual real presence; Zwinglian = memorial only.
Eastern Orthodox Distinctive Doctrines
Centered on Constantinople (Istanbul) · 14 autocephalous churches
recogTheosis (deification): humans can participate in divine nature (2 Pet. 1:4); salvation is union with God, not only forensic justification — the EO distinctive.
recogIcons: venerated (not worshipped) as "windows to heaven"; Iconoclasm controversy (726–843) — emperors Leo III and Constantine V ordered icon destruction; icons vindicated at Second Council of Nicaea (787).
powerEO governance: conciliar — no single head; Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople holds honorary primacy ("first among equals"). Seven Ecumenical Councils (325–787) are authoritative. Hesychasm: contemplative prayer tradition, defended by Gregory Palamas (14th c.) — distinction between God's essence (unknowable) and energies (participable).
wiki ↗
John Calvin & Reformed Theology
Geneva, 1536 onward · Institutes of the Christian Religion (1536/1559)
recogCalvin's Institutes: systematic Reformed theology; five points summarized by TULIP (Total depravity, Unconditional election, Limited atonement, Irresistible grace, Perseverance of the saints).
recogDouble predestination: God elects some to salvation and passes over others — the most controversial Calvinist claim.
powerCalvin governed Geneva as a theocracy; burned Michael Servetus for anti-Trinitarianism (1553). Reformed tradition includes Presbyterianism (church governed by elected elders), Puritanism, and the Westminster Confession (1646) — the standard Reformed creedal document. Jacobus Arminius reacted against TULIP: Arminianism (humans have free will to accept/reject grace) feeds later Methodism.

Recognition / Clue Bank

RC Roman Catholic Key Terms

  • Magisterium — teaching authority of Pope + bishops
  • Purgatory — purification after death (RC/some EO, rejected by Protestants)
  • Immaculate Conception — Mary conceived without original sin (dogma 1854, Pius IX)
  • Assumption of Mary — bodily taken to heaven (dogma 1950, Pius XII) — last ex cathedra definition
  • Indulgences — remission of temporal punishment; Luther's target
  • Celibacy of priests — RC Latin rite; not required in EO or most Protestant
  • Transubstantiation — Eucharist theology; substance changes, accidents remain
  • Thomism / Scholasticism — philosophical tradition (Aquinas)

EO Eastern Orthodox Key Terms

  • Theosis / Deification — becoming partakers of divine nature
  • Ecumenical Patriarch — Constantinople; "first among equals"
  • Autocephalous — self-governing national churches (Greek, Russian, Serbian, etc.)
  • Apophatic theology (via negativa) — God known by what he is not
  • Hesychasm — contemplative tradition; Jesus Prayer; uncreated light
  • Seven Ecumenical Councils — 325–787; authoritative for EO doctrine
  • Holy Tradition — Scripture + councils + Fathers; no papal infallibility
  • Leavened bread (prosphora) at Eucharist — distinguishes EO from RC

PR Protestant Key Terms & Figures

  • Sola scriptura — Scripture alone is authoritative
  • Sola fide — justification by faith alone
  • Sola gratia — salvation by grace alone
  • TULIP — Calvinist five points (Total depravity … Perseverance)
  • Priesthood of all believers — Luther; no special priestly caste
  • Book of Concord (1580) — collected Lutheran confessional documents
  • Methodism / John Wesley — sanctification; free grace; circuit riding
  • Baptists — believers' baptism by immersion; congregational polity

Shared & Cross-Branch Clues

  • Nicene Creed (325/381) — affirmed by all three; Filioque added only by RC/Protestant
  • Apostles' Creed — used by RC and most Protestant; not formally by EO
  • Council of Nicaea (325) — defined Trinity; homoousios; anti-Arian
  • Council of Chalcedon (451) — two natures of Christ; rejected by Oriental Orthodox
  • Augustine of Hippo — foundational to RC and Protestant (original sin, predestination)
  • Origen — early theologian; allegorical exegesis; controversial in EO
  • Pentarchy — five ancient patriarchates; EO ecclesiology concept

Common Confusions to Avoid

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Oriental Orthodox (Coptic, Armenian, Ethiopian)Rejected Council of Chalcedon (451); called miaphysite. Separate from Eastern Orthodox. Coptic Pope ≠ EO Ecumenical Patriarch.
Lutheranism vs. CalvinismBoth Protestant, but Luther = consubstantiation, state-church; Calvin = predestination/TULIP, presbyterian polity. Marburg Colloquy (1529) is the dividing moment.
Anglicanism / Church of EnglandProtestant in origin (Henry VIII, 1534) but retains bishops and some Catholic ritual — the "via media." Not RC (no papal authority), not purely Reformed.
Immaculate Conception vs. Virgin BirthImmaculate Conception = Mary conceived without original sin (RC dogma, 1854). Virgin Birth = Jesus born of a virgin Mary — affirmed by RC, EO, and most Protestant traditions. They are different doctrines.
Theosis vs. JustificationTheosis = EO salvation as union/participation in God. Forensic justification = Protestant (especially Lutheran/Reformed) — declared righteous by imputation of Christ's righteousness. RC blends infused grace with the juridical.
Assumption vs. DormitionAssumption = RC dogma (1950); Mary bodily taken to heaven. Dormition = EO feast (Aug. 15); Mary "fell asleep"; her body taken up — similar but not a defined dogma in EO.
