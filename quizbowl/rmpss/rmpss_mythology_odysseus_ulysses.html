Quick Overview

Greek nameOdysseus (Ὀδυσσεύς)
Roman nameUlysses
Father / motherLaertes (mortal king) / Anticlea; some traditions: Sisyphus
KingdomIthaca (Ionian island, western Greece)
Wife / sonPenelope / Telemachus
Divine patronAthena (goddess of wisdom, craft, war)
Core textsHomer's Iliad & Odyssey; Sophocles' Ajax, Philoctetes; Virgil's Aeneid; Dante's Inferno XXVI; Tennyson's Ulysses; Joyce's Ulysses
Defining traitCunning / craft (mêtis) over strength; the "man of many devices"

Defining epithets (quizbowl staples)

  • polytropos — "of many turns / ways" (opening word of the Odyssey)
  • polymetis — "of many counsels," man of resource and intellect
  • polymechanos — "of many devices"
  • Laertiades — "son of Laertes"
  • "The man of many wiles" — common translation in tossup clues

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • A bow no other suitor can string + the test of Penelope's suitors.
  • Blinding the Cyclops Polyphemus; calling himself "Nobody" (Outis).
  • Penelope's weaving and unraveling the shroud to delay suitors.
  • The Trojan Horse — Odysseus' stratagem.

The Trojan Horse
Trojan War · Homer, Virgil (Aeneid II), Quintus of Smyrna
powerOdysseus conceives the stratagem after ten years of siege; the horse is built by Epeius (craftsman) on Athena's instructions.
recogGreeks hide inside a giant hollow wooden horse; pretend to sail away, leaving Sinon to lure Trojans into accepting it.
recogCassandra and the priest Laocoon warn against it — ignored. Laocoon and sons are crushed by sea-serpents.

Polyphemus & the Cave of the Cyclops
Odyssey Book IX
powerPolyphemus is son of Poseidon — blinding him triggers Poseidon's enmity for Odysseus across the entire voyage.
recogOdysseus gives his name as "Nobody" (Outis/Noman); when Polyphemus cries out in pain, other Cyclopes ask who hurt him — he says "Nobody," so they leave.
recogOdysseus blinds the Cyclops with a sharpened olive-wood stake heated in embers. Escapes clinging to the bellies of sheep.
recogReveals his real name while sailing away — foolishly allowing Polyphemus to call down Poseidon's curse.

Odyssey Book X
powerCirce is a daughter of Helios and the Oceanid Perse; her island is Aeaea.
recogCirce transforms Odysseus' men into pigs using a magic wand (her rhabdos) and a drugged drink (kykeon).
recogOdysseus resists because Hermes gave him the herb moly as protection.

Odyssey Book XII
powerThe Sirens' names in post-Homeric tradition: Parthenope, Ligeia, Leucosia (or Aglaopheme, Thelxiepe).
recogOdysseus orders his crew to plug their ears with beeswax; has himself tied to the mast so he alone can hear but not act.
recogThe Sirens promise knowledge of "all that ever happened on this fruitful earth" — irresistible but deadly lure.

Nekuia — Descent to the Underworld
Odyssey Book XI
powerNot a true katabasis — Odysseus summons the dead at the edge of the world (Oceanus) rather than physically descending; a nekuia (ghost-raising ritual).
recogShades he speaks with: Tiresias (prophecy), his mother Anticlea, Achilles (famous: "better a slave on earth than king among the dead"), Agamemnon, Ajax.
recogTiresias tells Odysseus how to appease Poseidon: carry an oar inland until mistaken for a winnowing fan.

Return to Ithaca & the Slaughter of the Suitors
Odyssey Books XIII–XXIV
powerArrives disguised as a beggar; recognized only by his dog Argos (who dies on seeing him) and his nurse Eurycleia (from a scar on his thigh).
recogPenelope's contest: string Odysseus' great bow and shoot an arrow through twelve axe-heads. No suitor can string it.
recogOdysseus strings the bow and massacres the suitors with help from Telemachus, the swineherd Eumaeus, and the cowherd Philoetius.
recogRecognition scene: Penelope tests him with the secret of their olive-tree bed — unmovable because the bedpost is a living tree.

Calypso & the Island of Ogygia
Odyssey Books I, V
powerCalypso is a daughter of Atlas (or Oceanus); her island Ogygia is the "navel of the sea."
recogKeeps Odysseus for seven years, offering immortality; he refuses and longs for home.
recogZeus sends Hermes to order Calypso to release him; Odysseus builds a raft and departs.

Scylla & Charybdis
Odyssey Book XII
powerScylla is a six-headed monster on a cliff; Charybdis is a whirlpool thrice daily swallowing the sea. The strait is identified with the Strait of Messina.
recogCirce advises sailing nearer Scylla — lose six men — rather than risk the entire ship to Charybdis.
recogPhrase "between Scylla and Charybdis" = choose between two equally dangerous options.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-known episodes and literary treatments that appear as early/hard tossup clues. One-line ID each.

Land of the Laestrygonians
Giant cannibals destroy all but Odysseus' ship by hurling boulders.
Island of Aeolus & the Bag of Winds
Crew opens sealed bag thinking it holds treasure; winds blow them back to start.
Cattle of the Sun (Thrinacia)
Crew slaughters Helios' forbidden cattle; Zeus destroys their ship as punishment.
Land of the Lotus-Eaters
Crew eats lotus; loses desire to return home; must be dragged away.
Island of the Phaeacians (Scheria)
Nausicaa finds Odysseus; King Alcinous hosts him; he tells his story in Books IX–XII.
The Bow of Iphitus
Odysseus received the great bow from Iphitus as a guest-gift; kept unstrung for the contest.
Odysseus in the Iliad
Fetches Achilles to Troy; serves as envoy and spy; competes for Achilles' armor.
Contest for Achilles' Armor
Odysseus wins over Ajax; Ajax goes mad and kills himself in humiliation.
Philoctetes Episode
Odysseus (Sophocles' Philoctetes) tricks wounded archer into sailing to Troy for his bow.
Palimedes
Odysseus allegedly fabricates treason charge; Palamedes stoned — Odysseus' most villainous act in tradition.
Dante's Odysseus (Inferno XXVI)
Placed in Hell among "evil counselors"; made fatal voyage past Pillars of Hercules toward the Southern Hemisphere.
Tennyson's "Ulysses" (1842)
First-person monologue: aged Ulysses yearns to sail again — "to strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield."
Joyce's Ulysses (1922)
Leopold Bloom = Odysseus; Molly = Penelope; Stephen Dedalus = Telemachus; one day in Dublin.
Tiresias' Prophecy of the Oar
Must plant oar inland where it is mistaken for a winnowing fan; sacrifice to Poseidon to end his wandering.

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
AchillesAchilles = strength, glory (kleos), wrath; hero of the Iliad. Odysseus = cunning, endurance (nostos). Achilles dies at Troy; Odysseus survives. When both appear as answers, read for "cunning" vs "fastest/mightiest."
AeneasAeneas is the Trojan hero of Virgil's Aeneid who founds Rome. Both are wanderers after the Trojan War, but Aeneas = piety (pius Aeneas), Latin/Roman founding; Odysseus = Greek trickster returning home to Ithaca.
JasonJason leads the Argonauts for the Golden Fleece; his helper is Medea. Odysseus has no magic fleece, no Argonauts. Both encounter witches (Medea vs Circe) — the key differentiator is the quest object.
Heracles / HerculesHeracles = Twelve Labors, brute strength, lion-skin, club. Odysseus never has labors; he uses wit. Both go to the Underworld, but Heracles descends physically (true katabasis); Odysseus performs a nekuia at the shore.
DiomedesDiomedes is the other great Greek warrior at Troy who often pairs with Odysseus on raids (Iliad X: the Doloneia night raid). Questions naming a partner on a Troy night-raid may point to Diomedes, not Odysseus alone.
Penelope vs ClytemnestraPenelope = faithful, weaves & unravels. Clytemnestra = unfaithful, murders husband Agamemnon on his return. Foil pair; often compared in the Odyssey itself.
Dante's Ulysses vs Homer'sDante places Ulysses in Hell (Inferno XXVI) as an evil counselor; Homer's Odysseus is a hero who returns home. Dante's version sails past the Pillars of Hercules and drowns — a fate invented by Dante, absent in Homer.

Cross-discipline big-picture connections
  • Literature: The Odyssey is the template for the "hero's return" narrative — Joyce (Ulysses), Tennyson ("Ulysses"), Dante (Inferno XXVI), Kazantzakis (The Odyssey: A Modern Sequel), and Walcott (Omeros) all rework it.
  • Philosophy / ethics: Odysseus' cunning (mêtis) vs. heroic strength (biê) was debated by ancient ethicists — he is Plato's example of the silver-tongued manipulator, but also a Stoic model of endurance.
  • Geography / history: Ancient attempts to map the voyage placed Sirens near Capri, Scylla/Charybdis at the Strait of Messina, Calypso's Ogygia at Malta or Gibraltar, the Phaeacians at Corfu.
  • Cross-answer links: Polyphemus → Poseidon → Neptune → Roman mythology; Circe → Jason/Medea tossups (both encounter her island); Penelope → "faithful wife" tossups; Trojan Horse → Sinon, Cassandra, Laocoon.
