Quick Overview

TraditionHinduism — principal triad of deities
Sanskrit termTrimurti ("three forms/images")
Core functionsBrahma = Creator · Vishnu = Preserver · Shiva = Destroyer
Primary textsPuranas (esp. Vishnu Purana, Shiva Purana); Mahabharata; Bhagavata Purana
Key conceptCosmic cycle: creation → preservation → dissolution (srishti → sthiti → samhara)
Quizbowl weightCore mythology; appears in religion, world lit (Mahabharata/Ramayana), and philosophy packets

The Trimurti concept

  • One supreme reality (Brahman) manifests as three personal deities governing cosmic time.
  • Iconographically shown as a three-faced figure (trishiras) — each face representing one deity.
  • Most famous sculptural depiction: the Trimurti at Elephanta Caves, near Mumbai (c. 6th c. CE).
  • Brahma is least worshipped in practice; only two major temples (Pushkar, Rajasthan is the most famous).
  • Vaishnavism (Vishnu) and Shaivism (Shiva) are the two dominant Hindu sects; Brahma has no major sect.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Vishnu: blue-skinned god who sleeps on the serpent Shesha/Ananta; ten avatars (Dashavatara) — especially Rama and Krishna.
  • Shiva: trident (trishula), third eye, crescent moon, lingam, Nataraja dancing in a ring of fire.
  • Brahma: four heads (one burned off by Shiva), four arms, rides a hamsa (goose/swan), holds Vedas.

High-Yield Figures & Myths

The figures, myths, and texts most likely to be tossup answers or anchor clues. power = hard early clue. recog = recognition clue.

Brahma — the Creator
Sanskrit: ब्रह्मा · Role: Creation (srishti) · Consort: Saraswati · Vehicle: Hamsa (goose)
powerOriginally five-headed; Shiva burned off his fifth head because Brahma spoke disrespectfully — hence his curse of near-worship-lessness. Source: Shiva Purana.
recogFour remaining heads face all cardinal directions; represent the four Vedas (Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda).
recogSeated on or emerging from a lotus rooted in Vishnu's navel; wields four arms bearing Vedas, a lotus, a water-pot (kamandalu), and a rosary or spoon.
recogOnly major temple: Brahma Temple at Pushkar, Rajasthan — the most-clued location for Brahma questions.
powerHis wife Saraswati (also sometimes said to be his daughter, source of mythological tension) is goddess of learning, arts, and speech; rides a white swan, holds a veena.

Vishnu — the Preserver
Sanskrit: विष्णु · Role: Preservation (sthiti) · Consort: Lakshmi · Vehicle: Garuda
powerRests in yoga nidra (cosmic sleep) on the serpent Shesha (Ananta) floating on the primordial ocean (Kshira Sagara, the milk ocean); Brahma emerges from his navel-lotus.
recogBlue or dark-complexioned (associated with the infinite sky); four arms hold Shankha (conch — Panchajanya), Chakra (discus — Sudarshana), Gada (mace), and a lotus.
recogDashavatara — ten principal avatars descend when cosmic order (dharma) declines: Matsya (fish), Kurma (tortoise), Varaha (boar), Narasimha (man-lion), Vamana (dwarf), Parashurama, Rama, Krishna, Buddha, and the future Kalki.
recogVehicle is the eagle Garuda — natural enemy of the Nagas (serpents); itself a major tossup answer.
powerChurning of the milk ocean (Samudra Manthan): Vishnu takes the form of Kurma (tortoise) to support Mount Mandara as a churning rod; the event produces Amrita (nectar of immortality), the goddess Lakshmi, and the poison Halahala (drunk by Shiva).
wiki ↗
Shiva — the Destroyer / Transformer
Sanskrit: शिव · Role: Destruction/dissolution (samhara) · Consort: Parvati (also Sati) · Vehicle: Nandi (bull)
powerNataraja ("Lord of the Dance") — dances the Tandava (cosmic dance of destruction) within a ring of fire (prabhamandala); right foot stands on the dwarf demon Apasmara (symbol of ignorance); four arms hold fire, a drum (damaru), and gesture abhaya (protection).
recogThird eye on his forehead — burns anything in its gaze; crescent moon (Chandrashekhara) in his matted hair (jata); Ganga flows from his hair.
recogFive aspects (Panchabrahma): Sadyojata, Vamadeva, Aghora, Tatpurusha, Ishana. Iconographically holds a trident (trishula) and wears a serpent (Vasuki) as a garland; smears himself with ash (vibhuti).
recogWorshipped as the Shiva Lingam (aniconic pillar) in most temples — the most common form of Shaivite worship.
powerSati (first wife, daughter of Daksha) immolates herself at Daksha's yajna when Shiva is humiliated; Shiva carries her corpse in grief; Vishnu dismembers the body — the limbs fall to earth as Shakti Peethas (51 sacred sites).
recogSon Ganesha (elephant-headed) — Shiva unknowingly beheads him; replaces the head with an elephant's. Son Kartikeya / Murugan — god of war; rides a peacock.

Vishnu's Key Avatars — Rama & Krishna
7th and 8th avatars (Treta Yuga / Dvapara Yuga)
powerRama (7th avatar): protagonist of the Ramayana (attributed to Valmiki); rescues wife Sita from demon king Ravana of Lanka with help of Hanuman and the monkey army (Vanarasena); wields the divine bow.
recogKrishna (8th avatar): hero of the Mahabharata; delivers the Bhagavad Gita to Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. Childhood in Vrindavan, kills uncle Kamsa; associated with flute, peacock feather, butter-theft.
recogThe Bhagavad Gita opens as Arjuna refuses to fight; Krishna (as charioteer) expounds on dharma, karma, bhakti, and jnana yoga.
powerNarasimha (4th avatar, man-lion): kills the demon Hiranyakashipu who had a boon making him unkillable by man, beast, day, night, inside, outside, or by any weapon — Narasimha exploits every loophole (at dusk, on a threshold, with claws).
wiki ↗
Parvati / Shakti — Shiva's Consort & the Goddess Tradition
Sanskrit: पार्वती · Also: Durga, Kali, Devi, Uma
powerDurga ("the inaccessible") — warrior goddess created from the combined energies of all gods to kill the buffalo demon Mahishasura, whom no male god could defeat; rides a lion or tiger, ten arms, holds weapons given by each god.
recogKali — terrifying manifestation; black-skinned, garland of skulls, tongue out, stands on prostrate Shiva. Linked to the goddess of the Kalika Purana.
recogParvati reconverts the ascetic Shiva to household life — their union produces Ganesha and Kartikeya.
powerShakti Peethas: sacred sites where Sati's dismembered body parts fell — 51 total; major ones include Kamakhya (Assam) and Kalighat (Kolkata). "Shakti Peetha" clue almost always means Shiva/Sati mythology.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-asked myths and texts that appear as early/hard clues. One-line ID each.

Samudra Manthan (Churning of the Ocean)
Vishnu Purana, Bhagavata Purana
Gods and demons churn milk ocean using Mandara Mountain and Vasuki serpent as rope; yields Amrita, Lakshmi, and Halahala poison.
Vamana and King Bali (Trivikrama)
Vishnu's 5th avatar
Dwarf Vamana begs three paces of land from demon-king Bali; grows to cover heaven, earth, and underworld in three cosmic strides.
Daksha's Yajna and the Death of Sati
Shiva Purana / Devi Bhagavata
Daksha excludes Shiva from his great sacrifice; Sati immolates herself; Shiva's grief triggers cosmic chaos and the Shakti Peethas.
Ganesha's Origin
Shiva Purana
Parvati creates Ganesha from her body-dirt as a guard; Shiva beheads him unknowingly; restores him with an elephant's head.
The Burning of the God of Love (Kamadeva)
Shiva Purana
Kama (love god) tries to rouse ascetic Shiva's desire; Shiva's third eye reduces Kama to ash; Kama is eventually revived as Ananga ("bodiless").
Vishnu as Mohini
Mahabharata / Bhagavata Purana
Vishnu takes female form (Mohini) to enchant demons during Samudra Manthan and distribute Amrita only to the gods.
Prahlada and Hiranyakashipu
Bhagavata Purana
Devotee Prahlada survives repeated murder attempts by his demon father; Vishnu appears as Narasimha to rescue him.
Shiva Lingam Origin (Jyotir Lingam)
Shiva Purana
Vishnu and Brahma dispute who is supreme; a blazing pillar (lingam) appears; neither finds its end; Shiva declared supreme; 12 Jyotir Lingas are his holiest shrines.
Brahma's Fifth Head and Its Severing
Shiva Purana / Brahma Purana
Brahma grows a fifth head to watch Saraswati; the head speaks arrogantly; Shiva (or Bhairava) severs it — why Brahma has four heads today.
Garuda's Origin
Mahabharata, Adi Parva
Eagle-deity Garuda born to Vinata; steals Amrita to free his mother from the Nagas; becomes Vishnu's vehicle; national symbol of Indonesia.
Pralaya (Cosmic Dissolution)
Puranic cosmology
At the end of each kalpa (4.32 billion years) Shiva performs the Tandava; the universe dissolves into Vishnu, who sleeps until Brahma creates again.
Kurukshetra War & the Pandavas
Mahabharata (Vyasa)
Five Pandava brothers (Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula, Sahadeva) vs. 100 Kaurava cousins; Arjuna's charioteer is Krishna (Vishnu avatar); ends Dvapara Yuga.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Brahma — epithets & symbols

  • Chaturmukha — "four-faced"
  • Svayambhu — "self-born"
  • Pitamaha — "grandfather of all beings"
  • Hamsa (goose/swan) — his vehicle
  • Kamandalu — water pot he carries
  • Brahmaloka / Satyaloka — his realm
  • Temple: Pushkar, Rajasthan

Vishnu — epithets & symbols

  • Narayana — "refuge of humanity"
  • Hari — "remover of sins"
  • Vaikuntha — his celestial abode
  • Sudarshana Chakra — spinning discus weapon
  • Panchajanya — his conch shell
  • Kaumodaki — his mace
  • Garuda — eagle vehicle; enemy of Nagas
  • Shesha / Ananta — the cosmic serpent he rests on
  • Lakshmi — consort; goddess of wealth and fortune

Shiva — epithets & symbols

  • Mahadeva — "great god"
  • Mahakala — "lord of time/death"
  • Bhairava — fierce wrathful form
  • Rudra — Vedic precursor/storm god form
  • Nandi — sacred bull, his vehicle and gatekeeper
  • Damaru — hourglass drum; the sound of creation
  • Trishula — trident weapon
  • Kailash (Himalaya) — his mountain abode
  • Vibhuti — sacred ash he wears
  • Parvati / Uma / Gauri — consort (benevolent forms)

Key texts & concepts

  • Puranas — 18 major texts; Vishnu, Shiva, Brahma Puranas most cited
  • Bhagavad Gita — part of the Mahabharata; Krishna's teachings
  • Ramayana (Valmiki) — Rama's story; ~24,000 verses
  • Mahabharata (Vyasa) — ~100,000 verses; longest epic poem
  • Dharma — cosmic/moral order both Vishnu and Shiva uphold
  • Karma — law of action/consequence
  • Maya — cosmic illusion; Vishnu's power
  • Yugas: Satya, Treta, Dvapara, Kali — four cosmic ages
  • Vaishnavism (Vishnu-focused), Shaivism (Shiva-focused), Shaktism (Goddess-focused) — three major sects

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Brahma vs. BrahmanBrahma (long final -a) is the personal creator deity of the Trimurti. Brahman (neuter, no final -a in Sanskrit) is the impersonal absolute/universal soul in Vedanta philosophy. Different concepts entirely — an early clue about "impersonal ultimate reality" = Brahman, not Brahma.
Brahma vs. BrahminBrahmin (also Brahman) is the priestly caste in the Hindu varna system — unrelated to the deity Brahma despite the shared root brahm-.
Vishnu vs. IndraIndra is the Vedic king of the gods and god of thunder/rain (cognate with Zeus/Jupiter) — most prominent in the Rigveda. Vishnu is the Puranic preserver; far less prominent in the Vedas. "Vedic king of gods" = Indra, not Vishnu.
Shiva vs. RudraRudra is the Vedic storm/wilderness god who evolved into Shiva; the identification is explicit in the Shvetashvatara Upanishad. "Vedic forerunner of Shiva" or "Vedic storm god" = Rudra.
Nataraja vs. other dance formsNataraja specifically depicts Shiva's Tandava (destruction dance) in a ring of fire. Do not confuse with Vishnu or with the female dancer Saraswati.
Ganesha vs. KartikeyaBoth are sons of Shiva and Parvati. Ganesha = elephant-headed, remover of obstacles, vehicle is a mouse. Kartikeya / Murugan / Skanda = god of war, rides a peacock, six heads (Shanmukha).
Lakshmi vs. Saraswati vs. ParvatiLakshmi = wealth, fortune, lotuses; consort of Vishnu. Saraswati = knowledge, arts, veena, white swan; consort of Brahma. Parvati = domesticity, love; consort of Shiva. All three together = Tridevi (female counterpart of the Trimurti).
Rama vs. Krishna (both Vishnu avatars)Rama = hero of Ramayana, wields a bow, ideal king of Ayodhya, 7th avatar. Krishna = hero of Mahabharata, plays a flute, raised in Vrindavan, delivers Bhagavad Gita, 8th avatar. "Charioteer at Kurukshetra" = Krishna. "Rescues wife from Lanka" = Rama.
Hindu vs. Buddhist conceptsBrahma appears in Buddhist texts as a high god but is not supreme. Vishnu absorbs the Buddha as an avatar — Buddhism co-opted into Hindu narrative, not the reverse. "Samsara / karma / dharma" appear in both traditions but with distinct meanings.
