Quick Overview

Lived384–322 BCE; born in Stagira (Macedonia)
Teacher / SchoolStudent of Plato at the Academy; founded the Lyceum (~335 BCE)
PupilTutored Alexander the Great (~343–335 BCE)
Epithet"The Philosopher" (used by Aquinas); "The Stagirite"
MethodEmpirical observation + syllogistic logic; rejected Platonic Forms
LegacyEncyclopedic: logic, biology, physics, ethics, politics, rhetoric, poetics

Defining ideas

  • Teleology: everything has a telos (end/purpose); natural things strive toward their form.
  • Hylomorphism: all things = matter (hyle) + form (morphe) — form actualizes matter.
  • Four Causes: material, formal, efficient, final — "why?" has four answers.
  • Substance metaphysics: primary substance = the individual thing; rejected Platonic separated Forms.
  • Eudaimonia ("flourishing/happiness") as the highest human good — achieved via virtuous activity.
  • Peripatetic school: named for teaching while walking (peripatein) in the Lyceum.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Author of the Nicomachean Ethics — single most-given clue.
  • Tutor of Alexander the Great; student of Plato.
  • Coined syllogism; "all men are mortal" is the textbook example.
  • The Lyceum / Peripatetic school.
  • The Stagirite as a descriptor.

High-Yield Works & Ideas

The works and concepts most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard early clue. recog = recognition / mid-clue.

Nicomachean Ethics
c. 350 BCE · 10 books · dedicated to or named for his son Nicomachus
recogCenters on eudaimonia (happiness/flourishing) as the highest human good.
recogDoctrine of the mean (mesotes): virtue is the midpoint between excess and deficiency (courage = between recklessness and cowardice).
recogDistinguishes intellectual virtues (sophia, phronesis) from moral virtues (courage, justice, temperance).
powerBook X culminates in praise of the contemplative life (theoria) as highest eudaimonia; Book II defines virtue as a hexis (stable disposition) acquired by habit (ethos → ethics); the work is addressed/named for Nicomachus, either his father or son.

Politics
c. 350 BCE · 8 books
recog"Man is a political animal" (zoon politikon) — humans naturally live in a polis.
recogClassifies constitutions: monarchy, aristocracy, polity (correct forms) vs. tyranny, oligarchy, democracy (perversions).
recogArgues polity (constitutional rule by many for common good) is the best practical government.
powerCritiques Plato's Republic and the communal property of the Laws; defends private property; distinguishes natural from conventional slavery; Book II critiques Phaleas of Chalcedon and Hippodamus of Miletus as ideal-city theorists.
wiki ↗
Poetics
c. 335 BCE · survives in one book (tragedy); second book on comedy lost
recogDefines tragedy: imitation of a serious action arousing pity and fear, achieving catharsis.
recogSix elements of tragedy: plot (mythos), character, thought, diction, song, spectacle — plot is most important.
recogKey terms: hamartia (fatal flaw/error), anagnorisis (recognition), peripeteia (reversal of fortune).
powerUses Oedipus Rex by Sophocles as the ideal tragedy; disputes Plato's condemnation of poetry; argues mimesis is natural and educative; the "second book" on comedy is the premise of Umberto Eco's The Name of the Rose.
wiki ↗
Prior Analytics & the Organon
c. 350 BCE · six logical treatises collected as "Organon" by later editors
recogIntroduces the syllogism: deductive argument with two premises and a conclusion.
recogOrganon includes: Categories, On Interpretation, Prior Analytics, Posterior Analytics, Topics, Sophistical Refutations.
powerPosterior Analytics develops scientific demonstration (apodeixis): knowledge via syllogisms from necessary first principles; Topics treats dialectical reasoning from "endoxa" (reputable opinions); Barbara, Celarent are medieval mnemonics for syllogism moods.
wiki ↗
Metaphysics
c. 350 BCE · 14 books; title means "after the Physics" (ta meta ta physika)
recogOpens: "All men by nature desire to know" (Book Alpha, 980a).
recogUnmoved Mover (Book Lambda): eternal, immaterial first cause; "thought thinking itself" (noesis noeseos).
recogCritiques Plato's theory of Forms: "third man argument"; universals exist in things, not separately.
powerDistinguishes actuality (energeia) from potentiality (dynamis); substance (ousia) is the primary category of being; "being qua being" as the subject of first philosophy; Book Zeta's analysis of substance is the most-quoted chapter in Aristotle scholarship.
wiki ↗
De Anima (On the Soul)
c. 350 BCE · 3 books
recogDefines soul as the form of a natural body having the potential for life — soul is not separable from body.
recogThree-part soul: nutritive (plants), sensitive/perceptive (animals), rational (humans).
powerIntroduces the Active Intellect (nous poietikos) — the puzzle of whether it is immortal and separable; Book III's passive vs. active intellect distinction was debated by Alexander of Aphrodisias, Averroes (Ibn Rushd), and Aquinas; uses the wax and signet ring analogy for perception.

c. 350 BCE · 10 books
Systematic zoological observations; 500+ species described; foundational biology.
Parts of Animals / Generation of Animals
c. 350 BCE
Teleological anatomy; form determines function; males supply form, females matter.

c. 350 BCE · 4 books
Geocentric cosmology; fifth element aether fills celestial spheres; earth spherical.
Meteorologica
c. 350 BCE · 4 books
Weather, comets, Milky Way, rainbows explained via exhalations; "sublunary" phenomena.
Categories
c. 350 BCE · first book of Organon
Ten categories of being: substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, etc.

c. 350 BCE

Constitution of the Athenians
c. 330 BCE · rediscovered 1890
Historical account of Athenian governance; only extant constitutional study of 158 written.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key terms & concepts

  • Eudaimonia — flourishing/happiness, highest good
  • Arete — virtue/excellence
  • Phronesis — practical wisdom (highest intellectual virtue for action)
  • Sophia — theoretical wisdom
  • Catharsis — emotional purging via tragedy
  • Hamartia — tragic flaw or error
  • Peripeteia / Anagnorisis — reversal / recognition in drama
  • Syllogism — his logical deductive form
  • Enthymeme — rhetorical/probabilistic syllogism
  • Endoxa — reputable opinions used in dialectic

Metaphysical terms

  • Hylomorphism — matter + form compose all substances
  • Four Causes — material, formal, efficient, final
  • Actuality / Potentiality (energeia / dynamis)
  • Ousia — substance/being; primary subject of metaphysics
  • Unmoved Mover — eternal cause, "thought thinking itself"
  • Telos — end/purpose; teleological explanation
  • Prime Matter — pure potentiality underlying change
  • Aether — fifth element; fills heavenly spheres

People & institutions

  • Plato — teacher; Aristotle studied at his Academy ~20 years
  • Alexander the Great — his most famous pupil
  • Philip II of Macedon — hired Aristotle to tutor Alexander
  • Lyceum — his school in Athens; also called Peripatos
  • Theophrastus — his successor at the Lyceum
  • Averroes (Ibn Rushd) — "the Commentator" on Aristotle
  • Thomas Aquinas — Christianized Aristotle; called him "the Philosopher"
  • Alexander of Aphrodisias — "the Commentator" in antiquity

Famous quotes & sayings

  • "All men by nature desire to know." — Metaphysics, Book I
  • "Man is a political animal." — Politics
  • "We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act but a habit." — paraphrase of NE
  • "Amicus Plato, sed magis amica veritas" — attributed (Plato is dear, but truth is dearer)
  • Plato called him "the mind of the school" (ho nous)
  • Left Athens after Alexander's death: "lest Athens sin twice against philosophy" (alluding to Socrates)

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
PlatoPlato wrote dialogues (starring Socrates); his ethics = Forms/the Good/philosopher-king; soul is immortal and separable. Aristotle wrote treatises; soul = form of body (not separable); virtue via habit and the mean, not Forms. Plato = Republic/Symposium/Phaedo; Aristotle = NE/Politics/Poetics.
SocratesSocrates wrote nothing; known only via Plato and Xenophon. Socrates = irony, elenchus, "I know nothing." Aristotle is the one with systematic treatises, the Lyceum, and Alexander.
EpicurusEpicurus also pursues happiness but via ataraxia (tranquility/absence of pain) and withdrawal from politics; Aristotle's eudaimonia requires active political and social life. Epicurus founded the Garden; Aristotle founded the Lyceum.
Stoics (Zeno of Citium)Stoics: virtue is sufficient for happiness; external goods irrelevant; logos/fate. Aristotle: external goods (health, friends, wealth) also needed for full eudaimonia. Stoics founded the Stoa Poikile.
Thomas AquinasAquinas synthesized Aristotle with Christian theology (Summa Theologica); he called Aristotle "the Philosopher." Aquinas is 13th-century; Aristotle is 4th-century BCE. Tossups on Aquinas often mention Aristotle as an influence — but they are separate answers.
Averroes (Ibn Rushd)Averroes is the Islamic commentator on Aristotle (12th century CE, Cordoba); his own views are not Aristotle's. Aristotle questions may mention him as interpreter; Averroes questions may give "his commentaries on Aristotle" as a clue.
TheophrastusTheophrastus = Aristotle's student and Lyceum successor; wrote Characters (personality sketches) and Historia Plantarum (botany). Answer is Theophrastus, not Aristotle, if clues mention plant taxonomy or character sketches.

Cross-discipline big picture Aristotle sits at the intersection of nearly every quizbowl category: Philosophy (NE, Metaphysics, Logic), Literature (Poetics — hamartia/catharsis in every tragedy tossup), Science (Aristotle's biology, geocentric cosmology, and physics were canonical for 1,500 years), Political Theory (Politics feeds into Aquinas, Machiavelli, and Hobbes questions), and Medieval Islamic & Christian Thought (Averroes, Avicenna, Aquinas all answer to "commentator on Aristotle"). Recognizing a clue as "Aristotelian" — teleology, four causes, unmoved mover, eudaimonia, catharsis — lets you pivot across multiple subject areas instantly.
