Quick Overview

Born / Died1858–1917, Épinal, France
NationalityFrench
FieldSociology (founding figure)
Key positionFirst sociology professor in France; Bordeaux 1887, then Sorbonne
Core claimSociety is a reality sui generis — independent of individuals
JournalFounded L'Année sociologique, 1898

Defining ideas

  • Social facts — external, coercive norms that constrain individual behavior; must be studied like things (choses).
  • Collective conscience (conscience collective) — shared beliefs and moral attitudes that bind a society together.
  • Anomie — state of normlessness; breakdown of social norms leads to disorientation and deviance.
  • Mechanical vs. organic solidarity — small/homogeneous societies held by sameness vs. modern/complex societies held by interdependence/division of labor.
  • Sacred vs. profane — the fundamental binary underlying all religion.

High-Yield Works & Ideas

The 5 most frequently tested works and concepts. power = hard early clue. recog = recognition / mid-question clue.

Suicide (Le Suicide)
1897 · empirical sociological study
recogClassified suicide into four types: egoistic, altruistic, anomic, fatalistic.
recogEgoistic = too little social integration (loners); altruistic = too much (soldiers, hara-kiri); anomic = deregulation/normlessness; fatalistic = excessive regulation.
powerUsed official European statistics to show suicide rates vary by religion (Protestants > Catholics), marital status, and season — proving suicide is a social not merely individual phenomenon. First modern quantitative sociology.
wiki ↗
The Division of Labor in Society (De la division du travail social)
1893 · doctoral dissertation / book
recogIntroduced mechanical solidarity (pre-industrial, sameness) vs. organic solidarity (industrial, interdependence).
recogArgued increasing division of labor produces organic solidarity through mutual dependence of specialists.
powerIntroduced the concept of anomie here: when division of labor is not properly regulated, norms break down. Contrasted repressive law (mechanical societies, punitive) vs. restitutive law (organic societies, restorative).
wiki ↗
The Rules of Sociological Method (Les Règles de la méthode sociologique)
1895 · methodological manifesto
recogDefined social facts as external, coercive phenomena to be treated as "things" (choses).
recogDistinguished normal from pathological social facts (e.g., crime is normal and serves social functions).
powerArgued sociology must have its own independent method, not reducible to psychology or biology. Established sociological positivism. Famously said crime is functional: it clarifies moral boundaries and spurs social change.
wiki ↗
The Elementary Forms of Religious Life (Les Formes élémentaires de la vie religieuse)
1912 · major late work
recogArgued the sacred / profane dichotomy is the universal foundation of religion.
recogUsed Australian Aboriginal totemism (Arunta/Aranda) as the simplest case of religion; totem = the clan itself.
powerRadical thesis: when people worship the sacred, they are actually worshipping society itself — god is a symbolic projection of collective social power. Introduced concepts of collective effervescence (ritual excitement that renews social bonds) and the totem as emblem of collective identity.
wiki ↗
Anomie & the Concept of Social Integration
cross-cutting concept · first named 1893
recogAnomie = condition of normlessness; individuals lose moral guidance when social norms collapse or conflict.
recogUsed to explain deviance, high suicide rates, and economic crises (rapid industrialization disrupts norms).
powerDurkheim saw anomie as a symptom of insufficient moral regulation — distinct from lack of social integration. Robert Merton later adapted "anomie" in strain theory, so questions may distinguish Durkheim's anomie (norm breakdown) from Merton's (means/goals gap).

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-cited works, essays, and concepts — each in one line. These appear as early/hard clues.

Moral Education (L'Éducation morale) 1925 (posth.) Schools transmit collective moral norms; discipline is socializing, not punitive.
Professional Ethics and Civic Morals 1900 (lectures) Proposed occupational guilds (corporations) to reintegrate anomic modern workers.
Sociology and Philosophy (Sociologie et philosophie) 1924 (posth.) Essays on moral facts and value; defends sociology's claim over ethics.
"Individualism and the Intellectuals" 1898 · essay Written during Dreyfus Affair; defends individual rights as themselves a modern collective value.
Primitive Classification (with Marcel Mauss) 1903 · co-authored Classification systems (species, space, time) derive from social structure, not nature.
"The Dualism of Human Nature" 1914 · essay Individual body (profane) vs. soul/social self (sacred) — duality is universal.
Collective Effervescence concept in Elementary Forms Intense emotional energy in group ritual that renews social solidarity.
Organic Solidarity / Restitutive Law concept in Division of Labor Modern societies use restorative (not penal) law because interdependence replaces sameness.
Totemism / Clan Totem concept in Elementary Forms The totem emblem symbolizes the clan; worshipping it = worshipping society.
L'Année sociologique (journal) founded 1898 Annual journal Durkheim founded; assembled his "school" of collaborators.

Suicide type mnemonics

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Karl Marx Marx: conflict theory, class struggle, economic base determines superstructure, alienation. Durkheim: functionalism, social cohesion, integration. Both address industrial capitalism's social costs — Marx via conflict, Durkheim via normlessness (anomie).
Max Weber Weber: Verstehen (interpretive understanding of subjective meaning), Protestant ethic → capitalism, ideal types, bureaucracy. Durkheim: positivist, treats social facts as things, collective over individual. Both "founding trio" with Marx.
Auguste Comte Comte coined "sociology" and positivism; Durkheim built on him but distinguished sociology from philosophy more sharply and did real empirical research. Comte = ancestor; Durkheim = disciplinary founder.
Robert Merton Merton adapted Durkheim's anomie into "strain theory" (gap between culturally prescribed goals and legitimate means). Questions on "strain theory" or "five adaptations" → Merton, not Durkheim.
Ferdinand Tönnies Tönnies: Gemeinschaft (community, organic bonds) vs. Gesellschaft (society, contractual bonds) — similar to Durkheim's mechanical/organic but different labels. German vs. French tradition.
Georg Simmel Simmel: micro-level formal sociology (dyads, triads, the stranger, money as abstraction). Durkheim: macro-level, collective phenomena, institutions. Both contemporaries but very different scales.
Marcel Mauss Mauss was Durkheim's nephew and student; wrote The Gift (theory of reciprocal exchange). If clue mentions "gift exchange," "total social fact," or "potlatch" → Mauss, not Durkheim.

🌐 Cross-discipline big picture Durkheim is the anchor of structural functionalism in sociology — his ideas feed directly into Talcott Parsons's systems theory and Robert Merton's strain theory (anomie adapted). In religion studies, the sacred/profane binary influenced Mircea Eliade and later cognitive science of religion (why ritual works by marking boundaries). In statistics, his use of regression on aggregate social data (suicide rates) is an early example of ecological correlation — and a source lesson on the ecological fallacy (inferring individual-level causation from group statistics). Quizbowl tossups on functionalism, anomie, totemism, or sacred/profane all route through Durkheim.
