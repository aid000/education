Quick Overview

Greek nameArtemis
Roman nameDiana
ParentsZeus (Jupiter) & Leto (Latona)
TwinApollo (god of sun/music/prophecy)
DomainsHunt, wilderness, moon, chastity, childbirth
Sacred animalsDeer (hind), bear, hunting dog
SymbolsSilver bow & arrows, crescent moon, quiver
EpithetsPhoebe, Cynthia (from Mt. Cynthus), Potnia Theron ("Mistress of Animals")

Defining traits

  • Eternal virgin goddess — fiercely guards her chastity and that of her nymphs.
  • Born on the island of Delos; helped her mother Leto deliver Apollo immediately after her own birth.
  • Leads a band of hunting nymphs; punishes any who violate their vows.
  • Roman Diana worshipped especially at Lake Nemi (the "sacred grove") — her priest held office by killing his predecessor.
  • Often equated with the moon goddess Selene/Luna and the underworld goddess Hecate — the "triple goddess."

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Silver bow & arrows (Apollo has golden) — the single most reliable distinguisher.
  • Actaeon turned into a stag for seeing her bathe.
  • Requests eternal virginity from Zeus as her first gift.

High-Yield Myths & Figures (Very Common)

The myths and figures most likely to be the tossup answer or anchor a question. power = hard early clue. recog = recognition / mid-clue.

Actaeon
Key myth · Hunter transformed into a stag
recogActaeon accidentally stumbles upon Artemis bathing at a spring with her nymphs on Mt. Cithaeron.
recogArtemis splashes him with water, transforming him into a stag; his own 50 hunting dogs then tear him apart.
powerHis grandfather was Cadmus (founder of Thebes); his hounds are named in Ovid's Metamorphoses III. The myth is a paradigm for transgression against divine modesty. Ovid stresses Actaeon's innocence — he did not intend to look.
Links to: Ovid · Metamorphoses III · Cadmus · Thebes · hunting myths
wiki ↗
Orion
Key myth · Giant hunter & hunting companion
recogOrion is Artemis's beloved hunting companion; his death causes her grief — the rare instance of Artemis forming an attachment.
recogMost common version: Apollo, worried about Artemis's chastity, tricks her into shooting Orion by challenging her aim at a distant object in the sea (Orion's head).
powerAlternate version: Orion was killed by a scorpion sent by Gaia (or Artemis herself) after he boasted he'd kill every beast. Artemis places him in the sky as the constellation Orion; the scorpion becomes Scorpius, set opposite so they never meet.
Links to: Apollo · constellations · Scorpius · Greek astronomy
wiki ↗
Niobe
Key myth · Apollo & Artemis punish hubris
recogNiobe, queen of Thebes, boasts she is superior to Leto because she has 14 children (7 sons, 7 daughters) vs. Leto's 2.
recogApollo kills all 7 sons with silver arrows; Artemis kills all 7 daughters — Artemis's arrows = sudden death in women.
powerGrief-stricken Niobe is transformed into a weeping rock on Mt. Sipylus (in Lydia) — a natural rock formation with water seeping through it. Niobe is a classic quizbowl hubris paradigm; clue for both Apollo and Artemis tossups.
Links to: Apollo · Leto · hubris · Thebes · Ovid Metamorphoses VI
wiki ↗
Callisto
Key myth · Nymph violated & transformed
recogCallisto, one of Artemis's hunting nymphs, is seduced (or raped) by Zeus disguised as Artemis herself.
recogWhen her pregnancy is revealed during bathing — Artemis expels her from the band — she is turned into a bear: either by Artemis (for breaking chastity), Hera (jealousy), or Zeus.
powerHer son Arcas nearly kills her while hunting; Zeus places both in the sky as Ursa Major (Callisto) and Ursa Minor / Boötes (Arcas). Hera, still jealous, persuades Poseidon never to let them set below the ocean — hence circumpolar stars.
Links to: Zeus · constellations · Ursa Major · Arcadia · Ovid Met. II
wiki ↗
Iphigenia
Key myth · Trojan War cycle
recogAgamemnon kills a sacred deer of Artemis (or boasts superiority to her); Artemis stills the winds at Aulis, trapping the Greek fleet.
recogThe seer Calchas says Agamemnon must sacrifice his daughter Iphigenia to appease Artemis. He does so — or Artemis substitutes a deer and spirits Iphigenia to Tauris as priestess.
powerEuripides wrote two plays: Iphigenia at Aulis and Iphigenia among the Taurians. In the latter, Iphigenia oversees human sacrifice for Artemis until her brother Orestes rescues her. The myth is the engine of the Oresteia's guilt cycle.
Links to: Agamemnon · Oresteia · Euripides · Trojan War · Aeschylus
wiki ↗
Birth on Delos & First Gifts from Zeus
Foundational myth · Hymn to Artemis
recogArtemis born on the island of Delos (or according to some sources on nearby Ortygia); twin of Apollo; immediately assists Leto in Apollo's delivery — making her a patron of childbirth despite being a virgin.
recogWhen young, Artemis sits on Zeus's knee and asks for gifts: eternal virginity, a silver bow and arrows (made by the Cyclopes), 60 ocean nymphs and 20 river nymphs as attendants, and all the mountains.
powerPrimary source: the Homeric Hymn to Artemis (Hymn 9 & 27) and Callimachus's Hymn III to Artemis — the fullest account of her birth requests. Callimachus specifies she chose Amnisus in Crete for her horses and the first hill she visited for her hunting grounds.
Links to: Callimachus · Homeric Hymns · Delos · Apollo · Cyclopes
wiki ↗
Temple of Artemis at Ephesus
Wonder of the Ancient World · cult site
recogOne of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World — the largest temple in the ancient world at its height, located at Ephesus (modern Turkey).
recogBurned down on the night Alexander the Great was born (356 BCE) by Herostratus, who wanted fame; rebuilt even grander.
powerThe Ephesian Artemis was a distinct, syncretic goddess — her cult statue showed a figure with multiple breasts (or egg-shaped pendants), very different from the Greek Artemis. The temple was ultimately destroyed by the Goths (262 CE) and later by a Christian mob.
Links to: Seven Wonders · Herostratus · Alexander the Great · Ephesus · Asia Minor

Girls aged 5–10 "played the bear" for Artemis at Brauron sanctuary near Athens.
Diana Nemorensis
Roman cult · Lake Nemi
Priest-king of Lake Nemi held office by killing predecessor with a bough; Frazer's Golden Bough.
Aura

Nymph who taunted Artemis's femininity; raped by Dionysus as punishment.

Hunting heroine

Calydonian Boar Hunt
Aetolian myth
Artemis sends giant boar to ravage Calydon after king Oeneus forgot her in harvest offerings.
Hecate (triple goddess)
Syncretism
Artemis = moon/hunt; Selene = sky; Hecate = underworld — the "lunar triad."
Taurian Artemis
Black Sea cult
Barbarian goddess of Tauris identified with Artemis; received human sacrifice.
Apollo & Python (Delos context)
Birth aftermath
After birth, both twins avenge Leto — Apollo kills Python; Artemis kills Tityos who assaulted Leto.
Scythian Artemis / Bendis
Thracian syncretism
Thracian moon goddess Bendis equated with Artemis; torch-race festival at Piraeus, mentioned in Plato's Republic.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Epithets & titles

  • Phoebe — "bright one" (lunar epithet; also her aunt's name)
  • Cynthia — from Mt. Cynthus on Delos (birth mountain)
  • Potnia Theron — "Mistress of Animals" (Homeric)
  • Agrotera — "of the wilderness / the hunt"
  • Locheia — goddess of childbirth
  • Orthia — Spartan cult name; ritual flogging at her altar
  • Trivia (Roman) — "goddess of the crossroads" (equated with Hecate)
  • Lucina (Roman) — goddess of light and childbirth

Symbols & sacred things

  • Silver bow & arrows (Apollo = gold)
  • Crescent moon on her brow or crown
  • Deer / golden hind — her sacred animal
  • Bear — Callisto; Brauron rite ("playing the bear")
  • Quiver & hunting dogs
  • Cypress tree — sacred to her
  • Torch (as Hecate/Trivia; also Brauronia)

Key numbers & places

  • Born on Delos (or Ortygia); assisted Leto's labor
  • 60 ocean nymphs + 20 river nymphs in her retinue (Callimachus)
  • 7 sons & 7 daughters of Niobe slain
  • 50 hunting dogs that killed Actaeon
  • Ephesus — greatest temple (Seven Wonders)
  • Lake Nemi — Roman sacred grove
  • Brauron — Attic sanctuary for girls
  • Aulis — fleet-stranding, Iphigenia sacrifice

Primary sources

  • Callimachus, Hymn III — fullest portrait; birth requests from Zeus
  • Homeric Hymn 9 & 27 — brief, canonical
  • Ovid, Metamorphoses — Actaeon (III), Callisto (II), Niobe (VI)
  • Euripides — Iphigenia at Aulis, Iphigenia among the Taurians, Hippolytus
  • Apollodorus, Library — systematic genealogy
  • J.G. Frazer, The Golden Bough — Diana Nemorensis (opening myth)

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
ApolloTwin brother — golden bow vs. Artemis's silver bow; Apollo = sun, music, prophecy, plague in males; Artemis = moon, hunt, sudden death in women. Niobe and Tityos myths involve both — read which sex was targeted.
Selene / LunaSelene is the Titan personification of the moon (drives the lunar chariot); Artemis acquired lunar associations later via syncretism. Selene loves Endymion; Artemis does not. If the myth involves driving a chariot, it's Selene.
HecateHecate = crossroads, magic, underworld, torches, three-way crossroads, witchcraft. She is not primarily a huntress. The "triple goddess" = Artemis + Selene + Hecate — each is a distinct answer.
AthenaBoth are virgin goddesses, but Athena = wisdom, war strategy, crafts (owl, aegis, olive tree, spear); Artemis = wilderness, hunt, moon (bow, deer, crescent). Athena is a city goddess; Artemis roams the wild.
Aphrodite / VenusAphrodite and Artemis are frequent opposites — love vs. chastity. Hippolytus honors Artemis and scorns Aphrodite; he dies for it. The hunt clue points to Artemis; the love/beauty clue to Aphrodite.
PersephoneBoth associated with change of seasons, but Persephone = underworld, pomegranate, Demeter's daughter. Artemis hunts above ground; Persephone rules below.
Diana (Roman) vs. ArtemisFunctionally identical but Roman context: Diana = Lake Nemi sacred grove, Frazer's Golden Bough; Diana's festival = August 13 (Nemoralia). In Roman poetry she also = Trivia (crossroads) and Lucina (childbirth).

Big-Picture Cross-Discipline Links
  • Literature: Artemis / Diana anchors major texts — Callimachus's Hymn III, Ovid's Metamorphoses (Actaeon, Callisto, Niobe), Euripides' Hippolytus and Iphigenia plays, and Atalanta in Calydon by Swinburne.
  • Anthropology / Folklore: Frazer opens The Golden Bough with the Diana Nemorensis priest — making her the starting point of comparative religion as a discipline.
  • Astronomy: Orion / Scorpius opposition, Ursa Major (Callisto), and the asteroid 105 Artemis and 78 Diana; also NASA's Artemis moon program.
  • Fine Arts: Titian's Diana and Actaeon and Diana and Callisto (both National Gallery); Domenichino's Diana; Houdon's Diana the Huntress sculpture.
