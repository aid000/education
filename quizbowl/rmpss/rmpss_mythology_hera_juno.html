Quick Overview

Greek nameHera (Ἥρα)
Roman nameJuno
DomainMarriage, women, childbirth; Queen of the Olympians
ParentsCronus & Rhea (swallowed, then disgorged)
HusbandZeus (Jupiter in Rome); his sister and wife
Sacred animalsPeacock, cow (epithet Boopis = "ox-eyed")
Sacred plantPomegranate, lily, lotus
Roman monthJune named after Juno

Core epithets

Boopis / Boôpis Ox-eyed Queen Basileia (Queen) Teleia (of perfect marriage) Argeia (of Argos) Lacinia (Juno at Croton) Juno Moneta Juno Lucina (childbirth) Pronuba (wedding guardian)

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • The peacock — her signature bird; Argus's hundred eyes transferred to its tail feathers.
  • "Ox-eyed" (boopis) — Homer's repeated epithet in the Iliad.
  • Jealous persecution of Zeus's lovers/offspring — Heracles, Io, Semele, Leto, Callisto.
  • Hera is always on the Greek side at Troy (she backed Paris's judgment losers).
  • Roman Juno Moneta — her temple on the Capitoline where Roman coins were minted (moneta → "money").

How Hera appears in quizbowl

  • Frequently the antagonist in tossups about Heracles, Io, Semele, or the Trojan War.
  • Named as Zeus's wife when the answer is Zeus — and vice versa.
  • Her wrath at Zeus's infidelities is the most-clued aspect across all categories.
  • Art tossups: depicted in paintings of the Judgment of Paris and Birth of the Milky Way.

High-Yield Myths (Very Common)

The myths and figures most likely to generate Hera/Juno tossups. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

Persecution of Heracles
Core myth · Hera's longest-running vendetta in all of Greek mythology
powerHera sent two snakes to kill the infant Heracles in his crib; he strangled them — his first feat.
recogHera drove Heracles into a madness that caused him to kill his wife Megara and their children — the act that necessitated the Twelve Labors.
recogShe sent a gadfly to torment him during several labors; she allied with Eurystheus (who assigned the labors) against him.
recogZeus tricked her into nursing the infant Heracles; when she realized, she pulled away — her spilled milk formed the Milky Way (Via Lactea).

Io and Argus Panoptes
Core myth · Hera's surveillance and punishment of a Zeus lover
powerIo, priestess of Hera at Argos, was seduced by Zeus, who turned her into a white heifer to hide her; Hera demanded the heifer and set Argus Panoptes ("all-seeing," 100-eyed) to guard her.
recogHermes (Mercury), sent by Zeus, lulled Argus to sleep with stories/music and killed him — hence Hermes' epithet Argeiphontes ("Argus-slayer").
recogHera transferred Argus's hundred eyes to the tail of her peacock to honor him.
recogHera sent a gadfly to torment Io across the world; Io swam the strait later called the Bosphorus ("ox-ford") and reached Egypt, where she bore Epaphus by Zeus.
wiki ↗
Destruction of Semele
Core myth · Hera destroys the mother of Dionysus
powerHera disguised herself as Semele's old nurse Beroe (or a mortal woman) and convinced Semele to ask Zeus to appear in his full divine glory.
recogWhen Zeus appeared as lightning, Semele was incinerated; Zeus saved the unborn Dionysus by sewing him into his thigh — hence Dionysus is "twice-born."
recogSemele was the daughter of Cadmus, founder of Thebes. Her son Dionysus later retrieved her from the underworld and deified her as Thyone.
wiki ↗
Judgment of Paris and the Trojan War
Core myth · Hera's pro-Greek vendetta at Troy
powerEris (Discord) tossed the Apple of Discord ("for the fairest") at the wedding of Peleus and Thetis; Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite competed. Paris awarded it to Aphrodite — Hera was not chosen.
recogHera backed the Greeks throughout the war; she deceived Zeus with the help of Aphrodite's girdle (borrowed from Aphrodite) and Hypnos (Sleep), lulling Zeus so Poseidon could help the Greeks — the Dios apatê ("deception of Zeus"), Iliad Book 14.
recogAfter Troy fell, Hera prolonged suffering of some Greeks (e.g., shipwrecked fleets) and supported punishment of those who had dishonored Athena.
wiki ↗
Persecution of Leto
Core myth · Hera harries Zeus's pregnant lover across the world
powerHera forbade any land under the sun to receive Leto for childbirth; she also sent the serpent Python to pursue her.
recogOnly the floating island Delos (not "fixed" under the sun, per one tradition) received Leto; there she gave birth to Artemis first, then Apollo.
recogHera detained Eileithyia (goddess of childbirth) on Olympus to prolong Leto's labor; the other goddesses bribed her with a necklace to come and assist.
wiki ↗
Hera's Patronage of Jason
Core myth · Hera as benefactor in the Argonautica
powerHera favored Jason because King Pelias had neglected her worship; when Jason crossed a river and lost a sandal helping an old woman (Hera in disguise), the fulfilled oracle sealed Pelias's doom.
recogThroughout Apollonius of Rhodes' Argonautica, Hera and Athena protect the Argo; Hera persuades Aphrodite to send Eros to inflame Medea's love for Jason.
recogHera guided the Argo through the Clashing Rocks (Symplegades), one of the few myths where she acts as protector rather than persecutor.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Figures, myths, and texts that appear as early/hard tossup clues for Hera or Juno. One-line ID each.

Callisto
Zeus's lover → bear
Hera turned Callisto into a bear; Zeus placed her as Ursa Major; Arcas (son) nearly killed her.
Ixion
Hera deceived by cloud double
Zeus made a cloud-Hera (Nephele); Ixion impregnated it — siring the Centaurs; punished on a fiery wheel.
Tiresias and the blindness
Hera blinds the prophet
Hera blinded Tiresias for agreeing with Zeus that women enjoy more pleasure in sex; Zeus compensated with prophetic sight.
Birth of Hephaestus
Disputed parentage
One tradition: Hera conceived Hephaestus parthenogenetically in anger at Zeus's solo birth of Athena; then threw him from Olympus for his lameness.
Hera's children
Canonical offspring with Zeus
Ares, Hebe, Eileithyia (and sometimes Hephaestus) — all by Zeus. Hebe is the cup-bearer; Eileithyia governs childbirth.
Gerana / Pygmies
Hera punishes boastful mortal
Hera turned Gerana (who claimed she surpassed Hera in beauty) into a crane; origin of the Pygmy-Crane wars.
Binding of Hera
Zeus's punishment — Iliad 1
Zeus hung Hera from the sky with golden chains and anvils on her feet for instigating a revolt among the gods.
Hera at Samos
Major cult site
The Heraion of Samos — one of Greece's largest temples; Hera's birth/wedding-night said to be on Samos (or Euboea).
Juno Moneta
Roman cult — Capitoline
Temple of Juno Moneta housed the Roman mint; moneta → English "money" and "mint"; geese of Juno warned of the Gallic attack 390 BCE.
Aeneid — Juno's Wrath
Vergil, Aeneid Book 1
Juno's hatred of Aeneas drives the epic's plot; she asks Aeolus for winds to shipwreck him; Vergil opens: "Tantaene animis caelestibus irae?"
Dido and Carthage
Aeneid Books 1–4
Juno (with Venus) causes Aeneas and Dido to shelter in a cave during a storm, staging their "marriage"; Venus allows it to delay Aeneas from Italy.
Echo and Hera
Punishment of a nymph
Hera cursed Echo to only repeat others' last words, because Echo had distracted Hera with chatter while Zeus was with other nymphs.
Hera and the Golden Apples of Hesperides
Wedding gift myth
The golden apple tree in the garden of the Hesperides was a wedding gift from Gaia to Hera; guarded by the serpent Ladon.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Symbols & sacred attributes

  • Peacock — primary symbol; Argus's eyes on its tail
  • Cow / white heifer (epithet Boopis)
  • Pomegranate — symbol of marriage and fertility
  • Scepter and diadem — marks of queenship
  • Veil/crown — standard iconography in sculpture
  • Cuckoo — Zeus courted Hera disguised as a cuckoo
  • Lily / lotus

Key epithets (quizbowl clue phrases)

  • "Ox-eyed" / Boopis — Homeric epithet in Iliad
  • "White-armed" (leukolenos) — another Homeric tag
  • Basileia — "Queen"
  • Teleia — protector of lawful marriage
  • Argeia — worshipped chiefly at Argos
  • Juno Lucina — Roman goddess of light / childbirth
  • Juno Pronuba — goddess who attends brides
  • Juno Moneta — "Warner"; coins, Capitoline geese

Recurring myth roles / motifs

  • Gadfly — sent to torment Io and Heracles
  • Argus Panoptes — 100-eyed watchman, killed by Hermes
  • Eileithyia — Hera's daughter; childbirth goddess she weaponizes
  • Madness — sent to Heracles, Ino, Athamas, Dionysus
  • "Deception of Zeus" (Dios apatê) — Iliad 14, borrowing Aphrodite's girdle
  • Apple of Discord — origin of the Judgment of Paris
  • Milky Way — formed from her spurted breast milk (nursing infant Heracles)

Primary texts where Hera/Juno appears

  • Homer, Iliad — pro-Greek schemer; "ox-eyed queen"
  • Hesiod, Theogony — genealogy; birth from Cronus/Rhea
  • Homeric Hymns — Hymn 12 to Hera
  • Apollodorus, Library — persecution of Heracles, Io, Semele
  • Apollonius, Argonautica — Hera as Jason's patron
  • Vergil, Aeneid — Juno as primary divine antagonist
  • Ovid, Metamorphoses — Io, Callisto, Semele, Echo

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Athena / MinervaBoth are major Olympian queens and favor certain warriors, but Athena is goddess of wisdom/warfare/crafts (born from Zeus's head), never jealous of lovers, and backed Odysseus/Achilles. Hera = marriage, jealousy, maternity.
Aphrodite / VenusAphrodite is Zeus's daughter, goddess of erotic love. In the Iliad she sides with Troy (helped Paris win). Hera sides with Greeks. Hera borrows Aphrodite's girdle — they are allies here but distinct roles.
Demeter / CeresDemeter is Hera's sister, goddess of grain and harvest; her daughter Persephone/Proserpina defines the seasons. No peacock, no jealousy storyline. Demeter never appears in the Iliad as a military player.
Hestia / VestaAlso a sister of Hera; goddess of the hearth and household fire. Vestal Virgins in Rome. Entirely passive in myth — no wrath, no love affairs. Peacock = Hera; sacred flame = Hestia.
IrisIris is Hera's personal messenger (rainbow goddess), not Hera herself. Iris is often named as the one carrying Hera's commands — the clue is about Hera's message, but the answer could be Iris.
Juno vs. Hera roles in textsIn Vergil's Aeneid, Juno opposes Aeneas/Rome (a Roman epic concern). In Homer's Iliad, Hera opposes Troy (aiding Greeks). Same goddess, opposing sides in different literary traditions — read the source text.
EuropaEuropa was a Phoenician princess abducted by Zeus (as a bull) — she is a victim of Zeus, not Hera, though Hera's jealousy logic applies. Europa is not Io: Io was turned into a cow; Europa rode Zeus-as-bull to Crete.

Cross-discipline "answer web" Hera/Juno connects to multiple tossup categories: Art — Rubens' Birth of the Milky Way (her milk spurted from the suckling Heracles); Raphael's The Triumph of Galatea era; Cranach / Rubens' Judgment of Paris paintings. Literature — Vergil's Aeneid (Juno as antagonist), Ovid's Metamorphoses (Io, Callisto, Semele, Echo), Homer's Iliad. Science — the asteroid 3 Juno; the month June; NASA's Juno spacecraft (Jupiter orbiter). Etymology — "money" and "mint" from Juno Moneta; "Bosphorus" = "ox-ford" from Io's crossing.
