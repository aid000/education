Quick Overview

Datesc. 470–399 BCE, Athens
EraClassical Athens; the pre-Socratics → Socratic turn
Written worksNone — known only through others (Plato, Xenophon, Aristophanes)
Execution399 BCE; charged with impiety and corrupting the youth; sentenced to drink hemlock
Primary sourcePlato's dialogues (Socrates is almost always the main interlocutor)
Quizbowl categoryPhilosophy → Ancient Greek; appears in both philosophy and history tossups

Defining traits

  • Socratic method (elenchus): cross-examination by question — leads interlocutors to aporia (impasse), exposing false beliefs.
  • "I know that I know nothing" (Socratic ignorance / intellectual humility) — the Delphic oracle called him wisest because he alone admitted ignorance.
  • Oral philosopher: no writings; all knowledge via Plato's dialogues, Xenophon's Memorabilia, and Aristophanes's comedy The Clouds.
  • Daimon: Socrates claimed an inner divine voice (daimonion) that warned him away from wrong actions.
  • Virtue = knowledge (arete): believed wrongdoing is always a result of ignorance — no one does wrong willingly.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Death by hemlock in 399 BCE — single most given-away fact.
  • The Socratic method / elenchus and "the gadfly of Athens."
  • Depicted in David's The Death of Socrates (1787).

High-Yield Concepts & Texts (Very Common)

The core ideas and Platonic texts most likely to anchor a tossup on Socrates. power = hard, early buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

Plato's Apology
c. 399 BCE (written by Plato) · Socrates's defense speech at trial
recogSocrates defends himself against charges of impiety (not believing in city's gods) and corrupting youth.
recogInvokes the Delphic oracle ("no one is wiser than Socrates") — he investigated everyone and found they knew nothing, so he was wisest by admitting ignorance.
recogCalls himself the "gadfly of Athens" — Athens is a lazy horse; he stings it awake.
powerJury votes 280 to 220 to convict; Socrates proposes free meals at the Prytaneum as his "penalty," then reluctantly suggests a fine of 30 minae. Famous closing line: "The unexamined life is not worth living."

Plato's Phaedo
c. 380 BCE · the final hours before Socrates drinks hemlock
recogSocrates argues for immortality of the soul with four arguments: Opposites, Recollection, Affinity (soul resembles Forms), and Final Cause.
recogThe Theory of Forms is central here — soul gains knowledge of Forms before birth (anamnesis).
recogNarrator is Phaedo of Elis; dialogue addressed to Echecrates.
powerSocrates compares the body to a prison from which death releases the soul. His last words: "Crito, we owe a cock to Asclepius" — a debt of thanks to the god of healing for the "cure" of death.
wiki ↗
Plato's Symposium
c. 385–370 BCE · speeches on eros (love) at a dinner party
recogSeries of speeches praising Eros; Socrates's speech relays wisdom from the priestess Diotima of Mantinea.
recog"Ladder of Beauty" (Diotima's Ladder): ascent from love of a single beautiful body → all beautiful bodies → beautiful souls → beautiful activities → knowledge → the Form of Beauty itself.
recogAlcibiades enters drunk, praises Socrates's physical ugliness concealing inner beauty — compares him to a Silenus figure (ugly outside, gold inside).
powerAristophanes's speech (in the same dialogue) gives the "split-human" myth of origin of love — humans once had two faces, eight limbs; Zeus split them, so each seeks their other half. Socrates's speech immediately follows and corrects this account.
wiki ↗
Plato's Republic (Politeia)
c. 380 BCE · Socrates as main speaker on justice and the ideal state
recogSocrates argues the just soul mirrors the tripartite city: reason / spirit / appetite (philosopher-kings / warriors / producers).
recogContains the Allegory of the Cave (Book VII): prisoners see only shadows; the philosopher ascends to sunlight (Forms); reluctantly returns to lead.
recogContains the Allegory of the Sun (Form of the Good = the Sun) and the Divided Line.
powerOpens with Socrates forced to visit Polemarchus's house after the festival of Bendis at Piraeus; primary interlocutors include Thrasymachus ("justice is the advantage of the stronger") and Glaucon (Ring of Gyges thought experiment).
wiki ↗
Plato's Euthyphro
c. 399 BCE (dramatic date) · outside the law court just before Socrates's trial
recogSocrates meets Euthyphro, who is prosecuting his own father for murder — ironic counterpoint to Socrates's own impiety charge.
recogClassic Socratic elenchus on piety: every definition Euthyphro offers is refuted.
powerContains the Euthyphro dilemma: "Is the pious loved by the gods because it is pious, or is it pious because it is loved by the gods?" — still central to philosophy of religion.
wiki ↗
Plato's Crito
c. 399 BCE (dramatic date) · Socrates in prison before execution
recogFriend Crito offers to help Socrates escape; Socrates refuses — one must never do wrong even in response to wrong.
recogSocrates personifies the Laws of Athens as speaking to him — he agreed to abide by them by living in Athens.
powerEarly articulation of a social contract theory: citizens owe obedience to just laws in return for benefits received. Pairs with the Apology as the trial-and-death sequence.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-cited dialogues, figures, and concepts — appear as early/hard tossup clues. One-line ID each.

Meno
Slave-boy geometry proof; anamnesis (learning as recollection of prior knowledge).
Theaetetus
What is knowledge? Rejects perception = knowledge; ends in aporia.
Protagoras
Can virtue be taught? Socrates versus the Sophist Protagoras; ends inconclusively.
Gorgias
Rhetoric vs. philosophy; Socrates argues rhetoric is mere flattery, not genuine art.
Laches
What is courage? Two Athenian generals (Laches, Nicias) fail to define it.
Charmides
What is temperance (sophrosyne)? Features young Charmides and Critias.
Alcibiades I
Socrates urges Alcibiades to know thyself before pursuing politics.
Menexenus
Parody of funeral oratory; Socrates recites a speech he learned from Aspasia.
Xenophon's Memorabilia
Non-Platonic source; defends Socrates's character and piety against charges.
Aristophanes's The Clouds
Comic caricature of Socrates as a sophist who runs a "Thinkery"; possibly hurt his reputation before trial.
David's The Death of Socrates (1787)
Neoclassical painting; Socrates gestures upward, holding the hemlock cup on a prison bed.
Socratic problem
Scholarly difficulty: which Platonic portrait (early vs. late dialogues) reflects the historical Socrates.
Ion
Poets speak by divine inspiration, not knowledge — rhapsode Ion cannot explain Homer.
Philebus
On pleasure and the good life; the good is a mixture of knowledge and measured pleasure.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key terms & epithets

  • Elenchus — Socratic cross-examination leading to refutation
  • Aporia — productive impasse; conclusion of many dialogues
  • Maieutics — the "midwife" method; Socrates helps birth knowledge in others
  • Daimonion — inner divine voice; warned against, never prompted
  • Arete — virtue; Socrates identifies it with knowledge
  • Anamnesis — learning as recollection of Forms from pre-natal soul
  • Sophrosyne — temperance / self-knowledge
  • "Gadfly of Athens" — from the Apology

People around Socrates

  • Plato — primary student; absent at the execution (Phaedo)
  • Xenophon — soldier-historian; wrote Memorabilia, Symposium, Apology
  • Alcibiades — brilliant, reckless student; Socrates tried to restrain him
  • Crito — old friend; offered escape before execution
  • Xanthippe — Socrates's wife; proverbially shrewish; present at his death
  • Meletus, Anytus, Lycon — the three accusers at trial
  • Chaerephon — friend who asked the Delphic oracle about Socrates's wisdom
  • Diotima — priestess; Socrates's teacher on Eros in the Symposium

Key quotes & numbers

  • "The unexamined life is not worth living." (Apology)
  • "I know that I know nothing." (paraphrase of Apology)
  • "Crito, we owe a cock to Asclepius." — last words (Phaedo)
  • Tried 470 BCE (birth) / 399 BCE (death, age ~71)
  • Convicted 280–220; executed in Athens by hemlock
  • Oracle consulted at Delphi by Chaerephon
  • "Know thyself" (gnothi seauton) — Delphic maxim Socrates embodies

Cross-discipline links

  • Art: Jacques-Louis David, The Death of Socrates (1787, MMA New York)
  • Literature / poetry: Aristophanes, The Clouds (~423 BCE) — comic attack
  • History: Peloponnesian War context; Alcibiades link to Sicilian Expedition
  • Religion: Euthyphro dilemma still central in philosophy of religion
  • Political theory: Crito's social contract; Republic's philosopher-kings
  • Later philosophy: Stoics (virtue = good); Kierkegaard (irony); Nietzsche (Anti-Socrates)

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
PlatoPlato wrote everything; Socrates wrote nothing. Plato appears in own name rarely in dialogues — when you see "Socrates" speaking, that's Plato's voice. Theory of Forms is Plato's own development, not documented Socratic doctrine. Plato's death: 347 BCE (natural); Socrates: 399 BCE (hemlock).
AristotleAristotle was Plato's student (not Socrates's); founded the Lyceum; wrote prolifically. Known for empiricism and the syllogism — the opposite of Socratic oral questioning. Aristotle never met Socrates (born 384 BCE).
The Sophists (Protagoras, Gorgias)Sophists charged fees, taught rhetoric for success; Socrates charged nothing and sought truth. Socrates is distinguished from Sophists throughout the dialogues. Aristophanes's Clouds wrongly lumps him with them.
PythagorasPythagoras (~570–495 BCE) is a pre-Socratic, mystic, associated with mathematics and the theorem. Socrates explicitly marks the "Socratic turn" from cosmology to ethics. Different century.
EpicurusEpicurus (~341–270 BCE) is post-Socratic; founded the Garden school; focused on pleasure (ataraxia) as the highest good — opposite of Socratic virtue = knowledge. Much later.
Marcus Aurelius / StoicsStoics claim Socratic descent but are much later (3rd c. BCE onward). Marcus Aurelius is a Roman emperor and Stoic, not an Athenian. Stoic giveaway: Meditations, logos, providence — not hemlock or elenchus.

Big Picture: Socrates across the canon Socrates sits at the hinge of Western philosophy: the pre-Socratics asked "what is the cosmos?" — he turned the question to "what is the good life?" That pivot (ethics over cosmology) is called the Socratic turn. His insistence that virtue = knowledge threads directly into Plato's Theory of Forms, Aristotle's critique of that theory, the Stoic equation of reason with virtue, and even Kierkegaard's use of Socratic irony. In quizbowl, Socrates bleeds into art (David's painting), history (Athenian democracy, Peloponnesian War, Alcibiades), literature (Aristophanes), and religion/philosophy (Euthyphro dilemma). The hemlock cup and the gadfly metaphor are the fastest giveaways; the Phaedo last words and the Symposium's Diotima are the hardest power clues.
