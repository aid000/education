Quick Overview

ReligionIslam — world's second-largest religion, ~1.9 billion adherents
ProphetMuhammad ibn Abdullah (c. 570–632 CE), born Mecca
Holy TextThe Quran — literal word of God (Allah), revealed to Muhammad via angel Jibril
Calendar EpochHijra = 622 CE — Muhammad's migration from Mecca to Medina; Year 1 AH
Sacred LanguageArabic — Quran considered untranslatable; recitation a devotional act
Founding CityMecca (birthplace, Kaaba); Medina (city of the Prophet, first Islamic state)

Defining identifiers

  • Five Pillars of Islam: Shahada (profession of faith), Salat (prayer 5x daily), Zakat (almsgiving), Sawm (fasting in Ramadan), Hajj (pilgrimage to Mecca).
  • Quran structure: 114 chapters (suras), arranged roughly longest-to-shortest after the opening; subdivided into ayat (verses).
  • Hijra as calendar reset: the Islamic lunar calendar (AH = Anno Hegirae) begins at 622 CE; year ~1446 AH in 2024.
  • Sunni / Shia split originates in disagreement over Muhammad's rightful successor after his death in 632.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "Recitation" — the word Quran means "recitation" or "the reading."
  • "Night of Power" (Laylat al-Qadr) — first revelation of the Quran to Muhammad in a cave on Mount Hira during Ramadan.
  • "migration from Mecca to Medina" — the Hijra; marks Year 1 of the Islamic calendar.
  • "Seal of the Prophets" — Muhammad's title; final prophet in the Abrahamic chain.

High-Yield Figures, Texts & Events

The core items most likely to be tossup answers or anchor clues. power = hard early clue. recog = recognition / mid-clue.

The Quran (al-Qur'an)
Revealed c. 610–632 CE · Compiled c. 650 CE under Caliph Uthman
recogThe holy scripture of Islam; its name means "the recitation."
recogDivided into 114 suras; opens with al-Fatiha ("the Opening"), ends with al-Nas.
recogDelivered by the angel Jibril (Gabriel) to Muhammad over ~23 years.
powerThe first revelation came in the Cave of Hira on Mount Jabal al-Nour; the command was "Iqra!" ("Recite!") — opening of Sura 96 (al-Alaq, "The Clot"). The first word revealed is considered iqra.
powerThe standard written edition is the Uthmanic codex (compiled ~650 CE), standardized under Caliph Uthman ibn Affan; earlier variant collections (masahif) were destroyed. Suras are ordered roughly longest-to-shortest — NOT in chronological order of revelation.

Muhammad (the Prophet)
c. 570–632 CE · Born Mecca, died Medina
recogFounder of Islam; title "Seal of the Prophets" (Khatam al-Nabiyyin) — final prophet.
recogBorn to the Quraysh tribe in Mecca; orphaned early, raised by uncle Abu Talib.
recogFirst wife: Khadijah — a wealthy widow, first convert to Islam; married ~595 CE.
recogFirst male convert: Ali ibn Abi Talib (cousin); first adult male outside family: Abu Bakr.
powerNickname before prophethood: al-Amin ("the Trustworthy"). The Isra' wal-Mi'raj ("Night Journey and Ascension") took Muhammad from Mecca to Jerusalem (al-Aqsa Mosque) and then through the seven heavens — central to Islamic cosmology and the reason for the 5 daily prayers.
powerHis sayings and actions outside the Quran are recorded in the Hadith; his life is the Sira (biography). The Sira Rasul Allah by Ibn Ishaq (compiled by Ibn Hisham) is the earliest major biography.

The Hijra (al-Hijra)
622 CE · Mecca → Medina (then called Yathrib) · Year 1 AH
recogMuhammad's migration from Mecca to Medina in 622 CE; marks Year 1 of the Islamic lunar calendar (AH = Anno Hegirae).
recogProvoked by Quraysh persecution of the Muslim community in Mecca.
recogMedina was formerly called Yathrib; renamed "Madinat al-Nabi" (City of the Prophet) after the Hijra.
powerDuring the Hijra, Muhammad and Abu Bakr hid in the Cave of Thawr for three days while Quraysh searchers passed by — a spider spinning a web over the entrance is a famous legend of divine protection. The official route was via the town of Quba, where Muhammad built the first mosque.
powerThe Constitution of Medina (Sahifat al-Madinah), established shortly after the Hijra, is considered one of the world's first written constitutions; it created a confederation between Muslim and Jewish tribes of Medina. The Islamic calendar was formally adopted by Caliph Umar (~638 CE), backdating Year 1 to the Hijra.

Khadijah bint Khuwaylid
c. 555–619 CE · First wife of Muhammad; first Muslim
recogFirst Muslim — she immediately believed Muhammad after his first revelation.
recogWealthy merchant of Mecca; Muhammad worked as her trading agent before marriage.
powerHer death in 619 CE (the "Year of Sorrow," along with Abu Talib's death) left Muhammad politically and emotionally vulnerable, accelerating the circumstances leading to the Hijra.

Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (First Caliph)
573–634 CE · Caliph 632–634 CE
recogClosest companion of Muhammad; first adult male convert; accompanied Muhammad on the Hijra.
recogBecame first Caliph after Muhammad's death in 632 — the Sunni–Shia split hinges on whether Ali (Muhammad's cousin/son-in-law) should have succeeded instead.
powerAbu Bakr commissioned the first collection of Quranic verses into a single manuscript (suhuf) after many Quran memorizers (huffaz) were killed at the Battle of Yamama (632). This collection was later used by Uthman to standardize the Quran.

Key Suras of the Quran
Memorization targets for quizbowl
recogAl-Fatiha (Sura 1) — "the Opening"; 7 verses; recited in every unit of daily prayer.
recogAl-Baqara (Sura 2, "the Cow") — longest sura, 286 verses; contains the Ayat al-Kursi ("Throne Verse", 2:255), most memorized verse outside prayer.
recogAl-Ikhlas (Sura 112, "Sincerity") — 4-verse declaration of tawhid (divine unity); worth "one-third of the Quran" in hadith.
recogSura 96 (al-Alaq, "The Clot") — contains the first revealed verses: "Recite in the name of your Lord who created."
powerAl-Kahf (Sura 18, "the Cave") — contains the story of the Companions of the Cave (Ahl al-Kahf) — seven sleepers woken after 300 years; recommended for Friday recitation. Sura 12 (Yusuf) tells the complete story of Joseph — unique as a single narrative sura; called "the most beautiful of stories."

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-tested but real quizbowl items — appear as hard/early clues. One-line ID each.

Isra' wal-Mi'raj Muhammad's Night Journey: Mecca → Jerusalem → seven heavens on Buraq; revealed 5 daily prayers.
Battle of Badr (624 CE) First major Muslim military victory; Muslims outnumbered; divine assistance claimed; decisive for early Islam's credibility.
Battle of Uhud (625 CE) Muslims suffered setback vs. Quraysh; Muhammad wounded; Hamza (his uncle) killed.
Battle of the Trench (627 CE) Medina defended by a trench (idea from Salman al-Farisi); Quraysh siege failed.
Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (628 CE) 10-year peace pact between Muhammad and Mecca; allowed Muslim pilgrimage; called a "clear victory" in Quran (Sura 48).
Conquest of Mecca (630 CE) Muhammad entered Mecca largely peacefully; cleared 360 idols from the Kaaba; general amnesty granted.
Farewell Sermon (Khutbat al-Wada', 632 CE) Muhammad's last sermon at Mount Arafat during his only Hajj; announced completion of the religion.
The Hadith Recorded sayings/actions of Muhammad; major collections: Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim (the "Six Books").
The Sira (biography) Life-story of Muhammad; earliest source: Ibn Ishaq's Sirat Rasul Allah, preserved by Ibn Hisham.
Aisha bint Abi Bakr Muhammad's youngest wife; daughter of Abu Bakr; major hadith transmitter; central figure in Sunni-Shia split (Battle of the Camel, 656).
Ali ibn Abi Talib Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law (married Fatima); first Imam of Shia Islam; fourth Caliph for Sunnis.
Fatima al-Zahra Muhammad's daughter by Khadijah; wife of Ali; mother of Hasan and Husayn; revered in both Sunni and Shia Islam.
Cave of Hira (Jabal al-Nour) Site of first Quranic revelation; Muhammad received "Iqra!" (Sura 96) from Jibril here in Ramadan.
Tawhid Islamic concept of divine unity/oneness; the opposite of shirk (polytheism); central theological doctrine.
Jizya Poll tax levied on non-Muslim subjects (dhimmis) under Islamic rule; granted protection and religious autonomy in return.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key terms & epithets

  • Ummah — the global Muslim community
  • Rasul Allah — "Messenger of God" (Muhammad's title)
  • Khatam al-Nabiyyin — "Seal of the Prophets" (Muhammad)
  • al-Amin — "the Trustworthy" (Muhammad's pre-prophecy nickname)
  • Jibril / Gabriel — angel who delivered the Quran
  • Sura / Ayat — chapter / verse of the Quran
  • Hafiz (pl. huffaz) — one who has memorized the entire Quran

Sacred places

  • Kaaba (Mecca) — cube-shaped structure; qibla direction for prayer; said built by Ibrahim and Ismail
  • Masjid al-Haram — Grand Mosque, Mecca; surrounds the Kaaba
  • Masjid al-Nabawi — Prophet's Mosque, Medina; site of Muhammad's tomb
  • Masjid al-Aqsa — Jerusalem; destination of Isra'; third holiest site
  • Mount Arafat — key Hajj station; site of Farewell Sermon
  • Cave of Thawr — Muhammad and Abu Bakr hid here during Hijra

Numbers & dates

  • 114 suras in the Quran; 6,236 verses (ayat)
  • 610 CE — first revelation; 622 CE — Hijra (Year 1 AH)
  • 632 CE — Muhammad's death; ~650 CE — Uthmanic codex
  • 5 daily prayers; 30 fasting days in Ramadan; 2.5% Zakat rate
  • 360 idols destroyed at Kaaba (630 CE conquest)
  • Lunar calendar — 354 days/year; ~11 days shorter than solar

Companions & figures

  • Khadijah — first wife, first Muslim
  • Abu Bakr — first male adult convert, first caliph
  • Umar ibn al-Khattab — second caliph; adopted Islamic calendar
  • Uthman ibn Affan — third caliph; standardized the Quran
  • Ali ibn Abi Talib — fourth caliph (Sunni); first Imam (Shia)
  • Bilal ibn Rabah — first muezzin; freed Ethiopian slave
  • Salman al-Farisi — Persian companion; proposed the trench at Battle of the Trench

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Quran vs. HadithQuran = literal word of God; Hadith = reported sayings/deeds of Muhammad. The Quran is revelation; Hadith is human-transmitted tradition. Both are authoritative, but the Quran is primary.
Hijra vs. HajjHijra = the 622 CE migration Mecca→Medina (calendar event). Hajj = the annual pilgrimage TO Mecca (one of the Five Pillars). Same city, entirely different events.
Sunni vs. Shia splitDispute is over succession after Muhammad's death — Sunnis accept Abu Bakr as first caliph; Shia hold Ali should have been first. The split is political/theological, not about the Quran itself.
Muhammad vs. Ibrahim (Abraham)Ibrahim is the patriarch revered by all Abrahamic faiths — builder of the Kaaba with Ismail; father of monotheism. Muhammad is the final prophet in that chain, not the same person.
Ramadan vs. EidRamadan = the fasting month (ninth Islamic month; when the Quran was first revealed). Eid al-Fitr = feast ending Ramadan. Eid al-Adha = feast of sacrifice during Hajj, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice Ismail.
Torah/Bible vs. QuranIslam regards the Torah (Tawrat) and Gospel (Injil) as earlier authentic revelations, but believes they were corrupted — the Quran supersedes them as the final, preserved revelation. The Quran references many Biblical figures.
Sharia vs. FiqhSharia = divine law as revealed in Quran and Sunna. Fiqh = Islamic jurisprudence — human scholars' interpretation and elaboration of Sharia. Four main Sunni schools of fiqh: Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali.

Cross-Discipline Big Picture The Hijra calendar (622 CE = 1 AH) anchors Islamic history questions alongside parallel world events — it's ~100 years after the fall of Rome's western empire, ~60 years before the Tang Dynasty peak in China. The Quran's revelation in Arabic shaped Arabic as a prestige literary language across a continent; its first printed edition (1537–38 in Venice) connects to early printing-press history. The Constitution of Medina links to political-science questions about early constitutionalism. The Sunni–Shia split (632 CE) threads through every subsequent Middle Eastern history question — Umayyad vs. Abbasid caliphates, the Battle of Karbala (680 CE, death of Husayn), modern Iran vs. Saudi Arabia questions, and the Five Pillars connect directly to the dedicated Five Pillars study sheet.
