Quick Overview

Born / Died11 February 1847, Milan, Ohio — 18 October 1931, West Orange, New Jersey
NationalityAmerican
FieldInvention, electrical engineering, applied research; founder of the modern industrial R&D lab
PatentsHeld ~1,093 U.S. patents (a record in his lifetime)
Nickname"The Wizard of Menlo Park"
CompanyFounded Edison Electric Light Co.; merged into General Electric (GE) in 1892

Career arc (phases as clues)

  • Telegraph beginnings (1860s–1870s): Started as a telegraph operator; first patents were a vote recorder and an improved stock ticker.
  • Menlo Park "invention factory" (1876–1881): The phonograph (1877) and the practical incandescent light bulb (1879) made him world-famous.
  • Electric power systems (1880s): Built the Pearl Street Station (1882) in Manhattan and championed direct current (DC) in the War of the Currents.
  • West Orange & later years (1887–1931): Bigger lab; the kinetoscope (motion pictures), the alkaline storage battery, and the Edison Effect observation.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • The first commercially practical incandescent light bulb with a long-lasting carbon filament.
  • Inventor of the phonograph, whose first recording was "Mary Had a Little Lamb."
  • The "Wizard of Menlo Park" and his New Jersey "invention factory."
  • Backed DC against Tesla/Westinghouse's AC in the War of the Currents.

High-Yield Inventions & Principles (Very Common)

The most-asked topics. power = hard, buzz-early clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

Practical Incandescent Light Bulb
1879 · U.S. Patent 223,898 (granted 1880)
recogNot the first bulb, but the first commercially practical, long-lasting one — a high-resistance carbon filament in an evacuated glass bulb.
recogThe famous demonstration used a carbonized bamboo / cotton-thread filament burning for many hours; paired with the Edison screw base still used today.
powerEdison's team tested thousands of filament materials ("genius is one percent inspiration, ninety-nine percent perspiration"). Predecessors include Joseph Swan in Britain — they later merged interests as Ediswan — and Humphry Davy's early arc light. The key was a high-resistance filament + good vacuum enabling a parallel-wired distribution system.
wiki ↗
Phonograph
1877 · U.S. Patent 200,521
recogThe first device to record and reproduce sound; sound vibrations indented a groove on a tinfoil-wrapped cylinder (later wax).
recogEdison's first recorded words were "Mary had a little lamb"; the invention earned him the "Wizard" fame.
powerGrew out of his telegraph/telephone work. His cylinder format competed with Emile Berliner's flat disc gramophone, which ultimately won the market. Edison's record label and "Diamond Disc" came later.
wiki ↗
Menlo Park Laboratory ("Invention Factory")
established 1876 · Menlo Park, New Jersey
recogThe world's first industrial research-and-development laboratory, organizing teams of "muckers" to invent on a schedule.
recogEarned Edison the nickname "The Wizard of Menlo Park"; birthplace of the phonograph and the practical light bulb.
powerThe model of systematic team-based invention is arguably Edison's most consequential contribution — a template for later corporate labs. He later built a larger complex at West Orange.
wiki ↗
Direct Current & the War of the Currents
1880s · Pearl Street Station 1882
recogEdison built the first central power plant, the Pearl Street Station (1882) in Lower Manhattan, distributing direct current (DC).
recogFought Tesla and George Westinghouse's alternating current (AC) in the "War of the Currents."
powerTo brand AC as deadly, Edison's camp publicly electrocuted animals and promoted AC for the first electric chair (1890). DC ultimately lost because it couldn't be transmitted efficiently over distance; AC won the grid.
wiki ↗
Edison Effect (Thermionic Emission)
1880–1883
recogObserved that a current can flow across the vacuum from a hot filament to a separate electrode inside a bulb — electrons "boiling off" a hot metal.
recogThis thermionic emission is the basis of the vacuum tube (thermionic valve).
powerEdison patented it but didn't see its use; John Ambrose Fleming used it to build the diode valve (1904), and Lee de Forest added a grid for the amplifying triode (Audion) — launching electronics. It's the one major Edison contribution to pure physics.
wiki ↗
Kinetoscope & Carbon Microphone
carbon transmitter 1877–78 · kinetoscope 1891–1894
recogThe kinetoscope was an early peep-hole motion-picture viewer (one viewer at a time), developed with assistant W. K. L. Dickson.
recogThe carbon-button microphone (transmitter) dramatically improved the loudness of Bell's telephone and was used in phones for a century.
powerFilms were shot at his "Black Maria" studio in West Orange. Edison's motion-picture patents fed the monopolistic Motion Picture Patents Company (the "Edison Trust"), which helped push filmmakers toward Hollywood.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-known work that appears as early/hard tossup clues or power lines.

Quadruplex Telegraph
1874
Sent four signals (two each way) over one wire simultaneously; an early money-making breakthrough sold to Western Union.
Universal Stock Ticker
1869–1871
Improved stock-price printer; its sale (~$40,000) funded his first real workshop.
Nickel–Iron Alkaline Battery
~1901
Rechargeable storage battery (Edison cell) marketed for electric vehicles and industry.
Electric Pen / Mimeograph base
1876
Stencil-duplicating electric pen; precursor tech later linked to the mimeograph.
Fluoroscope
1896
Improved X-ray viewing screen; his assistant Clarence Dally died of radiation exposure, ending Edison's X-ray work.
"Black Maria" Film Studio
1893
First purpose-built movie studio, a tar-paper shack in West Orange that rotated to catch sunlight.
Talking Doll / Dictating Machine
1890; 1880s
Phonograph spin-offs: a creepy recorded-voice doll and office dictation machines.
Ore-Milling & Cement Ventures
1890s–1900s
Costly magnetic iron-ore separation scheme; later Portland cement business (poured-concrete houses).
Vote Recorder
1869 · first patent
His very first patent; legislators didn't want it, teaching him to invent things people will buy.
"Edison Trust" (MPPC)
1908
Motion Picture Patents Company pooled film patents into a monopoly; later broken up under antitrust law.

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Nikola TeslaTesla, a former Edison employee, championed alternating current (AC) and the rotating magnetic field. Edison backed DC. "Menlo Park," "phonograph," "incandescent bulb" → Edison; "AC induction motor," "Tesla coil," "Wardenclyffe" → Tesla.
George WestinghouseWestinghouse was the industrialist who bought Tesla's AC patents and beat Edison's DC; he also invented the railway air brake. He's Edison's business rival, not an inventor of the bulb.
Joseph SwanBritish chemist who independently developed an incandescent bulb around the same time; the two merged interests as Ediswan. "British inventor of the bulb" → Swan.
Alexander Graham BellBell patented the telephone (1876). Edison improved it with the carbon microphone but did not invent it. "Telephone" / "Watson, come here" → Bell.
Lee de Forest / J. A. FlemingThey turned the Edison Effect into working vacuum tubes (Fleming's diode 1904, de Forest's triode/Audion). Edison observed the effect but didn't build the amplifying tube. "Audion / triode" → de Forest.
The Lumière BrothersThe Lumières' Cinématographe projected film for an audience; Edison's kinetoscope was a single-viewer peep-box. "Projected movie / first public film screening" → Lumières.

💡 The through-line: invention as an industry Edison's real breakthrough wasn't any single gadget — it was turning invention into a systematic, team-based industry at Menlo Park. From the phonograph and incandescent bulb to the DC power grid, the kinetoscope, and the Edison Effect, his work spans sound, light, power, and film.
