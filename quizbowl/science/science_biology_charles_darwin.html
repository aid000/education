Quick Overview

Darwin, c. 1875
(Elliott & Fry)
NationalityBritish (English; born Shrewsbury, 1809–1882)
FieldNaturalist — biology, geology, botany
Core contributionTheory of evolution by natural selection
Key voyageHMS Beagle, 1831–1836
Landmark bookOn the Origin of Species, 1859
Buried atWestminster Abbey (near Newton)

Defining ideas

  • Natural selection: heritable variation + differential survival/reproduction → adaptation over generations.
  • Common descent: all life shares a common ancestor — "one long argument."
  • Gradualism: evolution proceeds by slow accumulation of small changes (natura non facit saltum).
  • Sexual selection: separately explained extravagant traits (peacock's tail) driven by mate choice.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • The HMS Beagle voyage and the Galápagos Islands (finches, tortoises).
  • On the Origin of Species published 1859; co-announced with Alfred Russel Wallace at the Linnean Society 1858.
  • Grandfather Erasmus Darwin also a naturalist (proto-evolutionary ideas).

High-Yield Discoveries & Works (Very Common)

The 6 results / works most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

Natural Selection
Developed ~1838; published in On the Origin of Species, 1859
powerConcept crystallized after reading Thomas Malthus's Essay on the Principle of Population (1838); Darwin kept it secret ~20 years.
recogMechanism: heritable variation exists in populations; individuals better suited to environment survive and reproduce more — "survival of the fittest" (Spencer's phrase, adopted by Darwin in 5th edition).
powerDarwin called the book "one long argument"; the word "evolution" does not appear until the 6th edition (he used "descent with modification").
wiki ↗
On the Origin of Species
1859 · John Murray, London
powerFull title: On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection, or the Preservation of Favoured Races in the Struggle for Life. First edition of 1,250 copies sold out the first day.
recogContains the famous "tangled bank" closing paragraph and the single diagram (branching tree of life).
powerWent through 6 editions during Darwin's lifetime; co-prioritized with Wallace's paper at the Linnean Society, July 1, 1858.
wiki ↗
The HMS Beagle Voyage
1831–1836 · circumnavigation
powerShip captain was Robert FitzRoy; Darwin was 22 when he boarded; original naturalist was Robert McCormick (who left the voyage). Darwin was recommended by professor John Stevens Henslow.
recogGalápagos Islands (Sep–Oct 1835): finches with differently shaped beaks on different islands; giant tortoises. Darwin's mockingbird specimens, not finches, were the initial puzzle.
powerAlso visited: Punta Alta (Argentina) — fossil megafauna; Tierra del Fuego — three Fuegians FitzRoy was returning; Coral reefs of Keeling Islands — led to Darwin's subsidence theory of atoll formation.
wiki ↗
The Descent of Man
1871 · John Murray
powerFull title: The Descent of Man, and Selection in Relation to Sex. First book to explicitly apply evolution to human origins; also the first full treatment of sexual selection.
recogArgued humans and African apes share a common ancestor; predicted fossils would be found in Africa — confirmed later by Australopithecus discoveries.
powerIntroduced the concept that emotions and moral sense evolved — prefiguring evolutionary psychology. Companion volume The Expression of Emotions in Man and Animals (1872) followed.
wiki ↗
Darwin–Wallace Co-Discovery (1858 Papers)
Linnean Society of London, July 1, 1858
powerWallace sent Darwin his "Ternate paper" from Maluku, Indonesia in June 1858, independently describing natural selection; Darwin feared being scooped. Charles Lyell and Joseph Dalton Hooker arranged the joint reading.
recogNeither Darwin nor Wallace was present at the Linnean Society meeting. Wallace graciously deferred priority to Darwin.
powerWallace's paper: "On the Tendency of Varieties to Depart Indefinitely from the Original Type." The joint papers received little attention at the time.
wiki ↗
Darwin's Finches (Adaptive Radiation)
Observed 1835; analyzed ~1837 with ornithologist John Gould
powerDarwin initially mislabeled his Galápagos specimens as wrens, blackbirds, and gross-beaks. John Gould identified all 13 as closely related finches in January 1837 — this revelation crystallized Darwin's thinking on common descent.
recogBeak shapes (ground finch, tree finch, warbler finch) adapted to different food sources — the textbook example of adaptive radiation and character displacement.
powerThe term "Darwin's finches" was coined by ornithologist Percy Lowe in 1936 and popularized by David Lack's 1947 book; Darwin himself rarely emphasized them in his published work.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-known results and works that appear as early/hard tossup clues. One-line ID each.

Barnacle Monographs (Cirripedia)
1851–1854 — 4 volumes
Eight years dissecting barnacles; established Darwin's credentials as a serious taxonomist.
The Voyage of the Beagle (Narrative)
1839 — journal of the voyage
Popular travel narrative; Darwin's most widely read book in his lifetime.
Coral Reef Subsidence Theory
1842 — The Structure and Distribution of Coral Reefs
Atolls form when volcanic islands slowly subside, leaving reef fringe; confirmed by drilling in 1950s.
The Expression of Emotions in Man and Animals
1872
Emotions cross-cultural and shared with animals; first major use of photography in a scientific book.
Insectivorous Plants
1875
Decades of experiments on sundews (Drosera) and Venus flytraps; showed plants "digest" prey.
The Power of Movement in Plants
1880 (with Francis Darwin)
Demonstrated phototropism; proposed coleoptile tip as the "sense organ" — anticipating auxins.
The Formation of Vegetable Mould through the Action of Worms
1881 — last book published
40-year study of earthworms; unexpected bestseller; shows Darwin's attention to humble mechanisms.
Pangenesis Hypothesis
1868 — in The Variation of Animals and Plants under Domestication
Wrong theory of heredity via "gemmules"; Galton's blood-transfusion experiments refuted it.
The Various Contrivances by which Orchids are Fertilised by Insects
1862 — first post-Origin book
Predicted long-tongued Madagascar moth pollinating Angraecum sesquipedale; moth found 1903.
Down House, Downe, Kent
Darwin's home, 1842–1882
The "Sandwalk" thinking path in the garden; now a museum (English Heritage).
The "Big Species Book" (unpublished)
Abandoned 1858 on receipt of Wallace's letter
Draft ~250,000 words; later published posthumously as Natural Selection.
Cross and Self Fertilisation in Vegetables
1876
11-year experiments showed cross-fertilization produces more vigorous offspring — early demonstration of hybrid vigor.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key terms & concepts

  • Natural selection — core mechanism
  • Descent with modification — Darwin's preferred phrase for evolution
  • Sexual selection — peacock's tail; mate-choice-driven traits
  • Adaptive radiation — rapid diversification into ecological niches
  • Malthusian struggle — population outstrips resources; key inspiration
  • Natura non facit saltum — "nature makes no leaps" (gradualism)

Collaborators & influencers

  • Alfred Russel Wallace — co-discoverer; Ternate paper 1858
  • Charles Lyell — geologist; Principles of Geology; uniformitarianism
  • Joseph Dalton Hooker — botanist; Darwin's closest scientific confidant
  • Thomas Henry Huxley — "Darwin's Bulldog"; 1860 Oxford debate vs. Wilberforce
  • John Stevens Henslow — Cambridge botanist who recommended Darwin for the Beagle
  • Thomas Malthus — Essay on Population; the trigger for natural selection idea
  • Asa Gray — Harvard botanist; Darwin's chief American ally
  • Robert FitzRoy — Beagle captain; later a bitter anti-evolutionist

Locations & instruments

  • Galápagos Islands — finches, marine iguanas, tortoises
  • Down House, Downe, Kent — Darwin's home; "Sandwalk"
  • Christ's College, Cambridge — B.A. 1831; collected beetles avidly
  • Edinburgh University — attended briefly (dropped medicine)
  • Shrewsbury, Shropshire — birthplace (The Mount)
  • Westminster Abbey — burial, near Newton and Herschel

Famous quotes & numbers

  • "There is grandeur in this view of life…" — closing line of Origin
  • "I see no good reason why the views given in this volume should shock the religious feelings of anyone."
  • 1859 — Origin published
  • 1858 — Linnean Society joint announcement
  • 1831–1836 — Beagle voyage
  • 1871 — Descent of Man
  • 1809 — birth (same year as Abraham Lincoln)
  • £10 note (UK, 2000–2017) — Darwin's portrait

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Alfred Russel WallaceWallace independently discovered natural selection from the Malay Archipelago (Ternate, 1858). He never had a long voyage like the Beagle. Darwin = Origin, Galápagos, Down House; Wallace = The Malay Archipelago (1869), biogeography, Wallace Line separating Asian/Australian fauna.
Jean-Baptiste LamarckLamarck proposed inheritance of acquired characteristics (use-and-disuse) — giraffes stretch necks, offspring born with longer necks. Darwin rejected this mechanism; the Neo-Lamarckism debate runs through the 19th century. Lamarck died 1829, before Darwin published.
Thomas Henry HuxleyHuxley was Darwin's publicist and defender, not a theorist of evolution. He coined "agnosticism" and debated Bishop Wilberforce at Oxford 1860 ("Would you rather have a gorilla for a grandfather or a bishop?"). He wrote Evidence as to Man's Place in Nature (1863).
Gregor MendelMendel's pea-plant experiments (1865) explained the mechanism of heredity that Darwin lacked. Darwin had no knowledge of Mendel's work; the Modern Synthesis (1930s–1940s: Fisher, Haldane, Wright, Dobzhansky) merged Darwinian selection with Mendelian genetics.
Erasmus DarwinCharles's grandfather; wrote Zoonomia (1796) with proto-evolutionary speculations and The Temple of Nature (poem). An influence but no mechanism of selection. Often used as an "early clue" in tossups to lead to Charles.
Herbert SpencerSpencer coined "survival of the fittest" (Darwin borrowed the phrase); applied evolution to sociology (Social Darwinism). Tossups: if the clue is about sociology or "social evolution," the answer is likely Spencer, not Darwin.
Ernst HaeckelGerman biologist who popularized Darwin in Germany; coined "ecology," "phylogeny," "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" (biogenetic law, now discredited). His famously falsified embryo drawings ≠ Darwin.

🔗 Cross-discipline big picture Darwin's work is the pivot of modern biology but threads into multiple quizbowl categories: geology (uniformitarianism from Lyell; coral reef subsidence theory), mathematics/statistics (the Modern Synthesis merged natural selection with Mendelian genetics via population genetics — Fisher's Genetical Theory of Natural Selection, 1930), philosophy of science (Karl Popper initially called natural selection unfalsifiable; later retracted), economics (Malthus was the direct trigger — making the answer chain Malthus → Darwin → Wallace a recurring cross-category link), and literature (Alfred Lord Tennyson's "Nature, red in tooth and claw" in In Memoriam, 1850, anticipated the evolutionary mood nine years before Origin).
