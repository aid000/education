Quick Overview

Core modelFluid Mosaic Model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972)
Membrane compositionPhospholipid bilayer + embedded proteins + cholesterol + carbohydrates
Passive transportMoves solutes down concentration gradient; no ATP
Active transportMoves solutes against gradient; requires ATP
OsmosisDiffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane
TonicityRelative solute concentration: hypertonic, hypotonic, isotonic

Governing formula — osmotic pressure

Van't Hoff equation
π = iMRT
π = osmotic pressure · i = van't Hoff factor (number of ions) · M = molar concentration · R = gas constant · T = temperature (K)
Fick's first law (simple diffusion flux)
J = −D (dC/dx)
J = flux · D = diffusion coefficient · dC/dx = concentration gradient across distance x

Defining characteristics of the membrane

  • Amphipathic phospholipids: hydrophilic phosphate heads face water; hydrophobic fatty-acid tails face inward.
  • Selectively permeable: small nonpolar molecules (O₂, CO₂, steroids) cross freely; ions and large polar molecules require proteins.
  • Fluid: lipids and proteins move laterally within the bilayer (lateral diffusion fast; flip-flop very rare).
  • Cholesterol buffers fluidity — keeps membrane flexible at low temp, restricts excess disorder at high temp.
  • Glycocalyx = glycoproteins + glycolipids on outer leaflet; cell recognition, adhesion, immune function.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "Proposed by S. J. Singer and Garth Nicolson" → Fluid Mosaic Model.
  • "Movement of water from low to high solute" or "through aquaporins" → osmosis.
  • "Protein that uses ATP to pump Na⁺ out and K⁺ in" → Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (sodium-potassium pump).

High-Yield Concepts (Very Common)

The concepts most likely to anchor a tossup. power = early/hard buzz clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

Fluid Mosaic Model
Singer & Nicolson, 1972 · replaces Davson-Danielli "sandwich" model
powerS. Jonathan Singer and Garth Nicolson proposed it in a 1972 Science paper; earlier Davson-Danielli model had proteins as a static coat outside a lipid bilayer.
recog"Mosaic" = proteins dispersed throughout; "fluid" = lateral mobility of both lipids and proteins.
recogMembrane proteins classified as integral (transmembrane) or peripheral; integral proteins span the full bilayer.
Phospholipid Bilayer
Basic structural unit of all cell membranes
powerEach phospholipid = glycerol backbone + two fatty-acid tails + phosphate group + a head group (e.g., choline in phosphatidylcholine). Saturated tails = straighter, more packed; unsaturated tails = kinked, more fluid.
recogSelf-assembles into bilayer in water due to hydrophobic effect; tails sequestered away from water.
recogCholesterol intercalates between phospholipids; critical for homeoviscous adaptation (membrane fluidity regulation).
Osmosis
Passive diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane
powerWater moves from high water potential (low solute) to low water potential (high solute); osmotic pressure is the pressure needed to stop net water flow — described by the van't Hoff equation π = iMRT.
recogIn hypertonic solution, cell loses water (animal: crenation; plant: plasmolysis). In hypotonic solution, water enters (animal: lysis; plant: turgid). Isotonic = no net movement.
recogWater channels = aquaporins; discovered by Peter Agre (2003 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, shared with Roderick MacKinnon).
Facilitated Diffusion
Passive transport via channel or carrier proteins; no ATP
powerChannel proteins form water-filled pores (ion channels are gated — voltage-gated, ligand-gated, or mechanically gated); carrier proteins (uniporters) change conformation to shuttle solutes — GLUT1 glucose transporter is canonical example.
recogStill moves down concentration gradient; faster than simple diffusion for polar/large molecules.
recogExhibits saturation kinetics (unlike simple diffusion) — rate plateaus when all carriers are occupied.
Active Transport
Moves solutes against gradient; requires ATP or electrochemical gradient
powerNa⁺/K⁺-ATPase (sodium-potassium pump): per cycle, pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in; hydrolyzes one ATP; maintains membrane potential (~−70 mV in neurons); inhibited by ouabain and cardiac glycosides (digoxin, digitalis).
recogSecondary active transport: uses Na⁺ gradient (built by Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase) to drive cotransport — e.g., SGLT symporter brings glucose + Na⁺ into intestinal cells together.
recogAntiporters move two solutes in opposite directions; symporters move two in the same direction — both are cotransporters.
Endocytosis & Exocytosis (Bulk Transport)
Vesicle-based; only in eukaryotes; requires energy
powerReceptor-mediated endocytosis: ligand binds surface receptor → clathrin-coated pit forms → clathrin-coated vesicle pinches off; canonical example is LDL (low-density lipoprotein) uptake; defect → familial hypercholesterolemia (described by Brown & Goldstein, 1985 Nobel).
recogPhagocytosis ("cell eating") = large particles, pseudopodia; pinocytosis ("cell drinking") = small droplets; both are forms of endocytosis.
recogExocytosis = vesicle fuses with plasma membrane to release contents; used by neurons to release neurotransmitters.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key people / Nobel prizes

  • S. Jonathan Singer & Garth Nicolson — Fluid Mosaic Model (1972)
  • Peter Agre — aquaporins; 2003 Nobel Chemistry (shared with MacKinnon)
  • Roderick MacKinnon — ion channel structure; 2003 Nobel Chemistry
  • Michael Brown & Joseph Goldstein — LDL receptor / endocytosis; 1985 Nobel Physiology
  • Jacobus van 't Hoff — osmotic pressure equation; first Nobel Chemistry (1901)

Key terms & vocabulary

  • Selectively permeable — allows only certain molecules across
  • Amphipathic — molecule with both hydrophilic and hydrophobic parts
  • Homeoviscous adaptation — adjusting membrane fluidity via cholesterol / fatty acid saturation
  • Uniporter / symporter / antiporter — carrier subtypes by direction of solute movement
  • Electrogenic pump — pump that contributes to membrane potential (e.g., Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase is electrogenic)
  • Osmolarity vs. tonicity — osmolarity is total solute concentration; tonicity accounts for membrane permeability

Numbers & specifics

  • Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase stoichiometry: 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, 1 ATP consumed per cycle
  • Resting membrane potential: approximately −70 mV in typical neurons
  • Membrane thickness: ~7–10 nm
  • Osmotic pressure of seawater ≈ 27 atm (relevant in desalination questions)
  • Crenation — animal cell shrinkage in hypertonic solution
  • Cytolysis / hemolysis — cell bursting in hypotonic solution

Inhibitors & pharmacology

  • Ouabain / digoxin / digitalis — Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibitors; digitalis from foxglove used for heart failure
  • Cytochalasin B — blocks GLUT transporters; also disrupts actin
  • Phloridzin — SGLT1/2 inhibitor (precursor to gliflozin drugs)
  • Valinomycin — K⁺ ionophore; carries K⁺ across membrane, dissipates gradient
  • Nigericin — H⁺/K⁺ antiporter ionophore; uncouples mitochondria

Comparison Traps

Don't confuseHow to tell them apart
Osmosis vs. diffusionOsmosis = only water; diffusion = any substance. Osmosis specifically requires a semi-permeable membrane. Both are passive.
Facilitated diffusion vs. active transportBoth use membrane proteins. Facilitated diffusion = down gradient, no ATP. Active transport = against gradient, requires ATP (or secondary gradient).
Channel protein vs. carrier proteinChannel = pore, no conformational change; selective, gated. Carrier = binds solute, changes shape; shows saturation kinetics.
Primary vs. secondary active transportPrimary = directly hydrolyzes ATP (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, H⁺-ATPase). Secondary = uses ion gradient built by primary pump (SGLT uses Na⁺ gradient).
Hypertonic vs. hypotonic solution (cell response)Hypertonic = solution has more solute → cell loses water (shrinks/crenates/plasmolyzes). Hypotonic = solution has less solute → cell gains water (swells/lyses/becomes turgid).
Turgor pressure vs. osmotic pressureOsmotic pressure = tendency of water to move in (solution property). Turgor pressure = mechanical pressure of cytoplasm against wall (physical force preventing further entry); at equilibrium, they are equal.
Endocytosis vs. exocytosisEndocytosis = membrane engulfs external material inward. Exocytosis = vesicle fuses and releases outward. Both require energy and only occur in eukaryotes.
Fluid Mosaic (Singer-Nicolson) vs. Davson-Danielli modelDavson-Danielli = proteins form a static sandwich coat; lipids are sandwiched. Fluid Mosaic = proteins float within a lipid bilayer; membrane is dynamic. The exam question will say "superseded" or "replaced."
Aquaporins (Agre) vs. ion channels (MacKinnon)Aquaporins = water only, no ions (charge exclusion). Ion channels = selective for specific ions; MacKinnon solved K⁺ channel structure (KcsA). Both are 2003 Nobel.
Symporter vs. antiporterSymporter = both solutes move the same direction (Na⁺ + glucose both into cell). Antiporter = solutes move opposite directions (Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger: Na⁺ in, Ca²⁺ out).
