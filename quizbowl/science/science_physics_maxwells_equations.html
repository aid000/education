Quick Overview

AuthorJames Clerk Maxwell (Scottish, 1831–1879)
PublishedA Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism, 1873
Count4 equations (in modern compact form)
What they unifyElectricity + magnetism + light (EM waves)
Key predictionEM waves travel at speed c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀)
Predecessor lawsGauss, Faraday, Ampère (with Maxwell's correction)

What they do

  • Completely describe how electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields are generated and altered by charges, currents, and each other.
  • Predicted that oscillating E and B fields propagate as waves at speed c ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s — identifying light as an electromagnetic wave.
  • Motivated Einstein's special relativity: the equations imply c is constant in all inertial frames.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "Four equations that unify electricity and magnetism" — answer is Maxwell's equations.
  • "Displacement current" — Maxwell's original addition to Ampère's law; most famous single contribution.
  • "c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀)" — the formula that links EM constants to the speed of light.

High-Yield — The Four Equations

Each equation has a name, an integral form, a differential form, and a physical meaning. power = hard early clue. recog = giveaway.

1. Gauss's Law (for Electric Fields)
∮ E · dA = Q_enc / ε₀
∇ · E = ρ / ε₀
recogElectric flux through any closed surface equals enclosed charge divided by ε₀ (permittivity of free space).
recogDifferential form: divergence of E equals charge density over ε₀.
powerGeneralizes Coulomb's law to arbitrary charge distributions; the foundation for calculating E from symmetric geometries (sphere, cylinder, plane). Named after Carl Friedrich Gauss.
2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism (No Magnetic Monopoles)
∮ B · dA = 0
∇ · B = 0
recogMagnetic flux through any closed surface is always zero — field lines have no sources or sinks.
recogAsserts there are no magnetic monopoles — every magnet has both a north and south pole.
powerContrast with Gauss's law for E: the zero on the right-hand side is the entire content of this equation. Search for magnetic monopoles (predicted by Dirac) continues and would require modifying this equation.
3. Faraday's Law of Induction
∮ E · dl = -dΦ_B/dt
∇ × E = -∂B/∂t
recogA changing magnetic flux induces an EMF (and thus a circulating electric field).
recogThe minus sign encodes Lenz's law: the induced EMF opposes the change that caused it.
powerNamed for Michael Faraday; independently formulated by Joseph Henry. The differential form uses the curl operator. Basis for transformers, generators, and electric motors.
4. Ampère's Law with Maxwell's Correction
∮ B · dl = μ₀(I + ε₀ dΦ_E/dt)
∇ × B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀ ∂E/∂t
recogA magnetic field circulates around both real current (I) and changing electric flux (Maxwell's addition).
recogMaxwell's addition is the displacement current term: ε₀ dΦ_E/dt — makes the equations consistent inside a charging capacitor.
powerThe original Ampère's law (without displacement current) violated charge conservation. Maxwell added ε₀ ∂E/∂t in 1861; this addition directly predicted electromagnetic waves and their speed c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀).
Key Consequence: Electromagnetic Waves
c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀) ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s
recogCombining equations 3 and 4 gives a wave equation for E and B propagating at c.
recogMaxwell noted c matched the measured speed of light — concluding light is an electromagnetic wave.
powerε₀ (electric permittivity) and μ₀ (magnetic permeability) were already measured by Weber and Kohlrausch (1856); Maxwell used their ratio to derive c purely from electromagnetism.

Deeper Cuts

Related results and formulations that appear as hard early clues. One-line ID each.

Lorentz Force Law
Companion equation: F = q(E + v × B); gives force on a charge moving through E and B fields.
Covariant (4-tensor) Form
All four equations compressed into two tensor equations: ∂_μ F^μν = μ₀J^ν and ∂_μ *F^μν = 0; manifestly Lorentz-covariant.
Gaussian Units Form
Older CGS system; ε₀ and μ₀ disappear, replaced by factors of c; common in physics literature pre-1970.
Helmholtz Decomposition
Any vector field splits into curl-free (electric-like) + divergence-free (magnetic-like) parts; underpins why two equations each for E and B suffice.
Electromagnetic Potentials (A, φ)
B = ∇ × A and E = -∇φ − ∂A/∂t; potentials satisfy simpler wave equations in Lorenz gauge.
Lorenz Gauge Condition
∇ · A + (1/c²) ∂φ/∂t = 0; symmetrizes the wave equations for A and φ. (Ludvig Lorenz, not Lorentz.)
Poynting Vector
S = (1/μ₀)(E × B); energy flux density of an EM field; derived from Maxwell's equations by Poynting in 1884.
Maxwell Stress Tensor
Encodes radiation pressure and electromagnetic momentum flux; used in optics and plasma physics.
Hertz's Experimental Confirmation
Heinrich Hertz, 1887–1888: first to produce and detect radio waves, confirming Maxwell's prediction experimentally.
Maxwell's Original 20 Equations
Maxwell's 1865 paper used 20 scalar equations; Heaviside (with Gibbs's vector calculus) reformulated them into the familiar four in the 1880s.
Macroscopic (Matter) Form
In matter: E → D = ε₀E + P and B → H = B/μ₀ − M; polarization P and magnetization M added to account for dielectric/magnetic media.
Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)
Maxwell's equations are the classical limit of QED; photons are quanta of the EM field (Feynman, Schwinger, Tomonaga — Nobel 1965).

Clue Bank

Key terms / concepts

  • Displacement current — Maxwell's addition; the ε₀ ∂E/∂t term
  • Divergence (∇·) — appears in Gauss's laws (equations 1 and 2)
  • Curl (∇×) — appears in Faraday's and Ampère-Maxwell (equations 3 and 4)
  • Magnetic monopoles — forbidden by eq. 2 (∇·B = 0)
  • Lenz's law — the minus sign in Faraday's law
  • Permittivity of free space — ε₀ ≈ 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m
  • Permeability of free space — μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m

Named contributors

  • James Clerk Maxwell — synthesized and completed the theory
  • Michael Faraday — experimental discovery of induction
  • André-Marie Ampère — relationship between current and B field
  • Carl Friedrich Gauss — flux laws for E and B
  • Oliver Heaviside — reformulated 20 equations → 4 vector form
  • Heinrich Hertz — experimentally confirmed EM waves (1887)
  • Ludvig Lorenz — Lorenz gauge (not the physicist H.A. Lorentz)

Famous formulas / numbers

  • c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀) — speed of light from EM constants
  • c ≈ 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s — numerical value
  • ∇ · B = 0 — most compact statement of no-monopoles
  • ∇ × E = −∂B/∂t — Faraday in differential form
  • S = (E × B)/μ₀ — Poynting vector
  • F = q(E + v × B) — Lorentz force

Historical milestones

  • 1820 — Oersted: current deflects compass (current → B)
  • 1831 — Faraday discovers electromagnetic induction
  • 1861 — Maxwell adds displacement current, derives wave equation
  • 1865 — Maxwell's paper "A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field"
  • 1873 — Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism
  • 1887–88 — Hertz detects radio waves experimentally
  • 1905 — Einstein's SR motivated by Maxwell's constant c

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Faraday's Law (alone)Faraday's law is one of the four Maxwell equations (eq. 3). Maxwell = the whole system. A tossup about "a changing B field inducing EMF" can answer Faraday's law or Maxwell's equations — read for whether the question asks for the broader theory or the specific law.
Ampère's Law (original)The original Ampère's law lacks the displacement current term. Maxwell's version adds ε₀ ∂E/∂t. Clue "displacement current" → Maxwell; "current through a wire" alone → could be just Ampère's.
Newton's LawsNewton describes mechanics; Maxwell describes fields. The Lorentz force law (F = qE + qv×B) bridges them — it is a separate equation, not one of the four Maxwell equations.
Coulomb's LawCoulomb's law is the static result derivable from Gauss's law for E. Maxwell's equations are the full dynamic generalization. Clue "force between two charges" → Coulomb; "closed-surface integral of E field" → Gauss's law / Maxwell.
Schrödinger EquationSchrödinger governs quantum mechanical wave functions; Maxwell governs classical EM fields. QED (Feynman etc.) is the quantum version of Maxwell's theory.
Einstein's Field EquationsEinstein's equations govern spacetime curvature (gravity, 10 independent equations). Maxwell's govern EM fields (4 equations). Both are relativistic field theories — do not conflate "field equations" phrasing.
Lorenz vs. LorentzLorenz gauge is named for Ludvig Lorenz (Danish physicist, 1829–1891). Lorentz transformations are named for Hendrik Antoon Lorentz (Dutch, 1853–1928). Different people, similar names — a known quizbowl trap.

Big Picture — Why Maxwell's Equations Matter Beyond Physics Maxwell's equations are one of the most cross-disciplinary answers in science quizbowl:
  • Physics: they motivated Einstein's special relativity (constant c in all frames) and inspired gauge theory (the template for the Standard Model).
  • Math: introduced divergence and curl as essential tools; the wave-equation derivation is a model of applied vector calculus.
  • Technology: basis for radio, radar, microwave ovens, fiber optics, MRI, and all modern wireless communication.
  • History of science: the synthesis of Gauss + Faraday + Ampère is the 19th century's greatest theoretical unification, matched only by Newton's mechanics and Darwin's evolution.
