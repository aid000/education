Quick Overview

Electron configuration describes how electrons are distributed among the energy levels (shells), subshells, and orbitals of an atom. It underpins the periodic table's block structure, chemical bonding, and spectroscopy.

Core principleAufbau principle — fill lowest-energy orbitals first
Subshell typess (2e), p (6e), d (10e), f (14e)
Governing equationSchrödinger wave equation; ψ = orbital wavefunction
Quantum numbersn, ℓ, mℓ, ms uniquely specify each electron
Max electrons / shell2n² electrons per principal quantum number n
Periodic table blockss-block (1–2), p-block (13–18), d-block (3–12), f-block (lanthanides/actinides)
Orbital notation shorthand
n ℓ → e.g., 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (Cu, Z=29)
n = principal quantum number (shell); ℓ = angular momentum (0=s, 1=p, 2=d, 3=f); superscript = electron count

The three filling rules — all testable by name

  • Aufbau principle: electrons fill lowest available energy orbitals first (1s before 2s, etc.)
  • Pauli exclusion principle: no two electrons in an atom may have identical sets of all four quantum numbers; each orbital holds max 2 electrons with opposite spins.
  • Hund's rule: within a subshell, electrons occupy separate orbitals singly before pairing; all unpaired electrons have the same (parallel) spin — maximizes total spin multiplicity.

Classic quizbowl giveaway angles

  • Questions name Niels Bohr, Erwin Schrödinger, Werner Heisenberg, or Friedrich Hund in the same tossup.
  • Any question mentioning "4s before 3d" anomalies (Cu, Cr) is about this topic.
  • The Pauli exclusion principle itself is a common answer and an early clue here.

High-Yield Concepts (Very Common)

Principal statements, rules, and applications most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard early clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

Pauli Exclusion Principle
Wolfgang Pauli, 1925 · Nobel Prize Physics 1945
recogNo two electrons in an atom may share all four quantum numbers (n, ℓ, mℓ, ms).
recogDirect consequence: each orbital holds exactly two electrons with opposite spins (↑↓).
powerPauli derived this from studying anomalous Zeeman splitting in atomic spectra before a complete quantum mechanical framework existed; he called it the "exclusion rule" (Ausschließungsprinzip). Extended to fermions generally — all particles with half-integer spin obey it.
Hund's Rule (Rule of Maximum Multiplicity)
Friedrich Hund, 1927
recogElectrons fill orbitals of a subshell one at a time (singly) before pairing; all singly-occupied orbitals have parallel spins.
recogExample: nitrogen 1s² 2s² 2p³ — all three 2p electrons are unpaired, giving 3 half-filled orbitals.
powerHund also formulated rules ordering spectroscopic terms by spin multiplicity and orbital angular momentum (Hund's three rules of atomic spectra, distinct from the filling rule); the ground state has maximum spin multiplicity (2S+1), then maximum L.
Quantum Numbers (n, ℓ, mℓ, ms)
Bohr / Schrödinger / Goudsmit–Uhlenbeck framework
recogn (principal) = shell; ℓ (azimuthal/angular) = subshell shape (0–n−1); mℓ (magnetic) = orbital orientation (−ℓ to +ℓ); ms (spin) = +½ or −½.
recogNumber of orbitals in subshell = 2ℓ + 1; s has 1, p has 3, d has 5, f has 7 orbitals.
powerSpin quantum number proposed by Samuel Goudsmit and George Uhlenbeck in 1925 to explain the Stern–Gerlach experiment's two silver-atom beams; spin had no classical analogue — an intrinsic angular momentum of ℏ/2.
Orbital Shapes: s, p, d, f
ψ² (probability density) = orbital shape
recogs orbital — spherically symmetric; one per shell; grows in size with n.
recogp orbitals — dumbbell-shaped; three per subshell along x, y, z axes; first appear at n=2.
recogd orbitals — five per subshell; four cloverleaf shapes (e.g. dxy, dxz) plus one dz² (dumbbell with torus); first appear at n=3.
powerf orbitals (14 electrons, 7 orbitals) — highly complex multi-lobed shapes; first appear at n=4; responsible for lanthanide contraction (poor shielding of f electrons causes 4f-filled elements to be smaller than expected, making Hf nearly the same size as Zr).
Electron Configuration Anomalies: Cu and Cr
The highest-frequency "exception" clues
recogExpected: [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s² for Cr; actual: [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ — half-filled d subshell extra stability.
recogExpected: [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s² for Cu; actual: [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ — fully-filled d subshell extra stability.
powerMechanistically: the small energy gap between 3d and 4s at these Z values means one electron transfers to give a lower-energy exchange-interaction-stabilized configuration; identical reasoning applies to Mo (Z=42) and Ag (Z=47) one period down.
Aufbau Order & Madelung's Rule (n + ℓ rule)
Erwin Madelung / Charles Janet, ~1920–1930
recogFill orbitals in order of increasing (n + ℓ); for equal sums, fill in order of increasing n.
recogPredicts 4s fills before 3d (4+0=4 < 3+2=5), and 4f before 5d (4+3=7 < 5+2=7 → tie → lower n first for f).
powerMadelung published this empirical rule in Die Mathematischen Hilfsmittel des Physikers (1936); it predicts the correct filling order in ~95% of elements but has no simple first-principles quantum mechanical derivation — it remains empirical.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Related results and terms that appear as early/hard clues in tossups about electron configuration or adjacent topics.

Schrödinger Equation (time-independent)
Ĥψ = Eψ; orbital wavefunctions ψnlm are solutions; |ψ|² = electron probability density.
Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2; explains why electrons have probability clouds, not fixed orbits; invalidates Bohr's planetary model.
Bohr Model (1913)
Quantized circular orbits; En = −13.6/n² eV for hydrogen; predicts spectral lines but fails for multi-electron atoms.
Stern–Gerlach Experiment (1922)
Silver atoms through inhomogeneous magnetic field split into two beams — first evidence of electron spin (ms = ±½).
Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff)
Net positive charge felt by an electron; Zeff = Z − σ (σ = shielding constant); rises across a period, driving ionization energy trend.
Shielding / Penetration
Inner electrons reduce Zeff for outer electrons; s > p > d > f penetration — s electrons are best shielded from core, least shielded from nucleus.
Slater's Rules
Empirical rules by John C. Slater (1930) to calculate σ and Zeff from electron configuration groups.
Lanthanide Contraction
Poor shielding by 4f electrons → elements after La are smaller than expected; Hf ≈ same radius as Zr; drives 5d metal density.
Aufbau Principle — name etymology
German: Aufbau = "building up"; Niels Bohr coined the term for his building-up procedure to construct periodic table.
Nodal Surfaces
Surfaces where ψ = 0; s orbitals have (n−1) radial nodes; p orbitals add 1 angular node (a plane); nodes = n − ℓ − 1 (radial) + ℓ (angular).
Magnetic Quantum Number & Zeeman Effect
External magnetic field splits mℓ states in energy (normal Zeeman effect); anomalous Zeeman requires spin — motivated Pauli's exclusion principle.
Valence Electron Configuration
Only the outermost s/p (and d for transition metals) electrons; determines chemical behavior, oxidation states, bonding.
Isoelectronic Species
Same electron configuration: Na⁺, Ne, F⁻, O²⁻, Mg²⁺ all have 10 electrons; frequently tested as a pattern recognition clue.
Periodic Table Block Structure
s-block (groups 1–2), p-block (groups 13–18), d-block (groups 3–12), f-block (lanthanides, actinides) — maps directly to subshell being filled.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Named rules (answer = rule name)

  • Aufbau principle — fill lowest energy first
  • Hund's rule — maximize spin multiplicity; degenerate orbitals fill singly before pairing
  • Pauli exclusion principle — no two identical quantum states
  • Madelung's rule (n + ℓ rule) — predicts filling order
  • Slater's rules — compute shielding constants empirically

Key physicists linked here

  • Wolfgang Pauli — exclusion principle; anomalous Zeeman
  • Friedrich Hund — Hund's rule; molecular orbital theory (with Mulliken)
  • Niels Bohr — Aufbau; quantized orbits (1913)
  • Erwin Schrödinger — wave equation; orbitals as wavefunctions (1926)
  • Werner Heisenberg — uncertainty principle; matrix mechanics
  • Samuel Goudsmit & George Uhlenbeck — electron spin (1925)

Key numbers / formulas

  • Max e per shell: 2n²
  • Orbitals per subshell: 2ℓ + 1
  • Subshell capacities: s=2, p=6, d=10, f=14
  • Filling order: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s 4f 5d 6p …
  • Hydrogen ground state: 1s¹
  • Helium (first noble gas): 1s²
  • Carbon (Hund's rule demo): 1s² 2s² 2p² — two unpaired p electrons

Experiments & instruments

  • Stern–Gerlach experiment (1922) — silver atoms, inhomogeneous B field, two beams → spin
  • Zeeman effect — spectral line splitting in magnetic field; anomalous form motivated quantum spin
  • Franck–Hertz experiment (1914) — confirmed discrete energy levels by electron collisions with mercury vapor
  • X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) — directly probes electron binding energies per orbital

Comparison Traps

Concept / RuleHow to tell them apart
Pauli exclusion vs. Hund's rulePauli: limits 2 electrons per orbital (opposite spins). Hund: governs how electrons spread among degenerate orbitals within a subshell (fill singly first). Different question, different rule.
Aufbau vs. Madelung's ruleAufbau: broad principle (fill lowest energy first). Madelung (n+ℓ rule): the specific algorithm predicting which orbital is lower energy. Madelung operationalizes Aufbau.
Orbital vs. orbitOrbit = classical, defined path (Bohr model, incorrect). Orbital = quantum mechanical probability cloud (wavefunction ψ); has shape and no defined trajectory.
Shell vs. subshell vs. orbitalShell = all orbitals with same n. Subshell = all orbitals with same n and ℓ (e.g., 3d). Orbital = specific (n, ℓ, mℓ) state holding ≤2 electrons.
Principal quantum number (n) vs. azimuthal (ℓ)n = which shell (energy level, distance). ℓ = shape of orbital within that shell (0=s sphere, 1=p dumbbell, 2=d cloverleaf). Often confused in "what determines the shape" questions.
Heisenberg uncertainty vs. Pauli exclusionUncertainty: cannot simultaneously know exact position + momentum of a particle. Pauli exclusion: two fermions cannot occupy the same quantum state. Both prevent "planetary orbit" electrons, but different reasons.
Normal vs. anomalous Zeeman effectNormal Zeeman: spectral line splits into 3 components; spin-0 states; explainable classically. Anomalous Zeeman: more than 3 components; requires electron spin; what Pauli studied to derive exclusion.
Chromium vs. Copper anomalyCr: gives one 4s electron to get half-filled 3d⁵ (stable). Cu: gives one 4s electron to get fully-filled 3d¹⁰ (stable). Both break naive Aufbau but for the same reason: exchange-energy stabilization of a symmetric subshell.

⚡ Physics / math through-line Electron configuration is quantum mechanics made visible in the periodic table: the Schrödinger equation's solutions (ψnlm) are the orbitals; the Pauli exclusion principle (a consequence of electrons being fermions — spin-½ particles obeying Fermi–Dirac statistics) fills them; and the resulting block structure explains every periodic trend (atomic radius, ionization energy, electronegativity, reactivity). The same exclusion principle underlies the stability of white dwarf stars (electron degeneracy pressure), making it one of the few chemical-physics principles that also appears in astrophysics tossups.
