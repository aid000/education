Quick Overview

Membrane-bound (and non-membrane-bound) structures inside eukaryotic cells, each performing a specialized function. The five organelles below are the highest-frequency quizbowl answers in cell biology.

Cell typeEukaryotic cells only (membrane-bound organelles absent in prokaryotes)
Endosymbiotic organellesMitochondria and chloroplasts — own DNA, double membrane, divide by binary fission
Non-membrane-boundRibosomes — found in all cells including prokaryotes
Endomembrane systemER + Golgi apparatus + vesicles work as a connected secretory pathway
Central formula — Mitochondria (aerobic respiration) C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + ~36–38 ATP
Central formula — Chloroplast (photosynthesis, net) 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6 O2

Endosymbiotic Theory (Lynn Margulis, 1967)

  • Mitochondria and chloroplasts descended from free-living alpha-proteobacteria and cyanobacteria, respectively.
  • Evidence: own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes, double membrane, divide independently.
  • Proposed by Lynn Margulis — a frequent quizbowl name associated with this theory.

High-Yield Organelles (Very Common)

The five organelles most likely to be tossup answers. power = obscure/early clue. recog = recognition / giveaway clue.

Mitochondrion
Function: ATP production via aerobic respiration · Present in: nearly all eukaryotes
powerThe F0F1-ATP synthase (complex V) uses a proton gradient across the inner membrane to phosphorylate ADP — described by Peter Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory (Nobel 1978).
recogSite of the Krebs cycle (TCA cycle) in the matrix; electron transport chain on the cristae (inner membrane folds).
recog"Powerhouse of the cell" — the single most common giveaway phrase in cell biology tossups.
recogDouble membrane: smooth outer + cristae-folded inner; matrix contains mitochondrial DNA (circular).
  • Produces ~30–32 ATP per glucose (revised modern count; older textbooks say 36–38).
  • Also regulates apoptosis — releases cytochrome c to trigger caspase cascade.
  • Inherited maternally in most animals (no paternal mitochondria in offspring).
Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)
Two types: Rough ER (RER) and Smooth ER (SER) · Connected to nuclear envelope
powerRough ER is studded with ribosomes translating proteins destined for secretion or membranes; signal recognition particle (SRP) directs ribosomes here.
recogSmooth ER lacks ribosomes; synthesizes lipids, steroids, and detoxifies drugs/alcohol (abundant in liver cells).
recogRER produces proteins that enter the secretory pathway: ER → Golgi → vesicle → destination.
  • Sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) is a specialized smooth ER in muscle cells storing Ca2+.
  • "Rough" = ribosome-studded; "Smooth" = no ribosomes.
  • RER also performs N-linked glycosylation of newly synthesized proteins.
Golgi Apparatus
Discovered by: Camillo Golgi, 1898 · Nobel Prize 1906 · "Post office of the cell"
powerOrganized as stacked flattened cisternae; proteins move from the cis face (receiving, near ER) to the trans face (shipping). KDEL sequence retains ER-resident proteins (retrieval signal).
recogModifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids into vesicles for delivery to lysosomes, plasma membrane, or secretion.
recogAdds O-linked carbohydrates (O-glycosylation) and phosphorylates mannose residues (mannose-6-phosphate) to tag lysosomal enzymes.
  • Discovered via silver staining — Golgi invented the "black reaction" (reazione nera).
  • Produces lysosomes by budding vesicles containing hydrolytic enzymes.
  • "Post office" or "shipping and receiving center" of the cell — common tossup metaphors.
Ribosome
Non-membrane-bound · Present in: ALL cells (prokaryotes + eukaryotes) · Site of translation
powerEukaryotic: 80S (60S large + 40S small subunits); Prokaryotic: 70S (50S + 30S). Many antibiotics target the 70S ribosome (e.g., streptomycin targets 30S; erythromycin targets 50S).
recogComposed of rRNA and proteins; translates mRNA codons into amino acid chains via A, P, E sites.
recogStructure solved by Ada Yonath, Venkatraman Ramakrishnan, Thomas Steitz — Nobel Chemistry 2009.
  • Ribosome is fundamentally a ribozyme — the peptidyl transferase activity is in the 23S rRNA (prokaryotes).
  • Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own 70S ribosomes (endosymbiotic evidence).
  • Multiple ribosomes on one mRNA = polyribosome (polysome).
Chloroplast
Function: photosynthesis · Present in: plants and green algae · Descended from cyanobacteria
powerLight reactions occur on thylakoid membranes (stacked grana); produce ATP, NADPH, and O2. Calvin cycle (light-independent/dark reactions) occurs in the stroma; fixes CO2 via RuBisCO.
recogThree membranes: outer, inner, and thylakoid. Grana = stacks of thylakoids; stroma = fluid surrounding grana.
recogContains green pigment chlorophyll (absorbs red and blue light, reflects green). Chlorophyll a is the primary pigment; b, c, d are accessory.
  • RuBisCO (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase) — most abundant enzyme on Earth; fixes CO2 in Calvin cycle.
  • Photosystem II (PSII) splits water (photolysis); Photosystem I (PSI) reduces NADP+ to NADPH.
  • Stroma contains circular DNA and 70S ribosomes — endosymbiotic evidence.
  • C4 plants (corn, sugarcane) segregate Calvin cycle to bundle-sheath cells to minimize photorespiration.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Related organelles and structures — appear as hard early clues or as the answer itself in specialist tossups.

Lysosome
Acidic (pH ~4.5) vesicle; digests waste via hydrolytic enzymes. Budded from Golgi. Defects cause lysosomal storage diseases (Tay-Sachs, Gaucher). Animal cells only.
Vacuole (Central Vacuole)
Large in plant cells; maintains turgor pressure via osmosis. Tonoplast membrane. Storage of pigments, toxins, and waste. Animal cells have smaller contractile vacuoles.
Nucleus
Houses DNA; bounded by nuclear envelope (double membrane with nuclear pores). Contains nucleolus — site of rRNA synthesis. "Control center" of the cell.
Nucleolus
Dense region within the nucleus; site of rRNA transcription and ribosome subunit assembly. Not membrane-bound; disappears during cell division.
Peroxisome
Contains catalase; breaks down H2O2 and fatty acids (beta-oxidation). Abundant in liver/kidney. Zellweger syndrome caused by peroxisome biogenesis defects.
Cytoskeleton
Three types: microtubules (tubulin, 25 nm), microfilaments (actin, 7 nm), intermediate filaments (8–12 nm). Not membrane-bound.
Centriole / Centrosome
Organizes mitotic spindle; found in animal cells. Two centrioles form a centrosome (MTOC). Made of 9 triplets of microtubules (9+0 arrangement).
Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane)
Fluid mosaic model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972) — phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins. Cholesterol maintains fluidity.
Thylakoid (standalone)
Membrane-bound disk inside chloroplast; embedded with Photosystem I and II, ATP synthase, electron carriers. Lumen is site of water splitting (PSII).
Cristae (standalone)
Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane; increase surface area for ATP synthase and electron transport chain complexes I–IV.
Signal Recognition Particle (SRP)
Recognizes signal peptide on nascent proteins; docks ribosome to rough ER. Hard early clue for RER tossups.
SNARE Proteins
Mediate vesicle fusion with target membranes; v-SNAREs (vesicle) pair with t-SNAREs (target). Botulinum toxin cleaves SNAREs to block neurotransmitter release.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Named structures / parts

  • Cristae — inner mitochondrial membrane folds
  • Matrix — mitochondrial interior (Krebs cycle here)
  • Stroma — chloroplast interior (Calvin cycle here)
  • Grana / Thylakoids — stacked disks in chloroplast
  • Cis / trans face — Golgi receiving / shipping ends
  • Cisternae — Golgi flattened sacs
  • A, P, E sites — ribosome tRNA binding sites

Key proteins / enzymes

  • ATP synthase (F0F1) — makes ATP in mitochondria and chloroplasts
  • RuBisCO — CO2 fixation in Calvin cycle; most abundant enzyme on Earth
  • Cytochrome c — electron carrier; apoptosis signal
  • Catalase — in peroxisomes; breaks down H2O2
  • rRNA — catalytic component of ribosome (ribozyme)

Discoverers / Nobel links

  • Camillo Golgi — Golgi apparatus; Nobel 1906 (shared with Cajal)
  • Lynn Margulis — endosymbiotic theory (1967)
  • Peter Mitchell — chemiosmosis; Nobel Chemistry 1978
  • Ada Yonath, Ramakrishnan, Steitz — ribosome structure; Nobel Chemistry 2009
  • Singer & Nicolson — fluid mosaic model (1972)

Metaphors & mnemonics

  • "Powerhouse of the cell" → mitochondrion
  • "Post office of the cell" → Golgi apparatus
  • "Blueprint room" → nucleus
  • 70S = prokaryotes (and mitochondria/chloroplasts); 80S = eukaryotes
  • Rough ER = ribosomes; Smooth ER = lipids/detox

Comparison Traps

Trap / ConfusionHow to distinguish
Mitochondria vs. ChloroplastBoth double-membrane endosymbiotic organelles with own DNA. Key split: mitochondria consume O2 / produce ATP from glucose; chloroplasts produce O2 / capture light to make glucose. Mitochondria = all eukaryotes; chloroplasts = plants/algae only.
Rough ER vs. Smooth ERRough ER has ribosomes, makes secretory proteins. Smooth ER has no ribosomes, makes lipids/steroids and detoxifies. "Rough" = protein factory; "smooth" = lipid factory.
Ribosome vs. LysosomeRibosomes = protein synthesis, no membrane, all cells. Lysosomes = digestion of waste, single membrane, acidic, animal cells. Ribosomes are smaller (20–30 nm); lysosomes ~0.1–1.2 µm.
Golgi vs. Rough ERRER is the first stop after translation — initial folding and N-glycosylation. Golgi is the second stop — further modification, sorting, packaging. Vesicles flow ER→Golgi→destination, not backward (normally).
Nucleolus vs. NucleusNucleus = membrane-bound organelle housing all chromosomal DNA. Nucleolus = dense, non-membrane-bound region inside the nucleus; makes rRNA. If the question mentions rRNA synthesis → nucleolus, not nucleus.
Vacuole vs. VesicleVacuoles are large, permanent, storage organelles (central vacuole in plants). Vesicles are small, transient, transport bubbles (e.g., from Golgi). Plants have central vacuoles; animals have lysosomes as functional analog.
Mitochondrion vs. PeroxisomeBoth found in all eukaryotes; both involved in metabolism. Mitochondria = ATP via ETC/Krebs. Peroxisomes = fatty acid beta-oxidation and H2O2 breakdown (catalase). Peroxisomes have no DNA or ribosomes of their own.
70S vs. 80S ribosome70S = prokaryotes, mitochondria, chloroplasts (50S + 30S). 80S = eukaryotic cytoplasm (60S + 40S). Most antibiotics target 70S — safe for host 80S ribosomes (but toxic to mitochondria at high doses).

Cross-discipline big picture Organelle biology bridges multiple quizbowl categories. Chemistry: ATP synthase and the proton-motive force (thermodynamics, electrochemistry). Evolutionary biology: endosymbiotic theory — chloroplasts descend from cyanobacteria (also a tossup answer in their own right), mitochondria from alpha-proteobacteria. Medicine: mitochondrial apoptosis pathway (cytochrome c, caspases) underlies cancer biology; lysosomal storage diseases (Tay-Sachs, Gaucher, Pompe) are inherited metabolism tossup answers. Antibiotic pharmacology: the 70S vs. 80S ribosome distinction directly explains why streptomycin and erythromycin kill bacteria without harming eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes — but can damage mitochondria at toxic doses.
