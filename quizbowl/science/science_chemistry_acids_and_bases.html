pH scaleLogarithmic 0–14; pH 7 = neutral (25 °C)
Key equationpH + pOH = 14; pH = −log[H⁺]
Neutralization productAcid + base → salt + water
Quizbowl frequency⭐⭐⭐ — Highest in AP-level science tossups

The three definitions at a glance

  • Arrhenius: acid produces H⁺ in water; base produces OH⁻ in water. Narrow — applies only in aqueous solution.
  • Brønsted–Lowry: acid is a proton donor; base is a proton acceptor. Wider — works in non-aqueous solvents too.
  • Lewis: acid is an electron-pair acceptor; base is an electron-pair donor. Widest — covers reactions with no protons at all.
Governing formulas pH = −log[H⁺]  |  pOH = −log[OH⁻]  |  pH + pOH = 14 (25 °C)
Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ (25 °C)
Ka · Kb = Kw  (conjugate acid–base pair)

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "Proton donor" or "proton acceptor" → Brønsted–Lowry.
  • "Electron-pair acceptor" → Lewis acid.
  • "Produces H⁺ / OH⁻ in water" → Arrhenius.
  • pH calculation or Ka/Kb → acid–base equilibrium.
  • "Conjugate acid–base pair" — instantly signals Brønsted–Lowry.

High-Yield Concepts (Very Common)

The principal statements and applications most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early clue. recog = recognition / mid-question clue.

Arrhenius Theory
Svante Arrhenius, 1884 — Nobel Prize Chemistry 1903
powerArrhenius's 1884 doctoral thesis proposed ionic dissociation in water; his committee nearly rejected it. He later also explained the greenhouse effect quantitatively.
recogAcid: ionizes in water to give H⁺ (or H₃O⁺). Example: HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻.
recogBase: ionizes in water to give OH⁻. Example: NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻.
Limitation: only applies in aqueous solutions; cannot explain NH₃ as a base (no OH⁻ produced directly).
Brønsted–Lowry Theory
Johannes Brønsted & Thomas Lowry, independently, 1923
powerBrønsted (Danish) and Lowry (British) published simultaneously in 1923 without collaboration — a classic simultaneous-discovery pair.
recogAcid = proton (H⁺) donor. Base = proton acceptor.
recogConjugate pair: when an acid donates H⁺, the residue is its conjugate base; when a base accepts H⁺, the product is its conjugate acid.
Amphoteric / amphiprotic species (e.g., H₂O, HCO₃⁻) act as either acid or base depending on the partner.
Explains NH₃ + H₂O: NH₃ (base) accepts H⁺ from H₂O to give NH₄⁺ (conjugate acid) and OH⁻.
Lewis Theory
Gilbert N. Lewis, 1923 (same year as Brønsted–Lowry)
powerLewis introduced this in the same 1923 paper that contained his valence-bond / electron-dot (Lewis structure) framework — often asked as a "same-year" trap against Brønsted–Lowry.
recogLewis acid = electron-pair acceptor (electrophile). Lewis base = electron-pair donor (nucleophile).
recogPrototypical Lewis acid: BF₃ (empty p-orbital accepts lone pairs). Prototypical Lewis base: NH₃ (lone pair on N).
Covers metal–ligand bonds (coordination chemistry): the metal ion acts as Lewis acid, ligands as Lewis bases. No proton transfer required.
Widest scope — every Brønsted–Lowry acid is a Lewis acid, but not vice versa.
pH Scale & Water Autoionization
Concept introduced by Søren Sørensen, 1909
powerSørensen introduced "pH" notation in 1909 while working on enzyme activity at the Carlsberg Laboratory (the Danish brewery). The "p" stands for potenz (German: power).
recogpH = −log[H⁺]. Acid: pH < 7; neutral: pH = 7; base: pH > 7 (at 25 °C).
recogEach pH unit = 10× change in [H⁺]; pH 1 is 10× more acidic than pH 2.
Water autoionization: 2 H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻; Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C.
Strong acids/bases dissociate completely (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄, NaOH, KOH). Weak acids/bases only partially (acetic acid, NH₃).
Ka, Kb, and Acid–Base Equilibrium
Fundamental to quizbowl AP chemistry tossups
powerThe relationship Ka · Kb = Kw for a conjugate pair is a hard early clue; it means a stronger acid has a weaker conjugate base.
recogKa = acid dissociation constant; larger Ka = stronger acid. pKa = −log Ka; smaller pKa = stronger acid.
Buffer solution: mixture of weak acid + its conjugate base (or weak base + conjugate acid); resists pH change. Henderson–Hasselbalch: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]).
Titration — equivalence point: moles acid = moles base. Indicator changes color near the endpoint.
Neutralization & Salt Hydrolysis
Direct consequence of acid + base reaction
recogStrong acid + strong base → neutral salt + water (e.g., HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O).
recogWeak acid + strong base → basic solution (conjugate base hydrolyzes). Weak base + strong acid → acidic solution.
powerSalt hydrolysis: the anion of a weak acid (e.g., CH₃COO⁻ from sodium acetate) reacts with water to produce OH⁻, making the solution basic — a frequent hard-clue misdirect.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Related results and specialized terms that appear as early / hard tossup clues. One-line ID each.

Hydronium ion (H₃O⁺)Actual form of H⁺ in water; proton bonded to one water molecule.
Amphoteric speciesActs as acid AND base (H₂O, HCO₃⁻, HSO₄⁻, amino acids).
AutoprotolysisSolvent transfers proton to itself; Kw is water's autoprotolysis constant.
Leveling effectAny acid stronger than H₃O⁺ appears equally strong in water; solvent "levels" them.
Hard/Soft Acids & Bases (HSAB)Pearson's 1963 extension of Lewis theory; hard prefers hard, soft prefers soft.
SuperacidsStronger than 100% H₂SO₄; example: fluoroantimonic acid (HSbF₆), strongest known.
ZwitterionMolecule with both + and − charges; amino acids at isoelectric point.
pKa of water15.7 (pure water as acid); 0 if acting as base accepting a proton.
Diprotic / polyprotic acidsH₂SO₄, H₂CO₃, H₃PO₄ — donate multiple protons; each step has its own Ka.
Henderson–Hasselbalch equationpH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA]); used for buffer calculations.
Acid rainSO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃; NOx + H₂O → HNO₃; pH below 5.6.
Litmus indicatorRed in acid, blue in base — classic qualitative test; derived from lichens.
PhenolphthaleinColorless below pH ~8.2; turns pink/magenta in base; common titration indicator.
Ksp (solubility product)Related equilibrium constant; pH affects solubility of ionic compounds.
Carbonate buffer systemH₂CO₃/HCO₃⁻ pair maintains blood pH ~7.4; key in physiology.
Organic acids/basesCarboxylic acids (R-COOH), amines (R-NH₂); pKa tunable by substituents.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key definitions (buzzword → theory)

  • "proton donor" → Brønsted–Lowry acid
  • "proton acceptor" → Brønsted–Lowry base
  • "electron-pair acceptor" → Lewis acid
  • "electron-pair donor" → Lewis base
  • "produces H⁺ in water" → Arrhenius acid
  • "produces OH⁻ in water" → Arrhenius base
  • "conjugate pair" → Brønsted–Lowry framework

Named scientists & years

  • Svante Arrhenius — 1884, Nobel 1903
  • Johannes Brønsted — Danish, 1923
  • Thomas Martin Lowry — British, 1923
  • Gilbert N. Lewis — American, 1923
  • Søren Sørensen — pH notation, 1909, Carlsberg Lab
  • Ralph Pearson — HSAB theory, 1963

Strong acids & bases (memorize)

  • Strong acids: HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄ (1st H), HClO₄
  • Strong bases: Group 1 hydroxides (LiOH, NaOH, KOH, RbOH, CsOH); heavy Group 2 hydroxides (Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂)
  • Weak acid examples: acetic acid (CH₃COOH, pKa 4.76), carbonic acid, ammonium ion
  • Weak base examples: NH₃, pyridine, aniline

Common numeric clues

  • pH 7 = neutral (25 °C)
  • Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C
  • Blood pH ≈ 7.35–7.45
  • Stomach acid pH ≈ 1.5–3.5
  • Vinegar (acetic acid) pH ≈ 2–3
  • Each pH unit = 10× difference in [H⁺]
  • pKa + pKb = 14 (conjugate pair)

Comparison Traps

Don't confuseHow to tell them apart
Arrhenius vs. Brønsted–LowryArrhenius: requires water, defines by H⁺/OH⁻ produced. Brønsted–Lowry: proton transfer, works without water. NH₃ is a base under B–L but not Arrhenius (no OH⁻ from NH₃ alone).
Brønsted–Lowry vs. LewisB–L requires proton transfer. Lewis requires only electron-pair transfer — no proton needed. BF₃ is a Lewis acid but not a B–L acid. Every B–L acid is a Lewis acid; not vice versa.
Lewis acid vs. Lewis baseAcid = accepts electrons (electrophile, electron-deficient). Base = donates electrons (nucleophile, lone pair available). Mnemonic: acid = Acceptor.
Ka vs. KbKa = acid dissociation (acid strength). Kb = base dissociation (base strength). They multiply to Kw: stronger acid ↔ weaker conjugate base.
pH vs. pOHpH = −log[H⁺]; pOH = −log[OH⁻]. They sum to 14 (not multiply). Acidic solution: pH < 7, pOH > 7.
Strong acid vs. concentrated acidStrength = degree of dissociation (Ka). Concentration = amount per volume. Acetic acid can be concentrated yet still weak (low Ka). Nitric acid is strong at any concentration.
Equivalence point vs. endpointEquivalence point: stoichiometric moles acid = moles base (theoretical). Endpoint: where indicator changes color (experimental approximation). They need not coincide.
Brønsted & Lowry (two people)Brønsted = Danish. Lowry = British. Simultaneous 1923 publications — a quizbowl "who published first" trap. Neither is credited as sole discoverer.

Cross-discipline big picture Acid–base chemistry bridges chemistry, biology, and physics: the carbonate buffer (H₂CO₃/HCO₃⁻) maintains blood pH 7.35–7.45 — a physiology tossup answer; acid rain (SO₂, NOx) connects to environmental science; Lewis acid–base theory underpins coordination chemistry and transition-metal catalysis. The Brønsted–Lowry proton-transfer framework directly links to enzyme mechanisms (Asp/His/Glu act as proton shuttles in active sites). pH calculations themselves are a recurring math tossup lead-in ("this logarithmic function of hydrogen-ion concentration").
