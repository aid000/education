Quick Overview

The Central Dogma of Molecular Biology describes the directional flow of genetic information, first articulated by Francis Crick in 1958 and formally published in 1970.

DNA ⟶ replication ⟶ DNA ⟶ transcription ⟶ RNA ⟶ translation ⟶ Protein

Special flows: reverse transcription (RNA→DNA, retroviruses); RNA replication (RNA→RNA, RNA viruses)

Coined byFrancis Crick, 1958 lecture; published 1970 in Nature
Transcription localeNucleus (eukaryotes); cytoplasm (prokaryotes)
Translation localeRibosome — free in cytoplasm or on rough ER
Key enzyme — transcriptionRNA polymerase (no primer needed)
Key enzyme — translationRibosome (peptidyl transferase activity)
Information unitCodon = 3 nucleotides = 1 amino acid

Core vocabulary

  • Template strand (antisense): read 3'→5' by RNA polymerase; determines mRNA sequence.
  • Non-template strand (sense / coding): same sequence as mRNA (T→U).
  • mRNA — messenger RNA; carries codon sequence; read 5'→3' at ribosome.
  • tRNA — transfer RNA; anticodon loop base-pairs with mRNA codon; carries amino acid at 3' CCA tail.
  • rRNA — ribosomal RNA; catalytic core of the ribosome (ribozyme).
  • Genetic code: 64 codons; 61 sense (coding) + 3 stop (UAA, UAG, UGA); redundant but nearly universal.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "One gene, one enzyme" hypothesis (Beadle & Tatum, 1941) — precursor idea to the dogma.
  • RNA polymerase II transcribes protein-coding genes in eukaryotes.
  • AUG = start codon; codes for methionine (Met / fMet in prokaryotes).
  • Ribosome subunits: 50S + 30S = 70S (prokaryote); 60S + 40S = 80S (eukaryote).

High-Yield Processes & Concepts

Core topics most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

Transcription
DNA → RNA · nucleus (eukaryotes)
powerSigma factor (prokaryotes) allows RNA pol to recognize and bind the promoter; eukaryotes use general transcription factors (TBP, TFIID, etc.) and RNA pol II for mRNA.
recogRNA pol unwinds DNA at the transcription bubble; synthesizes RNA 5'→3' antiparallel to the template strand (3'→5').
recogThree stages: initiation (promoter binding), elongation (chain extension), termination (Rho-dependent or intrinsic hairpin in prokaryotes; poly-A signal in eukaryotes).
recogEukaryotic pre-mRNA processing: 5' cap (7-methylguanosine), 3' poly-A tail, and splicing of introns by the spliceosome (snRNPs).

Translation
mRNA → Protein · ribosome
powerRibosome has three tRNA binding sites: A site (aminoacyl — incoming), P site (peptidyl — growing chain), E site (exit — deacylated tRNA). Peptide bond formed by peptidyl transferase (23S/28S rRNA ribozyme).
recogInitiation: small subunit binds mRNA at Shine-Dalgarno sequence (prokaryotes) or 5' cap scanning (eukaryotes); initiator tRNA (fMet-tRNA in prokaryotes) occupies P site.
recogElongation: EF-Tu (prokaryote) / eEF1A (eukaryote) delivers aminoacyl-tRNA to A site; translocation moves ribosome 3'.
recogTermination: stop codons UAA, UAG, UGA recognized by release factors (RF1/RF2 in prokaryotes); polypeptide released and ribosome recycled.

The Genetic Code
64 codons · cracked 1961–1966
powerCode cracked by Nirenberg & Matthaei (1961, poly-U = poly-Phe), and completed by Khorana (synthetic polynucleotides) and Nirenberg (ribosome-binding assay). Nobel Prize 1968 shared with Holley (tRNA structure).
recogCode is degenerate (redundant): multiple codons per amino acid; wobble at 3rd position (Crick's wobble hypothesis).
recogCode is nearly universal; exceptions in mitochondria (e.g., UGA = Trp in human mito) and some protists.
recogStart codon: AUG. Stop codons: UAA ("ochre"), UAG ("amber"), UGA ("opal/umber").
wiki ↗
RNA Polymerase
key enzyme of transcription
powerProkaryotic core enzyme: α₂ββ'ω; adding σ (sigma) forms the holoenzyme. Different sigma factors recognize different promoters (σ70 = housekeeping in E. coli).
recogThree eukaryotic nuclear RNA polymerases: RNA Pol I (rRNA precursor, 45S), RNA Pol II (mRNA + snRNA), RNA Pol III (tRNA, 5S rRNA).
recogNo proofreading 3'→5' exonuclease (unlike DNA pol) — higher error rate (~1/10,000); no primer required.
wiki ↗
tRNA Structure & Aminoacyl-tRNA Synthetases
adapter molecule · "second genetic code"
powerAminoacyl-tRNA synthetases — 20 enzymes, each specific to one amino acid and its cognate tRNA(s). The specificity of charging is called the "second genetic code." Error rate ~1/10,000; proofreading (editing) domain in many synthetases.
recogtRNA folds into a cloverleaf (secondary) and L-shaped (tertiary) structure; ~73–93 nucleotides. Contains modified bases (e.g., inosine, pseudouridine).
recogAmino acid attaches to 3' CCA-OH terminus (universal). Anticodon loop (positions 34–36) base-pairs antiparallel with mRNA codon.
wiki ↗
Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Gene Expression
key contrast for traps
powerIn prokaryotes, transcription and translation are coupled — ribosomes begin translating the 5' end of mRNA while RNA pol is still elongating. Not possible in eukaryotes (nuclear envelope separates the two).
recogProkaryotes: polycistronic mRNA (multiple ORFs one transcript); eukaryotes: monocistronic (one ORF per mature mRNA, typically).
recogEukaryotes undergo RNA processing (5' cap, poly-A tail, splicing) before export to cytoplasm; prokaryotes do not.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key enzymes & factors

  • RNA polymerase — transcribes DNA to RNA; no primer
  • Sigma (σ) factor — prokaryote promoter recognition
  • RNA Pol II — eukaryote mRNA synthesis
  • Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase — charges tRNA; "second genetic code"
  • Peptidyl transferase (23S/28S rRNA) — forms peptide bonds; ribozyme
  • Reverse transcriptase — RNA→DNA; HIV, retroviruses
  • Telomerase — reverse transcriptase; extends chromosome ends (not central dogma per se, but same enzyme class)

  • Kozak consensus — eukaryote translation initiation
  • AUG — start codon (Met/fMet)
  • UAA/UAG/UGA — stop codons (no tRNA)
  • 3' CCA-OH — universal tRNA amino-acid attachment site
  • A, P, E sites — ribosome tRNA positions

Comparison Traps

Don't confuseHow to tell them apart
Transcription vs ReplicationTranscription: DNA→RNA, uses RNA pol, no primer, one strand template, produces mRNA/rRNA/tRNA. Replication: DNA→DNA, uses DNA pol, needs primer, both strands templated, semi-conservative.
Template strand vs Coding strandTemplate (antisense) strand is read 3'→5' by RNA pol; the coding (sense/non-template) strand has the same sequence as the mRNA (with T→U). Questions often ask which is "read" — it is the template.
mRNA codon vs tRNA anticodonCodon = on mRNA, read 5'→3'. Anticodon = on tRNA, antiparallel to codon. They base-pair; anticodon is written 3'→5' when aligned with codon.
Intron vs ExonIntron: "intervening sequence," removed by spliceosome. Exon: "expressed sequence," retained in mature mRNA. Mnemonic: introns in the trash, exons exit.
Prokaryote 70S vs Eukaryote 80SSvedberg units are not additive (shape affects sedimentation). 50S+30S=70S (not 80S); 60S+40S=80S (not 100S). Antibiotics often target prokaryote ribosome subunits (streptomycin→30S; erythromycin→50S).
Reverse transcription vs RNA replicationReverse transcription: RNA→DNA (retroviruses; enzyme = reverse transcriptase). RNA replication: RNA→RNA (RNA viruses; enzyme = RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, RdRp). Neither is the "normal" dogma flow.
Sigma factor vs Transcription factorSigma: prokaryote-specific; dissociable subunit of RNA pol holoenzyme; directs promoter binding. Eukaryotic general transcription factors (TFIIA–TFIIH) are separate proteins assembling the pre-initiation complex at the TATA box.
tRNA charging vs TranslationCharging (aminoacylation) is a pre-translation step: synthetase links amino acid to tRNA using ATP (→AMP + PPi). This happens in the cytoplasm before the tRNA enters the ribosome.

Cross-discipline big picture The Central Dogma connects to physics (X-ray crystallography by Rosalind Franklin revealed the double helix; the ribosome's 2009 Nobel in Chemistry used cryo-crystallography), chemistry (peptide bond formation is thermodynamically driven by GTP hydrolysis of elongation factors), computer science (codons as a ternary code — 4 bases in triplets = 64 words — informed early information-theory discussions), and medicine (CRISPR edits the DNA layer; mRNA vaccines like the COVID-19 shots exploit the translation layer directly, delivering mRNA that ribosomes read to produce antigen). Every layer of the dogma has its own Nobel Prize.
