Quick Overview

DefinitionEmission of electrons when light hits a metal surface
DiscoveredHeinrich Hertz, 1887 (UV on spark gap)
Explained byAlbert Einstein, 1905 — Nobel Prize 1921
Key insightLight comes in discrete quanta (photons), not waves
ThresholdOnly works above a minimum frequency — intensity irrelevant
Confirmed byRobert Millikan's precise 1916 measurements

Governing equations

Ephoton = hf h = Planck's constant (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s); f = frequency of light
KEmax = hf − φ Einstein's photoelectric equation: KE = kinetic energy of emitted electron; φ (phi) = work function of metal
eVstop = KEmax Stopping potential Vstop: voltage needed to halt the fastest emitted electrons

Physical picture — what actually happens

  • A single photon is absorbed by a single electron in the metal.
  • If hf > φ, the electron escapes; leftover energy becomes kinetic energy.
  • If hf < φ (below threshold frequency f₀), no emission — regardless of brightness.
  • Higher intensity = more photons = more electrons emitted, but does not increase KE.
  • Higher frequency = more energetic photons = higher KE of emitted electrons.
  • Emission is essentially instantaneous — no time-delay (contradicts wave theory).

High-Yield Statements & Applications

The 5 ideas most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard early clue. recog = middle / giveaway clue.

Einstein's 1905 Explanation ("Miracle Year")
Published in Annalen der Physik · Nobel Prize in Physics 1921
powerEinstein's paper was titled "On a Heuristic Viewpoint Concerning the Production and Transformation of Light" — often called the light-quantum hypothesis paper, one of his 1905 Annus Mirabilis papers.
recogEinstein did not receive the Nobel for relativity — he received it specifically for discovering the law of the photoelectric effect.
recogThe explanation required treating light as discrete photons — a radical departure from Maxwell's wave theory, and the founding act of quantum mechanics.
wiki ↗
Work Function (φ) and Threshold Frequency
Core concept — metal-dependent energy barrier
powerThreshold frequency f₀ = φ / h: the minimum photon frequency needed to eject any electron. Below f₀, no electron is released even with an extremely bright light source.
recogThe work function is the binding energy of the least-tightly-bound electrons (conduction electrons) and is characteristic of the specific metal — e.g., cesium has a very low φ (~2.0 eV), gold is higher.
recogOn a graph of KEmax vs. frequency, the slope is h (Planck's constant) and the x-intercept is f₀.
Millikan's Confirmation Experiment (1916)
Robert A. Millikan · Nobel Prize in Physics 1923
powerMillikan initially set out to disprove Einstein's light-quantum hypothesis; his precise measurements of the stopping potential vs. frequency instead confirmed it exactly and gave the first accurate experimental value of Planck's constant.
recogUsed a vacuum tube with a cleaned metal surface and a variable opposing voltage (stopping potential Vstop).
recogThe linear relationship eVstop = hf − φ plots as a straight line with slope h — Millikan measured h to within 0.5% of its true value.
wiki ↗
Wave vs. Particle Paradox — What Classic Wave Theory Predicted (Wrong)
The "classical failure" — a core quizbowl clue frame
powerWave theory predicted: (1) any frequency could eject electrons if the wave were intense enough; (2) higher intensity → higher KE; (3) there should be a time delay while energy accumulated. All three predictions are wrong.
recogThe observed instantaneous emission and the intensity-independent KE could not be explained by a wave model — this is the canonical argument for the quantization of light.
Hertz's Discovery and Lenard's Investigation
Heinrich Hertz 1887 · Philipp Lenard 1902 (Nobel 1905)
powerHertz noticed that UV light shining on the negative electrode of a spark gap made it spark more easily — he identified the phenomenon but could not explain it. His student Philipp Lenard showed experimentally that it was electrons being emitted and that KE was independent of intensity.
recogLenard won the 1905 Nobel for cathode-ray work but bitterly opposed Einstein's photon explanation — a famous case of a scientist resisting a theory that explained his own data.

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Related results and extensions that appear as hard early clues in tossups.

Compton Effect (1923)
Arthur Compton — X-ray scattering off electrons
Photons carry momentum p = h/λ; scattering shifts wavelength. Complementary photon-particle evidence alongside photoelectric effect.
de Broglie Hypothesis (1924)
Wave-particle duality extended to matter
If light has particle character, electrons have wave character: λ = h/p. The photoelectric effect was the direct inspiration.
Planck's Blackbody Radiation (1900)
Max Planck — energy quanta E = nhf
Planck introduced quantization to fix the ultraviolet catastrophe; Einstein adopted the idea and extended it to light itself.
Stopping Potential Measurement
KEmax = eVstop
Reverse-biasing the collector halts photoelectrons; V at zero current gives maximum KE directly. Key lab technique.
Photoelectric Cells / Photomultipliers
Practical applications of the effect
Early TV cameras (iconoscope), photomultiplier tubes, solar cells, and photodiodes all rely on photoelectron emission.
X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)
Technique using photoelectric effect on surfaces
High-energy X-rays eject core electrons; binding energies identify elements and chemical states. Also called ESCA (Electron Spectroscopy for Chemical Analysis).
Photoemissive Cathodes
Cesium and cesium-oxide coatings
Cesium's very low work function (~2 eV) makes it ideal for phototubes activated by visible light, not just UV.
Ultraviolet Catastrophe Connection
Two quantum-revolution crises solved together
Blackbody radiation (Planck 1900) and photoelectric effect (Einstein 1905) together founded quantum theory; tossups often link them as "two problems classical physics couldn't explain."

Recognition / Clue Bank

Key terms & symbols

  • h — Planck's constant (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)
  • φ (phi) — work function (in eV)
  • f₀ — threshold / cutoff frequency
  • Vstop — stopping potential
  • Photon — Einstein's name for the light quantum (term coined by Lewis, 1926)
  • Photoelectrons — electrons emitted by the effect
  • eV (electron-volt) — natural unit for photon/work-function energies

Key people

  • Heinrich Hertz — discovered the phenomenon (1887)
  • Philipp Lenard — showed electrons emitted, KE independent of intensity (1902); Nobel 1905
  • Albert Einstein — photon explanation (1905); Nobel 1921
  • Robert Millikan — precise confirmation, measured h (1916); Nobel 1923
  • Max Planck — quantization precursor (1900); Nobel 1918
  • G.N. Lewis — coined the word "photon" (1926)

Canonical clue phrases

  • "Light shining on a metal surface ejects electrons"
  • "Explained as evidence that light is quantized"
  • "Einstein won the Nobel for discovering its law, not relativity"
  • "Millikan intended to disprove Einstein but confirmed him"
  • "Below the threshold frequency, no electrons emitted"
  • "Intensity affects number of electrons, not their energy"
  • "Named in analogy to electric effect of light"

Numbers & experiments

  • Planck's constant h ≈ 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s ≈ 4.136 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV·s
  • Typical work functions: Cs ~2.0 eV, Na ~2.3 eV, Cu ~4.5 eV, Au ~5.1 eV
  • Visible light energies: ~1.8 eV (red) to ~3.1 eV (violet)
  • UV needed for most metals; cesium responds to visible light
  • Millikan's 1916 h value was accurate to within 0.5%

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Compton EffectCompton scatters X-rays off free/loosely-bound electrons, shifting wavelength — it's photon-electron elastic scattering. Photoelectric effect absorbs the photon entirely and ejects the electron. Both prove light has particle properties, but different mechanisms.
Thermionic emissionElectrons emitted by heating the metal (thermal energy), not light. Used in vacuum tubes. Photoelectric emission is triggered by photon absorption.
Fluorescence / PhosphorescenceLight absorbed then re-emitted as light at a longer wavelength. No electron escapes the material. Common confusion because both involve light hitting a material.
Pair productionGamma photon converts into an electron-positron pair near a nucleus. Requires photon energies >>1 MeV. Photoelectric effect involves low-energy UV/visible photons and works only on bound electrons.
PhotoionizationClosely related — photon ejects electron from a gas-phase atom or molecule, not a metal. The photoelectric effect is specifically on solid metal surfaces; the physics is the same but the context differs.
Planck's blackbody radiationPlanck quantized oscillator energies to fix blackbody spectra — he did not say light itself was quantized. Einstein's step (photoelectric effect) is the one that quantizes the light field directly.

⚛️ Big Picture — Concept Cross-Links The photoelectric effect sits at the founding junction of quantum mechanics: Einstein's photon (1905) built on Planck's quantization (1900) and directly inspired de Broglie's matter waves (1924) and Bohr's atomic model (1913). In a tossup, it can bridge to Albert Einstein (Nobel 1921), Robert Millikan (Nobel 1923), Planck's constant, wave-particle duality, the Compton effect, and the broader quantum revolution category. The phrase "Einstein did not win the Nobel for relativity" is one of the most reliable giveaway clues in all of physics.
