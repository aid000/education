Quick Overview

Born / Died10 July 1856, Smiljan (then Austrian Empire, now Croatia) — 7 January 1943, New York City
NationalitySerbian-American; ethnic Serb born in the Austrian Empire; naturalized U.S. citizen (1891)
FieldElectrical & mechanical engineering; alternating current, electromagnetism, wireless power
Eponymous unitThe tesla (T) — SI unit of magnetic flux density (1 T = 1 Wb/m²); adopted 1960
Key affiliationWorked for Edison Machine Works, then partnered with George Westinghouse; founded his own labs
Signature workPolyphase AC induction motor & the rotating magnetic field; the AC power system that won the "War of the Currents"

Career arc (phases as clues)

  • European training (1875–1884): Studied at Graz Polytechnic; worked for the Continental Edison Company in Paris before emigrating to the U.S.
  • Edison & the break (1884–1886): Worked briefly for Thomas Edison in New York; left over a disputed bonus and a clash over AC vs DC.
  • Westinghouse partnership (1888–1893): Sold AC patents to George Westinghouse; their AC system lit the 1893 Chicago World's Fair and powered the 1895 Niagara Falls hydroelectric plant.
  • Wireless dreams & decline (1899–1943): Colorado Springs experiments; the unfinished Wardenclyffe Tower; died nearly penniless in the Hotel New Yorker.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • The SI unit of magnetic flux density ("magnetic field strength") is named for him — a near-automatic giveaway.
  • "This inventor" who developed alternating current (AC) against Edison's direct current in the War of the Currents.
  • The high-voltage, high-frequency Tesla coil and its dramatic electrical arcs.
  • The unfinished Wardenclyffe Tower on Long Island for wireless transmission of power.

High-Yield Inventions & Principles (Very Common)

The most-asked topics. power = hard, buzz-early clue. recog = recognition / middle clue.

AC Induction Motor & Rotating Magnetic Field
1887–1888 · U.S. Patent 381,968
recogUses a rotating magnetic field produced by out-of-phase AC currents to spin a rotor with no electrical contact (no brushes/commutator).
recogThe basis of the modern polyphase (two- and three-phase) AC power system; patents bought by Westinghouse.
powerTesla said the idea of the rotating field came to him in a flash while walking in a Budapest park reciting Goethe's Faust (1882). His 1888 AIEE lecture "A New System of Alternating Current Motors and Transformers" laid out the polyphase scheme; Galileo Ferraris independently described a rotating field around the same time (a priority footnote).

Polyphase Alternating Current System
1888–1895 · Niagara Falls power plant 1895
recogAC can be stepped up to high voltage by transformers for low-loss long-distance transmission, then stepped down for use — DC could not be transmitted far in the 1890s.
recogTesla's AC system, built by Westinghouse, powered the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago and the Adams Power Station at Niagara Falls (1895).
powerThis is the heart of the "War of the Currents" against Edison's DC; Edison ran propaganda electrocuting animals and promoted AC for the electric chair to brand it deadly. Tesla later tore up his royalty contract to save the cash-strapped Westinghouse company.

Tesla Coil
1891 · patented air-core resonant transformer
recogAn air-core resonant transformer producing very high-voltage, high-frequency, low-current AC and dramatic spark discharges.
recogBuilt around two tuned LC circuits in resonance; used to light wireless gas tubes and demonstrate "wireless" energy transfer.
powerScaled up enormously at his Colorado Springs lab (1899), where he generated artificial lightning and claimed to detect terrestrial standing waves. The iconic photo of Tesla reading amid towering arcs is a double-exposure publicity trick.
wiki ↗
Wardenclyffe Tower
1901–1917 · Shoreham, Long Island, New York
recogA ~187-foot tower meant for wireless transmission of power and global communication, funded largely by J. P. Morgan.
recogDesigned by architect Stanford White; never became operational; demolished in 1917 as Tesla's debts mounted.
powerMorgan withdrew funding partly because Marconi's cheaper transatlantic radio (1901) undercut the project's commercial case. The site is now a museum operated by the nonprofit that crowdfunded its purchase (the "Tesla Science Center").
wiki ↗
Radio & Wireless Control (Priority vs Marconi)
1893–1898 · radio patents; 1898 radio-controlled boat
recogDemonstrated principles of radio transmission and tuned circuits in the 1890s; in 1898 showed a radio-controlled boat ("teleautomaton") at Madison Square Garden.
recogLocked in a long priority dispute with Guglielmo Marconi over the invention of radio.
powerIn 1943, months after Tesla's death, the U.S. Supreme Court (case Marconi Wireless Telegraph Co. v. United States) upheld Tesla's earlier patents over several of Marconi's — often cited as Tesla being "credited" with radio, though the ruling was narrower and partly about wartime patent claims.
wiki ↗
The Tesla (SI Unit)
named 1960 · symbol T
recogThe SI unit of magnetic flux density (magnetic B-field): 1 T = 1 Wb/m² = 1 kg·s⁻²·A⁻¹.
recogReplaces the older CGS unit the gauss (1 T = 10,000 gauss); Earth's surface field is ~25–65 microtesla.
powerAdopted at the 1960 General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM). A clinical MRI machine runs at ~1.5–3 T; the strongest continuous lab magnets reach tens of tesla. Don't confuse magnetic flux density (tesla) with magnetic flux (the weber).

Deeper Cuts (Hard Clues)

Lesser-known work that appears as early/hard tossup clues or power lines.

Colorado Springs Experiments
1899–1900
High-altitude lab where Tesla produced millions of volts, artificial lightning, and claimed to observe terrestrial standing electromagnetic waves.
Teleautomaton (Radio-Controlled Boat)
1898
Wirelessly steered model boat shown at Madison Square Garden — an early demonstration of robotics and remote control.
Tesla Turbine
1909–1913
Bladeless "boundary-layer" turbine using smooth spinning disks and fluid viscosity instead of vanes.
Tesla Valve (Valvular Conduit)
patented 1920
A fixed-geometry, no-moving-parts check valve that lets fluid flow easily one way and resists the reverse.
"Death Ray" / Teleforce
1930s
Late-life proposal for a charged-particle beam weapon; never built; fueled posthumous conspiracy lore.
Wireless Power Transmission
1891 onward
Lifelong goal of transmitting electrical energy without wires through the Earth and atmosphere; basis of Wardenclyffe.
Egg of Columbus
1893
World's Fair demo: a copper egg made to stand on end by a rotating magnetic field, illustrating his AC motor principle.
Niagara Falls (Adams) Power Plant
1895
First large-scale AC hydroelectric station, sending power to Buffalo; vindicated the polyphase system.
Tesla Coil → X-rays
1894–1895
Captured early "shadowgraph" images using vacuum tubes before Röntgen's announcement; lab fire destroyed much of the work (1895).
Personal eccentricities
lifelong
Obsession with the number 3, germophobia, fondness for pigeons; never married; died at the Hotel New Yorker.
Mark Twain in Tesla's Lab
1894
Author Mark Twain, a friend, photographed in Tesla's South Fifth Avenue laboratory holding a phosphorescent wireless lamp.
Wireless / Phosphorescent Lamps
1890s
Tesla holding a gas-discharge bulb lit wirelessly by a high-frequency field — his wireless-lighting demonstrations.

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Thomas EdisonEdison championed direct current (DC) and ran a smear campaign against AC. Tesla (with Westinghouse) backed alternating current (AC). Clues like "Menlo Park," "phonograph," or "incandescent bulb" → Edison; "rotating magnetic field," "polyphase," "Wardenclyffe" → Tesla.
George WestinghouseWestinghouse was the industrialist/financier who bought Tesla's AC patents and commercialized the system; he also invented the railway air brake. The inventor of the AC motor is Tesla; the company behind it is Westinghouse.
Guglielmo MarconiMarconi achieved the first transatlantic radio transmission (1901) and won the 1909 Nobel Prize. Tesla holds earlier U.S. radio patents (upheld 1943). "Nobel Prize" or "transatlantic signal" → Marconi; "radio-controlled boat 1898" → Tesla.
Michael FaradayFaraday discovered electromagnetic induction and built the first primitive motor/dynamo decades earlier (1820s–30s). Tesla engineered the practical polyphase AC motor. "Law of induction" / "lines of force" → Faraday; "rotating magnetic field" → Tesla.
Hermann von Helmholtz / HertzHeinrich Hertz first generated and detected electromagnetic (radio) waves (1887), confirming Maxwell. Tesla built on this for power and wireless. "First to produce EM waves in the lab" → Hertz.
Galileo FerrarisItalian engineer who independently described the rotating magnetic field (~1885–88). A classic "priority dispute" trap; tossups may credit either, but the practical induction motor and patents are Tesla's.

⚡ The through-line: harnessing the rotating field Tesla's whole career flows from one 1882 insight — that out-of-phase AC currents create a rotating magnetic field. That idea gives the induction motor, the polyphase grid that won the War of the Currents, the resonant Tesla coil, and his dream of wireless power at Wardenclyffe. The quizbowl through-line: whenever a question invokes AC vs DC, rotating magnetic fields, the unit of magnetic flux density, or wireless transmission of energy, Tesla is rarely far from the answer — but watch the traps with Edison, Westinghouse, and Marconi.
