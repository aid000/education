Quick Overview

Full nameMary Wollstonecraft Shelley (née Godwin, 1797–1851)
NationalityBritish (English)
PeriodRomantic era; active 1818–1851
Genre claim to fameOften called founder of science fiction / Gothic novel
ParentsWilliam Godwin (philosopher) & Mary Wollstonecraft (feminist); mother died at her birth
HusbandPercy Bysshe Shelley (married 1816); he drowned 1822

Origin of Frankenstein

  • Summer 1816, Villa Diodati, Lake Geneva — with Percy Shelley, Lord Byron, and John Polidori during the "Year Without a Summer" (volcanic eruption of Mt. Tambora).
  • Byron proposed a ghost-story contest; 18-year-old Mary conceived the idea in a waking dream/nightmare.
  • Published anonymously in 1818; Percy wrote the preface — many assumed he was the author.

Classic Quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Any mention of Frankenstein's creature, the name Victor Frankenstein, or Ingolstadt (where Victor studies).
  • Author described as daughter of Mary Wollstonecraft and William Godwin.
  • Wife of Percy Shelley; the Geneva / Villa Diodati ghost-story contest.

Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus

powerVictor animated the creature using galvanism (electricity); inspired partly by Luigi Galvani and Erasmus Darwin's experiments. Victor studied at the University of Ingolstadt.
recogThe creature educates himself partly by secretly observing the De Lacey family and reading Paradise Lost, Plutarch's Lives, and The Sorrows of Young Werther.
recogVictor destroys his half-completed female creature on the Orkney Islands; the creature retaliates by strangling Elizabeth Lavenza (Victor's adopted cousin/fiancée) on their wedding night.
recogThe creature murders William (Victor's younger brother) and frames Justine Moritz, who is executed.
recogVictor's friend Henry Clerval is also killed by the creature; Victor dies chasing the creature to the Arctic; creature disappears to die on the ice.

The Last Man
1826 · novel · sci-fi / post-apocalyptic
powerFramed as a Cumaean Sibyl's prophetic leaves found in a cave near Naples — the narrator assembles them into a future history.
recogSet in the late 21st century; narrator Lionel Verney watches civilization wiped out by a global plague.
recogCharacters are thinly veiled portraits: Adrian = Percy Shelley; Lord Raymond = Lord Byron. Raymond dies fighting in Greece (Byron-parallel).
giveLionel Verney is the sole survivor of humanity at novel's end, sailing an empty Mediterranean alone — first major English post-apocalyptic novel.

Mathilda
written 1819–20, published 1959 · novella · Gothic
powerSuppressed by Shelley's father William Godwin; not published until 1959 — the long posthumous suppression is itself a clue.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Characters (Frankenstein)

  • Victor Frankenstein — creator; from Geneva; studies in Ingolstadt
  • The Creature / Monster — never named; 8 feet tall; eloquent
  • Robert Walton — framing narrator; Arctic captain
  • Henry Clerval — Victor's best friend; killed by creature
  • Elizabeth Lavenza — Victor's adopted cousin / fiancée; strangled
  • William Frankenstein — young brother; first victim
  • Justine Moritz — falsely accused; executed for William's murder
  • De Lacey family — blind old man & family; creature observes & imitates them
  • Alphonse Frankenstein — Victor's father; dies of grief

Recurring motifs & themes

  • Prometheus myth — creation, punishment, hubris
  • Paradise Lost — creature reads it; parallels to Satan/Adam
  • Galvanism / electricity — animating force (never explicitly named in 1818 text)
  • Arctic / polar ice — frame narrative; Victor's final pursuit
  • The Sublime — Alps, Mont Blanc, Arctic wasteland
  • Epistolary / nested narrative — Walton's letters contain Victor's account, which contains creature's account
  • Abandonment & parenthood — creature as abandoned child
  • The Sibylline Leaves framing device (The Last Man)

Biographical clues

  • Born 30 August 1797, London; died 1 February 1851
  • Mother Mary Wollstonecraft died days after her birth (childbirth fever)
  • Father William Godwin wrote Political Justice and Caleb Williams
  • Eloped with Percy Shelley at 16 (1814)
  • Ghost-story contest: 1816, Villa Diodati, Geneva
  • Percy drowned off Lerici, Italy, 1822
  • Son: Percy Florence Shelley (sole surviving child)
  • Published Frankenstein anonymously, 1818; first named edition 1823

Style & form markers

  • Gothic / dark Romantic atmosphere; sublime landscapes
  • Frame narratives / nested tales (letters within tales within tales)
  • Science as transgression — not magic, but experiment-gone-wrong
  • Creature is articulate, sympathetic — complicates easy villainy
  • Strong feminist subtext: motherless women, absent mothers, created life
  • Intertextual — Paradise Lost, Prometheus Unbound, Enlightenment science
  • Epistolary framing signals Romantic-era novel (compare Richardson, Goethe)

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Percy Bysshe ShelleyHer husband; a poet (odes, lyric verse — "Ozymandias," "Ode to the West Wind," Prometheus Unbound). Mary = prose novelist. If the tossup asks about Frankenstein it is Mary, not Percy.
Ann RadcliffeEarlier Gothic novelist (The Mysteries of Udolpho, 1794); supernatural explained away rationally in the end. Mary's creature is never explained away — the horror is real and scientific.
Matthew LewisAuthor of The Monk (1796) — another Gothic; supernatural is genuine, lurid, demonic. No science; no sympathetic monster.
Horace WalpoleThe Castle of Otranto (1764) — first Gothic novel; medieval setting, feudal ghosts. Far earlier and no science-fiction element.
H. G. Wells / Jules VerneLater sci-fi founders; Wells's monsters are Martians / social critiques, Verne's focus is adventure technology. Frankenstein predates both and frames science as moral transgression, not adventure.
Bram Stoker (Dracula)Also Gothic, also 19th-century British; but Dracula (1897) is vampire horror, no creature-creation plot. Stoker is Irish, Shelley is English.
Mary WollstonecraftHer mother (not the same person); Wollstonecraft wrote A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792) — a feminist polemic, not fiction.
