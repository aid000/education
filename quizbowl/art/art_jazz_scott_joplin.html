Quick Overview

Scott Joplin, c. 1903
InstrumentPiano (composer & pianist)
Era / StyleRagtime — the dominant style c. 1895–1918; precursor to jazz
Born / Diedc. 1868 (Texas) — died New York City 1917
Nickname"The King of Ragtime" / "King of Ragtime Writers"
Key partnerPublisher John Stark; the Maple Leaf Club, Sedalia, MO
Key citySedalia, Missouri and St. Louis (Missouri ragtime hub)

Defining traits

  • Syncopation: ragtime's hallmark — a "ragged," off-the-beat right-hand melody over a steady marching left-hand "oom-pah" bass.
  • Multi-strain march form: rags use several 16-bar strains (AABBACCDD-type schemes), borrowed from the march and cakewalk.
  • "Notice!" — do not play fast: Joplin printed warnings on his scores that "it is never right to play ragtime fast."
  • Aspired to elevate ragtime to art music; wrote the instructional School of Ragtime (1908).
  • Composed two operas, including the full-length Treemonisha — his great ambition.

Classic Quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "The King of Ragtime."
  • "Maple Leaf Rag" (1899) — the most famous rag ever written.
  • "The Entertainer" — revived as the theme of the 1973 film The Sting.
  • His opera Treemonisha, awarded a posthumous Pulitzer Prize (1976).

Signature Rags & Compositions

Maple Leaf Rag
published 1899 · publisher John Stark, Sedalia, MO
recogThe most famous rag ever written; named for the Maple Leaf Club in Sedalia, Missouri.
recogReportedly the first instrumental sheet music to sell one million copies; it made Joplin's name.
powerPublished by John Stark, who paid Joplin a one-cent royalty per copy — an unusually generous deal for a Black composer in 1899. Its opening strain in A-flat is instantly recognizable.

The Entertainer
published 1902 · subtitled "A Rag Time Two Step"
recogA classic multi-strain rag; today probably Joplin's single most recognizable melody.
recogRevived in 1973 as the central theme of the film The Sting, arranged by Marvin Hamlisch (a #3 pop hit).
powerDedicated "to James Brown and his Mandolin Club"; the 1970s revival, sparked by Joshua Rifkin's recordings and The Sting, created a nationwide ragtime renaissance.
wiki ↗
Treemonisha
completed 1911 (opera) · fully staged 1972 · Pulitzer 1976
recogJoplin's full-length opera, whose heroine, a foundling named Treemonisha, leads her community through education out of superstition.
recogSet on an Arkansas plantation after the Civil War; the rousing finale is "A Real Slow Drag."
powerJoplin self-published it in 1911 and bankrupted himself trying to stage it (only a bare run-through in his lifetime). It received its first full production in 1972 and Joplin a posthumous Pulitzer Prize in 1976.

The Easy Winners
published 1901
recogA bright, sporting-themed rag; the cover depicts horse racing, baseball, football, and yachting.
recogOne of the rags Joplin both composed and published himself, outside the Stark catalog.
powerHeavily featured (with "The Entertainer") in The Sting and in modern violin/cello arrangements; a staple of the recorded Joplin revival.

The Maple Leaf Club / Sedalia & "Original Rags"
"Original Rags" published 1899 (his first published rag)
recog"Original Rags" (1899) was Joplin's first published rag, appearing just before "Maple Leaf Rag."
recogJoplin trained and played in Sedalia, Missouri, studying at the George R. Smith College and frequenting the Maple Leaf Club.
power"Original Rags" was credited "arranged by Charles N. Daniels" — a publisher's condition; weeks later "Maple Leaf Rag" made Joplin the genre's leading figure.

Magnetic Rag & The School of Ragtime
"Magnetic Rag" 1914 (last rag) · "School of Ragtime" 1908
recog"Magnetic Rag" (1914) is generally considered Joplin's last published rag, with an unusually complex, almost classical structure.
recogThe School of Ragtime: Six Exercises for Piano (1908) is his instruction manual, insisting ragtime be played strictly and never fast.
powerHis late works (the "School," Treemonisha) show Joplin trying to formalize and dignify ragtime as a serious American art form rather than dance-hall novelty.

Recognition / Clue Bank

People / collaborators

  • John Stark — publisher of "Maple Leaf Rag" and most classic Joplin rags
  • Marvin Hamlisch — adapted "The Entertainer" for The Sting (1973)
  • Joshua Rifkin — pianist whose 1970 Nonesuch LP launched the Joplin revival
  • Arthur Marshall & Scott Hayden — pupils and co-composers
  • Louis Chauvin — collaborator on "Heliotrope Bouquet"
  • Gunther Schuller — conducted/championed the 1970s Treemonisha revival

Innovations / hallmarks

  • Ragtime — syncopated melody over a steady march bass; precursor of jazz
  • Multi-strain march form — multiple 16-bar strains (AABBACCDD-type)
  • From the cakewalk and the march tradition
  • "Classic ragtime" — Joplin's refined, composed school vs. dance-hall novelty
  • Insisted ragtime be played strictly, never fast (School of Ragtime)
  • Ambition to bring ragtime into opera and concert music

Key works for buzzing

  • "Maple Leaf Rag" (1899) — named for a Sedalia club; sold a million
  • "The Entertainer" (1902) — theme of The Sting
  • Treemonisha — opera; Pulitzer 1976; "A Real Slow Drag"
  • "Original Rags" (1899) — first published rag
  • "The Easy Winners," "Gladiolus Rag," "Solace"
  • School of Ragtime (1908) — instruction manual

Biographical / "also" clues

  • Born in Texas (near Texarkana), c. 1868
  • Rose to fame in Sedalia, Missouri; later St. Louis and New York
  • "The King of Ragtime"; wrote a lost first opera, A Guest of Honor (1903)
  • Died in 1917 in New York; syphilis-related dementia ended his career
  • 1970s revival: Rifkin's recordings + The Sting → ragtime renaissance
  • Posthumous Pulitzer Prize (1976); on a U.S. postage stamp (1983)

Answer-Line / Buzz Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Other ragtime composersJames Scott ("Frog Legs Rag," "Grace and Beauty") and Joseph Lamb ("American Beauty Rag") form, with Joplin, the "big three" of classic ragtime. Joplin is the King, wrote "Maple Leaf Rag," and uniquely composed an opera, Treemonisha.
Jelly Roll MortonMorton claimed to have "invented jazz" and bridged ragtime into early New Orleans jazz ("King Porter Stomp," with the Red Hot Peppers). He postdates Joplin and improvised; Joplin wrote fixed, notated rags.
Eubie BlakeA later ragtime/stride pianist who lived to 100; wrote the Broadway show Shuffle Along ("I'm Just Wild About Harry"). Far longer career; not the "classic ragtime" composer Joplin was.
"The Entertainer" vs. The StingThe 1973 film made the tune ubiquitous, but it is an 1902 Joplin rag arranged by Marvin Hamlisch — the film is set in the 1930s, an anachronism. A clue may want the film, the arranger, or Joplin.
"Maple Leaf Rag" vs. the Maple Leaf ClubThe rag is named after the club in Sedalia, Missouri where Joplin played — not the Canadian symbol. Watch for "Sedalia," "John Stark," or "a million copies."
W. C. HandyThe "Father of the Blues" ("St. Louis Blues," "Memphis Blues") — a contemporary, but a blues figure, not ragtime. Don't swap the King of Ragtime for the Father of the Blues.
