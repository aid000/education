Quick Overview

Miles Davis, 1955
Instrument / roleTrumpet (often muted, Harmon mute); bandleader & conceptualist
Born / diedAlton, Illinois 1926 (raised East St. Louis) – Santa Monica, CA 1991
Era / styleSpanned bebop → cool → hard bop → modal → jazz fusion
Home cityNew York (came east to study at Juilliard, joined Charlie Parker)
Key ensemblesThe First & Second Great Quintets; label home Columbia Records
Nicknames"Prince of Darkness"; the cool, understated tone

Defining traits

  • Restless reinventor: led or launched five major jazz movements — cool, hard bop, modal, orchestral collaborations, and fusion.
  • Spare, lyrical, muted tone: famous for the Harmon mute sound and "less is more" phrasing.
  • Recorded Kind of Blue (1959), the best-selling jazz album of all time and the landmark of modal jazz.
  • Mentored a remarkable lineage of sidemen — Coltrane, Cannonball, Bill Evans, Herbie Hancock, Wayne Shorter, Chick Corea, Tony Williams.
  • Famous orchestral collaborations with arranger Gil Evans.

Classic Quizbowl giveaway clues

  • "This trumpeter recorded Kind of Blue."
  • "Birth of the Cool," "Bitches Brew," "Sketches of Spain."
  • "Worked with arranger Gil Evans."
  • "His sidemen included John Coltrane and Herbie Hancock."

Signature Albums (Very Common)

The albums most likely to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

Kind of Blue
1959 · Columbia · the landmark of modal jazz
recogThe best-selling jazz album of all time; defined modal jazz (improvising on scales/modes rather than fast chord changes).
recogOpens with "So What"; includes "Freddie Freeloader," "Blue in Green," "All Blues," "Flamenco Sketches."
powerRecorded with minimal rehearsal from sketches; sidemen John Coltrane, Cannonball Adderley, Bill Evans (and Wynton Kelly), with Paul Chambers and Jimmy Cobb. Pianist Bill Evans wrote the liner notes comparing the sessions to Japanese ink painting.
wiki ↗
Bitches Brew
1970 · Columbia · the launch of jazz fusion
recogThe pioneering jazz-rock fusion album — electric instruments, rock rhythms, studio editing.
recogProduced by Teo Macero, who tape-spliced long jams; gatefold cover art by Mati Klarwein.
powerPlayers who went on to lead fusion bands include Wayne Shorter, Chick Corea, John McLaughlin, Joe Zawinul (Weather Report) and Herbie Hancock. Preceded by the more restrained In a Silent Way (1969).
wiki ↗
Birth of the Cool
recorded 1949–50, compiled 1957 · Capitol · with Gil Evans
recogThe nonet recordings that helped launch cool jazz as a reaction against frantic bebop.
recogUnusual nonet instrumentation including French horn and tuba; arranged by Gil Evans, Gerry Mulligan, John Lewis.
powerThe sessions grew out of meetings at Gil Evans's apartment; the title was retroactively applied by Capitol on the 1957 LP compilation of the 1949–50 sides.
wiki ↗
Sketches of Spain
1960 · Columbia · orchestral collaboration with Gil Evans
recogOrchestral album reworking Spanish classical music — adapts Rodrigo's Concierto de Aranjuez.
recogOne of three landmark Davis–Gil Evans orchestral collaborations (with Miles Ahead and Porgy and Bess).
powerAlso draws on the Andalusian flamenco "Saeta." The Evans collaborations recast Davis as a soloist against a large, painterly orchestral backdrop.
wiki ↗
'Round About Midnight
1957 · Columbia · debut of the First Great Quintet
recogHis first Columbia album; title track is the Thelonious Monk ballad with Davis's muted trumpet.
recogShowcases the First Great Quintet: Coltrane, Red Garland, Paul Chambers, Philly Joe Jones.
powerHis acclaimed Monk performance at the 1955 Newport Jazz Festival won him the Columbia contract. The quintet also cut the four "Prestige marathon" albums (Workin', Steamin', Cookin', Relaxin').
wiki ↗
Milestones
1958 · Columbia · early modal experiment
recogIts title track was an early step toward modal jazz, anticipating Kind of Blue.
recogFeatures the sextet with Coltrane and Cannonball Adderley.
powerBridges the hard-bop quintet and the modal sessions; some pressings are titled Milestones while the tune appears as "Miles." Don't confuse with the 1947 bebop tune also called "Milestones."

Recognition / Clue Bank

Sidemen / collaborators

  • John Coltrane & Cannonball Adderley — sax front line on Kind of Blue
  • Bill Evans & Wynton Kelly — Kind of Blue pianists
  • Gil Evans — arranger of the orchestral albums
  • Second Great Quintet: Wayne Shorter, Herbie Hancock, Ron Carter, Tony Williams
  • Teo Macero — producer who edited the electric albums
  • Charlie Parker — his early bebop bandleader/mentor

Innovations / "first to…"

  • Helped launch cool jazz (Birth of the Cool)
  • Defined modal jazz (Kind of Blue)
  • Pioneered jazz fusion (In a Silent Way, Bitches Brew)
  • Signature Harmon-mute "less is more" trumpet sound
  • Repeatedly reinvented his style — bebop to fusion to '80s pop-jazz

Key albums for buzzing

  • Kind of Blue — 1959, modal, "So What"
  • Bitches Brew — 1970, fusion
  • Birth of the Cool — cool jazz nonet
  • Sketches of Spain — Rodrigo's Aranjuez
  • 'Round About Midnight — First Quintet debut
  • In a Silent Way — first electric album

Biographical / "also did" trivia

  • Born 1926 in Alton, IL, raised in East St. Louis; dentist father, middle-class background
  • Came east to Juilliard but dropped out to play with Charlie Parker
  • Scored Louis Malle's film Ascenseur pour l'échafaud (1958)
  • Wrote Miles: The Autobiography (1989) with Quincy Troupe
  • Famously turned his back to audiences; raspy speaking voice after a throat operation
  • Inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (2006); died 1991

Buzz Traps (Answer-Line Confusions)

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
John ColtraneColtrane was Davis's tenor saxophonist who went solo (Giant Steps, A Love Supreme). If the answer is a trumpeter and bandleader of Kind of Blue, it's Davis; if it's a sheets-of-sound saxophonist, it's Coltrane.
Dizzy GillespieBoth are bebop-rooted trumpeters, but Gillespie is the bent-horn, puffed-cheek bebop virtuoso ("A Night in Tunisia," with Parker). Davis is cooler, sparer, and the restless stylistic reinventor.
Charlie ParkerParker (alto sax, "Bird") was Davis's early bandleader. Clues about the founding of bebop point to Parker; clues about cool/modal/fusion point to Davis.
Gil EvansEvans is the arranger behind Miles Ahead, Sketches of Spain, Porgy and Bess. If the answer is the trumpet soloist/leader, it's Davis; if it's the orchestrator, it's Evans.
Wynton MarsalisMarsalis is a later neo-traditional trumpeter who publicly criticized Davis's fusion turn. The fusion/electric and modal innovations belong to Davis.
Chet BakerBaker is the other famous cool, lyrical trumpeter-vocalist of the West Coast scene. Davis recorded on the East Coast for Columbia and reinvented jazz repeatedly; Baker did not.

Cross-discipline big picture Davis is the great connector of postwar music: he sits at the start of cool, modal, and fusion, and his sidemen seeded a whole generation of leaders (Coltrane, Hancock, Shorter, Corea, Zawinul). Kind of Blue's modal approach influenced rock and minimalism; Bitches Brew linked jazz to rock and funk and even Stockhausen's electronic ideas; Sketches of Spain ties jazz to Spanish classical music (Rodrigo, flamenco); and his film score for Ascenseur pour l'échafaud connects to French New Wave cinema. In quizbowl terms he bridges music, film, and 20th-century cultural history.
