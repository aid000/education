Quick Overview

📷 Image note: Le Corbusier's buildings are largely still under copyright; photos are loaded via Wikipedia's Special:FilePath redirect and require an internet connection. If any image fails to load, the file title may differ slightly.

BornCharles-Édouard Jeanneret; La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland, 1887–1965
NationalitySwiss-French; naturalized French citizen 1930
MovementInternational Style / Modernism; co-founded Purism with Ozenfant
DiedDrowned swimming at Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, August 27, 1965

The Five Points of Architecture (1926)

  • Pilotis — columns raise the building off the ground
  • Free plan — non-load-bearing walls; open interior layout
  • Free facade — exterior wall independent of structure
  • Ribbon (horizontal) windows — fenêtres en longueur
  • Roof garden — flat roof reclaims land lost by the footprint

Signature design vocabulary

  • Béton brut ("raw concrete") — exposed, board-marked concrete; gave Brutalism its name
  • Modulor — his proportional system based on the golden ratio + average human height (183 cm); symbol is the standing figure with one arm raised
  • Promenade architecturale — a designed path guiding movement through a building (ramps, not stairs)
  • Unité d'Habitation concept — self-contained vertical city: shops, gym, nursery all inside one slab block

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Pilotis + flat roof + ribbon windows — the visual give-away for Villa Savoye
  • Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut at Ronchamp — curved white roof, asymmetric towers, colored light shafts
  • The pseudonym "Le Corbusier" is taken from a maternal ancestor's name (Lecorbésier); he adopted it 1920

High-Yield Works (Very Common)

The 6 works most likely to be the answer or to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

Villa Savoye
1931 · Poissy, France · (UNESCO World Heritage Site)
recogWhite box raised on pilotis; long ribbon windows wrap the facade; flat roof with a garden terrace.
recogLocated in Poissy, near Paris; a country retreat for Pierre and Emilie Savoye, completed 1931.
powerThe most famous demonstration of all Five Points in a single building; a curved entrance drive dictated the plan's circular geometry — the ground-floor walls curve to match the turning radius of a 1920s automobile.

Chapelle Notre-Dame-du-Haut (Ronchamp)
1954 · Ronchamp, France · (UNESCO World Heritage Site)
recogSweeping curved concrete roof resembling a nun's wimple or ship's hull; white rough-cast walls; three asymmetric cylindrical towers.
recogWalls studded with irregularly placed coloured-glass windows filtering light into the dark interior.
powerBuilt atop a Burgundian hilltop pilgrimage site; the roof is a double concrete shell — hollow inside, filled with water drainage channels — inspired by a crab shell Le Corbusier picked up on a Long Island beach. Marks his break from the rationalist grid.

Unité d'Habitation, Marseille
1952 · Marseille, France · (UNESCO World Heritage Site)
recogMassive concrete slab raised on pilotis; 337 duplex apartments, shops on the 7th floor, a rooftop pool, track, and nursery.
recogThe brise-soleil (deep concrete sun-louver) grid on the facade creates a cellular, sculptural surface.
powerCalled the "Cité Radieuse" (Radiant City); dimensions scaled by the Modulor proportional system. Became the template for post-war social housing worldwide and is held as the defining work of béton brut / Brutalism.
wiki ↗
Chandigarh Capitol Complex
1952–1965 · Chandigarh, India · (UNESCO World Heritage Site)
recogThree monumental civic buildings: the Secretariat, the High Court (with brightly colored brise-soleil), and the Assembly building with its hyperbolic cooling tower.
recogCommission from Jawaharlal Nehru after partition; Chandigarh became the new capital of Punjab, India.
powerLe Corbusier designed not only the Capitol but the entire urban grid of Chandigarh — a sector-based plan with a head (Capitol), heart (City Centre), lungs (parks), and limbs (industrial sectors). The "Open Hand" monument was his symbol for the city. Named UNESCO World Heritage in 2016.
wiki ↗
Villa La Roche / Jeanneret (Fondation Le Corbusier)
1925 · Paris (16th arr.) · now Fondation Le Corbusier
recogPaired Purist villas; the La Roche half features a gallery wing cantilevered on pilotis and a famous curved interior ramp.
recogBuilt for Swiss banker Raoul La Roche to house his collection of Purist art; now the Fondation Le Corbusier headquarters.
powerFirst building to fully demonstrate the promenade architecturale — you move through it on a ramp as through a narrative sequence of spaces and paintings. An early landmark before the Five Points were formally codified.
wiki ↗
Palace of the Assembly, Chandigarh / Monastery of La Tourette
La Tourette: 1960 · Éveux, France · (UNESCO World Heritage Site)
recogDominican monastery raised on pilotis above a sloping hillside; concrete "ondulatoires" — undulating vertical mullions of irregular rhythm — fill the cloister windows.
recogCollaborating with musician Iannis Xenakis on the window-mullion rhythms, which follow a musical stochastic distribution.
powerThe chapel interior has pure "light cannons" — cylindrical light guns punched through the roof — that flood the altar with colored light, echoing Ronchamp's spiritual use of natural light. A key late work demonstrating Brutalist sacred architecture.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Architectural motifs

  • Pilotis — columns lifting building off grade
  • Brise-soleil — projecting sun-shading fins/louvers
  • Ribbon windows (fenêtres en longueur)
  • Flat / roof garden
  • Ramp instead of stair (promenade architecturale)
  • Raw / board-marked concrete (béton brut)

Key systems & texts

  • Five Points of Architecture (1926, with Pierre Jeanneret)
  • Modulor (1948; sequel 1955) — proportional man figure
  • Vers une Architecture ("Toward an Architecture," 1923)
  • The City of Tomorrow and Its Planning (1925)
  • Purism — movement with Amédée Ozenfant; after Cubism
  • CIAM — Congrès Internationaux d'Architecture Moderne, co-founded 1928

Collaborators & context

  • Pierre Jeanneret — cousin, 20-year partnership
  • Charlotte Perriand — furniture designer, joined the studio 1927
  • Iannis Xenakis — composer/architect, worked on La Tourette
  • Jawaharlal Nehru — commissioned Chandigarh after Indian partition
  • Furniture: LC2 "Grand Confort" armchair, LC4 chaise longue (with Perriand)

Biography trivia

  • Born Charles-Édouard Jeanneret; pseudonym adopted 1920
  • Apprenticed under Auguste Perret (reinforced concrete) and Peter Behrens (in Berlin, same time as Mies and Gropius)
  • Failed bid to design the League of Nations headquarters (1927) — controversy over his technical drawings
  • UNESCO named 17 of his buildings World Heritage Sites (2016)
  • Drowned at his own Cabanon, Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, 1965

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Mies van der RoheMies = steel & glass minimalism, "less is more," Barcelona Pavilion, Farnsworth House, Seagram Building. Never exposed raw concrete. Le Corbusier = béton brut, plasticity, the Five Points. Both modernists, but Mies is sleeker.
Walter GropiusGropius founded the Bauhaus in Dessau and emphasized craft-meets-industry pedagogy. Le Corbusier was never part of the Bauhaus; he ran his own Paris studio. For "Dessau building" — Gropius; for "Chandigarh" or "pilotis" — Le Corbusier.
Frank Lloyd WrightWright = organic architecture, Prairie Style, Fallingwater (cantilevers over a waterfall, Pennsylvania), Guggenheim Museum spiral ramp. Horizontal lines tie to earth. Le Corbusier's buildings are raised off the earth on pilotis and celebrate abstraction.
Alvar AaltoFinnish; known for humanist Modernism, warm wood interiors, Finlandia Hall, Paimio Sanatorium, bent-plywood furniture. Not a béton-brut architect. "Scandinavian warmth" clue = Aalto, not LC.
Oscar NiemeyerBrazilian; worked with Le Corbusier on the UN Headquarters design. Niemeyer = fluid, white sculptural curves (Brasília National Congress dome/bowl). Le Corbusier's curves (Ronchamp) are rougher and one-of-a-kind, not the repeated curvature of Niemeyer.
Auguste PerretLe Corbusier's early mentor in reinforced-concrete technique. Perret = classical proportions + exposed concrete frame; Le Corbusier extended this to the radical Five Points. For "teacher/mentor" = Perret; for "Five Points" = Le Corbusier.

🌐 Cross-discipline big picture Le Corbusier sits at the intersection of architecture, urban planning, painting, and social theory. His 1923 manifesto Vers une Architecture declared "a house is a machine for living in" — tying him to science/technology tossups. The Modulor bridges architecture and mathematics (golden ratio, Fibonacci sequence, human-scale measurement). His urban plans (Plan Voisin, Ville Radieuse) connect to 20th-century political history: they influenced postwar housing policy worldwide and are often invoked — positively and critically — in sociology tossups on urban renewal and urban theory. Chandigarh's "Open Hand" monument links to Indian independence and Nehru-era modernism.
