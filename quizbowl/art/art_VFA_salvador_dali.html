Quick Overview

📷 Image note: Dalí died in 1989, so his works are still copyrighted and live as fair-use files on English Wikipedia (not Wikimedia Commons). Images are loaded via Wikipedia's Special:FilePath redirect and need an internet connection. If any image fails to load, the file title likely differs slightly — see the STRUCTURE-NOTES.md for how to swap it.

NationalitySpanish (Catalan; born Figueres, 1904–1989)
Period20th-century modernism; active 1920s–1980s
MovementSurrealism (expelled from the group 1934)
Signature method"Paranoiac-critical method"

Defining traits

  • Hyper-realistic, academic technique applied to irrational, dream-logic subjects — "hand-painted dream photographs."
  • Double images & paranoiac-critical method — one form reads as two things at once (faces become landscapes, swans become elephants).
  • Recurring private symbols: melting/soft watches, ants, crutches, drawers in bodies, distant Catalan plains (Empordà), and his wife/muse Gala.
  • "Nuclear mysticism" in his later religious phase — Catholic subjects fused with atomic-age physics and DNA.

Classic Quizbowl giveaway clues

  • The melting/soft pocket watches of The Persistence of Memory — the single most given-away clue.
  • The flamboyant upturned mustache and the catch-all "this Surrealist."

High-Yield Works (Very Common)

The 6 works most likely to be the answer or to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

The Persistence of Memory
1931 · oil on canvas · MoMA, New York
recogMelting/soft pocket watches draped over a branch, a table edge, and a soft self-portrait "face" on the ground.
recogAnts swarm a closed orange watch; the cliffs of Cap de Creus (Catalonia) sit in the background.
powerThe soft watches were reportedly inspired by runny Camembert cheese; the limp central form is a distorted self-portrait. The 1954 sequel "disintegrates" this same scene into floating blocks.

Christ of Saint John of the Cross
1951 · oil on canvas · Kelvingrove, Glasgow
recogChrist on the cross seen from a dramatic bird's-eye angle above his head, with no nails, blood, or crown of thorns.
recogBelow the floating cross is the bay of Port Lligat with fishermen and a boat.
powerThe composition is based on a drawing by the 16th-c. mystic St. John of the Cross; the cross's geometry derives from a hyper-cube/triangle Dalí saw in a dream — a key work of his "nuclear mysticism."

Soft Construction with Boiled Beans (Premonition of Civil War)
1936 · oil on canvas · Philadelphia Museum of Art
recogA monstrous body tears itself apart — a giant hand strangles a breast while a foot crushes the ground.
recogScattered boiled beans litter the foreground beneath the writhing figure.
powerPainted as a premonition of the Spanish Civil War; Dalí said the self-strangling body is "a vast human body breaking out into monstrous excrescences." Compare to Picasso's Guernica as a Civil-War answer.

The Sacrament of the Last Supper
1955 · oil on canvas · National Gallery of Art, Washington
recogChrist and 12 disciples at a table inside a glowing dodecahedron (12-sided figure); a giant translucent torso floats above.
recogThe setting is again the bay of Port Lligat; the disciples bow with hands clasped.
powerBuilt on "divine geometry" / the golden ratio — another "nuclear mysticism" work fusing Catholicism with mathematics.

Galatea of the Spheres
1952 · oil on canvas · Dalí Theatre-Museum, Figueres
recogA portrait of Gala made entirely of floating spheres suspended in space.
recogThe spheres evoke atomic particles — the face is fragmented yet coherent.
powerTitle alludes to Galatea (Pygmalion's statue / Raphael's nymph); a hallmark of Dalí's atomic period, painting matter as discontinuous particles.
wiki ↗
Swans Reflecting Elephants
1937 · oil on canvas · private collection
recogThree swans on a lake whose reflections become elephants — the classic Dalí double image.
recogBare autumn trees mirror as the elephants' bodies; a small figure (Dalí) stands at left.
powerA textbook demonstration of the paranoiac-critical method; the inverted-reflection trick is the give-here clue for "double image" tossups.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Visual motifs

  • Melting / soft objects (watches, faces, "soft construction")
  • Ants (decay) and a single locust/grasshopper
  • Crutches propping up soft forms
  • Elephants on long spider legs
  • Open drawers in bodies (Freudian)
  • Eggs, bread/loaves, pomegranates

Subjects & symbols

  • Gala — wife & recurring model (Galatea, Leda, Madonna)
  • Catalan coast: Port Lligat, Cap de Creus, Empordà plain
  • Religious nuclear mysticism (Christ, Last Supper, Madonna)
  • Atomic particles / DNA / rhinoceros horns (late period)

Techniques & terms

  • Paranoiac-critical method (his term)
  • Double images / hidden second readings
  • "Hand-painted dream photographs"
  • Academic, hyper-realistic finish
  • Influenced by Freud & Vermeer/Meissonier

"Also did" trivia

  • Films with Luis Buñuel: Un Chien Andalou (sliced eyeball), L'Âge d'Or
  • Dream sequence for Hitchcock's Spellbound
  • Designed the Chupa Chups logo
  • The Dalí Theatre-Museum in Figueres (his tomb)
  • Memoir: The Secret Life of Salvador Dalí

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
René MagritteCleaner, flat, deadpan realism: bowler-hatted men, pipe ("Ceci n'est pas une pipe"), green apple, clouds. No melting, no virtuoso shading. Magritte = wit & paradox; Dalí = lurid, hyper-detailed delirium.
Max ErnstFrottage & collage textures, bird-creature "Loplop," forests. Rougher, more experimental surface than Dalí's slick finish.
Yves TanguyAbstract biomorphic blobs on empty plains — no recognizable objects or double images.
Joan MiróFellow Catalan but abstract, playful, primary-color biomorphs — nothing photographic.
Giorgio de ChiricoPre-Surrealist (Metaphysical): empty Italian plazas, long shadows, mannequins, arcades. An influence on Dalí, not a melting-clock painter.
Picasso (Guernica)For "Spanish Civil War painting," Dalí's answer is Soft Construction with Boiled Beans; Picasso's is Guernica. Both Spanish — read the visual clue.

⚛️ Physics / math link Dalí's late "nuclear mysticism" deliberately borrowed from science: Galatea of the Spheres renders matter as discrete particles, Crucifixion (Corpus Hypercubus) uses the unfolded net of a tesseract (4-D hypercube), and The Sacrament of the Last Supper is framed by a dodecahedron and golden-ratio geometry — a clean bridge from art tossups to math/physics ones.
