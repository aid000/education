Quick Overview

📷 Image note: Haydn died in 1809; his portrait paintings are in the public domain. Images are loaded via Wikipedia's Special:FilePath redirect and need an internet connection. If any image fails to load, the file title likely differs slightly.

NationalityAustrian (born Rohrau, Lower Austria, 1732–1809)
PeriodClassical era (c. 1750–1820)
PatronEsterházy family (Princes Paul Anton & Nikolaus I) for ~30 years
Epithets"Father of the Symphony," "Father of the String Quartet"
Output106 symphonies, 68 string quartets, 14 masses, 3 oratorios, 62 piano sonatas
Late-career triumphTwo London visits (1791–92, 1794–95) — "London symphonies" composed for Salomon

Defining traits

  • "Surprise" / dramatic humor: sudden loud chords, false endings, unexpected pauses mid-phrase — Haydn's "jokes."
  • Monothematic sonata form: develops a single theme rather than contrasting two — his structural innovation.
  • Slow introduction before a fast Allegro (especially in the London symphonies).
  • Programmatic nicknames for many symphonies — nearly all were assigned after composition, not by Haydn.
  • Long Esterháza court isolation forced originality: "I was cut off from the world… I had to be original."

Classic Quizbowl giveaway clues

  • The nickname "Surprise" (Symphony No. 94) — the fortissimo chord in the slow movement.
  • The Esterházy patron family — nearly unique to Haydn.
  • "Father of the Symphony" or "Father of the String Quartet."
  • The "Farewell" Symphony — musicians leave the stage one by one.

High-Yield Works (Very Common)

The 6 works most likely to be the answer or to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

Symphony No. 94 in G major ("Surprise")
1791 · orchestral · composed for London / Salomon concerts
recogThe slow (Andante) second movement begins softly then erupts with a single fortissimo chord to startle drowsing audience members.
recogPart of the 12 "London" (Salomon) symphonies; written for impresario Johann Peter Salomon.
powerHaydn reportedly said he wanted "the women to scream"; the theme with the surprise chord then becomes a set of four variations. One of the first Classical-era works composed deliberately for a paying public concert audience rather than courtly patronage.

Symphony No. 45 in F-sharp minor ("Farewell")
1772 · orchestral · Esterháza court
recogFinal movement: players stop and leave the stage one by one, extinguishing their candles, until two muted violins finish alone.
recogA programmatic hint to Prince Nikolaus that musicians wanted to go home to their families.
powerWritten in the unusual key of F-sharp minor — rare in the 18th century. The four-movement symphony adds a slow fifth section for the theatrical departure. The prince received the message: the court left Esterháza shortly after the premiere.

The Creation (Die Schöpfung)
1798 · oratorio · premiered Vienna, Schwarzenberg Palace
recogSetting of Genesis and Psalms via an English libretto (van Swieten's German adaptation of Lidley/Milton) for soprano, tenor, bass soloists, chorus, and orchestra.
recogOpens with the famous "Representation of Chaos" overture — dissonant, chromatic, tonally unmoored.
powerThe sudden fortissimo C-major chord at "And there was light!" ("und es ward Licht!") is one of Classical music's most famous dramatic moments. Haydn was inspired to write it after hearing Handel's oratorios in London. Three vocal soloists are Gabriel (soprano), Uriel (tenor), Raphael (bass).
wiki ↗
String Quartet in C major, Op. 76 No. 3 ("Emperor")
1797 · string quartet · dedicated to Count Erdődy
recogThe slow second movement is a set of variations on "Gott erhalte Franz den Kaiser" — the imperial Austrian anthem Haydn himself composed.
recogThe hymn tune later became the German national anthem ("Deutschlandlied") and is still used today.
powerHaydn wrote "Gott erhalte" in 1797 as a rival to "God Save the King" after hearing it in London. He reportedly played the quartet's slow movement on his deathbed, May 1809, days before Napoleon's bombardment of Vienna. Part of the Op. 76 "Erdődy" quartets.
wiki ↗
Symphony No. 104 in D major ("London")
1795 · orchestral · last of the 12 London symphonies
recogHaydn's final symphony; the last of the 12 Salomon/London symphonies. Subtitled "London" specifically.
recogOpens with a slow, dotted-rhythm D-minor introduction that yields a brisk D-major Allegro — the archetypal London-symphony opening.
powerThe finale's main theme is thought to derive from a London street-vendor cry. Often appears in questions about "Haydn's last symphony" or the "culmination of Classical symphonic form."
wiki ↗
The Seasons (Die Jahreszeiten)
1801 · oratorio · premiered Vienna, Schwarzenberg Palace
recogSecular oratorio in four parts (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter) after James Thomson's poem "The Seasons", libretto by van Swieten.
recogHaydn's second major oratorio; regarded as a companion piece to The Creation.
powerHaydn reportedly disliked van Swieten's German libretto — especially the croaking frogs in "Summer" and the word-painting of "industry" (Fleiss). He called the oratorio "the last child of an old man." The Autumn section includes a hunting chorus with horn calls, one of Classical music's most quoted rural scenes.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Patrons & employers

  • Esterházy family — Princes Paul Anton & Nikolaus I ("the Magnificent"); ~30 years at court
  • Palace of Esterháza (Fertőd, Hungary) — his remote "Versailles" workplace
  • Johann Peter Salomon — London impresario who brought Haydn to England twice
  • Baron Gottfried van Swieten — supplied librettos for Creation and Seasons

Biographical hard clues

  • Born Rohrau, Lower Austria, 1732; father a wheelwright
  • Chorister at St. Stephen's Cathedral, Vienna; sacked at voice-break c. 1749
  • Studied briefly under Nicola Porpora
  • Married Maria Anna Aloysia Keller 1760 — unhappy marriage; no children
  • Close friend of Mozart (mutual admiration; Haydn dedicated Op. 33 to him); teacher of Beethoven (unsatisfactory lessons, 1792–94)
  • Died Vienna, 31 May 1809, weeks after Napoleon's bombardment; famously calm on his deathbed

Style markers & techniques

  • Monothematic sonata form — single main theme developed throughout
  • "Haydn's joke" — false endings, silences, sudden dynamics
  • Sturm und Drang period (~1768–72) — minor keys, dramatic intensity
  • Slow introduction before allegro (London symphonies)
  • Minuet and trio as standard third movement
  • Pioneered the 4-movement symphonic cycle as the Classical norm

Numbers & output trivia

  • 106 symphonies (Nos. 1–104 + 107 & 108 now renumbered)
  • 68 string quartets — more than any other Classical composer
  • 12 "London" (Salomon) symphonies — Nos. 93–104
  • 14 masses, including the six late "Esterházy" masses (1796–1802)
  • Estimated 340+ works for baryton (bowed string instrument) for Prince Nikolaus
  • Honorary doctorates from Oxford University (1791) and Paris

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
W. A. MozartMozart's symphonies peak at No. 41 ("Jupiter"); he has 27 piano concertos. Haydn = 106 symphonies, Esterházy patron, "Father of." They were close friends — if the question mentions "dearest friend" between them, it could be either; look for the Esterházy anchor for Haydn.
Ludwig van BeethovenBeethoven studied under Haydn (unsatisfactory lessons). Beethoven's 9 symphonies go up to Op. 125 with a choral finale; Haydn's are courtly, lighter, pre-Romantic. Any "Ode to Joy" / minor-to-major arc = Beethoven.
C. P. E. BachEmpfindsamer Stil (sensitive style), keyboard sonatas, "pre-Classical" — a major influence on Haydn. C. P. E. worked for Frederick the Great in Berlin, then Hamburg; not Esterházy.
Antonio SalieriCourt composer in Vienna, Mozart's rival in legend. No Esterházy connection; primarily opera and sacred music. Haydn's oratorios vs. Salieri's operas: look for "symphony" or "Esterháza" to anchor Haydn.
Johann Stamitz / Mannheim schoolMannheim = the famous "Mannheim rocket" (ascending arpeggio) and dynamic crescendo. Haydn's humor and monothematicism are quite different; Mannheim composers predate the London symphonies by decades.
George Frideric HandelHandel's oratorios (Messiah, Israel in Egypt) were Haydn's explicit inspiration for The Creation. "And there was Light" moment = Haydn's Creation; "Hallelujah" = Handel's Messiah.

Cross-discipline big picture Haydn's career spans the birth of the Classical style (breaking from Baroque counterpoint), the Sturm und Drang literary-aesthetic movement (minor-key emotional intensity, c. 1768–72), and the Vienna-to-London cultural circuit that made Classical music an international commercial enterprise. His invention of the modern string quartet and codification of the 4-movement symphony cycle made him the structural template for both Mozart and Beethoven — questions on either often circle back to Haydn as the "before." The Emperor quartet's slow-movement hymn tune crosses into political history (Austrian and German national anthems), making Haydn one of the few composers whose work appears in non-music tossups.
