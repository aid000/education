Quick Overview

📷 Image note: Van Gogh died in 1890, so his works are in the public domain. Images load via Wikipedia's Special:FilePath redirect and require an internet connection. If any image fails to load, the filename likely differs slightly on Wikimedia Commons.

NationalityDutch (born Zundert, Netherlands, 1853–1890)
PeriodPost-Impressionism; most active 1881–1890 (only ~9 years)
MovementPost-Impressionism (bridge to Expressionism & Fauvism)
Key phasesDark Dutch period (1880–85) → Paris (1886–88) → Arles (1888–89) → Saint-Rémy / Auvers (1889–90)
Signature techniqueThick impasto, swirling brushwork; short, energetic strokes; bold non-naturalistic color
DeathDied Auvers-sur-Oise, 1890; gunshot wound (self-inflicted or accidental)

Defining traits

  • Impasto and visible brushwork — paint laid on thick, strokes directional and expressive.
  • Swirling, dynamic skies — stars become spirals, cypress trees writhe upward.
  • Saturated, non-naturalistic color — influenced by Japanese woodblock prints and Delacroix's color theory.
  • Cypresses and sunflowers as signature motifs; wheat fields, peasants, night scenes.
  • Nearly all major work done in the last 10 years of life; sold only one painting (The Red Vineyard) during his lifetime.
  • Brother Théo van Gogh (art dealer) — funded Vincent, preserved the letters; their correspondence is a primary source.

Classic Quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Swirling/spiraling night sky and The Starry Night — the single most given-away van Gogh clue.
  • Cut off his own ear (December 1888, Arles) — biographical giveaway.
  • "Yellow House" in Arles, collaboration with Paul Gauguin that ended violently.

High-Yield Works (Very Common)

The 6 works most likely to be the answer or to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

The Starry Night
1889 · oil on canvas · MoMA, New York
powerPainted from memory in the Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum (Saint-Rémy-de-Provence); the small village below is not Arles but invented, with a Dutch-style church steeple.
recogMassive swirling, spiraling sky with 11 ovoid stars and a crescent moon; a dominant dark cypress tree surges up at left.
recogThe most reproduced work in Western art; housed at MoMA. The song "Vincent" by Don McLean is named for it.

Sunflowers (the Arles series)
1888 · oil on canvas · National Gallery, London (F454) / Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam (F456)
powerPart of a series intended to decorate Gauguin's room in the Yellow House; van Gogh painted at least five versions of cut sunflowers in a vase.
recogVase of sunflowers in varying stages of bloom — from full yellow to brown, withering heads.
recogPainted on a yellow ground; yellow-on-yellow palette is a signature clue. Two primary versions: National Gallery London and Van Gogh Museum Amsterdam.

The Bedroom (Bedroom in Arles)
1888 (first version) · oil on canvas · Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam
powerVan Gogh painted three versions (1888, 1889 ×2) — the second sent to his mother; intentional perspective distortion to convey "absolute repose."
recogSimple wooden bed, two chairs, small portraits on the wall, basin and pitcher; bold flat colors with almost no shadows.
recogVan Gogh wrote to Théo that the colors were meant to evoke "rest or sleep" — the perspective tilts floors and walls unnaturally.
wiki ↗
Café Terrace at Night
1888 · oil on canvas · Kröller-Müller Museum, Otterlo
powerOne of van Gogh's first night paintings; no black used in the night sky — shadows rendered in blue and violet. Located at Place du Forum, Arles.
recogBright yellow café awning blazing against a deep blue cobblestone street and starry night sky; gas lamps illuminate outdoor tables.
recogHoused at Kröller-Müller Museum in Otterlo, Netherlands. Sometimes confused with The Night Café (interior, green/red), which is at Yale.
wiki ↗
Wheat Field with Crows
1890 · oil on canvas · Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam
powerPainted in Auvers-sur-Oise in the final weeks before his death; the turbulent sky and three diverging paths (one leading nowhere) long read as suicidal despair, though scholars debate this.
recogDark crows rising from a yellow wheat field against a deep blue and black roiling sky — one of the most dramatic van Gogh landscapes.
recogWide horizontal format; triple-path composition. Often cited as his last painting (contested: Tree Roots also a candidate).
wiki ↗
The Potato Eaters
1885 · oil on canvas · Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam
powerVan Gogh considered this his first serious masterpiece; the deliberately dark, earthy palette echoes the color of potatoes — showing peasants "have tilled the earth with the very hands they are putting in the dish."
recogFive gaunt Dutch peasants eating potatoes by lamplight; the lamp casts a sickly yellow-green glow over coarse, rough-hewn faces.
recogFrom his dark Nuenen period (Netherlands); marks his only major pre-Paris work. Influenced by Millet and Rembrandt.

Recognition / Clue Bank

Visual motifs

  • Swirling, spiraling skies — stars become vortices
  • Cypress trees — upward-surging, dark, flame-like
  • Sunflowers — yellow-on-yellow palette
  • Wheat fields under turbulent skies
  • Thick, directional impasto — textured brushstrokes visible
  • Night scenes without black (blue/violet shadows)

Biographical clues

  • Born Zundert, Netherlands, 1853
  • Worked as art dealer for Goupil & Cie (London, Paris)
  • Brief theology studies; evangelism among Belgian miners (Borinage)
  • Cut off (part of) his left ear, Dec. 1888, Arles — gave it to a woman named Rachel
  • Voluntarily entered Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum, Saint-Rémy, 1889
  • Died Auvers-sur-Oise, July 29, 1890, under care of Dr. Paul Gachet
  • Letters to brother Théo — primary source for his thought

Influences & relationships

  • Influenced by Jean-François Millet (peasant subjects)
  • Japanese ukiyo-e woodblock prints (Hiroshige, Hokusai) — flat color, bold outlines
  • Paul Gauguin — shared the Yellow House in Arles (Oct.–Dec. 1888); falling out preceded ear incident
  • Georges Seurat / Pointillism — influenced Paris palette shift
  • Influenced later Expressionism and Fauvism (Matisse, Kirchner)

Key locations & museums

  • Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam — largest collection
  • MoMA, New York — The Starry Night
  • Kröller-Müller Museum, Otterlo — Café Terrace, The Sower
  • Musée d'Orsay, Paris — Rhône Starry Night, many more
  • Arles — Yellow House period (1888)
  • Auvers-sur-Oise — final 70 days; buried there with Théo

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Paul GauguinGauguin: flat, decorative surfaces, Tahitian subjects, bold black outlines, non-Western figures. Van Gogh: impasto texture, swirling motion, European/peasant subjects. Both Post-Impressionists; they shared the Yellow House but are visually opposite.
Paul CézanneCézanne: geometric, analytical — building form with color planes (proto-Cubism); Mont Sainte-Victoire series. Van Gogh: emotional, kinetic — brushstrokes convey feeling, not form construction.
Georges SeuratSeurat: pointillism — tiny uniform dots of pure color (A Sunday on La Grande Jatte). Van Gogh: thick, directional strokes, not dots. Seurat = cool scientific method; van Gogh = hot emotional expression.
Edvard MunchMunch: Norwegian Expressionist; wavy lines convey existential anxiety (The Scream). Van Gogh died before Munch's major works; Munch is an heir, not a contemporary rival. If the answer is "the Scream," it's Munch.
The Night Café vs. Café Terrace at NightNight Café (1888) = billiard-table interior, clashing red & green, Yale. Café Terrace at Night = outdoor awning, deep blue street, Kröller-Müller. Same city (Arles), same year — very common tossup confusion.
Starry Night vs. Starry Night Over the RhôneThe Starry Night (MoMA) = swirling sky, village, cypress; from asylum. Starry Night Over the Rhône (Musée d'Orsay) = calmer, river reflections, city lights, couple; painted from the riverbank in Arles months earlier.

Cross-discipline big picture Van Gogh bridges multiple quizbowl categories: his Letters to Théo are a primary literary source (hundreds survive, published as a collection); the ear incident and Gauguin relationship anchor tossups on Gauguin, friendship, and 19th-century French culture; and his influence on Expressionism (Kirchner, Nolde) and Fauvism (Matisse's color liberation) make him the causal link in any "influence" tossup spanning Post-Impressionism to early 20th-century movements. His work also inspired Don McLean's "Vincent" (1971), a recurring music/pop-culture bridge clue.
