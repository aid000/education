Quick Overview

📷 Image note: Picasso died in 1973; his works are still in copyright and appear as fair-use files on English Wikipedia (not Wikimedia Commons). Images load via Wikipedia's Special:FilePath redirect and require an internet connection. If an image fails to load, the filename likely differs slightly.

NationalitySpanish (born Málaga, 1881–1973)
Active~1895–1973; one of the longest careers in modern art
Key movementCubism (co-founder with Georges Braque)
Notable periodsBlue Period, Rose Period, Analytic & Synthetic Cubism, Neoclassicism, Surrealism
MediaOil painting, prints, sculpture, ceramics, collage
Key musesFernande Olivier, Olga Khokhlova, Marie-Thérèse Walter, Dora Maar, Françoise Gilot

Defining traits

  • Cubism: simultaneous multiple viewpoints, fractured geometric planes, abolition of single-point perspective.
  • Blue Period (1901–1904): monochromatic blue-green palette, melancholy subjects — beggars, prisoners, the blind.
  • Rose Period (1904–1906): warmer pinks/reds, circus performers (saltimbanques), harlequins.
  • Primitivism: influenced by African masks and Iberian sculpture; visible in angular, mask-like faces.
  • Collage: pioneered papier collé with Braque — pasting newspaper, wallpaper, etc. onto canvas.

Classic quizbowl giveaway clues

  • Guernica — the single most commonly clued Picasso work; grey/black/white, screaming horse, Spanish Civil War.
  • Les Demoiselles d'Avignon — five angular nude women, African-mask faces on the right two.
  • The word "Cubism" paired with a Spanish artist almost always points to Picasso or Braque.

High-Yield Works (Very Common)

The 6 works most likely to be the answer or to anchor a tossup. power = hard, early, buzz-here clue. recog = recognition / middle-clue.

Guernica
1937 · oil on canvas · Museo Reina Sofía, Madrid
recogMonochromatic grey, black, and white; a screaming horse at center, a bull at upper left, and a wailing woman holding a dead child.
recogPainted in response to the Nazi bombing of the Basque town of Guernica on April 26, 1937, during the Spanish Civil War.
powerCommissioned for the Spanish Pavilion at the 1937 Paris International Exposition; Picasso refused to let it return to Spain until democracy was restored — it lived at MoMA until 1981, then moved to the Reina Sofía. A screaming electric light bulb hangs at the top center like a harsh interrogation lamp.

Les Demoiselles d'Avignon
1907 · oil on canvas · MoMA, New York
recogFive angular nude women stare frontally; the two on the right have African-mask faces, the others Iberian-sculptural.
recogConsidered a proto-Cubist proto-painting; it shattered Renaissance single-viewpoint conventions.
powerOriginally titled Le Bordel d'Avignon — the "Avignon" refers to Carrer d'Avinyó, a street in the red-light district of Barcelona. Cézanne's Large Bathers and Iberian sculpture are the key influences. A bowl of fruit sits at bottom center.
wiki ↗
Girl Before a Mirror
1932 · oil on canvas · MoMA, New York
recogA young woman in a bold patterned dress gazes into an oval mirror; her reflection is darker, more sinister.
recogThe model is Marie-Thérèse Walter, Picasso's mistress, depicted with his characteristic split-face simultaneity.
powerThe woman's face shows a profile and frontal view simultaneously — a signature Picasso device; the wallpaper-like diamond pattern and intense color mark the 1930s Synthetic Cubist style. Art historians read the dark reflection as the subject's psychological "dark side."
wiki ↗
Weeping Woman
1937 · oil on canvas · Tate Modern, London
recogA woman in vivid color clutches a handkerchief to her face, her features disjointed and angular, tears streaming in shards.
recogThe model is Dora Maar, Picasso's companion during the Civil War period; painted in the same year as Guernica.
powerPart of a series of weeping-woman works derived from the mourning mother figure in Guernica; the jagged, fragmented face is the most extreme expression of Picasso's emotional Cubism. The hat and handkerchief are rendered in jewel-like blues, yellows, and greens against the anguished face.
wiki ↗
La Vie (The Life)
1903 · oil on canvas · Cleveland Museum of Art
recogA nude couple at left (man in blue loincloth, woman embracing him) face a robed woman holding an infant at right — dominant blue-grey palette.
recogThe male figure bears the face of Picasso's friend Carlos Casagemas, who committed suicide in 1901 — the direct trigger for the Blue Period.
powerX-ray studies show Picasso originally painted himself in Casagemas's place. Two small paintings-within-the-painting on the back wall — crouching melancholy figures — echo Blue Period themes. A pivotal Blue Period capstone.
wiki ↗
Three Musicians
1921 · oil on canvas · MoMA, New York (one version; Philadelphia Museum of Art holds the other)
recogThree masked figures — Harlequin, Pierrot, and a monk — rendered as flat interlocking planes of color playing instruments.
recogA pinnacle of Synthetic Cubism; the flat, puzzle-piece shapes mimic the papier collé technique in oil paint.
powerPicasso painted two nearly identical versions simultaneously in Fontainebleau, summer 1921. The MoMA version has a hidden dog beneath the table; the Harlequin is thought to represent Picasso himself, the Pierrot Guillaume Apollinaire, and the monk Max Jacob.
wiki ↗

Recognition / Clue Bank

Style & technique markers

  • Simultaneous viewpoints — face shows profile + front at once
  • Fractured/faceted geometric planes
  • Papier collé / collage — newspaper clippings, wallpaper, oilcloth
  • Analytic Cubism: monochrome earth tones, barely legible forms
  • Synthetic Cubism: flat colored shapes, decorative patterns
  • Blue monochrome + melancholy figures (Blue Period)
  • Pinks/reds + circus performers (Rose Period)

Recurring subjects & motifs

  • Harlequin / Pierrot (Commedia dell'arte)
  • Minotaur — in prints and the Minotauromachy etching
  • Bullfighting imagery (Spanish identity)
  • Guitar / still life with instruments
  • Women with distorted/fragmented faces — his muses
  • Weeping / mourning women

Key people & places

  • Georges Braque — Cubism co-founder; virtually inseparable 1908–14
  • Guillaume Apollinaire — poet friend; depicted as Pierrot
  • Gertrude Stein — patron & collector, sat 90 times for portrait
  • Dora Maar — photographer/muse; the "Weeping Woman"
  • Marie-Thérèse Walter — secret mistress, Rose/oval-faced works
  • Fernande Olivier — first major companion, Blue/Rose periods
  • Barcelona (formative); Montmartre, Paris (Bateau-Lavoir studio)
  • Vallauris — where he made ceramics, 1948–55

Biographical hard clues

  • Full birth name: Pablo Diego José Francisco de Paula Juan Nepomuceno María de los Remedios Cipriano de la Santísima Trinidad Ruiz y Picasso
  • Born Málaga, Spain, 1881; died Mougins, France, 1973
  • Father: José Ruiz Blasco, also a painter and drawing teacher
  • Communist Party member from 1944; awarded Stalin Peace Prize
  • Joined Communist Party partly due to Paul Éluard's influence
  • The Musée Picasso in Paris (Hôtel Salé) holds the largest collection
  • Co-invented papier collé with Braque (~1912)

Comparison Traps

Don't confuse withHow to tell them apart
Georges BraqueBraque co-invented Cubism but his works are quieter, more harmonious, still-life focused (musical instruments, fruit). No political works. If the question mentions Spain, the Civil War, or large figurative ambition — lean Picasso.
Juan GrisThird major Cubist — works are highly geometric, decorative, and colorful; mostly still lifes. No political content, no Blue Period.
Fernand LégerTubular, mechanistic figures (Tubism); celebrates modern industry and workers. Very different emotional register from Picasso.
Salvador Dalí (Guernica trap)For "Spanish Civil War painting": Dalí's answer is Soft Construction with Boiled Beans; Picasso's is Guernica. Both Spanish — always read the visual description.
Henri MatisseFlat bright color, arabesques, pattern-on-pattern — no fractured geometry. Matisse & Picasso were great rivals and friends; if the clue says "Fauvism" or "color as pure sensation," it's Matisse.
Diego RiveraMexican muralist who also knew Picasso; large-scale political murals in Mexico, not easel paintings. Clue says "mural" or "Mexico" → Rivera.

🌐 Big-picture cross-discipline connections
  • Spanish Civil War: Guernica directly links to the Bombing of Guernica (April 1937, Nazi Condor Legion), Francisco Franco, the Basque Country, and — as a comparison trap — Dalí's Soft Construction with Boiled Beans.
  • African art & primitivism: Les Demoiselles d'Avignon links to Picasso's visit to the Trocadéro ethnographic museum in 1907; connects to broader colonial-era questions about the appropriation of African art by European modernists.
  • Literature: Picasso and Gertrude Stein are a recurring cross-category link; Stein's 1938 monograph Picasso is a quizbowl literature answer. Guillaume Apollinaire was both Picasso's friend and the coiner of the term "Surrealism."
  • Music: Picasso designed costumes and sets for Diaghilev's Ballets Russes (Parade, 1917, with music by Satie; The Three-Cornered Hat, 1919, Falla). This opens Ballet Russes / Stravinsky / Satie tossups.
