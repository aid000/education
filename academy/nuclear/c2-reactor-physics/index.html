🛠️ Hands-On Project

A criticality (k-eff) calculation in OpenMC (Python)

Build a small reactor-physics model and compute its effective multiplication factor. Define a fuel-and-moderator geometry and materials, run an eigenvalue (k-eff) calculation in OpenMC, and study how k changes as you vary enrichment, moderator ratio, or core size. You deliver a short report comparing your simulation's k-eff and flux shape against analytic estimates worked by hand (one-group diffusion, buckling, the four-/six-factor formula). Carry over your Nuclear C1 Python + OpenMC environment (a quick re-check is included in Lecture 1).

Wk 1: confirm Python + OpenMC + NumPy/Matplotlib environment Wk 4: analytic critical-size / buckling estimate Wk 8: OpenMC k-eff run + flux tallies Wk 10: report + figures submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: Diffusion theory replaces the full transport equation with a single, much simpler PDE. What does that approximation throw away, and where would you expect it to break down inside a real core?
  • Week 6: A good moderator slows neutrons in as few collisions as possible without absorbing them. Why does that pull material choice in two competing directions, and how do real reactors resolve the tension?
  • Week 9: Delayed neutrons are a tiny fraction of all neutrons, yet they make reactors controllable. How can something so small dominate the time behavior, and what does a negative temperature coefficient add on top?
