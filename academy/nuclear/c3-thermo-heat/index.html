🛠️ Hands-On Project

A 1-D core thermal-hydraulics model (axial temperature profiles + DNBR) in Python

Build a 1-D thermal-hydraulics model of a single reactor coolant channel: prescribe an axial heat-flux shape, march the coolant energy balance up the channel, and compute the fuel-centerline, clad, and coolant temperature profiles along with the departure-from-nucleate-boiling ratio (DNBR). You deliver a short report with axial temperature and DNBR plots, comparing your numerical results against the hand-derived analytic estimates from Weeks 4–8. All work uses Python + NumPy + SciPy + Matplotlib (Octave optional) — all free.

Wk 4: heat-source / linear-heat-rate model Wk 8: coupled conduction + convection + DNBR Wk 10: report + axial profile & DNBR figures submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: The Carnot limit caps every thermal cycle, yet real Rankine plants run far below it. Where do the largest losses actually come from, and which are worth engineering away?
  • Week 6: The same coolant carries heat away by conduction, convection, and (later) boiling. Why does the convective heat-transfer coefficient dominate the fuel-to-coolant temperature drop, and what raises it?
  • Week 9: Even after shutdown a core keeps making decay heat. Why is passive natural circulation considered a safety feature, and what are its limits?
