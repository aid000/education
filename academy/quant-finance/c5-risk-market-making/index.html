🛠️ Hands-On Project

An Order-Book / Market-Making Simulator with Inventory & VaR (Python)

Build a small, reusable simulator: model a limit order book and incoming order flow, post bid/ask quotes that skew with your inventory, and track your PnL, inventory, and risk in real time. You'll measure the desk's downside with VaR and expected shortfall and tune your quoting strategy against adverse selection. You deliver a short report with plots and analysis. The same NumPy / SciPy / CVXPY / Matplotlib stack is used throughout — all free and pip-installable.

Wk 4: Kelly-style bet sizer + loss-distribution VaR check Wk 8: order-book + quoting engine with inventory tracking Wk 10: full simulator + report & plots submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: VaR can say "you'll lose at most $1M, 99% of the time" yet tell you nothing about the other 1%. Why does expected shortfall fix this, and when does VaR's lack of subadditivity actually bite?
  • Week 6: Mean-variance optimization and risk parity can recommend very different portfolios from the same data. When would you trust risk parity over the efficient frontier, and why?
  • Week 9: A market maker quoting a tighter spread captures more flow but takes on more adverse selection. How should inventory and risk-aversion shift your quotes, and what does the Avellaneda–Stoikov reservation price capture?
