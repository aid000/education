🛠️ Hands-On Project

A Monte-Carlo Engine for Random Walks & Brownian Paths (Python)

Build a small, reusable simulation library: generate random walks and Brownian-motion paths, estimate expectations and variances by Monte Carlo, and watch the law of large numbers and the diffusive scaling emerge from your own code. You deliver a short report with plots and analysis. Week 1 includes a one-time Python + NumPy + Jupyter install (step-by-step in Lecture 1) — the same stack you'll use all course.

Wk 1: install Python + NumPy + Jupyter Wk 4: random-walk simulator + LLN check Wk 8: Brownian-path generator Wk 10: report + plots submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: Bayes' rule overturns a lot of "obvious" intuition. Share a real case where conditioning on new information flips the answer.
  • Week 6: A martingale is a "fair game". Why does the optional-stopping theorem imply you can't beat one with a clever stopping rule?
  • Week 9: Ordinary calculus assumes smooth paths; Brownian motion is nowhere differentiable. What breaks, and why do we need a new (Itô) calculus?
