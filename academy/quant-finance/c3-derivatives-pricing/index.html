🛠️ Hands-On Project

Price & Delta-Hedge an Option Three Ways (Python)

Build a small, reusable pricing library that values the same European option three independent ways — the Black-Scholes closed-form, a binomial tree, and a Monte-Carlo simulation — then confirm they agree as you refine each. You also run a delta-hedging experiment: replicate the option with stock and cash, rebalance over time, and measure the replication error. You deliver a short report with plots and a side-by-side comparison of the three methods. The same NumPy + SciPy + Matplotlib stack carries the whole course.

Wk 4: binomial-tree pricer + backward induction Wk 8: closed-form pricer + delta-hedge simulation Wk 10: Monte-Carlo pricer + 3-way report submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: Risk-neutral pricing replaces the "real" probability of an up-move with a synthetic one. Why does an option's price not depend on how likely the stock is to actually rise?
  • Week 6: Black-Scholes assumes constant volatility, yet markets show a volatility smile. Share a reason real option prices deviate from the textbook formula.
  • Week 9: Closed-form, tree, and Monte-Carlo can all price the same option. When would you reach for each, and what does Monte-Carlo buy you that the others can't?
