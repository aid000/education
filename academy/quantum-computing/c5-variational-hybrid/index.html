🛠️ Hands-On Project

QAOA Max-Cut Solver (worked track) or VQE Ground-State Energy (DIY track)

Choose one track: use QAOA to approximately solve a max-cut problem on a small graph — Lecture 10's capstone walks through this track as a complete, fully-worked script you can follow end to end — or use VQE to estimate the ground-state energy of a toy Hamiltonian, building on Lectures 3–4's VQE theory and toy 1-qubit worked example (extending that pattern to a real molecule like H₂ is a DIY extension beyond what the lectures build for you). Either way you build a parameterized circuit, wire it to a classical optimizer, and run the hybrid loop to convergence — then deliver a short report with code, the optimization trajectory, and the final result. Week 1 includes a one-time Python + PennyLane + SciPy install (step-by-step in Lecture 1) — the same toolkit you'll use all course.

Wk 1: install PennyLane + SciPy Wk 4: VQE/QAOA ansatz + cost function working Wk 8: full optimization loop converging Wk 10: report + plots submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: VQE is sold as a near-term, noise-tolerant alternative to full quantum phase estimation. What exactly is it trading away for that tolerance?
  • Week 6: QAOA's mixer and cost layers alternate at increasing depth p. Why doesn't "just increase p" straightforwardly make QAOA better on real NISQ hardware?
  • Week 9: Variational methods are the most hyped near-term application of quantum computers. Based on what you now know about barren plateaus, noise, and classical competition, what's a fair and honest claim to make about them today?
