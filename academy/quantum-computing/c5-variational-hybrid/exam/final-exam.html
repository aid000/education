Hybrid Loops & Parameterized Circuits · Weeks 1–2
1. What makes an algorithm "hybrid" in the sense used throughout this course?
Answer: B. The classical optimizer being iteratively inside the loop, deciding each next circuit run from the last measurement, is exactly what makes these algorithms hybrid (Lecture 1).
2. Why do hybrid algorithms specifically suit NISQ hardware?
Answer: B. Shallow circuits repeated many times, rather than one long fragile computation, is the NISQ-era bargain Lecture 1 built the whole course on.
3. An "ansatz" is best described as…
Answer: C. Lecture 2 defines an ansatz exactly this way — rotation gates plus entangling layers, parameterized for the hybrid loop to search over.
4. A more expressible ansatz…
Answer: B. Lecture 2's expressibility-vs-depth trade-off, later sharpened by Lecture 5's barren plateaus, is exactly this cost.
VQE Fundamentals · Weeks 3–4
5. The variational principle states that for any normalized trial state |ψ⟩…
Answer: B. Expanding |ψ⟩ in H's eigenbasis shows ⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩ is a weighted average of eigenvalues all ≥ E₀ — the bedrock VQE stands on (Lecture 3).
6. After second quantization and a qubit mapping, a molecular Hamiltonian takes the form…
Answer: D. This Pauli-string sum is exactly the form Lecture 4's term-by-term measurement recipe is built around.
7. In VQE, the classical optimizer's role is to…
Answer: C. The optimizer's search is licensed by the variational principle: every value it sees is a safe upper bound on E₀.
8. For the toy Hamiltonian H = aZ (a > 0) with an RY(θ) ansatz, ⟨H⟩ = a·cos(θ) is minimized at…
Answer: D. cos(θ) reaches −1 at θ=π, giving ⟨H⟩=−a, exactly matching H's true ground eigenvalue (Lecture 3's fully worked toy VQE).
9. To measure ⟨X⟩ on a device whose native measurement is the Z basis, you should…
Answer: A. A Hadamard rotates the X-eigenbasis onto the Z-eigenbasis so the native measurement reports the X-basis outcome (Lecture 4).
10. The full VQE loop proceeds in which order?
Answer: D. This is the Lecture 4 loop — the quantum device handles preparation and term-by-term measurement; the classical side combines and updates.
Barren Plateaus & QAOA · Weeks 5–7
11. A "barren plateau" refers to…
Answer: C. Barren plateaus are landscapes where gradient variance shrinks exponentially with system size, leaving no usable downhill signal (Lecture 5).
12. How does ansatz expressibility relate to barren plateaus?
Answer: D. High expressibility and susceptibility to barren plateaus are two faces of the same concentration-of-measure effect (Lecture 5, building on Lecture 2).
13. The Max-Cut problem asks you to…
Answer: A. Max-Cut is the canonical NP-hard combinatorial benchmark for QAOA (Lecture 6).
14. In the cost Hamiltonian Hc = Σ edges (1−ZᵢZⱼ)/2, a single edge term contributes 1 to ⟨Hc⟩ exactly when…
Answer: D. ZᵢZⱼ=−1 exactly when the qubits disagree — i.e. the edge is cut — which is when (1−ZᵢZⱼ)/2 equals 1 (Lecture 6).
15. What does the mixer Hamiltonian Hm = Σᵢ Xᵢ provide that the cost Hamiltonian Hc cannot?
Answer: C. Hm's Pauli-X terms redistribute probability between bitstrings, complementing Hc's phase-only effect (Lecture 7).
16. A p-layer QAOA circuit has how many total classical parameters?
Answer: D. Each of the p layers contributes an independent γₖ and βₖ, for 2p total parameters (Lecture 7).
Optimizers & NISQ Reality · Weeks 8–9
17. The parameter-shift rule computes an exact quantum gradient from…
Answer: A. Parameter shift gives an exact, not approximate, derivative from exactly two shifted circuit evaluations (Lecture 8).
18. What is the key advantage of gradient-free optimizers like COBYLA or Nelder-Mead?
Answer: B. By using only cost-function values, gradient-free methods sidestep the mechanics of explicit gradient computation — but per Arrasmith et al. (2021), the cost-function differences they depend on also vanish exponentially in a barren plateau, so they don't actually escape barren-plateau vanishing, and they're generally slower to converge (Lecture 8).
19. What makes SPSA particularly well-suited to NISQ devices?
Answer: C. SPSA's cheap, simultaneous-perturbation estimate scales well with parameter count and tolerates real hardware's noisy measurements (Lecture 8).
20. What has VQE/QAOA actually demonstrated on real quantum hardware, as of this course?
Answer: D. This is Lecture 9's honest status check: real, meaningful demonstrations at small scale, but no demonstrated advantage over classical methods yet.
21. Which three obstacles does this course identify as compounding together on real NISQ hardware?
Answer: A. These three obstacles worsen each other as circuits scale up toward practically useful sizes (Lecture 9).
Capstone Synthesis · Week 10
22. What is the fundamental relationship between VQE and QAOA?
Answer: B. Both are Lecture 1's exact hybrid loop, differing only in ansatz and cost Hamiltonian — the capstone's central synthesis point (Lecture 10).
23. A complete capstone project, per Lecture 10, should visibly combine…
Answer: C. Lecture 10 explicitly lists all six pieces, including the easy-to-skip "awareness-only" items.
24. Why can a 4-node cycle (square) graph have its maximum cut equal to all 4 edges, unlike the triangle from Lecture 6?
Answer: A. A bipartite graph's natural 2-coloring cuts every edge; an odd cycle like the triangle can never have all its edges cut simultaneously (Lecture 10).
25. How does the 5-course Quantum Computing specialization's arc connect to this course's capstone?
Answer: A. Lecture 10's specialization recap traces the full arc: C1's state vectors and measurement underpin everything, through C4's NISQ constraints that directly motivate C5's hybrid-loop strategy.
