Building a Small "Circuit Zoo"

Bell states generalize to three or more qubits. And some circuits aren't about preparing a state at all — they're clever tricks for answering a question without fully measuring anything.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Write the n-qubit GHZ state and build it with H plus a chain of CNOTs.
  • Explain how GHZ states generalize the Bell-pair idea to three or more qubits.
  • Describe the SWAP gate and what it does to two qubits' states.
  • Describe the swap test circuit and what measurement outcome it uses to estimate overlap.
  • Build a 3-qubit GHZ state in Qiskit and verify it via a measurement histogram.

9.1GHZ States: Bell Pairs for Three or More Qubits

The Bell states from Lectures 5–8 are maximally entangled states of exactly two qubits. The GHZ state (Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger) is the natural generalization to three (or more) qubits — an equal superposition of "all zeros" and "all ones":

|GHZ₃⟩ = (|000⟩ + |111⟩)/√2Like a Bell state, GHZ is a two-term equal superposition — but now three qubits are correlated simultaneously, not just two. Measuring any one qubit instantly fixes what all the others must read.

GHZ states are built with the exact same tools as Bell states, just extended: H on the first qubit, then a chain of CNOTs cascading the entanglement out to every other qubit:

|GHZ_n⟩ recipe: H(q₀), then CNOT(q₀→q₁), CNOT(q₀→q₂), … , CNOT(q₀→q_{n−1})Each CNOT uses the same control qubit q₀ — once q₀ is in superposition, every CNOT off of it copies that correlation onto one more qubit, without ever needing to measure or re-superpose anything in between.

9.2GHZ Correlations Are Stronger Than Bell Correlations

Measuring all n qubits of a GHZ state always yields either all-0s or all-1s, each with probability 1/2 — the direct n-qubit generalization of Course 1 §7.3's two-qubit correlation. What's new with three or more qubits is that GHZ states violate a stronger form of Bell's inequality (not covered in depth here) with a starker, more dramatic mismatch between quantum and classical predictions than a two-qubit Bell pair can produce — one reason GHZ states are a standard benchmark for testing how well real quantum hardware preserves multi-qubit entanglement.

AnalogyA Bell pair is two coins magically forced to always match. A GHZ state is three (or more) coins all forced to match each other simultaneously — flip any one and you instantly know what all the others read, no matter how many there are. The "spookiness" doesn't dilute as you add qubits; if anything, it gets harder to explain away classically, which is exactly why GHZ states are used to stress-test real hardware.

9.3The SWAP Gate

Before the swap test, meet the plain SWAP gate — a two-qubit gate that simply exchanges the states of its two qubits:

SWAP|ab⟩ = |ba⟩     e.g. SWAP|01⟩ = |10⟩SWAP can be built from three CNOTs (CNOT(A→B), CNOT(B→A), CNOT(A→B)) — another small circuit-equivalence fact in the spirit of Lecture 8, included here mainly so the controlled-SWAP in §9.4 doesn't appear out of nowhere.

9.4The Swap Test: Estimating Overlap Without Full Measurement

Suppose you have two unknown states |ψ⟩ and |φ⟩ (each on its own qubit, or register) and want to know how similar they are — specifically, the overlap |⟨ψ|φ⟩|² — without fully measuring either one (which would destroy the very superpositions you're trying to compare). The swap test answers this with a clever three-part circuit: an ancilla qubit, a Hadamard, and a controlled-SWAP.

ancilla |0⟩ ──[H]──●──[H]──[M]
       |ψ⟩ ───────⨉─────────
       |φ⟩ ───────⨉─────────
P(ancilla measures 0) = (1 + |⟨ψ|φ⟩|²) / 2The controlled-SWAP (⨉–⨉ connected by the control dot) only swaps |ψ⟩ and |φ⟩ when the ancilla is |1⟩ — exactly the controlled-U pattern from Lecture 4, with SWAP in the role of U.

Reading off the formula: if |ψ⟩=|φ⟩ exactly (overlap 1), P(0) = 1 — the ancilla always reads 0. If |ψ⟩ and |φ⟩ are orthogonal (overlap 0), P(0) = 1/2 — a fair coin, the worst case. Running the test many times and tallying how often the ancilla reads 0 lets you estimate |⟨ψ|φ⟩|² statistically, without ever learning anything else about either state individually — a preview of exactly the kind of indirect, interference-based measurement trick Course 3's algorithms (Deutsch-Jozsa, Grover) build on heavily.

Common mistakeDon't expect the swap test to tell you |ψ⟩ or |φ⟩ individually, or even their overlap exactly from one run. A single shot only gives one bit (ancilla 0 or 1); the overlap is recovered statistically, from the fraction of 0s across many repeated runs — exactly like the Lecture 5 Bell-state histogram, just with a different formula connecting the measured fraction to the quantity of interest.
✦ Worked Example — tracing the GHZ circuit, and reading the swap test formula

Part A: trace the 3-qubit GHZ circuit from |000⟩. Part B: use the swap test formula to find P(0) for an overlap of |⟨ψ|φ⟩|² = 1/2.

  1. Part A, step 1: Start at |000⟩ (q₀,q₁,q₂). Apply H to q₀: (|000⟩+|100⟩)/√2.
  2. Part A, step 2: Apply CNOT(q₀→q₁): flips q₁ wherever q₀=1. (|000⟩+|110⟩)/√2.
  3. Part A, step 3: Apply CNOT(q₀→q₂): flips q₂ wherever q₀=1. (|000⟩+|111⟩)/√2 = |GHZ₃⟩. ✓ matches §9.1 exactly — note both CNOTs shared the same control, q₀, per the chain recipe.
  4. Part B: plug |⟨ψ|φ⟩|²=1/2 into P(0) = (1+|⟨ψ|φ⟩|²)/2 = (1+0.5)/2 = 0.75. The ancilla should read 0 about 75% of the time across many runs — more often than the 50% "orthogonal" floor, consistent with the two states being partially (but not fully) similar.
  5. Sanity check on the formula's endpoints: overlap 1 (identical states) gives P(0)=(1+1)/2=1 ✓; overlap 0 (orthogonal states) gives P(0)=(1+0)/2=0.5 ✓ — both match the extremes described in §9.4.

🛠️ Hands-On: build a 3-qubit GHZ state in Qiskit

Build the circuit from the worked example and confirm the measurement histogram shows only '000' and '111'.

# ghz_state.py — Lecture 9
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit_aer import AerSimulator

qc = QuantumCircuit(3, 3)
qc.h(0)             # superposition on qubit 0
qc.cx(0, 1)          # chain CNOT: entangle qubit 1 with qubit 0
qc.cx(0, 2)          # chain CNOT: entangle qubit 2 with qubit 0 too
qc.measure([0, 1, 2], [0, 1, 2])

sim = AerSimulator()
counts = sim.run(qc, shots=1000).result().get_counts()
print(counts)        # e.g. {'000': 512, '111': 488} -- only these two outcomes, ~50/50

# extend to n qubits: qc = QuantumCircuit(n, n); qc.h(0)
# for i in range(1, n): qc.cx(0, i)   -- the general chain-CNOT recipe from Section 9.1

As with the Lecture 5 Bell-state histogram, only two bitstrings should ever appear — now '000' and '111' instead of '00' and '11' — confirming the GHZ state's three-way correlation, not just the pairwise correlation a Bell pair alone could show.

🔗 Where this is heading

GHZ states and the swap test round out this course's "zoo" of named circuits — Bell states, teleportation, superdense coding, GHZ, and swap test together form a working toolkit of entanglement-based tricks. Course 3 builds its first real quantum algorithms on exactly this kind of circuit cleverness: Deutsch-Jozsa and Grover's algorithm both use interference and controlled operations in the same spirit as the swap test to answer a question using dramatically fewer queries than any classical approach could.

Q1. The 3-qubit GHZ state is…
A. |000⟩, unchanged from the input
B. (|000⟩+|011⟩+|101⟩+|110⟩)/2
C. (|000⟩+|111⟩)/√2
D. (|001⟩+|110⟩)/√2
Show answer

C. |GHZ₃⟩ = (|000⟩+|111⟩)/√2 — the direct three-qubit generalization of a two-qubit Bell state's two-term equal superposition.

Q2. The standard circuit for building an n-qubit GHZ state uses…
A. n separate Hadamard gates, one per qubit
B. H on one qubit, then a chain of CNOTs from that same qubit to every other qubit
C. only CNOT gates, no Hadamard at all
D. measurement, performed before any gates
Show answer

B. One H creates the initial superposition; each subsequent CNOT (sharing the same control qubit) extends that correlation out to one more qubit.

Q3. The SWAP gate's action on |01⟩ is…
A. |10⟩
B. |01⟩, unchanged
C. |00⟩
D. |11⟩
Show answer

A. SWAP exchanges the two qubits' values: SWAP|ab⟩=|ba⟩, so SWAP|01⟩=|10⟩.

Q4. The swap test's ancilla qubit, measured many times, reads 0 with probability…
A. always exactly 1, regardless of |ψ⟩,|φ⟩
B. (1 + |⟨ψ|φ⟩|²)/2 — 1 if the states are identical, 1/2 if they're orthogonal
C. always exactly 0
D. a value unrelated to the overlap of |ψ⟩ and |φ⟩
Show answer

B. P(0) = (1+|⟨ψ|φ⟩|²)/2 directly encodes the overlap, ranging from 1/2 (orthogonal states) up to 1 (identical states).

Q5. Measuring all three qubits of a GHZ state many times, the possible outcomes are…
A. only '001' and '110'
B. all eight 3-bit strings equally often
C. only '000', with certainty
D. only '000' and '111', roughly 50/50
Show answer

D. |GHZ₃⟩ has amplitude only on |000⟩ and |111⟩, so those are the only two possible measurement outcomes — the three-qubit generalization of a Bell pair's two-outcome correlation.
