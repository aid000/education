Quantum Teleportation

A Bell pair plus two classical bits lets Bob reconstruct a state he never saw, Alice never told him in any ordinary sense, and that was destroyed on her end the moment she measured it.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • State the teleportation protocol end to end: shared Bell pair, Bell measurement, classical bits, corrective gate.
  • Explain why Bob's correction is one of exactly four gates (I, X, Z, XZ), chosen by Alice's two measured bits.
  • Trace the full 3-qubit circuit algebraically and verify the protocol reconstructs the original state.
  • Explain why teleportation is not faster-than-light communication, and why the original is destroyed.
  • Build the teleportation circuit in Qiskit and verify it reconstructs a known state.

7.1The Setup: Three Qubits, One Unknown State

Teleportation involves three qubits. Qubit C holds an unknown state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩+β|1⟩ that Alice wants to send to Bob. Qubits A and B are pre-shared between Alice and Bob as the Bell pair |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2 from Lecture 5 — Alice holds A, Bob holds B, and this pair was prepared and distributed before the protocol starts, using no information about |ψ⟩ at all.

Alice has: qubit C (unknown |ψ⟩) and qubit A (her half of the Bell pair)     Bob has: qubit B (his half)Crucially, Alice does not know α and β any more than Bob does — the protocol works for a genuinely unknown state, which is exactly why it can't be replaced by Alice simply reading off and phoning in the amplitudes.

7.2Alice's Bell Measurement

Alice applies CNOT with C as control and A as target, then applies H to C — the exact reverse of the H+CNOT recipe Lecture 5 used to build a Bell state, now run to measure in the Bell basis instead. She then measures both C and A in the computational basis, getting two classical bits mC, mA.

|ψ⟩_C ──●────[H]──[M]── m_C
|Φ⁺⟩_A ──⊕──────────[M]── m_A
|Φ⁺⟩_B ───────────────────── (untouched by Alice, held by Bob)This CNOT+H+measure sequence is a "Bell measurement" — it projects the pair (C,A) onto one of the four Bell states, and reading out which one is exactly what the two classical bits m_C, m_A encode.

These two bits are the only thing Alice sends to Bob, over an ordinary classical channel (phone, internet, shouting across the room — anything slower than or equal to light speed).

7.3Bob's Correction: One of Four Gates

Once Bob receives mC and mA, he applies a correction to his qubit B — and the full algebra (worked out in §7.4) shows the correction needed is always exactly one of four possibilities, selected purely by the two classical bits:

(m_C, m_A) = (0,0) → apply I    (1,0) → apply Z    (0,1) → apply X    (1,1) → apply Z then XAfter the correct gate, qubit B is left in exactly |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩+β|1⟩ — the state Alice started with, now sitting on Bob's qubit, with no further ambiguity.

Every one of these four corrections is built entirely from gates this course already has: the identity and the Pauli gates from Lecture 1. Teleportation doesn't need any new gate — it needs exactly the entangling (CNOT) and basis-changing (H) machinery already in hand, applied in a specific order with a classical bit of routing logic at the end.

Common mistakeDon't picture Bob's qubit as "already secretly holding |ψ⟩" before the correction arrives. Before Bob applies the right gate, his qubit genuinely is in one of four different states depending on Alice's (unknown-to-Bob) measurement outcome — he needs the two classical bits to know which correction to apply. Skip the correction and Bob's qubit is the wrong state three times out of four.

7.4Why This Isn't Faster-Than-Light, and Why the Original Is Destroyed

Two features of the protocol are easy to misread, and both matter:

No signal travels faster than light: Bob's qubit is useless until the classical bits (m_C, m_A) physically arriveBefore the classical bits arrive, Bob's qubit B is in a mixed, uninformative state averaged over all four possible corrections — exactly like Course 1 §7.3's correlated-but-unusable Bell-pair outcomes. There is no way for Bob to extract |ψ⟩ early, no matter how he measures B alone.

The word "teleportation" is evocative but can mislead: nothing is instantaneous, and the classical channel in §7.2 is bound by the ordinary speed-of-light limit like any classical signal. The second feature closes the loop back to Lecture 6: Alice's measurement in §7.2 destroys qubit C's original state — it collapses to a definite basis state the moment she measures it. At no point do two valid copies of |ψ⟩ exist simultaneously; the state is moved, not copied, fully consistent with no-cloning.

✦ Worked Example — tracing the full teleportation circuit

Trace the 3-qubit circuit for a general unknown state |ψ⟩=α|0⟩+β|1⟩ on qubit C, with a pre-shared |Φ⁺⟩ on qubits A,B, confirming the correction table in §7.3.

  1. Initial state: (α|0⟩+β|1⟩)C ⊗ (|00⟩+|11⟩)AB/√2, expanded: (α|000⟩+α|011⟩+β|100⟩+β|111⟩)/√2, in order (C,A,B).
  2. Apply CNOT(C→A): flips A whenever C=1. The β-terms have C=1: β|100⟩→β|110⟩ and β|111⟩→β|101⟩. Result: (α|000⟩+α|011⟩+β|110⟩+β|101⟩)/√2.
  3. Apply H to C: H|0⟩=(|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2 on the α-terms, H|1⟩=(|0⟩−|1⟩)/√2 on the β-terms. Expanding and collecting by Bob's qubit B gives four groups, one per (m_C,m_A) outcome: (0,0)→α|0⟩_B+β|1⟩_B; (1,0)→α|0⟩_B−β|1⟩_B; (0,1)→β|0⟩_B+α|1⟩_B; (1,1)→−β|0⟩_B+α|1⟩_B (full algebra identical in structure to Course 1's tensor-product expansions).
  4. Identify each group as a Pauli-transformed |ψ⟩: (0,0) is |ψ⟩ itself (apply I); (1,0) is Z|ψ⟩ (apply Z to undo); (0,1) is X|ψ⟩ (apply X to undo); (1,1) is XZ|ψ⟩ (apply Z then X to undo) — exactly the §7.3 table.
  5. Conclusion: whichever of the four outcomes Alice measures, Bob's qubit B is always exactly one Pauli correction away from |ψ⟩ — and since Alice's two classical bits tell Bob precisely which outcome occurred, the protocol always succeeds, regardless of the unknown α, β.

🛠️ Hands-On: build and verify teleportation in Qiskit

Teleport a known test state so the result can be checked, using Qiskit's control-flow builder (with qc.if_test(...)) for Bob's correction — Qiskit 2.0 removed the old .c_if() method entirely.

# teleportation_circuit.py — Lecture 7
import numpy as np
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit, QuantumRegister, ClassicalRegister
from qiskit_aer import AerSimulator

q = QuantumRegister(3, 'q')          # q0=C (unknown), q1=A (Alice's half), q2=B (Bob's half)
c = ClassicalRegister(2, 'c')        # c[0]=m_C, c[1]=m_A
check = ClassicalRegister(1, 'check') # holds the verification bit, defined below
qc = QuantumCircuit(q, c, check)

# prepare a KNOWN test state on q0 so we can verify the result later
theta = 0.9
qc.ry(theta, 0)        # |psi> = Ry(theta)|0>, standing in for the "unknown" state

# 1) share a Bell pair between q1 (Alice) and q2 (Bob)
qc.h(1)
qc.cx(1, 2)

# 2) Alice's Bell measurement on q0 (C) and q1 (A)
qc.cx(0, 1)
qc.h(0)
qc.measure(0, 0)
qc.measure(1, 1)

# 3) Bob's correction, conditioned on the two classical bits -- built with the
# control-flow builder BEFORE calling sim.run() (.c_if() no longer exists in Qiskit 2.0)
with qc.if_test((c[0], 1)):    # m_C=1 -> apply Z
    qc.z(2)
with qc.if_test((c[1], 1)):    # m_A=1 -> apply X (fires after the Z above, matching "Z then X")
    qc.x(2)

# 4) ACTUAL verification: undo the known test rotation on q2. If teleportation
# worked, q2 is exactly Ry(theta)|0>, so Ry(-theta) collapses it back to |0>.
qc.ry(-theta, 2)
qc.measure(2, 2)

sim = AerSimulator()
counts = sim.run(qc, shots=1000).result().get_counts()
print(counts)   # 'check' bit reads 0 on (almost) every shot -- the real confirmation it worked

The circuit above performs its own check rather than just running silently: after Bob's correction fires, the extra Ry(-theta) on q2 undoes the known test rotation, and the check classical bit should read 0 on nearly every one of the 1000 shots — direct evidence that q2 ended up in exactly the state Alice started with, not merely a claim that it should have.

🔗 Where this is heading

Teleportation sends an unknown quantum state using a shared Bell pair plus two classical bits. Lecture 8 flips this exactly around: superdense coding sends two classical bits using a shared Bell pair plus one qubit — the two protocols are dual to each other, a symmetry worth sitting with. Lecture 8 also introduces circuit equivalences (like HXH=Z) that make rewriting circuits like this one more tractable.

❓ Lecture 7 Quiz

5 questions · click "Show answer" to check yourself.

Q1. The teleportation protocol requires Alice and Bob to pre-share…
A. a copy of the unknown state |ψ⟩
B. a Bell pair, prepared before the protocol begins
C. nothing at all
D. four classical bits
Show answer

B. A Bell pair (e.g. |Φ⁺⟩) must be shared and distributed in advance, using no knowledge of |ψ⟩ — it's the entanglement resource the protocol spends.

Q2. Alice's Bell measurement on qubits C and A consists of…
A. a single measurement with no gates beforehand
B. measuring only qubit B
C. applying X to both qubits
D. CNOT(C→A), then H on C, then measuring both C and A
Show answer

D. This is the reverse of the Lecture 5 Bell-state-preparation recipe — CNOT then H — now used to measure in the Bell basis rather than to prepare a Bell state.

Q3. Bob's corrective gate, chosen from {I, X, Z, XZ}, is selected by…
A. the two classical bits Alice measured and sent him
B. Bob's own random guess
C. the unknown amplitudes α and β directly
D. measuring qubit B before any correction
Show answer

A. The two classical bits from Alice's Bell measurement determine exactly which one of the four Pauli corrections restores |ψ⟩ on Bob's qubit.

Q4. Teleportation does not violate relativity because…
A. no entanglement is actually used
B. Alice's measurement has no effect on Bob's qubit at all
C. the protocol only works over short distances
D. Bob's qubit is useless until the classical bits physically arrive, and that transmission is light-speed-limited
Show answer

D. Before the classical bits arrive, Bob's qubit carries no usable information about |ψ⟩ — the protocol's effective "speed" is capped by the classical channel, not instantaneous.

Q5. After Alice's Bell measurement, the original qubit C's state |ψ⟩…
A. still exists unchanged on qubit C
B. is copied onto both A and B
C. is destroyed — collapsed by the measurement — consistent with no-cloning
D. travels instantly to Bob before any classical communication
Show answer

C. Measurement collapses qubit C; the state is moved, not duplicated — at no point do two valid copies of |ψ⟩ coexist, exactly as Lecture 6's no-cloning discussion requires.
