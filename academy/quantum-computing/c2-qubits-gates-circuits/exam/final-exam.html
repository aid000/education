Pauli Gates & the Circuit Model · Weeks 1–2
1. A quantum gate U, acting on a qubit state, must always satisfy…
Answer: A. Unitarity (U†U = I) is the exact condition that preserves normalization, mapping every valid state to another valid state — required of every gate, not just special ones.
2. The Pauli-X gate acting on α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ produces…
Answer: B. X swaps the two amplitudes — the bit-flip gate, acting on amplitudes rather than a single classical bit.
3. H|0⟩ equals…
Answer: C. The Hadamard gate creates the equal superposition |+⟩ = (|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2 from the basis state |0⟩. Option B, |−⟩, is H|1⟩ instead.
4. On the Bloch sphere, the S gate…
Answer: D. S is diagonal and only multiplies the |1⟩ amplitude by i — a pure rotation of φ around the z-axis, with θ (and the poles) fixed.
Universal & Multi-Qubit Gates · Weeks 3–4
5. A gate set is called "universal" when circuits built from it can…
Answer: A. Universality is about arbitrarily close approximation, not exact reproduction — the Solovay–Kitaev theorem guarantees this approximation is also efficient.
6. In the universal set {H, T, CNOT}, which gate supplies the entangling, multi-qubit ingredient?
Answer: B. No sequence of single-qubit gates alone, acting on separate wires, can ever entangle two qubits — CNOT is the specific multi-qubit gate that makes it possible.
7. CNOT acting on |10⟩ (control=1, target=0) produces…
Answer: C. The control is 1, so the target flips from 0 to 1, giving |11⟩ — CNOT's defining rule: flip the target iff the control is |1⟩.
8. A controlled-U gate, for an arbitrary single-qubit unitary U, applies U to the target…
Answer: D. Controlled-U generalizes CNOT (controlled-X): apply U to the target exactly when the control reads |1⟩.
9. In CNOT, the control/target asymmetry means…
Answer: A. The control is read but never altered; the target is the one conditionally flipped — swapping which wire plays which role produces a genuinely different gate.
Bell States & No-Cloning · Weeks 5–6
10. The standard circuit for preparing |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2 from |00⟩ is…
Answer: B. H creates the superposition on the control first; CNOT then correlates the target with it — CNOT alone on |00⟩ does nothing, since both rows it would swap already agree.
11. Measuring both qubits of |Φ⁺⟩ many times, the possible outcomes are…
Answer: C. |Φ⁺⟩ has amplitude only on |00⟩ and |11⟩, so the two measurement outcomes are perfectly correlated — the signature Bell-state statistics.
12. The no-cloning theorem states that…
Answer: D. This is the precise statement — it forbids a universal copier working on an unknown state, not copying of already-known classical information.
13. Attempting to "clone" a superposed control qubit using CNOT instead produces…
Answer: A. Linearity forces CNOT to distribute over the superposition, producing an entangled two-term sum — not the four-term product state true cloning would require.
14. After the CNOT "cloning" attempt on a superposed control, the reduced state of the target qubit alone is…
Answer: B. The attempt produced entanglement, not duplication — tracing out an entangled partner always leaves a mixed state behind, the opposite of a faithful copy.
15. Feeding the input |10⟩ into the standard H(control)+CNOT circuit produces…
Answer: C. Flipping the control's input bit (via X beforehand) introduces the relative minus sign while leaving the same pair of terms connected, giving |Φ⁻⟩.
Teleportation & Superdense Coding · Weeks 7–8
16. The teleportation protocol requires Alice and Bob to pre-share…
Answer: D. The pre-shared entanglement is the resource the protocol spends — it must be distributed using no knowledge of the unknown |ψ⟩.
17. Bob's corrective gate, chosen from {I, X, Z, XZ}, is selected by…
Answer: A. The two classical bits Alice measures and transmits determine exactly which of the four Pauli corrections restores |ψ⟩ on Bob's qubit.
18. Teleportation does not violate relativity because…
Answer: B. Before the classical bits arrive, Bob's qubit is in an uninformative average over all four possible corrections — the protocol's effective speed is capped by the classical channel.
19. Superdense coding lets Alice send Bob…
Answer: C. One transmitted qubit, leveraging pre-shared entanglement, carries two classical bits — double the usual one-qubit-one-bit capacity. Option A describes teleportation instead.
20. The circuit equivalence HXH equals…
Answer: D. Conjugating X by Hadamards gives Z — direct matrix multiplication confirms it, and it reflects H swapping the roles of the Z-basis and X-basis.
Circuit Zoo & Capstone · Weeks 9–10
21. The 3-qubit GHZ state is…
Answer: A. |GHZ₃⟩ is the three-qubit generalization of a Bell state's two-term equal superposition, now across three qubits.
22. The standard circuit for building an n-qubit GHZ state uses…
Answer: B. One H creates the initial superposition; each CNOT off that same control qubit extends the correlation to one more qubit.
23. In the swap test, the ancilla qubit (measured over many runs) reads 0 with probability…
Answer: C. This formula ranges from 1/2 (orthogonal states) to 1 (identical states), letting many repeated runs statistically estimate the overlap |⟨ψ|φ⟩|².
24. A complete capstone project report, for each named circuit, should include…
Answer: D. Diagram, data, and theory, together, is the shape the project asks for across every circuit in the zoo.
25. Course 3 (Quantum Algorithms) builds directly on this course's toolkit by…
Answer: A. Deutsch-Jozsa and Grover's algorithm both lean directly on this course's circuit-level tools, now aimed at provable speedups rather than demonstration circuits.
