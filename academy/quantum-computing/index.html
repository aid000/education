🧬 Quantum Computing

From quantum-mechanics foundations to hybrid algorithms. State vectors and measurement, qubits and gates, the canonical quantum algorithms, noise and error correction, and variational quantum-classical methods — all built in free Python simulators on a laptop, no quantum hardware required.

🧭 Course Progression

Take them in order — each course assumes the formalism and circuits of the last.

C1 QM Foundations → C2 Qubits & Gates → C3 Algorithms → C4 Noise & QEC → C5 Variational & Hybrid

🎯 Internship readiness

This sequence mirrors a university intro-to-quantum-computing track — QM formalism, the circuit model, core algorithms, error correction, and NISQ-era variational methods. The AP Physics C / Calculus base plus basic linear algebra and Python is all you need to begin, and it targets quantum internships at IBM, Google, and academic labs.

📖 Books (optional)

The courses are self-contained — lectures cover everything, no required textbook. For extra depth: Nielsen & Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information (C1–C4, the standard reference); Hidary, Quantum Computing: An Applied Approach (C2–C5); the Qiskit and PennyLane documentation are excellent free companions.

⚠️ Simulation scope

Everything is done on classical simulators, which comfortably handle the small qubit counts these courses use. Free cloud access to real IBM Quantum hardware exists and can be a bonus for a capstone, but it is never required — all projects complete fully offline.
