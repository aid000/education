🚢 Naval Architecture

Built directly on SNAME's Principles of Naval Architecture — ship geometry & hydrostatics, intact stability, damage stability & strength, resistance, and propulsion & vibration. Every calculation done in Python from sample hull offsets or STL files, all on a laptop with open-source tools. No commercial licenses, no hardware.

🧭 Course Progression

Take them in order. C1–C3 cover PNA Vol. 1 (stability & strength); C4–C5 cover Vol. 2 (resistance, propulsion & vibration).

C1 Hydrostatics → C2 Intact Stability → C3 Damage & Strength → C4 Resistance → C5 Propulsion & Vibration

🎯 Internship readiness

This sequence mirrors a standard university naval-architecture track — hydrostatics & stability → structures → resistance & propulsion — scoped tightly to PNA Vols. 1–2. Built on AP Physics C (mechanics + fluids intro) and Calc BC (integrals, moments) plus basic Python, it builds a strong portfolio for shipyard, design-office, classification-society (ABS / DNV), and naval-research internships.

📖 Primary source

This track follows SNAME's Principles of Naval Architecture: Vol. 1 — Stability & Strength (C1–C3) and Vol. 2 — Resistance, Propulsion & Vibration (C4–C5). Lectures are self-contained, but the two volumes are the canonical reference and pair directly with the course order.

⚠️ Software to verify (*)

The core Python tools (NavalToolbox / PyNAT, Holtrop scripts) are pip-installable or a GitHub clone. OpenFOAM and code_aster are optional/advanced validation only — if they're awkward to install on your laptop, every project completes fully with the pure-Python path. Sample hull offsets / STL files are provided or freely downloadable.
