🛠️ Hands-On Project

N-Body Simulator or Double-Pendulum Chaos Study (Python)

Pick a track: build a gravitational n-body simulator (Sun–planet orbits, energy & momentum conservation, symplectic vs RK4) or a double-pendulum chaos study that estimates the largest Lyapunov exponent from diverging trajectories. Either way you code your own integrators (Euler, RK4, Verlet/leapfrog), validate them against analytic limits and conservation laws, and present phase portraits, convergence/energy-drift plots, and an error/conservation budget. You deliver a short report with clean code, figures, and a brief video demo. Week 1 includes a one-time Python + NumPy/SciPy install (step-by-step in Lecture 1).

Wk 1: install Python + NumPy/SciPy/Matplotlib Wk 4: RK4 + adaptive-step integrator working Wk 8: symplectic n-body / chaos engine + conservation checks Wk 10: report + Lyapunov/orbit plots + demo submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: RK4 takes four function evaluations per step but Euler takes one. When is the extra cost per step actually the cheaper way to hit a target accuracy?
  • Week 6: Explicit methods can blow up on a stiff system unless the step is tiny, while an implicit method stays stable. What is "stiffness" really measuring, and where does it show up in physics?
  • Week 9: Two double-pendulum runs from almost-identical starts diverge exponentially. If the equations are deterministic, in what sense is the motion "unpredictable"?
