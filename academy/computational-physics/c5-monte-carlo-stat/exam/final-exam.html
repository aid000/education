Random Numbers, MC Integration & Random Walks · Weeks 1–3
1. You set np.random.default_rng(seed=7) and run your Monte Carlo simulation twice. What happens?
Answer: A. A fixed seed sets the generator's starting state, so the stream of "random" numbers is bit-for-bit identical on every run — the basis of reproducible stochastic computing.
2. A Monte Carlo estimate currently has a given standard error. About how many times more samples are needed to shrink that error by a factor of 10?
Answer: B. Error ∝ 1/√N, so shrinking the error by a factor of 10 requires √N to grow by 10, i.e. N to grow by 10² = 100.
3. Importance sampling reduces the variance of a Monte Carlo integral estimate by…
Answer: C. Sampling where f is large and correcting with the weight f/p keeps the estimator unbiased while collapsing the variance of the weighted integrand; the ideal p ∝ |f| drives the variance toward zero.
4. For a simple symmetric 1D random walk of N unbiased ±1 steps, the root-mean-square displacement xrms scales as…
Answer: D. ⟨xN²⟩ = N because the cross terms ⟨sisj⟩ vanish for independent steps, so xrms = √⟨x²⟩ = √N — the hallmark of diffusive scaling.
5. To estimate π by hit-or-miss, you scatter points uniformly in the unit square and multiply the fraction landing inside the quarter circle by…
Answer: A. The quarter circle has area π/4 and the square has area 1, so the hit fraction estimates π/4; multiplying by 4 recovers π. Option B would require already knowing π.
Markov Chains & the Metropolis-Hastings Algorithm · Weeks 4–5
6. The defining "memoryless" (Markov) property says the next state of a chain depends on…
Answer: B. P(Xt+1|Xt,Xt-1,...) = P(Xt+1|Xt) — only the present state matters, never the path taken to reach it.
7. The detailed-balance condition for a Markov chain with transition matrix P and target distribution π is…
Answer: C. Detailed balance equates the per-pair probability fluxes πiPij = πjPji; summing over i proves it is sufficient (though not necessary) for π to be stationary.
8. Why is detailed balance such a useful design tool for sampling the Boltzmann distribution of a many-body system?
Answer: D. The condition depends only on πj/πi = e-β(Ej-Ei), in which the intractable normalizer Z cancels — the key fact that makes statistical-physics simulation possible at all.
9. A proposed Metropolis move has ΔE = +1.0 at temperature kT = 2.0, with a symmetric proposal. The acceptance probability is approximately…
Answer: A. A = min(1, e-ΔE/kT) = min(1, e-1.0/2.0) = e-0.5 ≈ 0.61. Since ΔE > 0 the move is not automatic, but it is also not vanishingly unlikely.
10. The purpose of discarding "burn-in" sweeps before measuring averages in a Metropolis simulation is to…
Answer: B. The chain starts from an arbitrary, usually atypical, configuration; early samples are contaminated by that start, so they are discarded until the trace shows only fluctuation around a stable mean.
The Ising Model & Phase Transitions · Weeks 6–7
11. In the Ising Hamiltonian E = -J Σ sisj with J > 0, two neighboring spins that agree contribute…
Answer: C. Agreeing spins give sisj = +1, so the term is -J(+1) = -J, the low-energy state — the Hamiltonian itself contains no T; temperature enters only through the Boltzmann acceptance rule.
12. A spin si = +1 has four neighbors summing to S = -2. The energy change to flip it, ΔE = 2J si S (J = 1), equals…
Answer: D. ΔE = 2(1)(+1)(-2) = -4. Since ΔE < 0, flipping this spin lowers the energy and the Metropolis rule accepts it unconditionally.
13. Why do we usually report ⟨|M|⟩ rather than ⟨M⟩ when detecting the ordered phase of the h = 0 Ising model?
Answer: A. The h = 0 Hamiltonian is symmetric under flipping every spin, so a long finite run occasionally tunnels between the two ordered states; the signed average washes toward 0 while ⟨|M|⟩ faithfully stays near 1 below Tc.
14. For the 2D square-lattice Ising model (kB = J = 1), the exact critical temperature (Onsager) is approximately…
Answer: B. Tc = 2/ln(1+√2) ≈ 2.269 — one of the few exactly solved interacting models and the gold-standard benchmark for any Ising Monte Carlo code.
15. Why do the magnetic susceptibility χ and specific heat C peak sharply at Tc?
Answer: C. χ and C equal scaled variances of m and E (fluctuation-dissipation); near Tc the system can't choose a phase, so those fluctuations explode. Tc is a middling temperature, not the maximum one simulated.
16. "Critical slowing down" near Tc means that…
Answer: D. As the correlation length ξ (and hence τ ∝ ξz) grows near Tc, single-spin updates need many more sweeps to decorrelate — exactly where you most want good statistics.
Autocorrelation & Error Estimation · Week 8
17. Why does the naive standard error σ/√N understate the true uncertainty of a Markov-chain Monte Carlo average?
Answer: A. Each Metropolis step is a small perturbation of the last, so consecutive samples resemble each other; the chain carries only N/(2τ) independent-sample's worth of information, and the honest SE is σ√(2τ/N).
18. In a blocking (binning) analysis, which feature of the standard-error-vs-block-size curve gives the correct error bar?
Answer: B. SE(b) rises as the block size grows past the correlation length and then flattens onto a plateau; that plateau is the correlation-corrected, honest standard error. It never dips to a minimum or crosses zero.
19. A chain has N = 800,000 samples and integrated autocorrelation time τ = 200. The effective number of independent samples is about…
Answer: C. Neff = N/(2τ) = 800,000 / 400 = 2,000. The divisor is always 2τ, never τ alone and never the raw count N.
20. A production run gives mean = -1.42 with a blocking-corrected standard error of 0.018. The best way to report the result is…
Answer: D. Round the uncertainty to one or two significant figures and match the value's last digit to it. A fakes precision the data cannot support; B omits the uncertainty; C invents an error far larger than computed.
Molecular Dynamics, Lennard-Jones & the Capstone · Weeks 9–10
21. In the Lennard-Jones potential U(r) = 4ε[(σ/r)12 - (σ/r)6], the r-12 term physically represents…
Answer: A. The steep r-12 wall models the repulsion that stops electron clouds from overlapping. The -r-6 term is the van der Waals attraction; gravity is utterly negligible at atomic scales.
22. Why is velocity-Verlet preferred over a higher-order Runge-Kutta integrator for long molecular-dynamics runs?
Answer: B. Symplectic, time-reversible integrators keep total energy oscillating within a tight bound over millions of steps; Runge-Kutta is more accurate per step but slowly bleeds energy over a long run.
23. A radial distribution function g(r) shows a tall sharp first peak, a weaker second peak, then settles to g(r) = 1 with no further structure. This is the fingerprint of…
Answer: C. One or two decaying near peaks settling to 1 is the classic liquid signature: neighbors are organized locally but order is lost at distance. A gas is nearly featureless at g ≈ 1; a solid keeps sharp peaks out to large r.
24. What is the single most important capability that molecular dynamics has and standard Metropolis Monte Carlo lacks?
Answer: D. MD produces real trajectories, so dynamical quantities (diffusion coefficients, viscosity, spectra) follow directly. Average potential energy and Boltzmann sampling are static averages both methods can give; LJ models neutral atoms, not charges.
25. You measure the Ising susceptibility peak at T ≈ 2.31 on an L = 16 lattice. Before quoting this as Tc, what should you do?
Answer: A. Finite-size effects shift the peak by roughly 1/L; running several L and extrapolating recovers the Onsager value ≈ 2.269. Precision does not affect the physics, and dropping equilibration corrupts the averages with transient data.
