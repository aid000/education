💻 Computational Physics

Physics you build, not just derive. Numerical methods, ODEs and classical dynamics, eigenvalue problems and quantum systems, PDE field simulations, and Monte-Carlo statistical physics — every algorithm implemented from scratch in Python before reaching for a library. 🎯 Internship readiness

This sequence mirrors a university computational-physics core — numerical analysis, computational mechanics, computational quantum mechanics, field/PDE simulation, and Monte-Carlo statistical physics. It builds the implement-from-scratch skill that simulation and research roles screen for, and the toolkit (Monte Carlo, eigenproblems, PDE solvers) feeds directly into the Quantum Computing, Plasma & Fusion, and Quant Finance tracks. Targets national-lab, quantum-computing-company, and tech simulation internships.

📖 Books (optional)

The courses are self-contained — lectures cover everything, no required textbook. For extra depth these pair well: Newman, Computational Physics (C1–C5, the closest match to this track); Press et al., Numerical Recipes (C1–C4, reference); Landau, Páez & Bordeianu, Computational Physics (C2–C5).

⚠️ Philosophy: build first, then use the library

Every core algorithm is implemented by hand before the SciPy version is introduced — that's deliberate. You'll validate your own RK4, Metropolis, and finite-difference solvers against analytic limits and against the library, which is exactly how you develop trustworthy numerical intuition. Numba is optional throughout (only for speed; never required to get correct results).
