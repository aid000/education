Week 8 · Lecture 8

Ideal Op-Amps

Two simple "golden rules" turn a mysterious amplifier into circuits you can analyze in your head.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • State the ideal op-amp assumptions and the two golden rules.
  • Derive and apply the inverting amplifier gain (−Rf/Rin).
  • Derive and apply the non-inverting amplifier gain (1 + Rf/Rin).
  • Explain the virtual short / virtual ground concept.
  • Recognize the op-amp as a summer, integrator, and active-filter building block.

8.1The Ideal Op-Amp & the Golden Rules

An operational amplifier has two inputs — non-inverting (+) and inverting (−) — and one output. It amplifies the difference between its inputs by an enormous gain. The ideal model makes three assumptions: infinite open-loop gain, infinite input impedance (no current into the inputs), and zero output impedance.

With negative feedback (output fed back to the − input), those assumptions collapse to two golden rules that solve almost every op-amp circuit:

① No current flows into either input.  ② The feedback drives V₊ = V₋ ("virtual short").
AnalogyAn ideal op-amp with feedback is like a cruise-control servo with infinite eagerness: it does whatever it takes at the output to make its two inputs agree. You never analyze the eagerness itself — you just assume it succeeds (V₊ = V₋) and solve the resistors around it.

8.2The Inverting Amplifier

Input through Rin to the − input, feedback Rf from output to the − input, and the + input grounded. Since V₊ = 0, rule ② forces V₋ = 0 too — a virtual ground. Rule ① (no input current) means the input current flows entirely through Rf, giving:

A = Vout / Vin = −Rf / RinThe minus sign means the output is inverted (180° flipped).

8.3The Non-Inverting Amplifier

Drive the + input directly; feed back from the output through a divider (Rf and Rin-to-ground) to the − input. Rule ② says V₋ = Vin, and the divider relation gives:

A = 1 + Rf / RinAlways ≥ 1 and non-inverting (positive). Set Rf = 0 (or Rin = ∞) for a unity-gain buffer.

A special case — Rf = 0 — gives gain 1: the voltage follower (buffer). Useless for amplifying, invaluable for isolation: its huge input impedance means it doesn't load the source, and its zero output impedance drives the next stage stiffly.

Common mistakeDon't confuse the two gain formulas. Inverting is −Rf/Rin (can be less than 1, and is negative); non-inverting is 1 + Rf/Rin (always ≥ 1, positive). Same two resistors, different wiring — the "+1" and the sign come from where the input is injected.

8.4Beyond Gain

The same golden rules build a whole toolkit. Feed several inputs through resistors into the virtual-ground node and you get a summing amplifier (a weighted adder). Replace Rf with a capacitor and the output becomes the integral of the input (an integrator). Mix R's and C's in the feedback and you get active filters with gain. These are the analog front-end blocks behind sensors, audio, and the data converters that feed every chip — and they're exactly what this course's project builds.

✦ Worked Example

Using Rf = 100 kΩ and Rin = 20 kΩ: (a) find the inverting gain and the output for Vin = 0.5 V; (b) find the gain if the same resistors are wired as a non-inverting amplifier.

  1. Inverting gain: A = −Rf/Rin = −100k/20k = −5.
  2. Inverting output: Vout = −5 × 0.5 V = −2.5 V (inverted and amplified).
  3. Non-inverting gain: A = 1 + Rf/Rin = 1 + 100k/20k = 1 + 5 = +6.
  4. Takeaway: identical resistors, but inverting gives −5 and non-inverting gives +6 — the wiring sets the formula.

Q1. Which is an assumption of the ideal op-amp?
A. zero open-loop gain
B. infinite input impedance (no input current)
C. large fixed input current
D. high output impedance
Show answer

B. Ideal: infinite input impedance (no input current), infinite open-loop gain, zero output impedance.

Q2. An inverting amplifier with Rf = 100 kΩ and Rin = 20 kΩ has a gain of…
A. −5
B. +6
C. −0.2
D. +5
Show answer

A. Inverting gain = −Rf/Rin = −100k/20k = −5.

Q3. A non-inverting amplifier with Rf = 100 kΩ and Rin = 20 kΩ has a gain of…
A. −5
B. +5
C. +6
D. +1
Show answer

C. Non-inverting gain = 1 + Rf/Rin = 1 + 5 = +6.

Q4. In an inverting amplifier, the inverting input node is called a "virtual ground" because…
A. it is physically connected to ground
B. feedback holds it at ~0 V even though no wire grounds it
C. the output is grounded
D. the supply is zero
Show answer

B. With the + input grounded, rule ② forces V₋ ≈ 0 V — a virtual ground, not a real connection.

Q5. A voltage follower (buffer) has a voltage gain of…
A. 0
B. 1
C. −1
D. infinite
Show answer

B. Output follows input (gain +1). It's used for isolation — high input impedance, low output impedance.

✓ What you learned

  • The ideal op-amp: infinite gain, infinite input impedance, zero output impedance.
  • The two golden rules (no input current; V₊ = V₋) solve feedback op-amp circuits by inspection.
  • Inverting gain = −Rf/Rin (with a virtual ground); non-inverting gain = 1 + Rf/Rin.
  • The buffer (gain 1) isolates stages; summers, integrators, and active filters extend the same rules.
  • Modeled an ideal op-amp as a high-gain VCVS in Ngspice and verified the gain.
