Week 7 · Lecture 7

Second-Order Circuits

Two storage elements together can ring. Damping, natural frequency, and resonance — the physics behind every tuned circuit.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Identify a second-order circuit (two independent storage elements, e.g. RLC).
  • Classify the response as overdamped, critically damped, or underdamped.
  • Compute the natural frequency ω₀ = 1/√(LC).
  • Explain resonance: where XL and XC cancel and the response peaks.
  • Relate the quality factor Q to the sharpness (bandwidth) of resonance.

7.1Two Storage Elements → Second Order

Put an inductor and a capacitor in the same circuit and KVL/KCL now produces a second-order differential equation. The series RLC is the canonical example: a resistor, inductor, and capacitor in a loop.

AnalogyAn LC pair is a mass on a spring. The inductor is the mass (inertia, resists current change); the capacitor is the spring (stores potential, pushes back). Energy trades between them and the system oscillates. The resistor is friction — it bleeds energy out and damps the swing.

7.2Damping: Three Regimes

How the resistor (friction) compares to the LC oscillation sets the response shape:

overdamped (lots of R) · critically damped (just enough) · underdamped (little R → it rings)
Common mistake"Critically damped" does not mean slowest or most stable — it's the fastest response that still has no overshoot. More resistance past that point (overdamped) makes the circuit slower, not better. And underdamped isn't "broken" — ringing is exactly what you want in a tuned/resonant circuit.

7.3Resonance

Drive a series RLC with a sinusoid and sweep the frequency. Inductive reactance (+jωL) rises with frequency while capacitive reactance (−j/ωC) falls. At one special frequency they're equal and opposite — they cancel:

ω₀ = 1 / √(LC)  (resonant frequency, rad/s)

At ω₀ the reactances vanish, so a series RLC looks purely resistive — impedance is at its minimum and current peaks. This is how a radio tunes: it resonates at one station's frequency and ignores the rest.

7.4Quality Factor & Bandwidth

The quality factor Q measures how sharp the resonance is — how much energy the circuit stores versus loses per cycle. For a series RLC:

Q = (1/R)·√(L/C)  ·  bandwidth BW = ω₀ / Q

High Q ⟹ a tall, narrow peak (very selective, e.g. a radio tuner); low Q ⟹ a broad, gentle peak. Less resistance means higher Q and a sharper resonance.

✦ Worked Example

A series RLC has L = 1 mH, C = 1 µF, R = 10 Ω. Find the resonant frequency (in rad/s and Hz), the quality factor, and the bandwidth.

  1. Resonant frequency: ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = 1/√(10⁻³·10⁻⁶) = 1/√(10⁻⁹) = 3.16×10⁴ rad/s.
  2. In Hz: f₀ = ω₀/2π ≈ 31623 / 6.283 ≈ 5.03 kHz.
  3. Quality factor: Q = (1/R)√(L/C) = (1/10)√(10⁻³/10⁻⁶) = 0.1 × √1000 = 0.1 × 31.6 ≈ 3.16.
  4. Bandwidth: BW = ω₀/Q = 31623 / 3.16 ≈ 1.0×10⁴ rad/s. (A modest Q → fairly broad peak.)

5 questions · click "Show answer" to check yourself.

Q1. A series RLC with L = 1 mH and C = 1 µF resonates at ω₀ ≈
A. 1×10³ rad/s
B. 3.16×10⁴ rad/s
C. 1×10⁶ rad/s
D. 1×10⁹ rad/s
Show answer

B. ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = 1/√(10⁻⁹) ≈ 3.16×10⁴ rad/s.

Q2. A series RLC circuit with very small resistance is…
A. overdamped
B. critically damped
C. underdamped (it rings)
D. non-responsive
Show answer

C. Little damping ⟹ the response overshoots and oscillates — underdamped. Large R gives overdamped.

Q3. At resonance, a series RLC circuit's impedance is…
A. at its minimum and purely resistive
B. at its maximum
C. purely inductive
D. zero
Show answer

A. XL and XC cancel, leaving only R — impedance is minimum, so current peaks.

Q4. "Critically damped" describes the response that is…
A. the slowest possible
B. the fastest with no overshoot
C. always oscillating
D. unstable
Show answer

B. Critical damping is the boundary case — the quickest approach to final value without overshooting.

Q5. A higher quality factor Q produces a resonance peak that is…
A. broader (less selective)
B. flat
C. shifted to a lower frequency
D. sharper and narrower (more selective)
Show answer

D. Q = ω₀/BW: higher Q ⟹ smaller bandwidth ⟹ a taller, narrower, more selective peak.

✓ What you learned

  • Two storage elements (RLC) give a second-order response — like a mass on a spring.
  • Three damping regimes: overdamped, critically damped (fastest, no overshoot), underdamped (rings).
  • Resonance at ω₀ = 1/√(LC): reactances cancel, series impedance is minimum, current peaks.
  • The quality factor Q sets the sharpness; bandwidth BW = ω₀/Q.
