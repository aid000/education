Fundamentals & DC analysis · Weeks 1–2
1. A 5 V source delivers 20 mA to a circuit. The power it delivers is…
Answer: A. p = v·i = 5 V × 0.02 A = 0.1 W = 100 mW.
2. In a single loop a 12 V source drives three resistors that drop 3 V, 4 V, and V₃. By KVL, V₃ =
Answer: A. KVL: 12 = 3 + 4 + V₃ ⟹ V₃ = 5 V.
3. Three 4 kΩ resistors in parallel have an equivalent resistance of about…
Answer: C. N equal R in parallel = R/N = 4 kΩ / 3 ≈ 1.33 kΩ (always less than the smallest).
4. 15 V is applied across 2 kΩ and 3 kΩ in series. The voltage across the 3 kΩ resistor is…
Answer: B. Divider: 15 · 3/(2+3) = 15 · 0.6 = 9 V.
Analysis techniques · Week 3
5. A network is a 10 V source feeding 3 kΩ in series, with 6 kΩ from the output node to ground. Looking into the output terminals, Rth =
Answer: C. Short the source; from the terminals you see 3 kΩ ∥ 6 kΩ = (3·6)/9 = 2 kΩ.
6. A source has Vth = 20 V, Rth = 10 Ω. The maximum power it can deliver to a matched load is…
Answer: B. Pmax = Vth²/(4Rth) = 400/40 = 10 W (load matched at RL = Rth = 10 Ω).
7. Superposition can NOT be applied directly to a circuit that contains…
Answer: C. Superposition needs linearity; a diode is nonlinear, so responses don't simply add.
Energy storage & first-order transients · Weeks 4–5
8. The energy stored in a 2 µF capacitor charged to 10 V is…
Answer: A. E = ½CV² = ½ · 2×10⁻⁶ · 10² = 1×10⁻⁴ J = 100 µJ.
9. In DC steady state (long after switching), an ideal inductor behaves like a…
Answer: D. With constant current, v = L·di/dt = 0, so the inductor looks like a wire. (A capacitor, by contrast, looks like an open circuit.)
10. An RC circuit has R = 10 kΩ and C = 10 µF. Its time constant τ is…
Answer: A. τ = RC = (10×10³)(10×10⁻⁶) = 0.1 s = 100 ms.
11. A capacitor discharging through a resistor falls to roughly what fraction of its initial voltage after one time constant?
Answer: B. v = V₀e−t/τ; at t = τ, e−1 ≈ 0.37, so ~37% remains.
12. A charging capacitor is considered essentially fully charged (~99%) after about…
Answer: C. After 5τ, e−5 ≈ 0.7%, so the capacitor is ~99.3% charged — the usual "settled" rule of thumb.
AC steady-state · Week 6
13. As frequency increases, the impedance magnitude of a capacitor…
Answer: D. |ZC| = 1/(ωC) — inversely proportional to frequency, so it drops as ω rises.
14. At ω = 1000 rad/s, a 50 mH inductor has an impedance magnitude of…
Answer: A. ZL = ωL = 1000 · 0.05 = 50 Ω.
15. In a purely capacitive element, the current…
Answer: A. i = C·dv/dt — the current leads the voltage by 90° in a capacitor ("ICE": in a Capacitor, I leads E). In an inductor it lags.
Second-order circuits & resonance · Week 7
16. A series LC with L = 1 mH and C = 1 µF resonates at approximately…
Answer: C. ω₀ = 1/√(LC) = 1/√(10⁻³·10⁻⁶) = 1/√(10⁻⁹) ≈ 3.16 × 10⁴ rad/s.
17. A series RLC circuit with very small resistance is…
Answer: C. Low R means little damping, so the response rings — underdamped. Large R gives overdamped; the boundary is critical damping.
18. The quality factor Q of a resonant circuit primarily describes…
Answer: D. High Q ⟹ a narrow, sharp resonance (more selective); low Q ⟹ a broad peak.
Operational amplifiers · Week 8
19. An inverting amplifier uses Rf = 100 kΩ and Rin = 10 kΩ. Its voltage gain is…
Answer: D. Inverting gain = −Rf/Rin = −100k/10k = −10.
20. A non-inverting amplifier uses Rf = 20 kΩ and Rin = 10 kΩ (to ground). Its gain is…
Answer: B. Non-inverting gain = 1 + Rf/Rin = 1 + 20k/10k = 3.
21. Which is a property of the ideal op-amp?
Answer: D. Ideal op-amp: infinite input impedance (no current into the inputs), infinite open-loop gain, and zero output impedance.
Diodes & transistors · Weeks 9–10
22. An ideal diode that is forward-biased behaves like a…
Answer: B. Forward-biased it conducts freely (closed switch); reverse-biased it blocks (open switch).
23. A half-wave rectifier's output…
Answer: D. A single diode conducts on only one polarity, so only one half of each cycle reaches the output.
24. An n-channel MOSFET begins to conduct (turns on) once its gate-source voltage exceeds the…
Answer: B. The channel forms only when VGS > Vth (the threshold voltage) — the basis of the MOSFET as a switch.
25. In a common-emitter BJT amplifier, the output signal is…
Answer: D. The common-emitter stage inverts: rising input raises collector current, pulling the output down — a 180° phase shift.
