Floorplanning & power/clock · Weeks 1–2
1. Floorplanning is…
Answer: B. Floorplanning kicks off physical design: it sets die size, places I/O and macros, and defines the power grid before placement and routing.
2. Target core utilization is typically…
Answer: C. Around 60–75% leaves slack for buffers, CTS cells, and routing tracks; packing too tight causes congestion.
3. Large hard macros (SRAM/IP) are placed…
Answer: A. Hard macros are committed early, pushed to edges/corners with pins oriented toward the standard-cell logic to keep the core open and routable.
4. The power delivery network (PDN)…
Answer: D. The PDN is the grid of rails and straps delivering VDD/VSS everywhere while keeping IR drop within budget.
5. Making the power grid denser…
Answer: B. A denser grid lowers resistance and IR drop, but the extra metal consumes routing tracks that signals could have used — a classic trade-off.
Placement & CTS · Weeks 3–4
6. Placement assigns…
Answer: A. Placement decides where every standard cell sits, balancing wirelength, congestion, and timing.
7. Global placement is followed by…
Answer: C. Global placement gives approximate locations; legalization then snaps cells onto rows and removes overlaps before later steps.
8. Timing-driven placement…
Answer: B. By clustering cells on critical paths, timing-driven placement shortens their nets and reduces delay.
9. Clock tree synthesis builds…
Answer: D. CTS inserts buffers/inverters to fan out the clock to all flops while balancing arrival times (minimizing skew).
10. Clock skew effectively…
Answer: A. Skew (clock arriving at different times) eats into the cycle available for logic, shrinking the usable period and timing budget.
Routing & timing signoff · Weeks 5–6
11. Detailed routing must produce…
Answer: C. Detailed routing lays the actual metal/vias, delivering a fully-connected, DRC-clean layout (global routing only plans).
12. Antenna violations are caused by…
Answer: B. During plasma etch, charge collects on large exposed metal and can punch through thin gate oxide before higher layers connect.
13. Antenna violations are fixed by…
Answer: D. Antenna diodes bleed off accumulated charge, and layer-jumping breaks up the long collecting metal so the gate is protected.
14. Setup timing is usually worst at the…
Answer: A. Setup needs data to arrive in time; the slow corner gives maximum delay, making setup hardest to meet.
15. Hold timing is usually worst at the…
Answer: C. Hold fails when data races through too quickly; the fast corner (minimum delay) is the worst case for hold.
16. A false path is…
Answer: B. A false path can't be sensitized in real operation, so it's marked and excluded from timing analysis (a multicycle path, by contrast, just gets extra cycles).
Signal & power integrity · Weeks 7–8
17. Crosstalk is caused by…
Answer: D. Adjacent wires couple capacitively, so a switching aggressor injects charge into its neighbor — that's crosstalk.
18. When an aggressor net switches it can…
Answer: A. Coupling has two effects: it shifts the victim's switching timing (delay) and, on a quiet victim, injects a noise glitch.
19. Shielding a critical net means…
Answer: C. Flanking a sensitive net with grounded (or power) wires absorbs coupling, isolating it from aggressors.
20. Electromigration is…
Answer: B. High current density gradually drags metal atoms along, thinning wires until they open — a long-term reliability limit that caps current per wire.
21. Decoupling capacitors…
Answer: D. Decaps act as local charge reservoirs, delivering fast switching current and smoothing dynamic IR drop / supply noise.
ECO & signoff/DFM · Weeks 9–10
22. A metal-only ECO…
Answer: A. A metal-only ECO rewires existing spare/filler cells by editing just the metal and via layers, leaving the base transistor layers untouched.
23. Metal-only ECOs are valued because…
Answer: C. Since the base layers and their masks are reused, only a few metal masks are remade — far cheaper and quicker than a full respin.
24. DFM (design for manufacturability)…
Answer: B. DRC just checks the hard rules; DFM adds yield-friendly practices (via doubling, wire spreading, OPC-friendly shapes) that go beyond merely passing DRC.
25. Tape-out requires…
Answer: D. Tape-out demands the entire signoff checklist pass — DRC, LVS, timing, IR drop, EM, antenna, and DFM — not just one check.
