Week 8 · Lecture 8

Interconnects & Metallization

Building the transistors is only half the job. Now we have to wire millions of them together — with a stack of metal layers laid above the silicon.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Explain why a chip needs many stacked metal layers joined vertically by vias.
  • Describe the copper damascene process and why it replaced etched aluminum.
  • Identify the role of M1–M10+ metal levels: thin/dense at the bottom, thick for power at the top.
  • State why interconnect RC delay can rival or exceed gate delay at advanced nodes.
  • Explain how copper and low-k dielectrics together reduce wire delay.

8.1Wiring the Chip

A modern chip holds billions of transistors, but a transistor on its own does nothing — it has to be connected to its neighbors. Fitting all those connections onto a flat surface is impossible, so the wiring is built upward: many metal layers are stacked above the devices, separated by insulating dielectric, and joined vertically by short metal plugs called vias.

The result is a dense three-dimensional mesh. Signals run horizontally along a metal layer, then drop or climb through a via to another layer, then run horizontally again — threading their way between any two of the billions of devices below.

AnalogyThe interconnect is the chip's road network, stacked in layers like a multi-level highway interchange. Each metal layer is a deck of roads; the vias are the ramps that let traffic move between decks. Without the ramps, the roads on each level would be isolated islands.

8.2From Aluminum to Copper Damascene

For decades the wires were aluminum, made the obvious way: deposit a blanket of metal, then etch it away everywhere except the wires you want to keep. Copper has lower resistance — better wires — but there's a catch: copper is very hard to etch. There's no clean plasma etch that carves copper into fine lines.

The fix is to turn the process inside out. The damascene process never etches the metal at all. Instead it:

etch trenches in the dielectric → fill them with copper → CMP-polish flushCMP = chemical-mechanical planarization, which grinds the surface smooth and removes the overfill.

So the dielectric is patterned (which etches easily), copper is deposited to overfill the trenches, and chemical-mechanical polishing scrapes the top down until only the copper inside the trenches remains. The surface is left perfectly flat, ready for the next layer.

Common mistakeCopper is deposited INTO etched trenches (damascene) precisely because it is hard to etch directly — this is the opposite of the older aluminum approach (deposit a sheet, then etch the metal away). And remember: it is the interconnect, not only the transistors, that can limit chip speed — through wire RC delay.

8.3Vias & Multilevel Interconnect

One layer of wires is nowhere near enough. Advanced chips stack ten or more metal levels — labeled M1, M2, M3 … M10+ from the bottom up — and connect adjacent levels with vias.

The levels are deliberately not all the same. The lower layers (M1, M2) are thin and densely packed, carrying the fine, short local signals between nearby transistors. As you climb, the wires get wider and thicker: the top layers carry global signals across the whole chip and distribute power and ground, where low resistance and high current-carrying capacity matter most.

M1, M2 → thin, dense, local  ·  M8, M9, M10+ → thick, wide, global / powerA signal may traverse several layers and vias to get from one block of the chip to another.

Every jump between levels costs a via, and a via is itself a tiny resistor. Designers route signals to minimize the number of layer changes on critical paths.

8.4Interconnect Delay & Low-k

In the early days, a chip's speed was set almost entirely by how fast its transistors (gates) could switch. Wires were treated as free. That is no longer true. At advanced nodes, the resistance R of a thin wire and the capacitance C between neighboring wires combine into an RC delay that can rival or even exceed the gate delay.

Two materials choices fight back against RC delay:

copper → lower R  ·  low-k dielectric → lower C  ⟹  lower R·C delay"low-k" means a dielectric with a small relative permittivity — less capacitance between wires.

Copper (from §8.2) cuts the resistance; replacing ordinary silicon dioxide with a low-k dielectric cuts the capacitance. Together they shrink the wire's RC product and keep the interconnect from throttling the chip.

✦ Worked Example

Why are long global wires especially slow? Estimate how a wire's RC delay scales with its length.

  1. Delay ∝ R·C. A wire's signal delay grows with the product of its resistance R and its capacitance C.
  2. Both R and C grow with length. A longer wire has proportionally more resistance (R ∝ length) and more capacitance to its surroundings (C ∝ length).
  3. So delay ∝ length². Since R·C = (∝ length)·(∝ length), the RC delay grows roughly with the square of the wire length.
  4. Numeric check: double the length ⟹ R → 2R and C → 2C ⟹ RC → (2R)(2C) = 4·RC. Doubling a wire's length roughly quadruples its RC delay.
  5. Consequences: long global wires are widened and broken up with buffers, and low-k dielectrics (lower C) plus copper (lower R) are used to keep the delay manageable.

❓ Lecture 8 Quiz

5 questions · click "Show answer" to check yourself.

Q1. Modern chips wire their transistors together using…
A. copper, via the damascene process
B. etched aluminum only
C. gold wires
D. polysilicon
Show answer

A. Advanced interconnect is copper, formed by the damascene process. Aluminum was the older approach; gold and polysilicon are not used for the main interconnect.

Q2. The damascene process is used for copper because…
A. copper is cheap
B. copper is hard to etch, so trenches are filled and then polished
C. copper etches easily
D. copper needs no barrier
Show answer

B. Because copper can't be cleanly etched, the dielectric is patterned, the trenches are filled with copper, and CMP polishes the surface flush.

Q3. Vertical connections between metal layers are called…
A. vias
B. pads
C. channels
D. wells
Show answer

A. Vias are the short vertical metal plugs that join one metal layer to the next. Pads are external bond points; channels and wells are transistor features.

Q4. At advanced nodes, interconnect RC delay…
A. is always negligible
B. can exceed the gate delay and limit speed
C. only affects power
D. disappears with scaling
Show answer

B. Wire RC delay can rival or exceed gate delay at small nodes, so the interconnect — not just the transistors — can set the chip's speed.

Q5. Low-k dielectrics are used to…
A. reduce interconnect capacitance (and thus RC delay)
B. increase resistance
C. speed up etching
D. raise the supply voltage
Show answer

A. A lower dielectric constant means less capacitance C between wires, which lowers the R·C delay. Copper handles the R side.

✓ What you learned

  • Billions of transistors are wired by many stacked metal layers joined vertically by vias, all embedded in dielectric.
  • The copper damascene process etches trenches in the dielectric, fills them with copper, and CMP-polishes flush — because copper is hard to etch directly.
  • Metal levels M1–M10+ go from thin/dense/local at the bottom to thick/wide for power distribution at the top.
  • At advanced nodes, interconnect RC delay can exceed gate delay; delay grows roughly with length².
  • Copper (lower R) plus low-k dielectrics (lower C) together reduce wire delay.
