What Is a Plasma? Debye Shielding · Week 1
1. Holding T_e fixed, doubling the electron density n_e changes the Debye length λ_D by a factor of about…
Answer: B. λ_D = √(ε₀k_BT_e/n_ee²) ∝ 1/√n_e, so doubling n_e scales λ_D by 1/√2 ≈ 0.71.
2. A gas satisfies λ_D ≪ L (criterion 1) but has a plasma parameter N_D ≈ 0.5. Which of the following is true?
Answer: C. All three plasma criteria matter independently. With only ~1 particle per Debye sphere, shielding is dominated by noisy individual close encounters rather than a smooth collective response — the "plasma" description of Lecture 1 requires N_D ≫ 1 too.
The Plasma Frequency & Plasma Parameter · Week 2
3. For n_e = 4×10¹⁸ m⁻³, the electron plasma frequency ω_pe is approximately…
Answer: A. ω_pe ≈ 56.4·√n_e[m⁻³] = 56.4·√(4×10¹⁸) ≈ 56.4×(2×10⁹) ≈ 1.13×10¹¹ rad/s.
4. Quadrupling the electron density n_e (holding everything else fixed) changes ω_pe by a factor of…
Answer: D. ω_pe ∝ √n_e, so quadrupling n_e scales ω_pe by √4 = 2.
5. The requirement N_D ≫ 1 (many particles per Debye sphere) is essential because it is exactly what licenses…
Answer: B. Both the Debye-shielding and plasma-oscillation derivations implicitly treat charge as a smooth fluid density — valid only when many particles populate the relevant volume, exactly what N_D ≫ 1 guarantees.
Single-Particle Motion in E and B Fields · Week 3
6. An electron with v_⊥ = 3×10⁵ m/s gyrates in B = 0.2 T. Its Larmor radius r_L is approximately…
Answer: C. r_L = m_ev_⊥/(eB) = (9.11×10⁻³¹ × 3×10⁵)/(1.602×10⁻¹⁹ × 0.2) ≈ 8.53×10⁻⁶ m = 8.53 µm.
7. At the same B field, the ratio of the electron cyclotron frequency to the proton cyclotron frequency, ω_c,e/ω_c,p, is approximately…
Answer: A. ω_c = qB/m is independent of energy, so ω_c,e/ω_c,p = m_p/m_e ≈ 1836. (The √(m_p/m_e) ≈ 43 figure is the Larmor-radius ratio at equal energy, a different comparison.)
8. Uniform E = 2000 V/m and B = 0.1 T, mutually perpendicular. The E×B drift speed is…
Answer: D. v_E = E/B = 2000/0.1 = 2×10⁴ m/s — charge- and mass-independent, so ions and electrons drift together.
Guiding-Center Drifts · Week 4
9. Unlike the E×B drift, the grad-B drift v_∇B…
Answer: B. v_∇B = (mv_⊥²/2qB³)B×∇B carries an explicit q, flipping direction with charge sign and generating a current — unlike the charge-blind E×B drift.
10. The curvature drift v_R scales with the particle's velocity components as…
Answer: C. v_R ∝ mv_∥²/(qB²R_c) — it comes from motion along the curved field line, so it depends on v_∥², not the perpendicular speed that drives grad-B drift.
11. A 200 eV (perpendicular KE) proton sits in B₀ = 0.5 T with a grad-B scale length L_B = 0.4 m. The grad-B drift speed v_∇B is approximately…
Answer: A. v_⊥ = √(2KE/m_p) ≈ 1.957×10⁵ m/s, r_L = v_⊥/ω_c ≈ 4.09×10⁻³ m, v_∇B = ½v_⊥r_L/L_B ≈ 1000 m/s.
Magnetic Mirrors & Adiabatic Invariants · Week 5
12. A magnetic mirror has B_min = 0.2 T and B_max = 2.0 T. The loss-cone half-angle θ_loss is approximately…
Answer: D. R_m = B_max/B_min = 10, θ_loss = arcsin(√(1/R_m)) = arcsin(√0.1) ≈ 18.4°.
13. For that same mirror (R_m = 10), the fraction of an isotropic midplane population that falls inside the loss cone and escapes (both cones combined) is approximately…
Answer: B. Lost fraction = 1 − cos(θ_loss) = 1 − cos(18.4°) ≈ 5.1%.
14. A particle sits well outside the loss cone (safely trapped) in a magnetic mirror with no collisions present. After many bounce periods, this particle…
Answer: C. The loss-cone fraction (e.g. the ~0.5% at R_m = 100) is the fraction of an isotropic population that starts inside the cone and escapes essentially immediately — not a per-bounce attrition rate on already-trapped particles. Bouncing conserves μ and energy exactly, so without collisions a trapped particle stays trapped forever; collisions are what continuously refill the loss cone.
Collisions & Cross-Sections · Week 6
15. Tripling the electron temperature T_e (holding density fixed) changes the electron-ion collision frequency ν_ei by a factor of about…
Answer: A. ν_ei ∝ T_e^(−3/2), so tripling T_e scales ν_ei by 3^(−3/2) ≈ 0.192 — faster electrons spend less time near any ion and are deflected less.
16. A plasma process is well-approximated as "collisionless" precisely when…
Answer: D. "Collisionless" is always relative to a timescale: if the process of interest completes many times before a collision randomizes velocity, collisions can be neglected for that process.
Fluid vs Kinetic Descriptions · Week 7
17. Which fluid quantity is the first velocity moment of the distribution function f(r,v,t)?
Answer: B. Density is the zeroth moment (∫f d³v), flow velocity u is the first moment (∫v f d³v, normalized by n), and pressure is the second moment. The magnetic field isn't a moment of f at all.
18. The "closure problem" in fluid plasma theory refers to the fact that…
Answer: C. Density's equation needs flow, flow's needs pressure, pressure's needs heat flux, and so on — a finite fluid model requires an extra closure assumption to cut the chain off somewhere.
Plasma Sheaths & Boundaries · Week 8
19. A floating surface in a hydrogen plasma with T_e = 8 eV sits at a floating potential V_f of approximately…
Answer: A. V_f = −(T_e/2)ln(m_i/2πm_e) ≈ −2.84×T_e[eV] ≈ −22.7 V for T_e = 8 eV.
20. The Bohm sheath criterion requires ions to enter the sheath at a speed of at least…
Answer: D. The Bohm speed uses T_e, not T_i, because electron pressure drives the presheath field that accelerates ions up to c_s before they reach the sheath edge.
21. If the sheath potential drop ΔV increases by a factor of 16 (holding T_e fixed), the Child–Langmuir sheath thickness s increases by a factor of about…
Answer: B. s ~ λ_D(ΔV/T_e)^(3/4), and 16^(3/4) = 8 — growing sublinearly (slower than proportionally) with the potential drop, not faster than linearly.
Diagnostics — Measuring a Plasma · Week 9
22. In a Langmuir probe's I-V trace, the ion-saturation region (flat, negative current) occurs when the probe voltage is…
Answer: C. At strongly negative bias, the probe repels essentially all electrons and collects only the Bohm ion flux — the flat "ion saturation" current.
23. To extract n_e from a Langmuir probe's ion-saturation current I_isat = 0.6 n_e e A c_s, you must first…
Answer: A. c_s appears explicitly in the Bohm current formula, so T_e (typically from the exponential-region fit) must be known before n_e can be solved for.
Capstone: Particle-Drift Simulator · Week 10
24. For a uniform field B = Bẑ, the correct guiding-center transform that removes a particle's gyration from its position r is…
Answer: D. The particle's displacement from its guiding center is exactly the gyro-radius vector ρ = (ẑ×v)/ω_c, so subtracting it from r removes the gyration to leading order.
25. The capstone's direct numerical integration of the Lorentz force, compared against the analytic E×B and grad-B drift formulas, demonstrates…
Answer: B. Direct numerical integration makes no guiding-center approximation at all, so comparing it to the analytic drift formulas is a genuine, independent check of both the formulas and the specific regime in which they hold.
