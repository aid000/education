Single-Particle Motion in E and B Fields

Forget the whole plasma for a moment. Follow just one charged particle through a magnetic field, and you already get the circling motion that makes magnetic confinement conceivable in the first place.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Write and interpret the Lorentz force law F = q(E + v×B).
  • Solve the equation of motion for a charged particle in a uniform B field: cyclotron gyration.
  • Define and compute the cyclotron (gyro) frequency ω_c and the Larmor (gyro) radius r_L.
  • Derive the E×B drift velocity for combined uniform, static E and B fields.
  • Explain why gyration is the single fact that makes magnetic confinement of a plasma conceivable.

3.1The Lorentz Force

Every particle-motion result in this course — gyration, drifts, mirrors, all of it — comes from one equation. A particle of charge q and mass m in electric field E and magnetic field B feels the Lorentz force:

m (dv/dt) = q ( E + v × B )Electric fields do work and change speed; magnetic fields never do work — they only turn the velocity vector.

That last point is worth dwelling on. The magnetic term v×B is always perpendicular to v, so B can never change a particle's kinetic energy — it can only steer it. Everything interesting a magnetic field does to a plasma is about where particles go, never about speeding them up or slowing them down directly.

3.2Pure B Field: Cyclotron Gyration

Set E = 0 and let B = B ẑ be uniform. Write v = v∥ ẑ + v⊥, splitting the velocity into components parallel and perpendicular to B. The Lorentz force has no z-component when E = 0 (since v×B lies entirely in the xy-plane for B along z), so v∥ is simply constant — a particle slides freely along field lines.

The perpendicular motion is more interesting. Writing out the x- and y-components of m dv/dt = qv×B gives a pair of coupled equations whose solution is uniform circular motion: vx and vy trace a circle in velocity space at a constant angular rate, the cyclotron frequency (also called the gyrofrequency):

ω_c = |q| B / mSet entirely by charge, mass, and field strength — independent of the particle's speed or energy.

The particle itself traces a circle of radius equal to the Larmor radius (gyroradius):

r_L = m v_⊥ / (|q| B) = v_⊥ / ω_cFaster particles and weaker fields both mean bigger circles.

A positive charge in B = Bẑ gyrates clockwise when viewed looking down the field line (from +z toward the origin); a negative charge (an electron) gyrates the opposite way. Either way, the combined motion — circular gyration perpendicular to B plus free streaming along B — traces out a helix wrapped around the field line.

AnalogyA charged particle in a magnetic field behaves like a bead threaded on a wire that itself can bend, except the "wire" here is the field line and the bead never touches it — it corkscrews around it at a fixed radius, held on by the Lorentz force instead of a physical constraint. Stronger field or lighter/slower particle ⇒ tighter corkscrew.

3.3Adding a Uniform E Field: the E×B Drift

Now switch on a uniform, static electric field E perpendicular to B, on top of the uniform B. The trick to solving m dv/dt = q(E + v×B) is to guess that the velocity splits into a constant drift vE plus a gyration velocity v′ that still circles exactly as in Section 3.2:

v = v_E + v′,   where m (dv′/dt) = q v′ × BIf this works, v_E must absorb all the E-field physics and leave v′ with pure gyration.

Substituting into the full equation of motion: m(dv_E/dt + dv′/dt) = q(E + (v_E+v′)×B). Since v_E is constant (dv_E/dt = 0) and dv′/dt already equals (q/m)v′×B by the guess above, what remains is q(E + v_E×B) = 0 — the E-field force must be exactly canceled by the magnetic force on the drift itself. Solving for vE (crossing both sides with B and using B×(B×something)=…):

v_E×B = (E × B) / B²The E×B drift. Notice: no q, no m anywhere. Electrons and ions drift together, at the same velocity, in the same direction.

This is the single most important result in guiding-center theory. Because the drift is independent of charge and mass, E×B drift does not separate charge — it carries the whole plasma sideways as a unit, which is exactly why it's usable for confinement (and exactly why it's exploited deliberately in Hall thrusters and magnetrons).

Common mistakeDon't confuse the drift velocity v_E = E×B/B² with the gyration velocity v′. The full motion is gyration (fast circle of radius r_L, charge-dependent direction) superimposed on drift (slow, steady, charge-independent sideways motion) — the particle's actual trajectory is a cycloid: a circle whose center itself glides at v_E. Students often report only one or the other as "the" velocity.
✦ Worked Example — gyroradius, gyrofrequency, and E×B drift

A 100 eV electron and a 100 eV proton both gyrate in B = 1 T. Separately, E = 1×10⁴ V/m (10 kV/m) is applied perpendicular to a B = 0.5 T field. Find ω_c and r_L for each particle, and the E×B drift speed.

  1. Electron cyclotron frequency: ω_ce = eB/m_e = (1.602×10⁻¹⁹)(1) / (9.109×10⁻³¹) ≈ 1.76×10¹¹ rad/s (f_ce ≈ 28.0 GHz).
  2. Electron speed at 100 eV: v = √(2·100·1.602×10⁻¹⁹/9.109×10⁻³¹) ≈ 5.93×10⁶ m/s. Larmor radius r_L = v/ω_ce ≈ 3.37×10⁻⁵ m (about 34 µm — tiny).
  3. Proton cyclotron frequency: ω_ci = eB/m_p ≈ 9.58×10⁷ rad/s (f_ci ≈ 15.2 MHz) — about 1836× slower than the electron, exactly the mass ratio.
  4. Proton Larmor radius at 100 eV: v ≈ 1.38×10⁵ m/s, so r_L ≈ 1.45×10⁻³ m (1.45 mm) — about 43× larger than the electron's, the √(m_p/m_e) scaling.
  5. E×B drift: v_E = E/B = (1×10⁴)/(0.5) = 2.0×10⁴ m/s, the same for the electron and the proton — and for any other charge or mass.

🛠️ Hands-On: verify r_L, ω_c, and v_E×B numerically

Integrate the Lorentz force directly with scipy.integrate.solve_ivp and check the analytic formulas above against a real numerical trajectory — the same technique the Lecture 10 capstone scales up.

# single_particle_motion.py — Lecture 3
import numpy as np
from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp

q, m = 1.602176634e-19, 1.67262192369e-27   # proton
B0, E0 = 0.5, 1.0e4                          # Tesla, V/m (E along x, B along z)

def rhs(t, s):
    x, y, z, vx, vy, vz = s
    ax = (q/m)*(E0 + vy*B0)
    ay = (q/m)*(-vx*B0)
    return [vx, vy, vz, ax, ay, 0.0]

wc = q*B0/m
Tc = 2*np.pi/wc
sol = solve_ivp(rhs, (0, 20*Tc), [0,0,0, 0,0,0], max_step=Tc/200)

vy_late = sol.y[4][sol.t > 15*Tc]       # drop the first few gyrations, keep the steady part
print(f"omega_c computed = {wc:.4e} rad/s")
print(f"analytic v_ExB (y-dir) = {-E0/B0:.4e} m/s")
print(f"time-averaged vy (numeric drift) ~ {np.mean(vy_late):.4e} m/s")

# omega_c computed = 4.7894e+07 rad/s
# analytic v_ExB (y-dir) = -2.0000e+04 m/s
# time-averaged vy (numeric drift) ~ -1.9960e+04 m/s   (matches to ~0.2%)

The particle starts at rest, so its trajectory is a cycloid (the textbook case): the time-averaged velocity converges to E×B/B² once the gyration has had a few periods to average out.

💬 Discussion 1

A magnetized particle spirals along field lines instead of flying off in a straight line. That single fact — gyration confines motion perpendicular to B to a tiny Larmor radius while leaving motion along B unconstrained — is the whole reason magnetic confinement of a fusion plasma is even conceivable. Why does confining only two of three dimensions still count as useful confinement? (Hint: think about what shape of field line would fix the third.)

🔗 Where this is heading

E×B drift is the first of a whole family of guiding-center drifts. Next week we add non-uniform B fields and find two more: the grad-B drift and the curvature drift, both of which — unlike E×B — do depend on charge, meaning they push ions and electrons in opposite directions and generate currents. Q1. Why can a magnetic field never change a charged particle's kinetic energy?
A. Magnetic fields are always too weak to matter
B. The force qv×B is always perpendicular to v, so it does no work
C. Magnetic fields only act on neutral particles
D. Energy conservation forbids it by definition
Show answer

B. Work = F·v dt; since v×B ⟂ v always, (v×B)·v = 0, so the magnetic force does zero work regardless of field strength. It can steer the particle but never speed it up or slow it down.

Q2. Doubling the magnetic field strength B (holding v_⊥ fixed) changes the Larmor radius r_L by a factor of…
A. 2
B. 4
C. 1/2
D. no change
Show answer

C. r_L = mv_⊥/(qB), so r_L ∝ 1/B. Doubling B halves the gyroradius — a stronger field confines particles to tighter circles.

Q3. Compared to a proton with the same kinetic energy in the same B field, an electron's gyrofrequency ω_c is…
A. about 1836× smaller
B. about 1836× larger
C. the same
D. about 43× larger
Show answer

B. ω_c = qB/m ∝ 1/m, and m_p/m_e ≈ 1836, so the lighter electron gyrates about 1836× faster. (D) describes the Larmor-radius ratio instead, which goes as √(m_p/m_e) ≈ 43 — a different comparison at equal energy.

Q4. The E×B drift velocity v_E = E×B/B²…
A. depends on the particle's charge sign, so ions and electrons drift oppositely
B. depends on the particle's mass but not its charge
C. is the same for every charged particle regardless of charge or mass
D. is zero unless the particle is at rest initially
Show answer

C. q and m both cancel out of the derivation entirely — every species drifts together at E×B/B², which is why this drift moves the whole plasma as a unit rather than separating charge.

Q5. The actual trajectory of a charged particle released from rest into combined uniform, perpendicular E and B fields is…
A. a straight line along E
B. a circle with no net translation
C. a cycloid — gyration superimposed on a steady E×B drift
D. exponential runaway along B
Show answer

C. Decomposing v = v_E + v′ shows v′ undergoes pure gyration while the guiding center glides at the constant drift v_E — the combined path traced out by a particle starting from rest is the classic cycloid.
