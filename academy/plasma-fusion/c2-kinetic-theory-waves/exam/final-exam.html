Distribution functions & moments · Week 1
1. The distribution function f(r,v,t) is defined so that f d³r d³v equals…
Answer: D. f is a phase-space number density: f·d³r·d³v counts particles in a small 6-D box around (r,v). The other options describe unrelated quantities (Lecture 1).
2. A cold electron beam (effectively a delta function in velocity) has density n = 2×10¹⁹ m⁻³ and drift velocity u = 3×10⁵ m/s. Its particle flux n·u is…
Answer: B. n·u = (2×10¹⁹)(3×10⁵) = 6×10²⁴ m⁻²s⁻¹ — the first velocity moment (flux) for a cold, single-velocity beam (verified with Python).
The Vlasov equation · Week 2
3. In the Vlasov equation ∂f/∂t + v·∇_r f + (q/m)(E+v×B)·∇_v f = 0, the term v·∇_r f represents…
Answer: A. v·∇_r f is spatial advection — free streaming. The Lorentz-force term handles velocity-space transport instead (Lecture 2).
4. A field-free 1-D distribution obeys f(x,v,t)=f₀(x−vt,v). For the velocity group v=1×10⁵ m/s, initially peaked at x=0, where is that peak located at t=2×10⁻⁶ s?
Answer: C. x = v·t = (1×10⁵)(2×10⁻⁶) = 0.2 m = 20 cm — direct substitution into the free-streaming solution (verified with Python).
Linearization & the dispersion-relation method · Week 3
5. The resonance denominator (ω−kv) in the linearized Vlasov solution f₁(v) vanishes at…
Answer: B. Particles moving at exactly the phase velocity ω/k satisfy ω−kv=0 — the resonant particles central to Landau damping and the two-stream instability (Lecture 3).
6. For a wave with ω=5×10¹¹ rad/s and k=3×10⁶ rad/m, the resonant velocity v=ω/k is…
Answer: D. v = ω/k = (5×10¹¹)/(3×10⁶) ≈ 1.667×10⁵ m/s (verified with Python).
Langmuir waves & the Bohm-Gross relation · Week 4
7. In deriving the Bohm-Gross relation, the ions are treated as…
Answer: A. On the fast electron-plasma-oscillation timescale, ions are far too massive to move — they just maintain charge neutrality (Lecture 4).
8. For an electron density n_e = 4×10¹⁹ m⁻³, the plasma frequency ω_pe = √(n_ee²/ε₀m_e) is approximately…
Answer: C. ω_pe = √(4×10¹⁹ · e²/(ε₀m_e)) ≈ 3.568×10¹¹ rad/s (verified with Python; note this is √4 ≈ 2× Lecture 4's n_e=1×10¹⁹ value of 1.78×10¹¹).
9. For kλ_De = 0.15, the Bohm-Gross relation ω/ω_pe = √(1+3(kλ_De)²) gives ω/ω_pe ≈…
Answer: B. √(1+3(0.15)²) = √1.0675 ≈ 1.0332 (verified with Python).
Ion-acoustic waves · Week 5
10. The electron Boltzmann relation n_e1/n₀ = eφ₁/k_BT_e, used in the ion-acoustic derivation, is valid because…
Answer: D. At ion-acoustic frequencies (far below ω_pe), electrons continuously re-equilibrate, justifying the instantaneous Boltzmann relation (Lecture 5).
11. A deuterium plasma has T_e=8 eV, T_i=2 eV (ion mass M≈2m_p). The ion-acoustic speed c_s=√[(T_e+3T_i)e/M] is approximately…
Answer: A. c_s = √[(8+3·2)(1.602×10⁻¹⁹)/(2×1.673×10⁻²⁷)] ≈ 2.589×10⁴ m/s ≈ 25.9 km/s (verified with Python).
EM waves in unmagnetized plasma & cutoffs · Week 6
12. An EM wave with ω < ω_pe incident on an unmagnetized plasma is…
Answer: C. k²=(ω²−ω_pe²)/c² is negative below cutoff, so the field is evanescent and the wave reflects rather than propagates or dissipates (Lecture 6).
13. For n_e = 5×10¹⁸ m⁻³, the plasma cutoff frequency f_pe = ω_pe/2π is approximately…
Answer: B. ω_pe ≈ 1.261×10¹¹ rad/s, so f_pe = ω_pe/2π ≈ 2.008×10¹⁰ Hz ≈ 20.1 GHz (verified with Python).
Waves in magnetized plasma: R, L, O, X · Week 7
14. For R- and L-wave cutoffs propagating along B₀, which identity always holds?
Answer: D. ω_R·ω_L=ω_pe² and separately ω_R−ω_L=ω_ce — exact algebraic identities from the quadratic cutoff formulas (Lecture 7).
15. For ω_ce = 2×10¹⁰ rad/s and ω_pe = 1.5×10¹¹ rad/s, the R- and L-wave cutoffs ω_R=[ω_ce+√(ω_ce²+4ω_pe²)]/2 and ω_L=[−ω_ce+√(ω_ce²+4ω_pe²)]/2 are approximately…
Answer: A. Direct substitution gives ω_R≈1.603×10¹¹ rad/s, ω_L≈1.403×10¹¹ rad/s — check: ω_R·ω_L≈2.25×10²²=ω_pe² ✓, ω_R−ω_L=2×10¹⁰=ω_ce ✓ (verified with Python). Note the R cutoff sits above ω_pe and L below it — never swapped.
16. The O-mode's dispersion relation matches the unmagnetized EM wave (ω²=ω_pe²+k²c²) because…
Answer: C. Electron velocity parallel to B₀ makes v×B=0 — the O-mode simply doesn't feel the field, even propagating perpendicular to it (Lecture 7).
Landau damping · Week 8
17. For a Maxwellian, the sign of ∂f₀/∂v at the resonant velocity v=ω/k (for ω/k>0) is…
Answer: B. A Maxwellian is monotonically decreasing for v>0, so ∂f₀/∂v<0 there — more slow resonant particles gain energy from the wave than fast ones give back, draining it (Lecture 8).
18. Using the asymptotic Landau-damping formula γ/ω_pe = −√(π/8)(kλ_De)⁻³exp[−1/(2(kλ_De)²)−3/2], the value at kλ_De=0.25 is approximately…
Answer: D. γ/ω_pe = −√(π/8)(0.25)⁻³exp[−1/(2·0.0625)−1.5] ≈ −3.00×10⁻³ (verified with Python) — between Lecture 8's kλ_De=0.2 (−6.5×10⁻⁵) and 0.3 (−2.0×10⁻²) values, as expected from the formula's extreme sensitivity to kλ_De.
19. Landau damping is best described as…
Answer: A. No entropy-generating collision appears anywhere in the derivation — the macroscopic field decays because fine-scale phase-space structure becomes too tangled to contribute coherently, not because information is destroyed (Lecture 8).
The two-stream instability · Week 9
20. For the symmetric two-cold-beam model, the instability band exists only while…
Answer: C. The smaller root X₋ of the dispersion quadratic is negative (real growth) exactly when q=kv₀/ω_pe<1. Push kv₀ past ω_pe and both roots become positive — ordinary stable oscillations (Lecture 9).
21. For two counter-streaming beams with total density n₀=2×10¹⁹ m⁻³ and beam speed v₀=8×10⁵ m/s, the maximum growth rate γ_max=ω_pe/(2√2) and fastest-growing wavenumber k_max=ω_pe√(3/8)/v₀ are approximately…
Answer: B. ω_pe≈2.523×10¹¹ rad/s for n₀=2×10¹⁹ m⁻³, giving γ_max≈8.92×10¹⁰ rad/s and k_max≈1.931×10⁵ m⁻¹ (verified with Python).
22. A single Maxwellian can never drive a two-stream-type instability because…
Answer: D. A Maxwellian is single-peaked; you need a non-monotonic, multi-humped f₀ (like two beams) for a region of positive slope to drive growth — the heart of the Penrose stability criterion (Lecture 9).
Capstone: dispersion-relation solver & synthesis · Week 10
23. The fluid (Bohm-Gross) dispersion solver returns zero imaginary part for every k because…
Answer: A. Damping and growth both require the resonant-particle information carried only by the full velocity-space integral (with its pole at v=ω/k). A moment-based closure structurally cannot see this, regardless of the closure chosen (Lecture 10).
24. In the capstone's kinetic solver, ζ=ω/(kλ_De√2) (all normalized to ω_pe=1, no leftover v_th factor). For ω/ω_pe=1.05 and kλ_De=0.3, ζ is approximately…
Answer: C. ζ = 1.05/(0.3·√2) ≈ 2.475 (verified with Python). This dimensionless normalization is exactly what makes the fix to the capstone's dispersion solver dimensionally consistent — no SI v_th should ever appear once ω and k are already normalized.
25. Which statement best summarizes the capstone's central takeaway?
Answer: B. Fluid theory is a legitimate, useful leading-order approximation for the real part of ω, but damping and instability are intrinsically kinetic phenomena no amount of fluid-closure refinement can recover (Lecture 10).
