Week 2 · Lecture 2

The Vlasov Equation

One equation governs the entire distribution function f(r,v,t) — and it falls out of a single, almost trivial fact: nobody is throwing particles away.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • State Liouville's theorem and explain why collisionless phase-space flow is incompressible.
  • Derive the Vlasov equation from df/dt = 0 along particle trajectories.
  • Interpret each term of the Vlasov equation physically (streaming vs. force-driven drift in velocity space).
  • Explain the collisionless validity criterion and how the Vlasov equation couples to Maxwell's equations.
  • Verify, by hand and numerically, that a simple free-streaming f solves the Vlasov equation.

2.1Liouville's Theorem: Nobody Gets Lost

Follow one particle through phase space. Its position and velocity evolve by the ordinary equations of motion:

dr/dt = v      dv/dt = (q/m)(E + v×B)Position changes by velocity; velocity changes by the Lorentz force per unit mass.

Now imagine a small cloud of nearby particles in phase space — a tiny 6-D blob — all obeying these same equations. Does the blob's density change as it's swept along? Liouville's theorem says: not if the flow is incompressible, i.e. not if the phase-space "velocity" (v, a) has zero divergence. Check it term by term: the divergence of the streaming part is ∂v_i/∂r_i = 0 (velocity doesn't depend on position), and the divergence of the Lorentz-force part in velocity space is also zero — a short vector identity shows ∇_v·(v×B) = 0 for any B, and E doesn't depend on v at all. So the full phase-space flow is incompressible: the blob can stretch, shear, and fold into filaments, but its local density never changes.

AnalogyThink of phase space as an incompressible fluid — like ink dropped into still water. Stirring (the plasma's dynamics) stretches the ink into long, thin, tangled filaments, but you can never create or destroy ink; the local concentration along any given filament stays exactly what it started as. That "ink concentration" is f. Phase mixing (Lecture 8) is exactly this stretching, carried to the point where the filaments become too fine to resolve macroscopically.

2.2From Liouville to the Vlasov Equation

"Density doesn't change along the flow" is a statement about the total (convective) derivative of f — the rate of change seen by an observer riding along with a particle's trajectory:

df/dt = ∂f/∂t + (dr/dt)·∇_r f + (dv/dt)·∇_v f = 0Chain rule for f(r(t), v(t), t) along a trajectory, set to zero by Liouville's theorem (no collisions).

Substitute the equations of motion from §2.1 for dr/dt and dv/dt, and you have the Vlasov equation — the collisionless Boltzmann equation, and the single governing law for every topic in this course:

∂f/∂t + v·∇rf + (q/m)(E + v×B)·∇vf = 0The distribution function is advected through phase space by streaming (v·∇_r) and by the Lorentz-force-driven drift in velocity space ((q/m)(E+v×B)·∇_v).

Read the three terms physically. ∂f/∂t is how f changes at a fixed point in phase space. v·∇_r f is streaming: particles carrying their velocity value into new positions, exactly as in ordinary free flight. (q/m)(E+v×B)·∇_v f is the force term: the Lorentz force pushes particles to new velocities, sweeping f through velocity space the same way streaming sweeps it through position space. Set them in balance and f is conserved along the full, curving 6-D trajectory — this is just §2.1's "no ink is created or destroyed," written as a PDE.

2.3Self-Consistency and the Collisionless Limit

The Vlasov equation alone is incomplete: E and B are themselves generated by the charges and currents that f describes. Close the system with Maxwell's equations (or, for the electrostatic waves of Lectures 3–5, just Poisson's equation), where the source terms are moments of f:

ρ(r,t) = Σs qs∫fs d³v     J(r,t) = Σs qs∫v·fs d³vCharge and current density are literally the zeroth and first moments (§1.2) summed over species s. Plug these into Maxwell's equations for E and B, then feed E and B back into Vlasov. This closed loop is the Vlasov-Maxwell system.

This is exactly self-consistent and has no closure problem — unlike a fluid theory, which must guess a pressure equation to close the moment hierarchy (§1.2), kinetic theory simply carries all the moments at once, forever, inside f. The price is that f lives in 6-D instead of 3-D, which is why fluid theories (C3's MHD) remain useful shortcuts whenever they're valid.

The Vlasov equation describes a collisionless plasma — it dropped collisions entirely when we set df/dt=0. That's an excellent approximation exactly when C1's third plasma criterion holds: ωpeτcoll > 1, i.e. collective oscillations complete many cycles before a collision randomizes a particle's trajectory. A full Boltzmann equation would add a collision operator on the right-hand side (often modeled as a Fokker-Planck term); every wave phenomenon in this course — and the Landau damping of Lecture 8 in particular — is a genuinely collisionless effect, which is precisely what makes it surprising.

Common mistake"Collisionless" does not mean "nothing happens." The Vlasov equation is a rich nonlinear(ly coupled) PDE — it produces oscillations, waves, damping, and instabilities with zero collisions anywhere in it. What collisionless really restricts is the mechanism: any damping or relaxation you find from Vlasov alone must come from phase-space rearrangement (streaming, resonance, mixing), never from entropy-generating particle collisions.
✦ Worked Example — free streaming solves Vlasov

With no fields at all (E=B=0), the Vlasov equation in one dimension reduces to ∂f/∂t + v∂f/∂x = 0. Show that f(x,v,t) = f₀(x−vt, v) is an exact solution, and track two velocity groups starting at x=0.

  1. Check the solution: let g = x−vt. By the chain rule, ∂f/∂t = f₀′(g)·(−v) and ∂f/∂x = f₀′(g)·(1). So ∂f/∂t + v∂f/∂x = −v·f₀′(g) + v·f₀′(g) = 0. ✓ — it solves the equation for any initial profile f₀.
  2. Interpretation: f(x,v,t) = f₀(x−vt, v) says each velocity group simply translates at its own velocity — f's value at (x,v,t) is whatever it was at (x−vt, v) at t=0. Nothing is created, destroyed, or mixed in velocity; phase space just shears (the "ink" of §2.1 stretching under pure streaming).
  3. Numbers: electrons start at x₀=0 with two velocity groups, v₁=1×10⁶ m/s and v₂=2×10⁶ m/s. After t = 1 ns = 1×10⁻⁹ s: x₁ = v₁t = 1.0 mm, x₂ = v₂t = 2.0 mm.
  4. Phase mixing: the two groups, initially on top of each other at x=0, are now 1 mm apart and separating linearly in time. A vertical line in (x,v) space at t=0 has sheared into a tilted line by time t — the geometric picture behind Lecture 8's phase mixing, before any field is even switched on.

🛠️ Hands-On: watch phase space shear

No fields needed for this one — just the equations of motion. Push a handful of free-streaming "characteristics" (particle trajectories) forward in time and confirm x(t) = x₀ + vt exactly, reproducing the worked example's 1 mm / 2 mm numbers.

# free_streaming.py — Lecture 2
import numpy as np

v0 = np.array([-2e6, -1e6, 0.0, 1e6, 2e6])   # m/s, 5 velocity groups
x0 = np.zeros_like(v0)                        # all start at the origin
t = 1e-9                                      # 1 ns

x = x0 + v0 * t
print("x(t) [mm] =", x * 1000)
# Verified output:
# x(t) [mm] = [-2. -1.  0.  1.  2.]

Each characteristic carries its own f-value unchanged (f is whatever it was at t=0, evaluated at x−vt) — only its position moves. Run this for a dense grid of v instead of five points and plot (x,v) at increasing t: you'll watch the vertical line in the figure above tilt in real time.

🔗 Where this is heading

The free-streaming example above had E = 0 — nothing fed back into the dynamics. Real plasma waves are the opposite: a small charge-density ripple creates an E-field via Poisson's equation, that E-field pushes f around via the Vlasov equation's force term, and the resulting current changes the charge density again. Lecture 3 linearizes exactly this feedback loop — perturbing both f and E together and demanding they stay self-consistent — which is how every dispersion relation in this course gets built, from the Bohm-Gross relation (Lecture 4) all the way to Landau damping (Lecture 8).

Q1. Liouville's theorem applies to collisionless phase-space flow because…
A. the phase-space "velocity" (dr/dt, dv/dt) has zero divergence — the flow is incompressible
B. collisions always conserve momentum
C. the electric field is always zero
D. particles are indistinguishable
Show answer

A. ∇_r·v = 0 and ∇_v·[(q/m)(E+v×B)] = 0, so the 6-D phase-space flow is incompressible and local density (f) is conserved along trajectories. (C) is false in general — E can be nonzero; the divergence-free property holds either way.

Q2. In the Vlasov equation ∂f/∂t + v·∇_r f + (q/m)(E+v×B)·∇_v f = 0, the term v·∇_r f represents…
A. collisions randomizing velocity
B. the magnetic force only
C. particles streaming into new positions at their own velocity
D. entropy production
Show answer

C. v·∇_r f is spatial advection — f being carried into new positions by free streaming at velocity v. The Lorentz-force term (q/m)(E+v×B)·∇_v f handles velocity-space transport instead.

Q3. f(x,v,t) = f₀(x−vt, v) solves the field-free 1-D Vlasov equation ∂f/∂t + v∂f/∂x = 0 because…
A. f₀ is always a Maxwellian
B. it only works at t=0
C. x−vt is always zero
D. the chain-rule derivatives ∂f/∂t = −v f₀′ and v∂f/∂x = +v f₀′ cancel exactly
Show answer

D. Direct chain-rule differentiation of f₀(x−vt,v) shows the two terms cancel for any f₀ — the free-streaming solution works for an arbitrary initial profile, at any time, not just a Maxwellian or t=0.

Q4. Why is the Vlasov-Maxwell (or Vlasov-Poisson) system exactly self-consistent, with no "closure problem"?
A. Because collisions are included
B. Because the equations are linear
C. Because f carries every moment (density, flow, pressure, …) at once, so no moment needs to be guessed
D. Because B is always zero in this course
Show answer

C. A fluid theory truncates the moment hierarchy and must assume a closure (e.g. an equation of state) for the next moment. Kinetic theory never truncates — f already contains all moments, so charge/current density (lower moments) are always exactly known from f. (A) is false — Vlasov is collisionless by construction.

Q5. The Vlasov (collisionless) approximation is justified when…
A. ω_pe·τ_coll > 1 — collective oscillations complete many cycles before a collision occurs
B. the temperature is exactly zero
C. the plasma is magnetized
D. the density is very low everywhere
Show answer

A. This is C1's third plasma criterion: if plasma oscillations vastly outpace collisions with neutrals (or other particles), dropping the collision term from Boltzmann's equation to get Vlasov is an excellent approximation. (C)/(D) are unrelated to the collisionless condition specifically.
