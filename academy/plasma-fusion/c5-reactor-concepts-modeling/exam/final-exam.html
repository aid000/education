Why Toroidal? Confinement concepts · Week 1
1. A linear magnetic mirror fails to confine plasma indefinitely because…
Answer: A. Every particle born inside the loss cone streams straight out one open end — no amount of coil shaping removes the ends of a linear device. A torus closes the field line on itself, removing this specific leak.
2. In a purely toroidal field (no poloidal twist), the curvature and grad-B drifts ultimately expel the whole plasma because…
Answer: B. Curvature/grad-B drift separates ions (up) from electrons (down), building a vertical E field. That field then drives a charge-independent E×B drift that pushes both species radially outward together — a purely toroidal field is unconfining almost immediately.
3. In the Lecture 1 worked example (B = 5 T, R = 6 m, a = 2 m, 10 keV deuteron), the naive, uncorrected vertical-drift loss time across the minor radius is approximately…
Answer: C. v_D ≈ 333 m/s at these parameters, so t_loss = a/v_D = 2 m / 333 m/s ≈ 6 ms — orders of magnitude shorter than the ≥1 s confinement a real device needs, demonstrating why a purely toroidal field cannot confine on its own.
The Tokamak Concept · Week 2
4. In the Lecture 2 worked example (R = 6 m, a = 2 m, Bφ = 5 T, Ip = 5.56 MA, giving Bθ(a) = 0.556 T), the edge safety factor q(a) is approximately…
Answer: D. q(a) = a·Bφ/(R·Bθ(a)) = (2×5)/(6×0.556) ≈ 3.0 — a typical edge-safety-factor operating target.
5. The central solenoid drives the plasma current by acting as…
Answer: A. Ramping the solenoid's current induces a toroidal electric field (transformer action) that drives Ip in the plasma, which acts as a single-turn secondary circuit.
6. Why can a disruption (sudden loss of plasma current and stored energy) be especially likely near q(a) ≈ 1?
Answer: B. Low-order resonances (q near a small integer, especially 1) are where kink/tearing modes grow most readily, which can trigger a disruption.
The Stellarator Concept · Week 3
7. A stellarator produces its poloidal (confining) field component primarily by…
Answer: C. The stellarator's defining feature is getting the full helical field directly from coil geometry, with no plasma current required at all.
8. The main engineering price a stellarator pays for currentless operation is…
Answer: D. The coil complexity (and resulting manufacturing/alignment demands) is the central engineering cost of getting the full helical field from coils alone, with no self-correcting plasma current to help.
Current Drive & Heating Methods · Week 4
9. At B = 5 T, the Lecture 4 worked example finds fci(deuteron) ≈ 38.12 MHz and fce(electron) ≈ 139.96 GHz. Their ratio fce/fci is closest to…
Answer: A. 139.96 GHz / 38.12 MHz ≈ 3,670 ≈ 2×1836 — the electron is roughly twice as light, relative to a proton, as a deuteron is heavy, so the ratio is about 2×1836, not the bare proton/electron mass ratio of 1836.
10. Neutral beam injection (NBI) must convert accelerated ions to neutral atoms before injection because…
Answer: B. A charged particle entering the confining field would gyrate/deflect immediately; a neutral atom crosses the field in a straight line, only ionizing once it collides deep inside the plasma.
11. ICRH and ECRH both heat the plasma by…
Answer: C. Both rely on the resonance condition ω = qB/m for the relevant species (ion for ICRH, electron for ECRH) to deposit wave energy efficiently at a chosen location.
Divertors & Plasma-Wall Interaction · Week 5
12. 150 MW of exhaust power concentrated onto a 5 m² divertor wetted area produces a peak heat flux of approximately…
Answer: D. q = P/A = 150×10⁶ W / 5 m² = 30 MW/m². Concentrating the exhaust onto a small, engineered, actively-cooled target — rather than spreading it over the whole first wall (≈0.32 MW/m²) — is the entire point of a divertor, not a flaw in the design.
13. Compared to a simple limiter, a divertor's main advantage is…
Answer: A. By creating an X-point and steering SOL field lines to a separate structure, the divertor moves plasma-wall contact away from the main chamber to a surface specifically designed to survive it — total exhaust power is unchanged.
Fusion Power Balance · Week 6
14. In D + T → He-4 + n, which product's energy stays in the plasma to provide self-heating?
Answer: B. The charged alpha is confined (at least initially) and heats the plasma via collisions; the neutral neutron escapes the field entirely and deposits its energy in the blanket instead.
15. For Pfusion = 2500 MW of D-T fusion power, approximately how much is carried by the neutron (14.1/17.6 of the total Q)?
Answer: C. Pn = Pfusion × (14.1/17.6) ≈ 2500 × 0.8011 ≈ 2002.8 MW, matching the Lecture 6 worked example.
16. Is reaching scientific ignition the same as a fusion plant producing net electricity?
Answer: D. Ignition only concerns the plasma's own energy balance (P_alpha ≥ P_loss). A plant can reach ignition and still fail to produce net electricity if conversion losses and recirculating power consume too much of the resulting thermal power.
The Breeding Blanket & Tritium Breeding · Week 7
17. Which lithium reaction is exothermic and has its largest cross-section for slow (thermalized) neutrons?
Answer: A. Li-6 + n → He-4 + T releases +4.79 MeV and is the dominant breeding reaction at thermal energies; Li-7's reaction (B) is endothermic and needs a fast (≥ a few MeV) neutron.
18. The lab-frame threshold kinetic energy for the endothermic Li-7(n,n'α)T reaction (Q = −2.47 MeV, projectile = neutron, stationary Li-7 target) is closest to…
Answer: B. KEth = |Q|·(mn+mLi)/mLi = 2.47×(8.0247/7.0160) ≈ 2.83 MeV — above |Q| itself because momentum conservation forces the products to keep some kinetic energy. The 14.1 MeV fusion neutron (D) clears this easily.
19. A neutron multiplier (e.g. beryllium or lead) in the blanket primarily works by…
Answer: C. The multiplier's (n,2n) reaction actually competes with Li-7 breeding for the same fast primary neutron; the payoff is an extra, down-scattered neutron that feeds the Li-6 path, which works at any neutron energy including thermalized.
Materials & Neutron-Damage Challenges · Week 8
20. What does "dpa" measure?
Answer: D. dpa is a neutron-displacement-damage metric; the underlying collision physics is identical whether the fast neutron originated from fission or fusion — only the energy spectrum differs.
21. Helium embrittlement in fusion structural materials arises because…
Answer: A. High-energy fusion neutrons trigger (n,α) transmutations readily; the resulting helium is nearly insoluble in most metals and collects into bubbles that embrittle the material — more severe in a fusion neutron spectrum than a fission one.
Real & Near-Term Devices · Week 9
22. The general, well-established demonstration goal of a large international tokamak program (e.g. ITER) is best described as…
Answer: B. This is the conservative, well-established framing this course uses — a physics/engineering demonstration milestone, not a commercial or deployment claim.
23. Regarding the National Ignition Facility (NIF), which statement is accurate?
Answer: C. NIF's December 2022 shot is a genuine, well-established scientific milestone — but a single nanosecond-scale pulse, nowhere near power-plant scale once total facility input energy (not just laser energy on target) is counted.
Capstone: 0-D Fusion Power-Plant Model · Week 10
24. In the capstone's verified run (Pfusion = 2500 MW, M = 1.15, ηth = 0.35), the approximate gross electric power is…
Answer: D. Pth = M·Pn + Pα = 1.15×2002.8 + 497.2 ≈ 2800.4 MW; Pe,gross = ηth×Pth = 0.35×2800.4 ≈ 980.1 MW. ≈789 MW (B) is the net output after subtracting recirculating power.
25. If the blanket's tritium breeding ratio (TBR) were set to exactly 1.00 instead of 1.05, the tritium net surplus would become…
Answer: A. surplus = (TBR−1)×consumed_rate; at TBR = 1.00 this is exactly zero — breakeven breeding with no margin for decay losses or startup inventory, which is exactly why Lecture 7 argued for a margin above 1.
