Capstone: A 0-D Fusion Power-Plant Model

Every number from Lectures 6 through 8 goes into one script: fusion power in, net electricity and fuel self-sufficiency out.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Assemble the neutron/alpha split (Lecture 6), blanket thermal power with multiplication, and the thermal-to-electric conversion into one power-balance chain.
  • Compute recirculating power (heating/current-drive electric draw plus house loads) and the resulting net electric output and engineering gain Q_eng.
  • Compute the plant's tritium consumption rate and net breeding surplus from the fusion power and TBR (Lecture 7).
  • Run a complete, verified Python script end-to-end and interpret every number it produces.
  • State explicitly which inputs are illustrative study assumptions versus genuine physical constants, and identify OpenMC as the natural next step for deriving M_blanket and TBR from a real neutron-transport calculation.

10.1What the Model Does and Doesn't Claim

This capstone is a 0-D model: no spatial geometry, no neutron transport, no plasma profile — just a single energy and particle ledger for the whole plant, built from the physics you've already derived in Lectures 6–8. That's a deliberate scope choice (flagged on the specialization landing page too): it reproduces the right relationships between fusion power, blanket performance, conversion efficiency, and net output, using illustrative input values rather than a specific real device's design numbers. A real design study replaces the illustrative M_blanket and TBR constants used here with results from an actual neutron-transport calculation (OpenMC, the tool shared with the Nuclear specialization) against a specific blanket geometry and material — exactly the next step flagged in Lecture 8's materials checkpoint.

10.2The Full Chain, Assembled

Four stages, each one you've already built in a previous lecture:

P_fusion → (P_n, P_α) → P_th = M·P_n + P_α → P_e,gross = η_th·P_th → P_e,net = P_e,gross − P_recircThe capstone's entire power-flow chain in one line. M is the blanket energy multiplication (defined in Lecture 7's extension section); η_th is the thermal-to-electric conversion efficiency; P_recirc is the heating/CD electric draw plus house loads.

Alongside this, the tritium module from Lecture 7 runs independently: reaction rate → tritium consumed → tritium bred (via TBR) → net surplus or deficit.

🛠️ The Capstone Script — fusion_plant_model.py

No new install — plain Python, no OpenMC dependency. Every function here was introduced piece-by-piece in Lectures 6 and 7; this assembles them into one runnable model and adds the electric power balance. The full output below was run and verified with python3 during authoring.

# fusion_plant_model.py — Lecture 10 Capstone
# A 0-D fusion power-plant model: fusion power -> neutron/alpha split ->
# blanket thermal power (with tritium breeding) -> plant electrical balance.
#
# All plant-level inputs (P_fusion, M_blanket, eta_th, heating/CD power,
# house load, TBR) are ILLUSTRATIVE STUDY VALUES -- not figures from any
# specific real device.

MEV_TO_J = 1.602176634e-13
U_TO_KG = 1.66053906660e-27

# --- D-T reaction constants (verified from atomic mass defect, Lecture 6) ---
Q_TOTAL_MEV = 17.6
E_NEUTRON_MEV = 14.1
E_ALPHA_MEV = 3.5
M_T_U = 3.016049            # tritium atomic mass, u


def dt_power_split(P_fusion_W):
    f_n = E_NEUTRON_MEV / Q_TOTAL_MEV
    f_a = E_ALPHA_MEV / Q_TOTAL_MEV
    return P_fusion_W * f_n, P_fusion_W * f_a


def blanket_thermal_power(P_neutron_W, P_alpha_W, M_blanket):
    # Blanket captures neutron power with multiplication M_blanket.
    # Alpha power stays in the plasma, then deposits as wall/divertor heat.
    P_blanket = M_blanket * P_neutron_W
    P_wall = P_alpha_W
    return P_blanket + P_wall


def electric_power_balance(P_th_W, eta_th, P_aux_delivered_W, eta_cd, P_house_W):
    P_e_gross = eta_th * P_th_W
    P_heating_elec = P_aux_delivered_W / eta_cd
    P_recirc = P_heating_elec + P_house_W
    P_e_net = P_e_gross - P_recirc
    Q_eng = P_e_gross / P_recirc
    return P_e_gross, P_recirc, P_e_net, Q_eng


def tritium_rates(P_fusion_W, TBR):
    reactions_per_s = P_fusion_W / (Q_TOTAL_MEV * MEV_TO_J)
    m_T_kg = M_T_U * U_TO_KG
    consumed_kg_per_day = reactions_per_s * m_T_kg * 86400
    surplus_kg_per_day = (TBR - 1) * consumed_kg_per_day
    return reactions_per_s, consumed_kg_per_day, surplus_kg_per_day


def main():
    # ---- Illustrative 0-D study inputs (not a real device's figures) ----
    P_fusion = 2500e6       # W, total D-T fusion power
    M_blanket = 1.15         # blanket energy multiplication
    eta_th = 0.35             # thermal-to-electric efficiency
    P_aux_delivered = 50e6   # W delivered to plasma for heating/CD
    eta_cd = 0.45              # wall-plug efficiency of heating/CD systems
    P_house = 80e6             # W, cryoplant/magnets/other house loads
    TBR = 1.05                  # target tritium breeding ratio

    P_n, P_a = dt_power_split(P_fusion)
    P_th = blanket_thermal_power(P_n, P_a, M_blanket)
    P_e_gross, P_recirc, P_e_net, Q_eng = electric_power_balance(
        P_th, eta_th, P_aux_delivered, eta_cd, P_house)
    rxn_s, consumed, surplus = tritium_rates(P_fusion, TBR)

    print(f"P_neutron = {P_n/1e6:.1f} MW   P_alpha = {P_a/1e6:.1f} MW")
    print(f"P_th      = {P_th/1e6:.1f} MW")
    print(f"P_e,gross = {P_e_gross/1e6:.1f} MW   P_recirc = {P_recirc/1e6:.1f} MW")
    print(f"P_e,net   = {P_e_net/1e6:.1f} MW   Q_eng = {Q_eng:.2f}")
    print(f"Tritium consumed = {consumed*1000:.2f} g/day, "
          f"surplus = {surplus*1000:.2f} g/day (TBR={TBR})")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

Verified output (run with python3 during authoring):

P_neutron = 2002.8 MW   P_alpha = 497.2 MW
P_th      = 2800.4 MW
P_e,gross = 980.1 MW   P_recirc = 191.1 MW
P_e,net   = 789.0 MW   Q_eng = 5.13
Tritium consumed = 383.63 g/day, surplus = 19.18 g/day (TBR=1.05)
✦ Reading the Output
  1. Power split: of 2500 MW fusion power, 2002.8 MW (80.1%) goes to the blanket via the neutron, 497.2 MW (19.9%) stays in the plasma as alpha self-heating before eventually reaching the wall — exactly the Lecture 6 split.
  2. Blanket multiplication: the blanket's M=1.15 turns 2002.8 MW of neutron power into 2303.3 MW of captured thermal power (the extra ≈300 MW comes from the exothermic Li-6 breeding reaction and any neutron multiplier, Lecture 7); adding the 497.2 MW wall/alpha heat gives P_th = 2800.4 MW.
  3. Conversion & recirculation: at η_th=0.35, gross electric output is 980.1 MW; the plant draws 191.1 MW back to run its own heating/current-drive systems (111.1 MW electric, since 50 MW delivered at η_cd=0.45 requires 111.1 MW from the grid-equivalent) and house loads (80 MW) — leaving 789.0 MW net.
  4. Engineering gain: Q_eng = 980.1/191.1 ≈ 5.13 means the plant produces about 5× more gross electricity than it consumes internally — a healthy margin for this illustrative design point.
  5. Tritium self-sufficiency: at TBR=1.05, the plant breeds 5% more tritium than it burns, a net surplus of ≈19.2 g/day (≈153 g/day per GW of fusion power, consistent with the standard order-of-magnitude rule of thumb for D-T tritium consumption) — enough margin to cover losses and seed a future plant's startup inventory.
Try it yourselfRe-run the script with TBR = 0.98 (what happens to the surplus — does the plant become fuel self-sufficient?). Then try eta_th = 0.45 (a more efficient conversion cycle) and see how much net electric power and Q_eng improve. Finally, push M_blanket down to 1.0 (no breeding-reaction energy bonus, no multiplier) and see how much thermal power — and therefore net electricity — that single design choice costs the whole plant.

Q1. In the capstone model, what does the blanket energy multiplication factor M represent?
A. The ratio of alpha power to neutron power
B. Extra thermal energy captured in the blanket beyond the neutron's raw 14.1 MeV, from exothermic breeding reactions and any neutron multiplier material
C. The plant's overall electrical efficiency
D. The tritium breeding ratio
Show answer

B. M_blanket > 1 captures the additional energy released by exothermic breeding reactions (like Li-6+n, Lecture 7) and any neutron-multiplier material, on top of the neutron's own kinetic energy.

Q2. In the verified run (P_fusion=2500 MW, M=1.15, η_th=0.35), what is the approximate gross electric power?
A. ≈500 MW
B. ≈789 MW
C. ≈980 MW
D. ≈2500 MW
Show answer

C. P_e,gross = η_th × P_th = 0.35 × 2800.4 ≈ 980.1 MW, matching the verified script output. 789 MW (B) is the net output after subtracting recirculating power.

Q3. The engineering gain Q_eng in this model is defined as…
A. P_fusion / P_recirc
B. TBR / 1.0
C. P_alpha / P_neutron
D. P_e,gross / P_recirc
Show answer

D. Q_eng compares gross electric output to what the plant recirculates to run itself (heating/CD plus house loads) — a measure of how comfortably the plant covers its own electrical overhead.

Q4. If TBR were set to exactly 1.00 instead of 1.05, what would the tritium net surplus become?
A. Zero — the plant would breed exactly as much tritium as it consumes, with no margin
B. It would double
C. It would become negative automatically regardless of TBR
D. It is unrelated to TBR
Show answer

A. surplus = (TBR−1) × consumed_rate; at TBR=1.00 this is exactly zero — breakeven breeding with no margin for decay losses or startup inventory, which is exactly why Lecture 7 argued for a margin above 1.
