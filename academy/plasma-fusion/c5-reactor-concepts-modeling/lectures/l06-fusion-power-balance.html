Fusion Power Balance

17.6 MeV per reaction isn't the end of the story — it's the start of a whole chain of where that energy goes, and how much of it ever becomes usable electricity.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Recall the D-T energy split between the neutron and the alpha particle, and explain the physical reason for it (momentum conservation).
  • Explain why the alpha's energy stays in the plasma (self-heating) while the neutron's energy escapes to the blanket.
  • Define the ignition condition in terms of alpha self-heating balancing plasma energy losses.
  • Trace the full power-flow chain from fusion power to net electrical output at a conceptual level.
  • Compute the neutron and alpha power for a given total fusion power.

6.1Recall: Where the 17.6 MeV Goes

Every D + T → He-4 + n reaction releases Q = 17.6 MeV, split inversely with mass between the two products (momentum conservation, as you verified in your fusion-fundamentals work): the light neutron carries ≈14.1 MeV (≈80.1%), and the alpha particle (He-4 nucleus) carries ≈3.5 MeV (≈19.9%).

D + T → He-4 (3.5 MeV) + n (14.1 MeV)    Q = 17.6 MeV

These two products have completely different fates, and that difference is the single most important fact in fusion reactor engineering.

6.2Two Products, Two Jobs

The neutron: escapes to do work outside the plasma

Being electrically neutral, the 14.1 MeV neutron is completely unaffected by the confining magnetic field — it flies straight out of the plasma and through the vacuum vessel wall, depositing its energy as heat deep inside the surrounding structure: the blanket (Lecture 7). This is useful — it's most of the plant's thermal power — but it's also the source of the materials-damage problem in Lecture 8.

The alpha: stays and does a different job

The charged 3.5 MeV alpha, by contrast, is confined by the same magnetic field as the rest of the plasma (at least initially) and collides with the background plasma, heating it — this is alpha self-heating. Only after it slows down and diffuses out across the SOL (Lecture 5) does its energy finally show up as heat on the first wall/divertor.

AnalogyThink of the D-T reaction as a cannon firing in the plasma's rest frame: the light "shell" (the neutron) flies straight through the magnetic "walls" of the room and out into the hallway (the blanket) to do its work there. The heavy "cannon" (the alpha) recoils gently and stays right in the room, its recoil energy warming the room itself (the plasma).

6.3Ignition: When the Plasma Heats Itself

As long as external heating (Lecture 4's NBI/RF) is needed to keep the plasma hot, the reactor is not yet self-sustaining. But as the fusion rate rises, alpha self-heating rises too — and if alpha heating alone becomes large enough to balance the plasma's energy losses (radiation, conduction/convection across field lines), the external heating can be reduced to zero and the plasma still holds its temperature. This condition is ignition:

P_alpha ≥ P_loss   (at ignition, external heating → 0)Ignition: alpha self-heating alone balances the plasma's energy losses, so the burn is self-sustaining without external heating power.

This connects directly back to the Lawson triple product from your fusion-fundamentals work: the triple product threshold is really just a density-temperature-confinement-time statement of exactly when P_alpha can clear P_loss. Ignition is a plasma-physics milestone; it is not the same as the plant producing net electricity, which also has to survive everything downstream — conversion losses, recirculating power — covered next.

✦ Worked Example — the neutron/alpha split for a plant-scale fusion power

An illustrative study plant produces P_fusion = 2500 MW of fusion power. Find P_neutron and P_alpha.

  1. Fractions: f_n = 14.1/17.6 = 0.8011, f_α = 3.5/17.6 = 0.1989.
  2. Neutron power: P_n = 2500 × 0.8011 ≈ 2002.8 MW.
  3. Alpha power: P_α = 2500 × 0.1989 ≈ 497.2 MW.
  4. Check: 2002.8 + 497.2 = 2500.0 MW ✓ — the split accounts for all the fusion power.

These two numbers feed directly into the capstone's blanket and plant-power-balance calculations in Lecture 10 — keep them in mind.

Model response

Scenario: the plasma reaches ignition (f_α=1, external heating P_aux=0), but the fusion power itself is modest and the blanket's energy multiplication M and thermal efficiency η_th are both on the low end, while recirculating power (magnets, cryoplant, pumps, tritium processing, "house load") stays roughly fixed regardless of plasma performance. Even with zero external heating needed for the plasma itself, those auxiliary systems still draw real electric power. If P_e,gross = η_th·P_th barely clears P_recirc — or doesn't — the plant nets zero or negative electricity despite having achieved the harder plasma-physics milestone.

The single number worth improving first: η_th, the thermal-to-electric conversion efficiency. It multiplies the entire captured thermal power, so a gain there raises P_e,gross across every operating point — unlike pushing f_α further once you're already at ignition (f_α=1), which can't add any more thermal power on the plasma side.

🛠️ Hands-On: neutron/alpha split calculator

No new install. This reproduces the worked example as a reusable function — you'll import this logic directly into the Lecture 10 capstone.

# power_split.py — Lecture 6
Q_TOTAL_MEV = 17.6
E_N_MEV = 14.1
E_A_MEV = 3.5

def dt_power_split(P_fusion):
    f_n = E_N_MEV / Q_TOTAL_MEV
    f_a = E_A_MEV / Q_TOTAL_MEV
    return P_fusion * f_n, P_fusion * f_a

P_fusion = 2500e6   # W, illustrative plant-scale input
P_n, P_a = dt_power_split(P_fusion)
print(f"P_fusion  = {P_fusion/1e6:8.1f} MW")
print(f"P_neutron = {P_n/1e6:8.1f} MW  ({P_n/P_fusion*100:.1f}%)")
print(f"P_alpha   = {P_a/1e6:8.1f} MW  ({P_a/P_fusion*100:.1f}%)")
# P_neutron = 2002.8 MW (80.1%); P_alpha = 497.2 MW (19.9%)

Q1. In D + T → He-4 + n, which product's energy stays in the plasma to provide self-heating?
A. The neutron, because it is heavier
B. The alpha (He-4), because it is charged and remains confined by the magnetic field
C. Both equally
D. Neither — all energy escapes immediately
Show answer

B. The charged alpha is confined (at least initially) and heats the plasma via collisions; the neutral neutron escapes the field entirely and deposits its energy in the blanket instead.

Q2. Ignition is defined as the condition where…
A. the plasma reaches the Coulomb barrier energy
B. the plant produces net electricity
C. the neutron flux reaches zero
D. alpha self-heating alone balances the plasma's energy losses, so external heating is no longer needed
Show answer

D. Ignition is a plasma self-sustainment condition (P_alpha ≥ P_loss), distinct from (B) plant-level net electricity, which also depends on conversion efficiency and recirculating power.

Q3. For P_fusion = 2500 MW of D-T fusion power, approximately how much is carried by the neutron?
A. ≈500 MW
B. ≈1250 MW
C. ≈2003 MW
D. ≈2500 MW
Show answer

C. P_n = P_fusion × (14.1/17.6) ≈ 2500 × 0.8011 ≈ 2002.8 MW, matching the worked example.

Q4. Is reaching scientific ignition the same as a fusion plant producing net electricity?
A. Yes, they are identical conditions
B. No — ignition is a plasma-level energy balance; net electricity also depends on thermal-conversion efficiency and recirculating power
C. No, ignition is strictly harder to achieve than net electricity
D. Net electricity always implies ignition has not occurred
Show answer

B. Ignition only concerns the plasma's own energy balance. A plant can reach ignition and still fail to produce net electricity if conversion losses and recirculating power consume too much of the resulting thermal power.

Q5. Which power-flow step directly follows "thermal power" in the plant chain?
A. Fusion power
B. Neutron/alpha split
C. Conversion to gross electric power (at some thermal efficiency η_th)
D. Tritium breeding
Show answer

C. The chain runs fusion power → neutron/alpha split → thermal power (blanket + wall) → gross electric power (via η_th) → net electric power (after subtracting recirculating power). Tritium breeding (D) runs alongside the neutron branch but isn't itself a step in the energy chain.
