🛠️ Hands-On Project

A 0-D/1-D Fusion Power-Plant Model (power balance + tritium breeding)

Build a single-file Python model of a D-T fusion power plant: split fusion power into its neutron and alpha components, compute blanket thermal power with an energy-multiplication factor, convert to gross and net electric power after recirculating losses, and check tritium self-sufficiency against a target breeding ratio. You deliver a short report with the full script, its verified output, and a sensitivity check (how net power and tritium surplus respond to your key assumptions). No new software install — the course reuses the Python stack from C1–C4; OpenMC is referenced conceptually as the natural next step beyond this course's illustrative constants.

Wk 4: heating/current-drive module (cyclotron-frequency calculator) Wk 8: materials-assumptions documentation checkpoint Wk 10: full plant model + report submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: Is a stellarator's currentless, disruption-free, steady-state operation worth the far greater coil-manufacturing complexity it demands — compared to the tokamak's simpler coils and ongoing current-drive problem?
  • Week 6: Reaching scientific ignition (alpha heating alone sustains the plasma) is not the same as a plant producing net electricity. Describe a scenario where a plasma ignites but the plant still fails to deliver net power.
  • Week 9: The field is funding large tokamaks, optimized stellarators, and private alternative concepts in parallel rather than converging on one approach. Make the case for parallel development, or argue for concentrating resources — using specific trade-offs from this course.
