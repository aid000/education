The MHD Approximation · Week 1
1. What is the key simplification a fluid (MHD) description makes relative to a kinetic description?
Answer: A. MHD replaces f(x,v,t) with position-space fields — density, velocity, pressure, B — discarding the detailed shape of the velocity distribution. (B) B is central to MHD. (C) MHD still describes a real particle fluid, via moments. (D) MHD uses dramatically fewer variables, which is its appeal.
2. A deuterium plasma has B = 3 T, Ti = 8 keV, and macroscopic scale L = 0.6 m. Using ρi = vth/Ωci with vth = √(2Ti/mi), L/ρi ≈ 98.5. What does this tell you?
Answer: B. L/ρi ≈ 98.5 ≫ 1 means macroscopic structures are far larger than a single gyro-orbit — exactly the regime where the fluid approximation is legitimate. (A) is the opposite conclusion. (C, D) are not supported by this ratio.
The MHD Equations · Week 2
3. Why can the displacement current be dropped from Ampère's law in MHD, leaving J = ∇×B/μ₀?
Answer: C. This "pre-Maxwell" ordering holds because the velocities and wave speeds considered are tiny compared to light speed. (A) electric fields do exist, e.g. E = −v×B. (B, D) are both false — B and a real conducting fluid are central to MHD.
4. A plasma has gradient scale L = 2 m, flow speed v = 3×10⁵ m/s, and resistivity η = 2×10⁻⁶ Ω·m. Using S = μ₀Lv/η, the Lundquist number is approximately…
Answer: D. S = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2)(3×10⁵)/(2×10⁻⁶) ≈ 3.77×10⁵ — advection dominates by a wide margin, so the field is nearly frozen in. (A, B, C) are off by many orders of magnitude.
Ideal MHD & Frozen-In Flux · Week 3
5. Alfvén's theorem, built on frozen-in flux, says that two fluid elements starting on the same field line…
Answer: A. This is a topological statement: connectivity is preserved even as length, shape, and separation all change. (B) is the opposite. (C, D) overstate the theorem — only topological connection is fixed.
6. Since τη ∝ L² while τA ∝ L, the Lundquist number S = τη/τA scales as L¹. If a lengthscale L shrinks by a factor of 10⁵ (keeping η and the flow speed fixed), S itself collapses by…
Answer: B. S ∝ L¹ (not L²) because the extra power of L in τη is cancelled by τA's own L-dependence — so S falls by exactly the same factor L shrinks by: log₁₀(10⁵) = 5. (A) confuses S's scaling with τη's L² scaling alone. (C, D) do not match L¹ scaling.
7. Magnetic reconnection is possible despite the frozen-in theorem because…
Answer: C. Shrinking the relevant length scale collapses the local Lundquist number, letting the diffusive term matter locally even while the bulk volume stays essentially ideal. (A) the theorem holds wherever ideal MHD genuinely applies. (B) all real plasmas have finite resistivity. (D) reconnection is defined by exactly this topological change.
MHD Equilibria & the Pinch · Week 4
8. In the θ-pinch equilibrium, the force-balance equation reduces to pure pressure balance, d/dr(p + B_z²/2μ₀) = 0, with no tension term at all. Why?
Answer: D. Tension is a pure curvature effect; a straight B_z contributes zero tension however strong it is, leaving only the pressure-gradient balance. (A, B, C) all misstate the setup.
9. A θ-pinch has centerline thermal pressure p₀ = 1.5×10⁵ Pa and zero pressure at the edge. Pressure balance B_edge²/2μ₀ = p₀ requires an edge axial field B_edge = √(2μ₀p₀) of approximately…
Answer: A. B_edge = √(2 × 4π×10⁻⁷ × 1.5×10⁵) ≈ 0.614 T. (B, C, D) are off by simple factors of 2, ½, or 10.
10. What distinguishes the Z-pinch's force-balance equation from the θ-pinch's?
Answer: B. B_θ wraps around the axis with finite radius of curvature, so it always carries a tension (hoop-stress) contribution that B_z(r) in a θ-pinch never does. (A, C, D) all misstate the comparison.
Magnetic Pressure, Tension & Plasma Beta · Week 5
11. Which statement about plasma beta β = 2μ₀p/B² is most accurate?
Answer: C. β is specifically a thermal-vs-magnetic-pressure ratio. Magnetic pressure and tension are a separate internal split of the field's own force, both of order B²/μ₀ — β is silent on which of those two wins. (A) conflates two different comparisons. (B, D) are both false.
12. A plasma has n = 8×10¹⁸ m⁻³, T = 300 eV, B = 0.8 T. β = 2μ₀p/B² ≈ 1.51×10⁻³. This plasma is…
Answer: D. β ≈ 1.5×10⁻³ ≪ 1 means B²/2μ₀ ≫ p — the field dominates the dynamics by nearly three orders of magnitude. (A) is the opposite case. (B, C) are not implied by this number.
Alfvén Waves · Week 6
13. The restoring force for the shear Alfvén wave is…
Answer: A. The wave carries zero density/pressure perturbation; only the magnetic-tension piece of J×B appears in its momentum equation. (B, C, D) play no role in this derivation.
14. A deuterium plasma has n = 3×10¹⁹ m⁻³ and B = 2.5 T. Using v_A = B/√(μ₀ρ) with ρ = n·m_i (m_i = 2m_p), v_A is approximately…
Answer: B. ρ = (3×10¹⁹)(2×1.673×10⁻²⁷) ≈ 1.00×10⁻⁷ kg/m³, so v_A = 2.5/√(4π×10⁻⁷ × 1.00×10⁻⁷) ≈ 7.04×10⁶ m/s. (A, C, D) are off by orders of magnitude or use the wrong quantity.
15. For propagation at angle θ to B₀, the shear Alfvén wave obeys ω = kv_A cos θ. At θ = 90° (perpendicular propagation)…
Answer: C. cos(90°) = 0, so ω = 0 for any k — the shear Alfvén wave vanishes exactly perpendicular to the field. (A) the opposite happens. (B) a different branch (fast magnetosonic) takes over, not sound. (D) the angle dependence is essential.
Magnetosonic Waves · Week 7
16. How does the fast magnetosonic speed compare to v_A and c_s in general?
Answer: D. Constructive (in-phase) compression of magnetic and thermal pressure means v_fast ≥ max(v_A, c_s), with equality only at θ=0° where the fast branch reduces exactly to the larger of the two. (A, B, C) all misstate the relationship.
17. A plasma has v_A = 6×10⁶ m/s, c_s = 1.5×10⁶ m/s. At θ = 90° (perpendicular propagation), the fast magnetosonic speed is approximately…
Answer: A. At θ=90°, v_fast = √(v_A²+c_s²) = √(36+2.25)×10⁶ ≈ 6.18×10⁶ m/s. (B) is just v_A alone. (C) is just c_s alone. (D) is v_A+c_s, not the quadrature sum.
18. At θ = 0° (propagation along B₀) with v_A > c_s, the fast and slow magnetosonic speeds become…
Answer: B. At θ=0 the discriminant becomes |v_A²−c_s²|, splitting the roots into exactly the larger and smaller of v_A and c_s — here v_A>c_s, so fast=v_A, slow=c_s. (A, D) don't match the θ=0 limit. (C) reverses the assignment.
MHD Instabilities: Kink & Sausage · Week 8
19. The Kruskal–Shafranov safety factor is q(a) = aB_z/(RB_θ(a)). What does a LARGE q physically mean?
Answer: C. q is the number of long-way (axial/toroidal) turns per single short-way (azimuthal/poloidal) turn; large q means B_z dominates and the field winds the long way many times before completing one short-way loop. (A, B) describe the small-q (1/q) regime instead. (D) q depends on field geometry, not the presence of current.
20. A screw pinch has a = 0.2 m, R = 0.9 m, B_z = 1.6 T, and B_θ(a) = 0.55 T. The safety factor q(a) = aB_z/(RB_θ(a)) is approximately…
Answer: D. q(a) = (0.2×1.6)/(0.9×0.55) ≈ 0.65, below the q=1 Kruskal–Shafranov threshold, so this configuration is kink-unstable. (A, C) invert the ratio. (B) gets the value right but the stability verdict backwards.
21. Is a purely azimuthal-field (B_θ-only) pinch always sausage (m=0) unstable, regardless of current profile?
Answer: A. The unconditional runaway argument is exact only for current concentrated in a thin skin sheath; a diffuse profile's pressure gradient supplies part of the restoring response a skin current lacks, so it can be stable. (B, C, D) all overstate or invert the rule.
Numerical Methods for 1-D MHD · Week 9
22. A grid has Δx = 0.005 m and the fast magnetosonic speed is 4×10⁶ m/s. The maximum stable timestep (C=1), Δt_max = Δx/v, is approximately…
Answer: B. Δt_max = 0.005/4×10⁶ = 1.25×10⁻⁹ s. (A, C) are off by orders of magnitude. (D) inverts the ratio.
23. In the Lecture 9 CFL-violation demo, after 300 timesteps with Δt = 1.5×Δt_CFL, the field amplitude reaches roughly…
Answer: C. Double-precision floats only overflow past ~1.8×10³⁰⁸; ~1.9×10²³⁴ is enormous but still representable, a huge-but-finite waypoint on the same exponential blow-up. (A, D) understate the runaway. (B) overstates it — it is not yet a true overflow at step 300.
Capstone: Equilibrium & Wave Solver · Week 10
24. A Bennett pinch has centerline pressure p₀ = 8×10⁴ Pa. The exact Bennett-profile edge-field coefficient B₁ = √(2μ₀p₀) is approximately…
Answer: D. B₁ = √(2 × 4π×10⁻⁷ × 8×10⁴) ≈ 0.448 T. (A, B, C) are off by simple factors of 2 or 4.
25. Which single equation from Lecture 4 underlies both the capstone's equilibrium solver (Part A) and, indirectly, the wave solver's background state (Part B)?
Answer: A. ∇p = J×B is the equilibrium Part A solves exactly (the Bennett profile), and it also defines the uniform background state Part B then perturbs with a wave. (B) CFL governs the numerical timestep, not the physical equilibrium. (C) Ohm's law underlies the induction equation, not the static force balance. (D) the gas law is one piece of the pressure closure, not the organizing equation.
