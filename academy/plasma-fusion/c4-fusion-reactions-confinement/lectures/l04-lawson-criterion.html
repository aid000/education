The Lawson Criterion: Power Balance & the Triple Product

Fusion power alone proves nothing — a plasma also leaks energy. Balance the two and a single number, the triple product, decides whether a reactor can ever pay for itself.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Write the energy balance of a confined fusion plasma in terms of a thermal energy content and a confinement time.
  • Derive the Lawson condition nτ_E from a power balance, for both the alpha-self-heating (ignition) and total-fusion-power (power-balance point) cases.
  • Explain why the triple product nTτ_E, not nτ_E alone, is the standard way the criterion is quoted — and why the two quantities have different minima.
  • Evaluate the minimum triple product and the temperature at which it occurs for D-T, distinguishing it from the minimum of nτ_E alone.

4.1Energy Balance in a Confined Plasma

A fusion plasma is a leaky bucket: it generates heat (fusion reactions, auxiliary heating) and loses heat (radiation, particle transport out of the confining field — the territory of C3's MHD and this specialization's broader confinement physics). We summarize all the loss channels with a single bookkeeping number, the energy confinement time τE — defined properly in Lecture 6, but usable now as "the time constant with which stored thermal energy would leak away if all heating stopped":

Ploss = W/τEW is the plasma's thermal energy content per unit volume; τ_E packages away the details of however that energy actually escapes.

For a plasma with equal electron and ion densities n and equal temperatures Te = Ti = T (both species present, each contributing (3/2)nT to the energy density):

W = (3/2)(neTe + niTi) = 3nTUsing n_e = n_i = n. T is here an energy (e.g. in keV, with an implicit factor of the Boltzmann constant absorbed into the units).

4.2A Power-Balance Point: Total Fusion Power Against Losses

The least demanding power balance simply requires that fusion power matches loss power — call this the power-balance point (deliberately not calling it "scientific breakeven" yet — that name is reserved below for a more careful, properly-named condition), using Lecture 3's Pfusion = (1/4)n²⟨σv⟩Efus:

(1/4)n²⟨σv⟩Efus = 3nT/τE   ⟹   nτE = 12T/(⟨σv⟩Efus)One power of n cancels; the Lawson condition nτ_E is a function of temperature alone, not of density on its own.

This is a useful stepping stone, but it is not the standard "scientific breakeven" — it silently credits the plasma with the full fusion power as if that output alone had to cover losses, without saying anything about how much external heating is actually supplied. Lecture 5 defines the real, standard scientific breakeven properly: Q = Pfusion/Paux = 1, where Paux is auxiliary heating alone and the full loss balance is Paux + Pα = Ploss (auxiliary heating plus alpha self-heating, both counted). Substituting Q=1 (so Paux=Pfusion) into that balance gives:

nτE|Q=1 = 12T/(⟨σv⟩(Efus+Eα))Stricter-looking denominator (E_fus+E_α instead of E_fus alone) — but this threshold is numerically the *weakest* of the three in this lecture, because at Q=1 a large auxiliary power is still doing most of the work, on top of a growing alpha contribution.

4.3Ignition: Balancing Alpha Heating Alone Against Losses

The strictest, most physically important condition is ignition: the plasma sustains itself on the energy of its own alpha particles alone (3.54 MeV of D-T's 17.6 MeV), with no external heating at all — the neutron's 14.05 MeV escapes to the blanket and does not help sustain the plasma:

(1/4)n²⟨σv⟩Eα = 3nT/τE   ⟹   nτE = 12T/(⟨σv⟩Eα)Same form as the power-balance point, but with E_α (3.54 MeV) in place of the full E_fus (17.6 MeV) in the denominator.

Because Eα < Efus, ignition demands roughly 5× the nτE of the power-balance point at the same temperature (ratio E_fus/E_α ≈ 17.6/3.5 ≈ 5.03, verified by python3). Ignition is stricter still than the real Q=1 scientific breakeven defined by the second equation in §4.2 above — by the ratio (E_fus+E_α)/E_α ≈ 21.1/3.5 ≈ 6.0×, independent of temperature (the ⟨σv⟩(T) factor cancels in the ratio). This 6× figure, not 5× or 10×, is the correct gap between scientific breakeven and ignition used consistently throughout this course.

Common mistakeThree distinct thresholds get casually lumped together: the power-balance point (fusion power = loss power, §4.2's first equation), the real scientific breakeven (Q=1, §4.2's second equation — i.e. P_aux=P_fusion with alpha heating also counted in the loss balance), and ignition (alpha power alone = loss power, §4.3). §4.2 derives both of the first two — don't let the section's title ("A Power-Balance Point") make you think it's only about the weaker one. Ignition is the strictest by construction; scientific breakeven (Q=1) is actually the weakest of the three at a given temperature, since it still relies on substantial external heating. A plasma can clear scientific breakeven while needing an enormous amount of external heating to stay hot; only ignition means it is truly self-sustaining.

4.4The Triple Product nTτE

Both conditions above give nτE as a function of T, not a single number — because ⟨σv⟩(T) itself depends on temperature. Multiplying both sides by T turns this into the more universally quoted triple product:

nTτE = 12T²/(⟨σv⟩Eα)The right-hand side, as a function of T, has a shallow minimum — meaning there is an optimal operating temperature that minimizes the required nTτ_E.

Careful: nτE and nTτE do not share the same minimum. Evaluating nτE = 12T/(⟨σv⟩Eα) numerically (Section 4's hands-on work) gives a minimum of ≈1.5×10²⁰ s/m³ near T≈25.7 keV — that number's units are s/m³, not keV·s/m³, because it is nτE alone, with no explicit T multiplied in. The triple product nTτE = 12T²/(⟨σv⟩Eα) is a genuinely different function of T — the extra explicit factor of T shifts its minimum to a lower temperature, not the same one — and its minimum works out to ≈2.8×10²¹ keV·s/m³ near T≈13-14 keV. The commonly quoted "≈3×10²¹ keV·s/m³" benchmark used throughout this course is this same nTτE curve evaluated at the round number T=10 keV, close to (but not exactly at) its true minimum. This single number is why fusion reactors must simultaneously push density, temperature, and confinement time — missing any one of the three dooms the other two.

✦ Worked Example — ignition nτ_E and triple product at T = 15 keV

Find the ignition nτ_E and the triple product at T = 15 keV, using ⟨σv⟩(15 keV) = 2.740×10⁻²² m³/s (Lecture 3) and E_α = 3.5 MeV.

  1. E_α in joules: 3500 keV × 1.602×10⁻¹⁶ J/keV = 5.608×10⁻¹³ J.
  2. nτ_E: = 12T/(⟨σv⟩E_α). With T in joules: T = 15 keV × 1.602×10⁻¹⁶ = 2.403×10⁻¹⁵ J. nτ_E = 12×2.403×10⁻¹⁵ / (2.740×10⁻²² × 5.608×10⁻¹³) = 2.884×10⁻¹⁴ / 1.537×10⁻³⁴ ≈ 1.88×10²⁰ s/m³.
  3. Triple product: nTτ_E = (nτ_E) × T[keV] = 1.88×10²⁰ × 15 ≈ 2.82×10²¹ keV·s/m³ — close to (slightly below) the 3×10²¹ benchmark quoted at 10 keV, consistent with T=15 keV sitting close to nTτ_E's own minimum (≈13.5 keV, Section 4's hands-on script) — not the ≈25.7 keV minimum of nτ_E alone.
  4. Cross-check against the T=10 keV table value: Lecture 3-style computation at T=10 keV gives nτ_E≈3.02×10²⁰ s/m³, triple product ≈3.02×10²¹ keV·s/m³. Going from 10→15 keV, nτ_E keeps falling (it won't bottom out until ≈25.7 keV) while nTτ_E has already nearly bottomed out (its minimum is ≈13.5 keV) — two different curves, two different minima, exactly as the hands-on script below shows.

🛠️ Hands-On: finding both minima numerically — nτ_E vs the triple product nTτ_E

Project checkpoint: this script becomes the reactivity-and-triple-product module your capstone calculator (Lecture 10) will build on directly.

# lawson.py — Lecture 4
import numpy as np

def sigmav_DT(T_keV):
    BG, mrc2 = 34.3827, 1124656.0
    C1,C2,C3,C4,C5,C6,C7 = 1.17302e-9,1.51361e-2,7.51886e-2,4.60643e-3,1.35e-2,-1.0675e-4,1.366e-5
    T = T_keV
    theta = T/(1 - (T*(C2+T*(C4+T*C6)))/(1+T*(C3+T*(C5+T*C7))))
    xi = (BG**2/(4*theta))**(1/3)
    return C1*theta*np.sqrt(xi/(mrc2*T**3))*np.exp(-3*xi)*1e-6  # m^3/s

E_ALPHA_KEV = 3500.0

def ntau_ignition(T_keV):              # s/m^3
    sv = sigmav_DT(T_keV)
    return 12*T_keV/(sv*E_ALPHA_KEV)

def nTtau_ignition(T_keV):             # keV s/m^3 — the actual triple product
    sv = sigmav_DT(T_keV)
    return 12*T_keV**2/(sv*E_ALPHA_KEV)

Ts = np.linspace(1, 100, 200000)
ntau_vals  = np.array([ntau_ignition(T)  for T in Ts])
nTtau_vals = np.array([nTtau_ignition(T) for T in Ts])

i_min1 = np.argmin(ntau_vals)
i_min2 = np.argmin(nTtau_vals)
print(f"minimum nτ_E  = {ntau_vals[i_min1]:.3e} s/m^3       at T = {Ts[i_min1]:.1f} keV")
print(f"minimum nTτ_E = {nTtau_vals[i_min2]:.3e} keV s/m^3  at T = {Ts[i_min2]:.1f} keV")

for T in [10, 12, 14, 15, 20, 25, 30]:
    nt  = ntau_ignition(T)
    ntt = nTtau_ignition(T)
    print(f"T={T:4.1f} keV   nτ_E={nt:.3e} s/m^3   nTτ_E={ntt:.3e} keV s/m^3")

# Verified output:
# minimum nτ_E  = 1.515e+20 s/m^3       at T = 25.7 keV
# minimum nTτ_E = 2.790e+21 keV s/m^3   at T = 13.5 keV
# T=10.0 keV   nτ_E=3.018e+20 s/m^3   nTτ_E=3.018e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=12.0 keV   nτ_E=2.354e+20 s/m^3   nTτ_E=2.825e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=14.0 keV   nτ_E=1.995e+20 s/m^3   nTτ_E=2.793e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=15.0 keV   nτ_E=1.877e+20 s/m^3   nTτ_E=2.816e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=20.0 keV   nτ_E=1.584e+20 s/m^3   nTτ_E=3.167e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=25.0 keV   nτ_E=1.516e+20 s/m^3   nTτ_E=3.789e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=30.0 keV   nτ_E=1.540e+20 s/m^3   nTτ_E=4.619e+21 keV s/m^3

The two curves have genuinely different minima, not the same one: nτ_E alone bottoms out at T≈25.7 keV (units s/m³), while the triple product nTτ_E — the one actually quoted as "the Lawson criterion" — bottoms out at the lower temperature T≈13.5 keV (units keV·s/m³), because the extra explicit factor of T in nTτ_E pulls the optimum down: nTτ_E is still falling from T=10 to T≈13-14 keV (3.018e21 → 2.793e21) even though nτ_E alone is still falling all the way out to 25.7 keV. Past ≈14 keV, nTτ_E turns around and climbs for the rest of the range shown. Don't assume the two minima coincide, and don't assume nTτ_E's minimum sits at a higher T than nτ_E's — it's the opposite.

Q1. The energy confinement time τ_E, as used in the Lawson power balance, is best described as…
A. the time it takes a single ion to fuse
B. the time constant with which stored thermal energy would leak away if all heating stopped
C. the total lifetime of the plasma discharge
D. the time for the Maxwellian distribution to form
Show answer

B. τ_E is defined by P_loss = W/τ_E — it packages all the energy-loss physics into one bookkeeping time constant. (A) is a fusion reaction rate concept, not confinement. (C) a discharge can run far longer or shorter than τ_E. (D) is a kinetic-equilibration timescale, unrelated.

Q2. Ignition (alpha heating alone balances losses) requires a larger nτ_E than the power-balance point (total fusion power balances losses) because…
A. ignition requires higher density, which the power-balance point does not
B. the alpha particle carries only E_α≈3.5 MeV of the full E_fus≈17.6 MeV, so ignition's effective heating source is ~5× smaller
C. ignition occurs at a different, higher Q-value reaction
D. the power-balance point ignores the neutron entirely
Show answer

B. Both conditions balance the same loss term 3nT/τ_E, but ignition's heating source is only the alpha's 3.5 MeV share, while the power-balance point credits the full 17.6 MeV — making ignition's required nτ_E larger by roughly E_fus/E_α≈5. (A) both conditions are stated for the same n. (C) same D-T reaction throughout. (D) the neutron's energy is simply not counted toward self-heating in either condition — it's lost to the blanket.

Q3. The Lawson condition nτ_E = 12T/(⟨σv⟩E) is a function of temperature alone (not an independent function of density) because…
A. density has no effect on fusion power
B. τ_E is defined to already include density
C. ⟨σv⟩ itself depends on density
D. fusion power scales as n² while the loss term scales as n¹, so one factor of n cancels in the balance
Show answer

D. P_fusion ∝ n² while P_loss = 3nT/τ_E ∝ n¹; setting them equal and dividing by n leaves nτ_E as a pure function of T. (A) density matters greatly for the absolute power, just not for the nτ_E threshold's T-dependence. (C) ⟨σv⟩ depends only on T by definition (Lecture 3). (B) τ_E is an independent confinement property, defined in Lecture 6.

Q4. The minimum required nτ_E for D-T ignition (≈1.5×10²⁰ s/m³) occurs near T≈…
A. 1 keV
B. 10 keV
C. 26 keV
D. 500 keV
Show answer

C. The numerical minimum of 12T/(⟨σv⟩E_α) for D-T occurs at T≈25.7 keV (Section 4's hands-on script), where ⟨σv⟩ has risen enough to offset the growing T in the numerator. (A) is far too cold — reactivity is still catastrophically suppressed there. (B)/(D) are both away from the actual numerical minimum.

Q5. The triple product nTτ_E and the plain condition nτ_E differ in that…
A. they are two names for exactly the same quantity
B. nτ_E depends on T while nTτ_E does not
C. nTτ_E has a minimum at a different (lower) temperature than nτ_E, because of the extra explicit factor of T
D. nTτ_E is only meaningful for D-D, not D-T
Show answer

C. nτ_E(T) alone has a minimum near T≈25.7 keV; multiplying by the extra explicit T to form nTτ_E pulls the optimum down, not up — nTτ_E's own minimum sits near T≈13.5 keV (Section 4's script), well below nτ_E's minimum. The extra T factor matters most at lower T where ⟨σv⟩ is still rising steeply, so nTτ_E is already climbing back up by the time nτ_E is still falling toward 25.7 keV. (A) they are related but numerically and structurally distinct. (B) both depend on T. (D) the triple product applies to any fusion reaction, D-T included.
