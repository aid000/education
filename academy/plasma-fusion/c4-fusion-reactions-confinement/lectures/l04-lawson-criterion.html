The Lawson Criterion: Power Balance & the Triple Product

Fusion power alone proves nothing — a plasma also leaks energy. Balance the two and a single number, the triple product, decides whether a reactor can ever pay for itself.

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Write the energy balance of a confined fusion plasma in terms of a thermal energy content and a confinement time.
  • Derive the Lawson condition nτ_E from a power balance, for both the alpha-self-heating (ignition) and total-fusion-power (breakeven) cases.
  • Explain why the triple product nTτ_E, not nτ_E alone, is the standard way the criterion is quoted.
  • Evaluate the minimum triple product and the temperature at which it occurs for D-T.

4.1Energy Balance in a Confined Plasma

A fusion plasma is a leaky bucket: it generates heat (fusion reactions, auxiliary heating) and loses heat (radiation, particle transport out of the confining field — the territory of C3's MHD and this specialization's broader confinement physics). We summarize all the loss channels with a single bookkeeping number, the energy confinement time τE — defined properly in Lecture 6, but usable now as "the time constant with which stored thermal energy would leak away if all heating stopped":

Ploss = W/τEW is the plasma's thermal energy content per unit volume; τ_E packages away the details of however that energy actually escapes.

For a plasma with equal electron and ion densities n and equal temperatures Te = Ti = T (both species present, each contributing (3/2)nT to the energy density):

W = (3/2)(neTe + niTi) = 3nTUsing n_e = n_i = n. T is here an energy (e.g. in keV, with an implicit factor of the Boltzmann constant absorbed into the units).

4.2Scientific Breakeven: Balancing Total Fusion Power Against Losses

The least demanding power balance simply requires that fusion power matches loss power — scientific breakeven, using Lecture 3's Pfusion = (1/4)n²⟨σv⟩Efus:

(1/4)n²⟨σv⟩Efus = 3nT/τE   ⟹   nτE = 12T/(⟨σv⟩Efus)One power of n cancels; the Lawson condition nτ_E is a function of temperature alone, not of density on its own.

4.3Ignition: Balancing Alpha Heating Alone Against Losses

A stricter, more physically important condition is ignition: the plasma sustains itself on the energy of its own alpha particles alone (3.54 MeV of D-T's 17.6 MeV), with no external heating at all — the neutron's 14.05 MeV escapes to the blanket and does not help sustain the plasma:

(1/4)n²⟨σv⟩Eα = 3nT/τE   ⟹   nτE = 12T/(⟨σv⟩Eα)Same form as breakeven, but with E_α (3.54 MeV) in place of the full E_fus (17.6 MeV) — a factor of ~5 stricter requirement on nτ_E.

Because Eα < Efus, ignition demands roughly 5× the nτE of scientific breakeven at the same temperature. This is the real Lawson criterion used when people talk about a plasma being "self-sustaining."

Common mistakeScientific breakeven (fusion power = loss power) and ignition (alpha power alone = loss power) are not the same condition — ignition is the stricter one, by roughly the ratio E_fus/E_α ≈ 5. A plasma can clear scientific breakeven while still needing substantial external heating to stay hot; only ignition means it is truly self-sustaining.

4.4The Triple Product nTτE

Both conditions above give nτE as a function of T, not a single number — because ⟨σv⟩(T) itself depends on temperature. Multiplying both sides by T turns this into the more universally quoted triple product:

nTτE = 12T²/(⟨σv⟩Eα)The right-hand side, as a function of T, has a shallow minimum — meaning there is an optimal operating temperature that minimizes the required nTτ_E.

Evaluating this for D-T numerically (Section 4's hands-on work) gives a minimum triple product of ≈1.5×10²⁰ keV·s/m³ near T≈25-26 keV — but at the more commonly operated T≈10-15 keV, the ignition triple product sits around 3×10²¹ keV·s/m³, the benchmark number used throughout this course. This single number is why fusion reactors must simultaneously push density, temperature, and confinement time — missing any one of the three dooms the other two.

✦ Worked Example — ignition nτ_E and triple product at T = 15 keV

Find the ignition nτ_E and the triple product at T = 15 keV, using ⟨σv⟩(15 keV) = 2.740×10⁻²² m³/s (Lecture 3) and E_α = 3.5 MeV.

  1. E_α in joules: 3500 keV × 1.602×10⁻¹⁶ J/keV = 5.608×10⁻¹³ J.
  2. nτ_E: = 12T/(⟨σv⟩E_α). With T in joules: T = 15 keV × 1.602×10⁻¹⁶ = 2.403×10⁻¹⁵ J. nτ_E = 12×2.403×10⁻¹⁵ / (2.740×10⁻²² × 5.608×10⁻¹³) = 2.884×10⁻¹⁴ / 1.537×10⁻³⁴ ≈ 1.88×10²⁰ s/m³.
  3. Triple product: nTτ_E = (nτ_E) × T[keV] = 1.88×10²⁰ × 15 ≈ 2.82×10²¹ keV·s/m³ — close to (slightly below) the 3×10²¹ benchmark quoted at 10 keV, consistent with 15 keV sitting nearer the shallow minimum.
  4. Cross-check against the T=10 keV table value: Lecture 3-style computation at T=10 keV gives nτ_E≈3.02×10²⁰ s/m³, triple product ≈3.02×10²¹ keV·s/m³ — both numbers drop as T rises toward the ≈25 keV minimum, exactly as the figure above shows.

🛠️ Hands-On: finding the minimum triple product numerically

Project checkpoint: this script becomes the reactivity-and-triple-product module your capstone calculator (Lecture 10) will build on directly.

# lawson.py — Lecture 4
import numpy as np

def sigmav_DT(T_keV):
    BG, mrc2 = 34.3827, 1124656.0
    C1,C2,C3,C4,C5,C6,C7 = 1.17302e-9,1.51361e-2,7.51886e-2,4.60643e-3,1.35e-2,-1.0675e-4,1.366e-5
    T = T_keV
    theta = T/(1 - (T*(C2+T*(C4+T*C6)))/(1+T*(C3+T*(C5+T*C7))))
    xi = (BG**2/(4*theta))**(1/3)
    return C1*theta*np.sqrt(xi/(mrc2*T**3))*np.exp(-3*xi)*1e-6  # m^3/s

E_ALPHA_KEV = 3500.0

def ntau_ignition(T_keV):
    sv = sigmav_DT(T_keV)
    return 12*T_keV/(sv*E_ALPHA_KEV)    # s/m^3  (T in keV used consistently on both sides)

Ts = np.linspace(5, 60, 5000)
ntau_vals = np.array([ntau_ignition(T) for T in Ts])
i_min = np.argmin(ntau_vals)
print(f"minimum nτ_E = {ntau_vals[i_min]:.3e} s/m^3  at T = {Ts[i_min]:.1f} keV")

for T in [10, 15, 20, 25, 30]:
    nt = ntau_ignition(T)
    print(f"T={T:4.1f} keV   nτ_E={nt:.3e} s/m^3   triple product nTτ_E={nt*T:.3e} keV s/m^3")

# Verified output:
# minimum nτ_E = 1.515e+20 s/m^3  at T = 25.7 keV
# T=10.0 keV   nτ_E=3.018e+20 s/m^3   triple product nTτ_E=3.018e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=15.0 keV   nτ_E=1.877e+20 s/m^3   triple product nTτ_E=2.816e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=20.0 keV   nτ_E=1.584e+20 s/m^3   triple product nTτ_E=3.167e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=25.0 keV   nτ_E=1.516e+20 s/m^3   triple product nTτ_E=3.789e+21 keV s/m^3
# T=30.0 keV   nτ_E=1.540e+20 s/m^3   triple product nTτ_E=4.619e+21 keV s/m^3

Notice nτ_E keeps falling slightly past 25 keV before turning around (minimum at 25.7, not 25 or 30 exactly) while the triple product nTτ_E keeps rising past the minimum of nτ_E — because the extra factor of T outweighs nτ_E's shallow improvement. The two curves have different minima; most textbooks quote the nτ_E minimum location.

Q1. The energy confinement time τ_E, as used in the Lawson power balance, is best described as…
A. the time it takes a single ion to fuse
B. the time constant with which stored thermal energy would leak away if all heating stopped
C. the total lifetime of the plasma discharge
D. the time for the Maxwellian distribution to form
Show answer

B. τ_E is defined by P_loss = W/τ_E — it packages all the energy-loss physics into one bookkeeping time constant. (A) is a fusion reaction rate concept, not confinement. (C) a discharge can run far longer or shorter than τ_E. (D) is a kinetic-equilibration timescale, unrelated.

Q2. Ignition (alpha heating alone balances losses) requires a larger nτ_E than scientific breakeven (total fusion power balances losses) because…
A. ignition requires higher density, which breakeven does not
B. the alpha particle carries only E_α≈3.5 MeV of the full E_fus≈17.6 MeV, so ignition's effective heating source is ~5× smaller
C. ignition occurs at a different, higher Q-value reaction
D. breakeven ignores the neutron entirely
Show answer

B. Both conditions balance the same loss term 3nT/τ_E, but ignition's heating source is only the alpha's 3.5 MeV share, while breakeven credits the full 17.6 MeV — making ignition's required nτ_E larger by roughly E_fus/E_α≈5. (A) both conditions are stated for the same n. (C) same D-T reaction throughout. (D) the neutron's energy is simply not counted toward self-heating in either condition — it's lost to the blanket.

Q3. The Lawson condition nτ_E = 12T/(⟨σv⟩E) is a function of temperature alone (not an independent function of density) because…
A. density has no effect on fusion power
B. fusion power scales as n² while the loss term scales as n¹, so one factor of n cancels in the balance
C. ⟨σv⟩ itself depends on density
D. τ_E is defined to already include density
Show answer

B. P_fusion ∝ n² while P_loss = 3nT/τ_E ∝ n¹; setting them equal and dividing by n leaves nτ_E as a pure function of T. (A) density matters greatly for the absolute power, just not for the nτ_E threshold's T-dependence. (C) ⟨σv⟩ depends only on T by definition (Lecture 3). (D) τ_E is an independent confinement property, defined in Lecture 6.

Q4. The minimum required nτ_E for D-T ignition (≈1.5×10²⁰ s/m³) occurs near T≈…
A. 1 keV
B. 10 keV
C. 26 keV
D. 500 keV
Show answer

C. The numerical minimum of 12T/(⟨σv⟩E_α) for D-T occurs at T≈25.7 keV (Section 4's hands-on script), where ⟨σv⟩ has risen enough to offset the growing T in the numerator. (A) is far too cold — reactivity is still catastrophically suppressed there. (B)/(D) are both away from the actual numerical minimum.

Q5. The triple product nTτ_E and the plain condition nτ_E differ in that…
A. they are two names for exactly the same quantity
B. nTτ_E has a minimum at a different (higher) temperature than nτ_E, because of the extra explicit factor of T
C. nτ_E depends on T while nTτ_E does not
D. nTτ_E is only meaningful for D-D, not D-T
Show answer

B. nτ_E(T) already has a minimum near 25.7 keV; multiplying by the extra explicit T to form nTτ_E shifts where the product is smallest, and in fact nTτ_E keeps climbing past nτ_E's minimum (Section 4's script) since the extra T factor eventually dominates. (A) they are related but numerically and structurally distinct. (C) both depend on T. (D) the triple product applies to any fusion reaction, D-T included.
