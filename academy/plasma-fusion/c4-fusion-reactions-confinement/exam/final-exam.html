Which reactions fuse? Binding energy & Q-values · Week 1
1. In D + T → He-4 + n (Q = 17.59 MeV), the neutron carries ≈14.05 MeV and the alpha ≈3.54 MeV. The neutron carries the larger share because…
Answer: A. Equal-and-opposite momenta with unequal masses mean the lighter particle (the neutron) moves faster and carries more kinetic energy than the heavier alpha. (B) charge is irrelevant to the kinematics. (C) is a false generalization. (D) the split is fixed by the mass ratio.
2. Fusing light nuclei into heavier ones (up to around iron) releases energy because the products…
Answer: B. Climbing the steep left edge of the B/A curve toward helium-4 (7.07 MeV/nucleon) means more tightly bound products; the freed binding energy becomes kinetic energy. (A) nucleon number is conserved. (C) irrelevant. (D) backwards — the products are lighter (mass defect).
3. A reaction's "Q-value" (MeV released per reaction) and the "fusion gain Q" (P_fusion/P_aux, dimensionless) are…
Answer: C. One is energy per reaction in MeV; the other is a dimensionless power ratio defined in Lecture 5. This course flags "Q-value" explicitly to avoid the classic mix-up. (A)/(B)/(D) all conflate the two.
Fusion cross-sections: the Coulomb barrier & the Gamow factor · Week 2
4. The Gamow factor exp(−√(E_G/E)) represents…
Answer: D. It is the WKB tunneling probability through the 1/r Coulomb tail. (A) tunneling is explicitly non-classical. (B)/(C) unrelated to this factor.
5. D-He3's Gamow energy (≈4727 keV) is roughly 4× D-T's (≈1182 keV — ratio verified by python3) mainly because…
Answer: A. E_G = 2μc²(παZ₁Z₂)², so doubling Z₂ roughly quadruples E_G through the squared charge product (4727/1182 ≈ 4.0, verified by python3). (B)/(D) irrelevant to the barrier physics. (C) the reduced masses are nearly equal.
Reactivity ⟨σv⟩ & fusion power density · Week 3
6. The reactivity ⟨σv⟩(T) is defined as…
Answer: B. ⟨σv⟩ is the Maxwellian average of σ(v)v, a function of temperature alone. (A) confuses a single-energy cross-section with the thermally-averaged quantity. (C) ignores the distribution weighting. (D) density enters the power formula separately.
7. Verified by python3 (Bosch-Hale fits, both D-D branches summed): at T = 10 keV, D-T's reactivity exceeds the total D-D reactivity (D+D→T+p plus D+D→He3+n) by roughly…
Answer: C. ⟨σv⟩_DT/⟨σv⟩_DD(total) ≈ 1.136×10⁻²²/1.18×10⁻²⁴ ≈ 96 ≈ 100×, verified by python3 summing both D-D branches. Comparing against only a single branch would double this to the commonly mis-cited ~200×. (B) is closer to the D-T/D-He3 ratio.
8. Verified by python3 using the Bosch-Hale D-T fit: at n = 1×10²⁰ m⁻³, going from T = 5 keV (P_fusion≈96.3 kW/m³) to T = 10 keV (P_fusion≈800.9 kW/m³) increases fusion power density by roughly…
Answer: D. 800.9/96.3 ≈ 8.3×, verified by python3. (B) is a stale figure from an incorrect P(5 keV)≈12.0 kW/m³ calculation — the correct value is ≈96.3 kW/m³.
The Lawson criterion: power balance & the triple product · Week 4
9. Verified by python3 (minimizing 12T/(⟨σv⟩E_α) over T using the Bosch-Hale fit): the plain condition nτ_E for D-T ignition reaches its minimum value of ≈1.5×10²⁰ s/m³ at approximately…
Answer: A. The numerical minimum occurs at T≈25.7 keV ≈ 26 keV. (B) is where the triple product (a different function) is minimized, not plain nτ_E — don't conflate the two curves.
10. The triple product nTτ_E = 12T²/(⟨σv⟩E_α) is a genuinely different function of T than plain nτ_E. Verified by python3 (minimizing 12T²/(⟨σv⟩E_α)): its minimum, ≈2.8×10²¹ keV·s/m³, occurs at approximately…
Answer: B. The extra explicit factor of T in nTτ_E pulls the optimum DOWN to ≈13.5 keV, not up — the opposite of a common misconception. (A)/(C) wrongly assume the two curves share a minimum; they do not.
11. Using W=3nT and P_loss=P_aux+P_α (so Q=1 scientific breakeven means P_aux=P_fusion, with alpha self-heating also counted toward the loss balance), verified by python3: the ignition threshold (alpha power alone balances losses) is stricter than the Q=1 scientific-breakeven threshold by a factor of roughly…
Answer: C. The ratio (E_fus+E_α)/E_α ≈ 6.0×, independent of temperature (verified by python3) — not the ~5× or ~10× figures sometimes quoted, and the two thresholds are emphatically not the same (ignition is far stricter).
Ignition & fusion gain Q · Week 5
12. Scientific breakeven, properly defined (Lecture 5), is the condition…
Answer: D. (A) is a weaker, differently-named "power-balance point" (Lecture 4) — not the same condition. (C) is the much harder engineering bar. (B) is unrelated.
13. Using Q = (E_fus/E_α)·f_α/(1−f_α) ≈ 5.03f_α/(1−f_α), verified by python3: at f_α = 0.75, Q is approximately…
Answer: A. 5.03 × 0.75/0.25 ≈ 15.1, verified by python3. (B) is the f_α=0.5 case. (C)/(D) don't match the formula.
14. JET's record D-T shot reached a real, measured fusion gain of Q≈0.67 (P_fusion/P_aux in the actual discharge). This means JET…
Answer: B. Q≈0.67<1 — below scientific breakeven, let alone ignition or ITER's Q=10 target.
Energy confinement time & scaling laws · Week 6
15. In steady state, the energy confinement time τ_E is defined as…
Answer: C. τ_E ≡ W/P_loss — the formal version of the "leaky bucket" picture from Lecture 4. (A)/(B)/(D) are all different timescales entirely.
16. This course's reduced confinement scaling law includes a NEGATIVE exponent on heating power (τ_E ∝ P_heat^−0.69). This means…
Answer: D. More injected power drives more turbulent transport, empirically shrinking τ_E even as it raises temperature — a robust, well-established empirical feature of H-mode confinement, not an error.
Plasma beta & the Troyon limit · Week 7
17. Plasma beta β = 2μ₀p/B² measures…
Answer: A. Beta directly compares plasma pressure p to magnetic pressure B²/2μ₀ — a dimensionless measure of how efficiently the field is being used. (B)/(C)/(D) are all unrelated quantities.
18. At fixed temperature and magnetic field, fusion power density scales as P_fusion ∝ n² ∝ β² (since density ties to pressure/beta at fixed T, B). Doubling beta therefore changes fusion power density by roughly…
Answer: B. P_fusion ∝ β², so doubling β quadruples P_fusion. (A) ignores the squared dependence. (C) overstates it. (D) ignores the n²-dependence entirely.
Confinement approaches: magnetic vs inertial · Week 8
19. MCF (n~10²⁰ m⁻³, τ_E~seconds) and ICF (n~10³¹ m⁻³, τ_E~10⁻¹⁰ s) can both target ignition despite differing by eleven orders of magnitude in both n and τ_E because…
Answer: C. Any combination of n, T, τ_E that reaches the same product works. (A)/(B) are false — the same criterion applies to both. (D) both typically use D-T.
20. In inertial confinement fusion, the confinement time τ_E is set by…
Answer: D. There is no confining field in ICF — the fuel holds together only as long as its own inertia resists disassembly. (A)/(B) are MCF concepts. (C) pulse energy affects compression, not the disassembly-time formula directly.
The fusion triangle: where real devices sit · Week 9
21. The Lawson diagram plots…
Answer: A. (B)/(C)/(D) describe different, unrelated plots from earlier lectures.
22. Verified by python3 (0-D power balance): a representative "ITER-target" point at n=1×10²⁰ m⁻³, T=13 keV, τ_E≈1.43 s — the confinement time that actually gives Q=10 at this n, T — has a triple product of ≈1.86×10²¹ keV·s/m³. Relative to the ≈3×10²¹ ignition benchmark, this point is…
Answer: B. Q=10 is a deliberately high-gain target, not full ignition — a device can have an impressively high Q while still sitting below its own ignition curve. (A)/(C) misstate the margin; (D) both are routinely computed together.
23. A NIF-class ICF hot spot (n≈2.4×10³¹ m⁻³, T≈5 keV, τ_E≈7×10⁻¹¹ s, triple product ≈8.2×10²¹ keV·s/m³) is sometimes scored against the flat ≈3×10²¹ ignition benchmark, giving a margin of ×2.74. Verified by python3 using the actual T-dependent ignition curve (whose value AT 5 keV is ≈6.3×10²¹, since 5 keV sits well below the curve's ≈13-14 keV minimum), the correct margin is closer to…
Answer: C. 8.2×10²¹/6.3×10²¹ ≈ ×1.3. The flat-benchmark comparison overstates the margin because that benchmark is only valid near the curve's own ≈13-14 keV minimum, far from ICF's 5 keV operating point.
Capstone: a Lawson + scaling-law calculator · Week 10
24. Verified by python3: running the capstone calculator at n=1×10²⁰ m⁻³, T=13 keV, τ_E=1.43 s (the Q=10 design point) gives a triple product of ≈1.86×10²¹ keV·s/m³, a fusion gain Q≈9.99, and an alpha self-heating fraction f_α of approximately…
Answer: D. f_α≈66.5% matches the value independently derived in Lecture 5 for Q=10 (solving Q=5.03f_α/(1−f_α)=10). (C) was the (now-corrected) old, over-ignited τ_E=3.0 s point's value.
25. The capstone calculator, and the whole Lawson + scaling-law toolchain built across this course, is best described as…
Answer: A. This is deliberately a 0-D reduced model — the same role diffusion theory plays relative to full Monte Carlo neutronics in the Nuclear specialization. (B)/(C) vastly overstate its fidelity. (D) the triple-product and power-balance functions are general to any D-T plasma.
