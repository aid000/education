Growth Fundamentals & the Big Picture · Week 1
1. Which of the following is correctly classified as a flow rather than a stock?
Answer: B. A flow is measured per unit of time; investment during the year is exactly that. Capital on hand, population, and a productivity level are all measured at a point in time — stocks, not flows.
2. By the "rule of 70," a country growing at a steady 2% per year doubles its income in about…
Answer: D. Doubling time ≈ 70 / (growth rate in %) = 70 / 2 = 35 years. Small, persistent differences in growth rates compound into enormous gaps over a few decades.
The Solow Model & Steady State · Week 2
3. In the fundamental Solow equation dk/dt = s·f(k) − δk, capital per worker stops changing exactly when…
Answer: A. The steady state is where actual investment s·f(k) exactly equals depreciation δk, so dk/dt = 0. Comparing the rates s and δ directly (B) is a classic error — it's the products that must balance.
4. An economy has f(k) = A·kα with A = 1, α = 0.4, s = 0.2, δ = 0.08. Using k* = (sA/δ)1/(1−α), the steady-state capital per worker k* is closest to…
Answer: C. k* = (0.2/0.08)1/0.6 = 2.51.667 ≈ 4.61. (A) is just s/δ with no exponent applied; (D) confuses the exponent's reciprocal.
5. For the per-worker production function y = A·kα, the marginal product f′(k) = αA·kα−1…
Answer: B. Because α < 1, the exponent α−1 is negative, so f′(k) shrinks toward zero as k grows — the engine behind every steady state in this course.
Convergence & the Golden Rule · Week 3
6. An economy saves s = 0.5, while its golden-rule savings rate is sgold = α = 0.35. This economy is…
Answer: A. s > sgold means k* > k_gold, i.e. f′(k*) < n+δ — capital so abundant it no longer pays for its own upkeep. Lowering s raises consumption immediately and at the new steady state: a true free lunch, not a trade-off.
7. For Cobb–Douglas output f(k) = kα, the golden-rule condition f′(k_gold) = n + δ implies a golden-rule savings rate of…
Answer: D. At the golden rule, (n+δ)k = f′(k)·k = α·f(k), and since s·f(k) = (n+δ)k at any steady state, sgold = α exactly — the n and δ values cancel out.
8. "Conditional convergence," as opposed to absolute convergence, predicts that…
Answer: C. Conditional convergence drops the assumption of a shared steady state: a poor country near its own (low) k* can grow slowly without contradicting the model. Absolute convergence (A, B) fails empirically across the whole world.
Growth Accounting & TFP · Week 4
9. An economy reports gY = 5%, gK = 4%, gL = 1%, and capital share α = 0.3. The Solow residual (TFP growth) is…
Answer: B. gA = gY − α·gK − (1−α)·gL = 5 − 0.3(4) − 0.7(1) = 5 − 1.2 − 0.7 = 3.1%. (A) is just the capital contribution alone; (C) omits the capital term.
10. The Solow residual is often called "a measure of our ignorance" chiefly because…
Answer: D. Because gA is whatever is left over after subtracting measured input contributions, any error in K, L, or α lands in it alongside real technical change — it is an accounting leftover, not a clean reading of innovation.
Consumption, Savings & Intertemporal Choice · Week 5
11. In the present-value budget constraint c1 + c2/(1+r) = y1 + y2/(1+r), the household is ultimately constrained by…
Answer: C. The household can borrow against future income or save current income — it is bound only by the present value of its total income stream, not by any single period's income.
12. With log utility and β(1+r) > 1, the optimal consumption path across the two periods…
Answer: A. From the Euler equation, c2 = β(1+r)·c1; when β(1+r) > 1 the reward for waiting beats impatience, so consumption rises. A flat path (B) only occurs in the knife-edge case β(1+r) = 1.
The Ramsey Model of Optimal Growth · Week 6
13. The single biggest structural difference between the Ramsey model and the Solow model is that in Ramsey…
Answer: D. Ramsey endogenizes saving via an optimizing household; Solow simply assumes a constant s. Depreciation, the production function, and population growth n all remain part of the Ramsey model.
14. Ramsey's steady state satisfies f′(k*) = ρ + δ, while Solow's golden rule requires f′(k) = n + δ. Compared with the golden-rule capital stock, Ramsey's k* is…
Answer: B. Since f′ is decreasing, a larger required marginal product (ρ + δ when ρ > n) must come from a smaller k*. Impatient households rationally accumulate less than the consumption-maximizing golden-rule amount.
15. "Saddle-path stability" in the Ramsey phase diagram means that…
Answer: A. A saddle has a unique convergent path; given inherited capital k(0), the household must jump consumption onto that one path. This is conditional, not global, stability — a feature that makes the optimum determinate.
Dynamic Optimization, Euler & Bellman · Week 7
16. The consumption Euler equation u′(ct) = βRt+1u′(ct+1) is best interpreted as…
Answer: C. It balances the marginal utility lost today against the discounted marginal benefit tomorrow — a reversible experiment that cannot improve on the optimum. It is a separate condition from the resource constraint (B) and the transversality condition (D).
17. With CRRA utility, ct+1/ct = (βR)1/σ. If βR < 1, consumption…
Answer: D. βR < 1 means (βR)1/σ < 1, so ct+1 < ct — impatience outweighs the return on saving, and the household tilts consumption toward the present.
18. The transversality condition is needed in addition to the Euler equation and the initial capital k0 because…
Answer: A. The Euler equation fixes only the path's shape between consecutive periods; the TVC rules out paths that hoard capital forever without ever enjoying it. Capital itself can settle at a positive k* (D is false) — it is the discounted shadow value of leftover capital that must vanish.
Solving Growth Models Numerically · Week 8
19. Why must most optimal-growth models be solved numerically rather than by hand?
Answer: B. A few knife-edge cases (log utility, full depreciation) solve in closed form, but realistic calibrations don't — the optimal path exists and is unique, but can only be computed. The Euler equation is correct (A); the steady state is well-defined (D).
20. In the shooting method for the Ramsey/optimal-growth model, the single unknown searched over is…
Answer: C. k0 is given and k* is computed from the steady-state condition; the missing number that threads a path between them is the initial consumption c0, found by bisection.
21. You guess an initial consumption c0 that is too high in a shooting-method simulation. The simulated capital path will…
Answer: D. Over-consuming starves investment, so capital declines and collapses. Over-saving (too-low c0) produces the overshoot described in (A); only the exact saddle value avoids both failure modes.
Business Cycles & Stochastic Dynamics · Week 9
22. In the AR(1) process zt+1 = ρzt + εt+1, a higher ρ (holding σ fixed) means…
Answer: B. ρ is the persistence coefficient; it controls how long a deviation lingers, not the size of each new innovation (that is σ's job, A). The process stays stationary as long as |ρ| < 1, with mean zero regardless of ρ.
23. The core claim of the real-business-cycle (RBC) model is that economic fluctuations are…
Answer: C. RBC's radical claim is that cycles are efficient reactions to real technology shocks, not failures to be smoothed away (B) and not driven by money (A). The dynamics are highly structured, not pure noise (D).
Capstone: Calibrate, Solve & Experiment · Week 10
24. Calibration differs from econometric estimation chiefly because calibration…
Answer: A. Calibration sets parameters to hit a few chosen long-run moments (α from capital's income share, δ from depreciation data, etc.) and validates against untargeted moments; estimation instead minimizes fit error over the whole sample.
25. A capstone report finds that a savings-rate hike raises long-run output by 12%. The most important thing still missing is…
Answer: D. A higher s mechanically raises steady-state output in this class of models — that is no surprise. The open question is whether welfare rises once the early-transition consumption dip is priced in.
