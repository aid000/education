Computational Foundations & Optimization · Weeks 1–2
1. Why do most realistic economic models require a numerical, rather than closed-form, solution?
Answer: A. The consumption Euler equation is the textbook example: once uncertainty and a general utility function enter, the unknown consumption function appears implicitly on both sides, with no formula to solve for it by hand.
2. In the five-stage computational workflow (Model → Discretize → Solve → Validate → Interpret), what happens if validation fails?
Answer: B. A converged solver is not automatically a correct one — if residual checks fail, the standard response is to refine the discretization and re-solve, looping back through the pipeline.
3. What makes BFGS a practical default for smooth, unconstrained optimization problems?
Answer: D. BFGS is a quasi-Newton method: it builds up curvature information from gradients it has already seen, avoiding the expense of forming and inverting a true Hessian.
4. In the Lagrangian L(x, λ) = f(x) + λ·g(x), economists interpret λ as…
Answer: C. In a budget problem λ is the marginal utility of income — how much better off you'd be with one more dollar of the constrained resource.
Dynamic Programming & Value Function Iteration · Weeks 3–4
5. In a dynamic program, a good choice of state variable must be…
Answer: A. The state must summarize everything needed to act optimally going forward; sunk history (like accumulated past reward) is not part of it.
6. The principle of optimality that justifies the Bellman equation states that…
Answer: D. If the continuation from the next state were not itself optimal, you could swap in a better continuation and beat the original plan — a contradiction. That is exactly the βV(s') term in the Bellman equation.
7. With log utility and discount factor β, the optimal infinite-horizon cake-eating policy is to eat…
Answer: B. The closed-form solution is c* = (1 − β)x: a patient eater (β near 1) eats a thin slice each period; an impatient eater (β near 0) devours most of it now.
8. Value function iteration is guaranteed to converge to a unique fixed point from any starting guess because the Bellman operator T is…
Answer: C. Blackwell's sufficient conditions (monotonicity plus discounting) make T a β-contraction, so by the Banach fixed-point theorem it has a unique fixed point reached from any starting guess, with error shrinking geometrically.
9. Using a dangerously coarse grid (say, only 20 points) to discretize the state space in value function iteration causes…
Answer: D. With only 20 grid points, the policy can only ever choose one of 20 savings levels, so both the policy and the value function come out visibly coarsened and biased; a denser grid (or interpolation) is the fix.
10. Once value function iteration converges to V*, the optimal policy function σ*(s) is obtained by…
Answer: A. The policy is exactly the maximizing choice computed inside the Bellman update — one more sweep over V*, recording the argmax rather than just the max, yields σ*.
Stochastic Growth & Markov Chains · Weeks 5–6
11. Precautionary saving — building an extra buffer against risky future income — is driven specifically by…
Answer: C. Mere risk aversion (u'' < 0) is not enough — quadratic utility is risk-averse but has u'''=0 and produces no precautionary motive. Convex marginal utility (prudence) is what generates the extra buffer.
12. A common coding bug when solving the stochastic growth model's Bellman update is…
Answer: B. Dropping the expectation over z' erases all the stochastic content, quietly turning the stochastic growth model into a deterministic one with no warning.
13. The Markov property states that the probability distribution of the next state depends on…
Answer: D. That is the "memoryless" property: history before the current state adds no predictive power once the current state is known.
14. A valid transition matrix P, under the row-vector convention used in this course, must have…
Answer: C. Each row is a probability distribution over next states — row-stochastic, not column-stochastic, under this course's ψP convention.
15. For an ergodic (irreducible and aperiodic) finite Markov chain, the long-run fraction of time spent in state i…
Answer: A. The ergodic theorem ties long-run time-averages to the unique stationary distribution, independent of where the chain started. (C) only holds in the special uniform case.
Equilibrium, Root-Finding & Convex Programming · Weeks 7–8
16. Before calling a bracketing root-finder like brentq on an excess-demand function f(p), you must confirm that…
Answer: B. A sign change brackets a root by the Intermediate Value Theorem; without one, brentq raises an error. It needs no derivative (ruling out A) and an empty interval (C) leaves nothing to search.
17. Compared with bisection, Newton's method for root-finding is typically faster because it uses the slope f'(p) — but the key risk is that it…
Answer: D. Newton trades bisection's guaranteed-but-slow safety for speed — it can fly off to infinity when the slope is nearly zero, which is exactly why brentq blends both approaches.
18. Fixed-point iteration pn+1 = g(pn) is locally stable — the cobweb spirals inward to p* — exactly when, at the fixed point…
Answer: A. A slope magnitude below 1 at the fixed point makes g a local contraction, so the cobweb staircase spirals inward rather than exploding outward.
19. The defining practical payoff of a convex optimization problem — minimizing a convex function over a convex set — is that…
Answer: C. Convexity means there is no wrong valley to get trapped in — a solver can certify the global optimum, even with thousands of variables.
20. Which of the following is a linear program (LP) in standard form?
Answer: D. A linear objective with linear inequality constraints is exactly the LP standard form. (A) is a quadratic program; (B) maximizes a convex quadratic (non-convex); (C) has a non-convex trigonometric objective.
21. The dual variable (shadow price) CVXPY reports on a binding constraint tells you…
Answer: B. The dual is the marginal value of the resource — d(optimal value)/d(bi) — and is zero for any slack (non-binding) constraint by complementary slackness.
Market Design & Capstone · Weeks 9–10
22. A matching produced by the deferred-acceptance algorithm is called stable because…
Answer: A. Stability means no blocking pair exists — it says nothing about maximizing total welfare, and a stable matching is usually not unique (the proposing side gets its best stable partner, the receiving side its worst).
23. In least-cost economic dispatch, the uniform market-clearing price equals…
Answer: C. The marginal (last-dispatched) unit sets the uniform price; cheaper, inframarginal generators earn rent above their own cost rather than setting the price themselves.
24. The four stages of an end-to-end computational-economics study, in their necessary order, are…
Answer: D. You must write the model down (pose) before solving it, fit it to data (calibrate), and only then change a lever and re-solve (counterfactual) — every other ordering puts a step before its prerequisite.
25. The strongest evidence that a calibrated model has captured the real economic mechanism — rather than just being forced to fit — is that it…
Answer: B. Matching targeted moments is circular — you forced that fit. Reproducing a moment you never targeted is genuine evidence the model's mechanism, not just its parameters, resembles reality.
