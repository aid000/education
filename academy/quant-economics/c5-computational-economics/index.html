🛠️ Hands-On Project

A Dynamic-Programming Model or Policy Simulation (Python)

Build and solve a small computational-economics model end to end: pick a dynamic-programming problem (optimal savings, job search, or an inventory/firm decision) or a policy simulation, then implement it with value function iteration or a CVXPY formulation. You deliver a short report with figures comparing your solver's output against analytical or benchmark cases. The toolkit (Python + QuantEcon + NumPy + SciPy + CVXPY + Matplotlib) is set up in Lecture 1 — all free and pip-installable.

Wk 1: install Python + QuantEcon + NumPy/SciPy/CVXPY Wk 4: working value-function-iteration solver Wk 8: model or CVXPY formulation complete Wk 10: report + figures submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: Dynamic programming breaks a lifetime decision into one step plus "everything after." Where in real economic life do you see people (fail to) think recursively about the future?
  • Week 6: Markov chains assume the future depends only on the present state, not the full history. When is that a reasonable model of the economy, and when does it mislead?
  • Week 9: Energy markets are cleared by solving an optimization every few minutes. What does it mean for "the price" to be the output of a computer program rather than a haggling crowd?
