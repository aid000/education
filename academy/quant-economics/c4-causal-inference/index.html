🛠️ Hands-On Project

A Causal Study of a Real Policy on Public Data (Python)

Pick a real-world policy — a minimum-wage change, a school-funding reform, a public-health mandate — find a public dataset, and estimate its causal effect using the design that best fits the setting (DiD, IV, RD, matching, or panel fixed effects). You justify the identifying assumptions, run robustness checks, and deliver a short report with figures comparing your estimate against naive correlations. Week 1 includes a one-time Python + statsmodels + linearmodels install (step-by-step in Lecture 1).

Wk 1: install Python + statsmodels + linearmodels + pandas Wk 4: policy + dataset + design chosen Wk 8: main causal estimate + robustness checks Wk 10: report + figures submitted

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 9

  • Week 3: "People who go to the gym are healthier" — is that the gym working, or the kind of person who joins one? Where else does selection bias masquerade as a treatment effect?
  • Week 6: A good instrument has to affect the outcome only through the treatment. Why is the exclusion restriction almost impossible to test — and how do credible studies defend it anyway?
  • Week 9: Fixed effects sweep away everything that doesn't change over time. What can they never rescue you from, and when does that limitation bite hardest in policy evaluation?
