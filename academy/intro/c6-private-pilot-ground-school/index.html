🛠️ Hands-On Project

Cross-Country Flight-Plan Package

Pick a real VFR cross-country route (60–150 nm) on the free SkyVector sectional-chart viewer, build a full nav log — true course, wind-corrected heading, groundspeed, time, and fuel for each leg — using a free E6-B flight-computer app (or a manual E6-B wheel), pull a live METAR/TAF for your route and write a go/no-go decision, run a weight-and-balance calculation for a sample training aircraft, and compile your own personal "Must-Know Tables" reference (VFR minimums, airspace requirements, right-of-way order). Everything runs on free, laptop-only tools.

Wk 1: set up SkyVector + a free E6-B app; sketch a candidate route Wk 3: route locked in on SkyVector, with the airspace along it identified Wk 7: checkpoint — planned cruise airspeed chosen (feeds Week 8's wind triangles) Wk 8: checkpoint — nav log built (course/heading/groundspeed/time per leg) Wk 9: checkpoint — weight & balance calculation for a sample aircraft Wk 10: package due — nav log + weather briefing/go-no-go + W&B + Must-Know Tables

💬 Discussions

Three checkpoints — weeks 3, 6, 10

  • Week 3: Airspace rules get stricter the busier and faster the traffic gets (G → E → D → C → B → A). Why does it make sense to tie the rules to traffic density and speed rather than just altitude, and where does ADS-B fit into that logic?
  • Week 6: You've decoded a METAR/TAF for your project route and the ceiling is marginal VFR. Walk through your go/no-go reasoning — what specific numbers would flip your decision, and why set a personal minimum stricter than the legal minimum?
  • Week 10: Pick one hazardous attitude (anti-authority, impulsivity, invulnerability, macho, or resignation). Describe a scenario where it could push a pilot toward a bad decision, and what the matching "antidote thought" would be.
