The money map, time value & compounding · Week 1
1. Using the Rule of 72, money earning a steady 9% annual return roughly doubles in about…
Answer: C. Rule of 72: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate = 72 ÷ 9 = 8 years.
2. $1,000 invested at 8%, compounded annually with nothing added, after 30 years is closest to. Use FV = PV(1 + r)^n.
Answer: B. 1.08^30 ≈ 10.06, so $1,000 × 10.06 ≈ $10,060. (Rule of 72 sanity check: ~9-yr doublings, ~3.3 doublings in 30 yrs ⟹ roughly 10×.)
3. Two savers each invest the same total dollars at the same return. Why does the one who starts earlier typically end with far more?
Answer: A. Time is the dominant lever in compounding — the earliest dollars earn returns on prior returns for the longest stretch.
Account types — IRA, Roth, brokerage & 401(k) · Week 2
4. The headline difference between a Traditional IRA and a Roth IRA is…
Answer: B. Traditional = pre-tax in / taxed out; Roth = after-tax in / tax-free qualified withdrawals. Both grow tax-sheltered.
5. As of 2026 (IRS), the IRA/Roth IRA annual contribution limit and the age-50+ catch-up are…
Answer: C. Per the IRS announced 2026 amounts: $7,500 IRA limit with a $1,100 age-50+ catch-up. Verify current figures at irs.gov — they change yearly.
6. As of 2026 (IRS), the standard employee elective deferral limit for a 401(k) is about…
Answer: D. The IRS-announced 2026 employee 401(k) deferral limit is $24,500 (plus an $8,000 age-50+ catch-up). This is separate from, and higher than, the IRA limit. Verify at irs.gov.
7. Your employer matches 100% of 401(k) contributions up to 4% of pay. The smartest first move is usually to…
Answer: A. A dollar-for-dollar match is a guaranteed 100% return on those dollars — capturing the full match is typically the highest-priority savings step.
Budgeting systems & cash flow · Week 3
8. Under the 50/30/20 guideline, the "20" is for…
Answer: B. 50% needs / 30% wants / 20% savings & debt paydown — applied to after-tax income.
9. In zero-based budgeting, the goal each month is to…
Answer: D. Zero-based means income − (spending + saving + investing) = 0; every dollar is intentionally assigned, including to savings.
Taxes — brackets, FICA & capital gains · Week 4
10. Your marginal tax rate is 22% but your effective rate is 13%. This is because…
Answer: A. Marginal = the rate on your next dollar (your top bracket); effective = total tax ÷ total income. Progressive brackets make effective < marginal.
11. The employee share of FICA is best summarized (current rates) as…
Answer: B. Employee FICA = 6.2% Social Security (only up to the SSA wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (no cap; an extra 0.9% applies above high-income thresholds).
12. As of 2026 (SSA), the Social Security wage base — the cap above which the 6.2% no longer applies — is closest to…
Answer: C. The SSA-announced 2026 Social Security wage base is $184,500. Medicare's 1.45% has no such cap. Confirm the current base at ssa.gov — it rises most years.
13. You sell a stock held 8 months for a gain. That gain is taxed as…
Answer: D. Held ≤ 1 year ⟹ short-term, taxed as ordinary income. Only assets held > 1 year get the preferential long-term 0/15/20% rates.
Investing I — index funds, diversification & allocation · Week 5
14. Fund A charges a 0.04% expense ratio; Fund B charges 0.84%. On a $100,000 balance in year one, the fee difference is about…
Answer: A. The gap is 0.80% of $100,000 = $800/year — and that drag compounds against you for decades, which is why low expense ratios matter so much.
15. A "total market" index fund is favored partly because it…
Answer: B. Broad index funds own a wide slice of the market cheaply; diversification reduces single-company risk but not overall market risk.
16. "Asset allocation" most directly refers to…
Answer: C. Allocation is the mix across asset classes; adding international exposure to a domestic fund is a diversification (allocation) decision.
Investing II — risk, return & why active underperforms · Week 6
17. The general evidence (e.g., SPIVA-style scorecards) on actively managed funds is best stated as…
Answer: C. The careful claim: net of fees, a majority underperform over long periods and past winners rarely persist — not that beating the market is impossible.
18. "Drawdown" measures…
Answer: D. Drawdown is the peak-to-trough drop. Large drawdowns are what tempt investors to sell at the bottom.
19. A classic behavioral pitfall that hurts long-term returns is…
Answer: A. Performance-chasing and panic-selling lock in losses and miss rebounds. The others are disciplined behaviors that help.
20. The basic risk–return relationship says that, in general…
Answer: B. Investors demand higher expected return to bear more risk — though higher risk also means a wider range of outcomes, not a guaranteed payoff.
Balanced vs focused portfolios & the startup mindset · Week 7
21. A startup employee is granted stock options with a 4-year vest and 1-year cliff. A key risk to understand is…
Answer: D. Startup equity is a concentrated, illiquid, high-variance bet: it can be hugely valuable or worthless, and private shares are hard to sell before a liquidity event.
22. Compared with a balanced, diversified portfolio, a "focused" (concentrated) bet…
Answer: D. Concentration widens the range of outcomes — bigger upside but bigger downside. Diversification trades away some upside for lower single-bet risk.
Hedge funds, fees & leverage · Week 8
23. The classic hedge-fund "2 and 20" fee structure means…
Answer: B. "2 and 20" = ~2% of assets annually + ~20% of profits. These high fees are a major reason net returns often disappoint relative to low-cost index funds.
24. Using leverage (borrowed money) to invest…
Answer: A. Leverage scales returns in both directions and adds the risk of forced liquidation; combined with illiquidity and limited access, it's why hedge funds are higher-risk and not retail-friendly.
Rent vs buy — opportunity cost & NPV · Week 9
25. In a rent-vs-buy comparison, the "opportunity cost of the down payment" refers to…
Answer: C. A proper NPV/break-even rent-vs-buy model counts the forgone investment return on the down payment (plus maintenance, taxes, transaction costs, and lost mobility) — not just the mortgage payment.
