Predictive vs Agile · Week 1
1. The clearest distinction between a project and ongoing operations is that a project…
Answer: B. A project is a temporary endeavor with a defined start and end that produces a unique deliverable; operations are ongoing and repetitive.
2. A predictive (waterfall/PMP) approach fits best when…
Answer: C. Predictive plans the whole scope up front; it shines when requirements are stable and re-work is expensive (e.g., construction). High uncertainty favors empirical/agile control.
The PMP backbone · Week 2
3. Which is the correct order of the five PMBOK process groups?
Answer: A. The five groups are Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring & Controlling, and Closing. Monitoring & Controlling runs alongside execution throughout.
4. The document that formally authorizes a project and names the project manager is the…
Answer: D. The project charter is produced in Initiating; it authorizes the project, states high-level objectives, and grants the PM authority over resources.
5. Recent editions of the PMBOK Guide shifted their primary emphasis toward…
Answer: B. The newer PMBOK moved from a process-heavy model to principles and performance domains, explicitly embracing predictive, adaptive, and hybrid approaches.
Scrum in depth · Week 3
6. Per the Scrum Guide, the three accountabilities on a Scrum Team are…
Answer: A. Scrum defines exactly three accountabilities: the Product Owner (value/backlog), the Scrum Master (the process and impediments), and the Developers (who build the Increment).
7. Who is accountable for ordering and managing the Product Backlog to maximize value?
Answer: C. The Product Owner owns and orders the Product Backlog. The Scrum Master coaches the process; the Developers decide how much they can pull into a Sprint.
8. Which set lists the three Scrum artifacts with their commitments correctly?
Answer: D. The three artifacts each carry a commitment: Product Backlog → Product Goal, Sprint Backlog → Sprint Goal, Increment → Definition of Done.
9. The Daily Scrum is best described as a…
Answer: B. The Daily Scrum is a ~15-minute event for the Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the plan — not a status report to a boss.
10. The Sprint Review and the Sprint Retrospective differ in that…
Answer: A. The Sprint Review inspects the Increment and adapts the backlog with stakeholders; the Retrospective is the team improving its own process. Both close out the Sprint.
Estimation, planning & backlogs · Week 4
11. Story points are a measure of…
Answer: C. Story points estimate relative effort/complexity. Teams convert points to a forecast over time using velocity rather than guessing hours directly.
12. A team completed 20, 24, and 22 points over its last three Sprints. Its velocity is most usefully treated as…
Answer: B. Velocity is the average points completed per Sprint (~22 here), used as a planning forecast for one team — not a quota, and never a cross-team comparison.
13. In MoSCoW prioritization, the letters stand for…
Answer: D. MoSCoW sorts work into Must-have, Should-have, Could-have, and Won't-have (for now) — a fast way to protect the essential when time is tight.
14. The Definition of Done exists primarily to…
Answer: A. The Definition of Done is the shared quality bar (tested, reviewed, documented, etc.) that makes "done" mean the same thing to everyone, preventing hidden incomplete work.
Theory of Constraints · Weeks 5–6
15. Goldratt's The Goal presents the Theory of Constraints in the form of a…
Answer: C. The Goal (Goldratt) is a business novel: plant manager Alex Rogo uncovers the five focusing steps while saving his factory.
16. The five focusing steps, in order, are…
Answer: B. Identify the constraint → Exploit it (get the most from it as-is) → Subordinate everything else to it → Elevate (add capacity) → Repeat, watching that inertia doesn't become the new constraint.
17. In ToC, the throughput of the whole system is governed by…
Answer: D. A system can't move faster than its constraint. Optimizing non-bottlenecks just piles up inventory; the bottleneck sets the pace of the whole chain.
18. Goldratt's three operational measures are…
Answer: A. Throughput (rate the system makes money via sales), Inventory (money tied up in things to sell), and Operating Expense (money spent turning inventory into throughput).
19. A manager proudly runs a non-bottleneck machine at 100% to maximize its local efficiency. In ToC terms this is a classic case of…
Answer: C. Maxing out a non-constraint creates excess inventory and ties up cash without increasing system throughput — a local optimum that damages the global goal. Non-bottlenecks should be subordinated to the constraint.
What great managers do · Weeks 7–8
20. First, Break All the Rules (Buckingham & Coffman) distills great management into four keys. Which list is correct?
Answer: B. The four keys: select for talent (not just experience), define the right outcomes (not steps), focus on strengths (not fixing weaknesses), and find the right fit (not just the next rung up).
21. The book draws a sharp line between talent, skills, and knowledge. Talent is best described as…
Answer: D. Skills can be taught and knowledge can be learned, but talent is a recurring pattern of thinking/feeling/behaving — which is why great managers select for it rather than trying to install it.
22. The Q12 questions matter because, in the research, a team's answers to them…
Answer: A. The twelve engagement questions were selected because they correlate with business outcomes — productivity, retention, and customer metrics — making them a practical measure of a strong workgroup.
23. Which item is representative of the Q12 engagement questions?
Answer: C. Those three are genuine Q12 items. Notice they target clarity of expectations, playing to strengths, and feeling cared about — things a local manager controls, not perks set by the company.
Feedback, delegation & capstone · Weeks 9–10
24. "Outcome-based delegation," consistent with defining the right outcomes, means a manager should…
Answer: B. Define the outcome (and the guardrails), then trust people to find their own route. Mandating steps suppresses the very talent you hired for and doesn't scale.
25. In the capstone, you run one real goal using Scrum + ToC + great-manager ideas together. The best synthesis is to…
Answer: D. The frameworks complement each other: Scrum gives the cadence of inspect-and-adapt, ToC tells you where to focus (the constraint), and the four keys make sure the right people own the right outcomes.
