Scene size-up & patient assessment · Weeks 1–2
1. You arrive at a collapse in a parking garage. Before touching the patient, your very first priority is to…
Answer: B. Scene safety and body-substance-isolation precautions come before patient contact — you can't help anyone if you become a second casualty.
2. A patient opens their eyes only when you pinch their shoulder but does not respond to your voice. On the AVPU scale they are…
Answer: A. AVPU = Alert, Verbal, Pain, Unresponsive. Responding only to a painful stimulus (not voice) is "P."
3. In the primary assessment (ABCDE), which problem must be corrected first?
Answer: C. ABCDE is an ordered priority list — Airway is treated before Breathing, Circulation, Disability, and Exposure. A compromised airway kills fastest, so it is fixed first.
4. The mnemonic SAMPLE is used to gather a patient history. The "M" stands for…
Answer: D. SAMPLE = Signs/symptoms, Allergies, Medications, Past history, Last oral intake, Events leading up. "M" is Medications.
5. A patient reports chest pain. You ask, "Does anything make it worse or better?" Which OPQRST letter does that question address?
Answer: A. OPQRST = Onset, Provocation/Palliation, Quality, Region/Radiation, Severity, Time. What makes pain better or worse is Provocation/Palliation.
Airway, respiration & ventilation · Week 3
6. An unresponsive patient was just pulled from a car wreck with a suspected spinal injury. The preferred way to open the airway is…
Answer: C. With possible cervical-spine trauma, the jaw-thrust opens the airway without extending the neck. Head-tilt/chin-lift is preferred only when no spinal injury is suspected.
7. An oropharyngeal airway (OPA) should NOT be inserted in a patient who…
Answer: D. An OPA is only for patients without a gag reflex; in someone who still gags it can trigger vomiting and aspiration. Consider an NPA instead.
8. A nasopharyngeal airway (NPA) is most appropriate for a patient who is…
Answer: A. An NPA is tolerated by patients who still have a gag reflex. It is avoided with suspected skull-base or severe facial fractures (risk of intracranial misplacement).
9. A breathing patient needs the highest practical oxygen concentration. The best simple delivery device is a…
Answer: B. A non-rebreather mask at 12–15 L/min delivers roughly 90%+ oxygen to a spontaneously breathing patient — the highest of the common devices.
10. When ventilating an apneic adult with a bag-valve-mask (BVM), the most important sign that you are doing it correctly is…
Answer: C. Adequate ventilation is judged by visible, gentle chest rise. Over-fast or over-forceful squeezing inflates the stomach (gastric distension) and raises aspiration risk.
CPR, AED & cardiac emergencies · Week 4
11. The recommended chest-compression rate for adult CPR is…
Answer: D. Current guidelines specify 100–120 compressions per minute — fast enough to perfuse but not so fast that the chest can't recoil and refill.
12. The recommended compression depth for an average adult is…
Answer: A. Push at least 2 in (5 cm) for an adult, allowing full recoil between compressions; excessive depth (>~2.4 in / 6 cm) can cause injury.
13. For a single lay rescuer performing CPR on an adult, the compression-to-ventilation ratio is…
Answer: B. Single-rescuer adult CPR uses 30 compressions to 2 breaths. (15:2 applies to two-rescuer infant/child CPR.)
14. An AED has been applied and announces "Shock advised." You should…
Answer: C. Confirm no one is touching the patient ("I'm clear, you're clear, everyone's clear"), then press the shock button. Resume compressions immediately afterward.
15. A conscious adult with crushing chest pain that may be acute coronary syndrome (ACS), with no contraindication, may be given…
Answer: D. Aspirin's antiplatelet effect can limit clot growth in a suspected heart attack. It is given only if there's no allergy or other contraindication, and per local protocol.
Bleeding control & trauma · Week 5
16. A patient has spurting, life-threatening bleeding from a mid-thigh wound that direct pressure isn't controlling. The next step is to…
Answer: A. For life-threatening extremity hemorrhage uncontrolled by direct pressure, a tourniquet placed proximal to the wound and tightened until bleeding stops is the priority; record the application time.
17. Wound packing is used to control bleeding in a…
Answer: B. Packing gauze tightly into a deep wound (often with continued pressure) controls bleeding where a tourniquet can't reach — junctional sites like the groin, armpit, or neck.
18. Which set of findings most suggests shock (hypoperfusion)?
Answer: C. Early shock = the body shunting blood to vital organs: pale/cool/clammy skin, a fast weak pulse, and restlessness/anxiety. A falling blood pressure is a late, ominous sign.
Medical emergencies · Week 6
19. A bystander suddenly has a drooping face, slurred speech, and can't hold one arm up. Beyond FAST, the "BE-" in BE-FAST adds checks for…
Answer: D. BE-FAST = Balance, Eyes, Face, Arms, Speech, Time. The "BE" captures sudden balance loss and vision changes that plain FAST can miss. Note the time of onset — it drives treatment.
20. An unresponsive person with pinpoint pupils and very slow, shallow breathing is found with empty pill bottles. The targeted reversal agent is…
Answer: A. Pinpoint pupils plus respiratory depression suggest opioid overdose. Naloxone is a competitive opioid-receptor antagonist; support ventilation, and re-dosing may be needed as it can wear off.
21. A person with a known bee-sting allergy develops hives, swelling, and wheezing within minutes. First-line treatment is…
Answer: B. Anaphylaxis is treated with intramuscular epinephrine (auto-injector into the outer thigh), which reverses airway swelling and low blood pressure. Call EMS — a second dose may be needed.
22. A known diabetic is conscious, shaky, sweaty, and confused but can still swallow and protect their airway. The appropriate treatment is…
Answer: C. Symptomatic hypoglycemia in a conscious patient who can swallow is treated with oral glucose. Never give anything by mouth to someone who can't protect their airway.
EMS operations, legal & ethics · Week 7
23. An alert, oriented adult who clearly understands the risks refuses care. Treating them anyway could expose you to a claim of battery. This illustrates the principle of…
Answer: D. A competent, informed adult may refuse care. Implied consent applies to the unresponsive; document the refusal and the risks you explained. (Negligence, by contrast, requires duty, breach, harm, and causation.)
Sleep, focus, addiction & frontier medicine · Weeks 8–10
24. The proposed mechanism behind the "morning sunlight" sleep protocol — and the honest caveat — is best stated as…
Answer: A. Morning light cues a cortisol peak and sets the circadian clock (and the body's temperature-minimum reference). The mechanism is sound; precise "X minutes at Y lux" prescriptions are still preliminary. Separately, delaying caffeine ~90–120 min lets overnight adenosine clear rather than be merely masked.
25. Which statement about addiction neuroscience and frontier medicine is the most accurate and appropriately hedged?
Answer: B. Reward floods the VTA→nucleus-accumbens dopamine pathway; with repetition, receptors down-regulate, yielding tolerance and craving. AI (AlphaFold structure prediction, generative molecular design, oncology models) is a promising accelerator — the dog-cancer idea is an aspiration, not a marketed cure — and most supplements have mechanistic rationale but modest, preliminary evidence.
